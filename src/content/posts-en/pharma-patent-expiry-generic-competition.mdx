## Pharmaceutical Patent Expiries and Portfolio Restructuring Pressure on Drugmakers

Major global drugmakers face a patent cliff. Over the past three months, the market has reflected this reality. **JNJ** rose 18.8% in three months from $269.99 on September 18, 2026, while **ABBV** climbed 22.79% to $263.96. Over the same period, **MRK** surged 29.74% to $146.87. Yet behind these gains lie different revenue-structure crises at each company. **PFE** managed only an 11.64% three-month gain to $27.66, suggesting the market is already distinguishing between each company's capacity to respond to patent expiries.

Pharmaceutical patent expiries are a fundamental threat to the industry. Once patent protection ends, generic drugs enter the market and prices collapse. The revenue loss on individual drugs is massive. For drugmakers where a single medicine generates core revenues, patent expiry means an unavoidable revenue cliff. The impact is especially severe for companies whose main earnings are concentrated in a handful of products.

### Why Act Now: Four Pressure Factors

**1. Slowing Revenue Growth in a Mature Market**

Drugmakers' annual revenue growth is sluggish. As of September 18, 2026, PFE's year-over-year growth stood at just 2.6%. MRK was at 5.1%, JNJ at 6.6%, and ABBV at 10.2%. Without patent expiry headwinds, these figures would signal industry maturity. But as existing revenue sources disappear, new growth must fill the gap.

**2. Diverging Net Margins Under Stress**

JNJ boasts a healthy net margin of 21.5%. ABBV came in at 9.8%, PFE at 6.8%. MRK trails at 4.8%. MRK's P/E of 119.7 reflects weak profitability paired with high valuations—attributable only to expectations of future growth. Patent expiries risk dampening those expectations.

**3. Forced R&D Investment and Intensifying Competition**

As legacy drug sales decline, firms must invest proportionally in new pipelines. Drug development takes over a decade and success rates are low. So while investing in their own R&D, drugmakers must also acquire smaller firms with novel candidates. This represents repeated cash outflows year after year.

**4. Rising M&A Dependence and Peak-Out Risk**

To sidestep the patent cliff, securing new drugs is essential. This animates the M&A market. But when all firms pursue the same targets simultaneously, acquisition prices rise. High-priced acquisitions also carry greater failure risk. ABBV's 10.2% year-over-year growth likely includes M&A-driven gains.

### Structural Differences Persist

**JNJ's Strong Net Margins**: At 21.5%, this reflects the advantage of a massive $652.7 billion market-cap firm. Economies of scale and asset diversification absorb patent-expiry shocks.

**ABBV's Growth Momentum**: Its 10.2% year-over-year rate leads the group. Recent acquisition integration may signal success. Yet whether this growth persists depends on the next pipeline.

**PFE's Vulnerability**: A 6.8% net margin and 2.6% year-over-year growth mark it as the weakest. At $158.5 billion in market cap, it is also the smallest of the four. This is why PFE ranks first among the group likely to feel patent expiry most acutely.

### Conclusion

While pharmaceutical patent expiries are an industry-wide fate, each company's capacity to respond differs starkly. In a period of sluggish revenue growth, the patent cliff mandates expensive paths: new drug development and M&A. The market has already priced this in, and going forward, each firm's pipeline progress and acquisition success will determine medium-term share performance.
