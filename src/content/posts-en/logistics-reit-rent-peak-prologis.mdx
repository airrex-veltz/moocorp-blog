## Is the 'Peak' in Logistics Rents Now in the Past?

Dividend coverage among logistics REITs is bifurcating. **PLD** (Prologis, Inc.) closed at $129.60 (as of November 5, 2026), down 6.80% over three months. If rent growth has stalled and vacancy recovery is slower than expected, which of the growth assumptions supporting dividends will crack first? The market is pricing in an answer to that question.

## The Market Environment PLD Faces: Growth Deceleration

PLD is the world's largest owner of logistics centers. Over the past six months, its stock has ranged from $123.43 to $152.14 (as of November 5, 2026), a swing of roughly $29. On fundamentals, it has a market cap of $126 billion, a P/E of 28.5, and a net profit margin of 43.1%. However, revenue growth has slowed to just 10.2% year-over-year, a sharp deceleration from the double-digit rises of 2021–2023.

## Why the Decline Now: Four Pressure Points

**1. The Inflection Point in Rent Growth**: Post-pandemic inflation and e-commerce boom drove logistics rents up in double digits for three straight years. But as rate hikes began, tenants negotiating new contracts started receiving smaller annual increases. The 6.80% three-month stock decline signals the market accepting this inflection point. Rent renewal gains from existing tenants are now certain to shrink.

**2. Uncertainty in Vacancy Recovery Speed**: Post-pandemic consolidation and tenant restructuring have left some logistics centers with above-normal vacancy rates. If economic growth disappoints, vacancy resolution will slow. This risks the tenant stability that underpins dividends.

**3. Pressure on Dividend Coverage Convergence**: A net margin of 43.1% paired with a P/E of 28.5 suggests the market both expects strong future growth and that the company is squeezing every dollar of current earnings to maintain its dividend. If rent growth stops, it must cut the dividend or raise debt.

**4. What 10.2% Revenue Growth Means**: Slowing sales growth signals that new project completions and tenant additions are falling short of plan. A REIT's fundamental model rests on rising property values plus rent growth; if the rent part stops, there is no guarantee that property values rise on their own.

## Why PLD Anyway: Market Dominance and Scale

**Geographic Moat**: PLD has diversified its logistics centers across the United States, Europe, Japan, and other major e-commerce markets. A single country's slowdown cannot easily dent overall performance. It also has top-tier liquidity and credit ratings among REITs.

**Long-Term Lease Contracts as a Cushion**: Post-pandemic tenants typically want 5–10 year terms. Existing tenants renewing their leases, even at lower increases, keep base rents and occupancy intact. Viewing long-term cash flow, the picture is stable even if short-term growth falters.

## In Summary

The "rent peak" debate among logistics REITs is a market reassessment in the face of rent growth deceleration and dividend coverage pressure. PLD's global scale and footprint offset its weaknesses, but the 6.80% three-month decline signals that uncertainty around growth rates has now materialized.
