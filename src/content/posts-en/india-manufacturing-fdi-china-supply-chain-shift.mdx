## Global Supply Chain Restructuring to Reduce China Dependency, with India Emerging as a New Manufacturing Hub

As supply chain security concerns deepen among major nations, the axis of manufacturing is shifting. With reshoring policies pursued by US and European governments and heightened geopolitical tensions accelerating change, India is rapidly evolving from a simple production site into an alternative hub within the global manufacturing ecosystem. This reflects how corporations and governments are reshaping economic structures across South Asia to diversify supply chains away from China.

## How Reshoring Policies Are Driving Supply Chain Restructuring

Reshoring and "friendshoring" policies pursued by the US, Europe, and Japan over recent years are not about reducing China dependency but rather **changing the object of dependence**. The US Inflation Reduction Act (IRA) and Europe's Green Deal industrial plan exemplify this. They provide subsidies and tariff preferences for products from specific countries under the guise of technological sovereignty and supply chain security. Simultaneously, escalating geopolitical tensions are shifting site selection from a "cost optimization" basis to a "risk avoidance" basis. Supply chain instability for strategic materials like Taiwanese semiconductors, Philippine minerals, and Chinese chemical feedstock has made production diversification a necessity rather than an option.

## Four Reasons Why India Commands Attention

**1. Availability of Low-Cost, Large-Scale Workforce**: India has developed a rapid workforce training system for manufacturing while maintaining wage levels still lower than China. Particularly as rising labor costs in China accelerate factory relocations in labor-intensive sectors like semiconductors, electronics, pharmaceuticals, and textiles, India possesses a demographic dividend enabling it to quickly recruit hundreds of thousands of additional workers.

**2. Government Manufacturing Promotion Policies (PLI and Beyond)**: India's central government is expanding Production-Linked Incentive (PLI) schemes under the "Make in India" policy. It provides long-term subsidies and tax benefits to promote domestic manufacturing across key sectors including semiconductors, displays, automobiles, and pharmaceuticals. Alongside regional industrial park development and labor flexibility reforms, these efforts lower entry barriers for businesses.

**3. Expansion of Direct Investment from the US and Japan**: The trend of Apple's supply chain diversifying to Vietnam and India is emblematic. As US intentions to reduce China dependency align with India's welcoming stance, joint ventures in advanced sectors like semiconductors, electronic components, and pharmaceuticals have surged. Japanese manufacturers are also restructuring their Indian operations under "post-China" strategies.

**4. Convergence of Geopolitical Security Interests**: In Indo-Pacific strategy, India is expected to serve as a counterbalance to the US sphere, with economic cooperation increasingly treated as an extension of security alliances. India itself, experiencing border disputes with China, has made economic self-sufficiency through manufacturing advancement a strategic priority. This is precisely the moment when these two interests not only align but reinforce each other.

## Opportunity and Constraint at Work Simultaneously

India's emergence as a new manufacturing hub is not a foregone conclusion. Physical infrastructure—roads, electricity, ports—remains underdeveloped compared to China, and closing the labor productivity gap requires training and technology transfer. Regulatory uncertainty and regional variation also hinder foreign investment. However, China's shift toward automation and upgrading has created a hollowing of low-value processes, while US and European pressure for supply chain diversification offers India a historic opportunity. The window where labor meets policy may close without warning.

Global manufacturing's geographical restructuring is not merely a matter of corporate site selection but rather a recalibration of economic influence between nations. India's rise does not necessarily signal China's decline, but the era of China-centric concentration is clearly coming to an end.
