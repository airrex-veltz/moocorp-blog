## Pharma Companies Ahead of Patent Cliff Eye Last Chance for Price Increases

Leading biopharmaceutical patent expirations loom, confronting incumbent pharma companies with structural crisis. Facing biosimilar price decline, individual company financial health and response strategies diverge.

## Current Position of Pharma Companies

As of 2026-10-14, major pharma companies' financials show notable variance. **JNJ** net profit margin 16.2%, **PFE** 6.8%, **AMGN** 22.9% show markedly different profitability. Simultaneously, revenue growth (YoY) traces different paths: **JNJ** 5.9%, **PFE** 2.6%, **AMGN** 9.5%, **RHHBY** -1.2%. **RHHBY** already records negative growth, indicating patent cliff pressure is currently ongoing.

## Why Patent Cliffs Determine Pricing Strategy: Four Factors

**1. Signal of revenue growth deceleration**: Low growth rates indicate high individual drug dependence. **PFE**'s 2.6% growth hints at profit cliff risk when core drugs' patents expire. Conversely, **AMGN**'s 9.5% growth indicates relatively progressed pipeline diversification.

**2. Profitability gap and price defense capability**: High and low net profit margins determine company resilience after biosimilar entry. **AMGN**'s 22.9% high net profit margin offers room to absorb biosimilar price impact. Conversely, **PFE**'s 6.8% makes price decline directly translate to loss risk.

**3. Market confidence variance revealed by PER**: **PFE**'s PER 38.2 is highest in sector, reflecting market's assessment that today's profits will collapse tomorrow. Conversely, **AMGN** 26.5 and **RHHBY** 22.8 relatively recognize current profitability substantively. High PER = patent cliff anxiety.

**4. Strategic choices in M&A and price increase timing**: Last chance before patent expiration offers two paths. One: price increases securing cash before cliff (2-3 year window). Other: combining with competitively strong pipeline companies to diversify long-term revenue. **JNJ**'s USD 612.6B market cap versus **RHHBY**'s 363.2B reflects M&A dominance gap.

## Yet Why Still Pharma: Structural Strengths and Weaknesses

**Strength: AMGN's growth and profitability combined**. Revenue growth 9.5% and net profit margin 22.9% signal shock absorption capacity against patent cliff impact. Indicates sufficiently matured pipeline.

**Weakness: PFE's extremely high PER**. 38.2 PER means current stock price relies on future earnings growth. When patent cliff materializes, revaluation pressure from earnings decline carries high probability.

**Weakness: RHHBY's already negative growth**. -1.2% revenue growth suggests patent cliff is not coming but already underway. Structural restructuring intensity could be highest.

## Summary

Pharma companies ahead of patent cliff split into two camps. Strong players like **AMGN** securing both profitability and growth have breathing room to cross cliff. Conversely, companies simultaneously exhibiting low growth, low profitability, and high PER face forced choice between price increases as final card and M&A as gamble. The cliff timeline is fixed, opportunities limited.
