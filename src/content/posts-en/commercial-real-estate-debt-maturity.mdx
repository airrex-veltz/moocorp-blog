## Office and Retail Vacancies Crush REIT Earnings

The pandemic surge in office vacancies combined with structural shifts in retail is creating a dual pressure on commercial real estate operators: plummeting tenant occupancy rates coupled with refinancing deadlines for massive mortgage debt borrowed at low interest rates now maturing at high rates. As asset values decline, collateral values decline with them, deepening structural crisis.

## The Reality for Commercial Real Estate Operators: Revenue Growth Turns to Contraction

**Realty Income** (**O**), one of the largest commercial real estate REITs in the U.S., closed at $59.26 per share on November 12 with a market cap of $56.2B. On the same day, industrial real estate specialist **Rexford Industrial** (**REXR**) traded at $37.64, representing an $8.2B market cap. The earnings disparity between the two companies reveals the depth of the crisis.

O maintains a net profit margin of 21.8% and revenue growth of 10.2% year-over-year, appearing relatively healthy. However, a P/E ratio of 40.4 exceeds historical averages, suggesting the market has already priced in substantial future growth. REXR's situation is far more severe. Its net profit margin stands at -47.1%, indicating loss-making operations, while revenue growth is -5.6%, contracting. Industrial real estate was thought to be weathering the storm relatively better, yet the evidence of vacancy pressure and rent hike resistance penetrating even this segment is clear.

The **State Street SPDR S&P Retail ETF** (**XRT**), which tracks the retail sector, closed at $90.67 on November 12, posting a one-month return of +7.05% and a three-month return of +2.15%. Despite the rally, a P/E ratio of 15.2 sits below the broader market (roughly 18–20), indicating the entire retail sector is trading at a discount in market sentiment.

## Why Commercial Real Estate Operators Are Under Pressure Now: Four Drivers

**1. Structural Office Vacancy from the Pandemic**: Since 2020, office occupancy has failed to recover as remote work became permanent. Companies have reduced office footprints and maintained flexible work policies, leaving high-cost urban offices unable to resolve chronic vacancy problems. As tenants shrink or decline to renew leases at previous rates, operator revenue pressure persists.

**2. Irreversible Structural Change in Retail**: E-commerce growth continues driving storefront closures. As traditional shopping malls and street retail lose tenants, property owners lose negotiating power over rents. Remaining tenants, struggling with deteriorating operations, lack the capacity to absorb rent increases.

**3. Mortgage Maturity and Refinancing at Higher Rates**: Mortgages borrowed in the low-rate mid-2010s are now maturing between 2024 and 2026. Capital borrowed in an ultra-low-rate environment must be refinanced at today's elevated rates. With property values depressed, debt-backed financing becomes scarce, while refinancing rates exceed debt-service capacity.

**4. Asset Depreciation Forces Loss Recognition**: Rising vacancy and falling rental rates force asset revaluation. Declining collateral values can breach existing loan covenants (particularly LTV and debt-to-equity ratios), requiring capital injections or asset sales for rebalancing. This signals long-term cash flow deterioration.

## Why REITs Can Still Survive: Structural Strengths vs. Vulnerabilities

**Strengths**: REITs are structured around dividend-based earnings models. O's 10.2% revenue growth and 21.8% net margin demonstrate sustained capacity to fund dividend distributions. Industrial real estate (REXR's focus) benefits from solid tenant demand driven by e-commerce logistics. With significant market price corrections already underway, downside room may be limited.

**Weaknesses**: High leverage and low liquidity are fatal. REXR's -47.1% net margin signals operating cash flow insufficient to cover interest expense and valuation losses. Even O, with stronger assets, trades at a P/E of 40.4 in overvalued territory, carrying substantial downside risk should rates rise further or economic growth slow.

## Bottom Line

The commercial real estate sector faces simultaneous shocks from structural change and financial environment shifts. High P/E ratios and negative net margins signal the market has already priced in crisis, yet continuing refinancing pressure through 2027 suggests further downside remains likely. Industrial real estate shows relative resilience, but office and retail face structural recovery obstacles.
