As regional AI companies like Deepseek and Mistral rapidly grow with open-source models, the structure of the global AI market is undergoing fundamental change. The large language model market dominated by OpenAI and Google is entering an inflection point where big tech monopolies visibly weaken.

This change stems from a deeper mechanism of technology democratization. The computing costs required to train large-scale pre-trained models are gradually declining, and as the open-source community's accumulated optimization techniques and fine-tuning methodologies become public, entry barriers for late arrivals are collapsing rapidly. Development companies that previously relied on closed-form API models are beginning to build their own models or seek lower-cost alternatives within this context.

**First, maturation of edge computing technology accelerates on-premises deployment.** Traditionally, the high infrastructure costs of generative AI could only be borne in the cloud. However, as lightweighting technologies such as quantization and knowledge distillation advance, enterprises can now run sufficiently practical AI models on their own servers or edge devices. The security advantage of not sending sensitive data externally and the performance advantage of reducing network latency are powerfully pushing on-premises investment.

**Second, regional companies' competitiveness emerges from global companies' high API pricing.** As cloud AI service costs become serious fixed expenses for application developers, switching to low-cost open-source models or self-deployment options has emerged as economic necessity. Not only startups but also established enterprises seek to reduce API dependence for margin improvement.

**Third, regulation and regional protectionism reinforce this trend.** As export restrictions on U.S. company AI models and regional data sovereignty policies strengthen, regional companies' models gain political-economic prominence. This guarantees regional AI companies a structural demand base.

**Fourth, early dominance does not function as sustained advantage.** As the performance gap in AI models gradually converges, the lock-in effect of "we must use OpenAI" weakens. The moment open-source models become sufficiently good, the reasons for choice rapidly shift from performance to price and deployment freedom.

Amid this, big tech's dilemma deepens. The more profitable AI business becomes, the greater the demand for competitors and self-deployment options simultaneously. Pressure to maximize short-term profit through monopoly collides with structural pressure to mitigate market fragmentation. As long as this paradox remains unresolved, the growth of regional AI companies and on-premises deployment will inevitably continue.
