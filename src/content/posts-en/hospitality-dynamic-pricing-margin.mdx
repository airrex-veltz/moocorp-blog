## Why Hotel and Cruise Room Rates Fluctuate So Dramatically

As AI revenue management systems proliferate, the price volatility of dynamic pricing is surging sharply. This inflates corporate short-term margins but simultaneously brings customer churn and regulatory risk.

## Dynamic Pricing Is Already the Standard

Both Marriott (**MAR**) and Royal Caribbean (**RCL**) have built AI-powered revenue management platforms as core infrastructure. These systems monitor booking timing, competitor pricing, demand forecasts, and inventory levels in real time to calculate optimal prices. As of October 1, 2026, **MAR** closed at $354.79 (1-month change +6.05%), while **RCL** was at $269.99 (1-month change +2.22%), showing near-term strength—but this signals that the illusion of fixed-price profitability is fading.

## Why Is Volatility Worsening Now: Four Factors

**1. Democratization of AI and Intensifying Competition**

Until recent years, dynamic pricing was the monopoly of large chains. But as cloud-based revenue management platforms (travel-focused SaaS) became mainstream, mid-size hotels and boutique chains entered the market. Now all competitors in the same area use the same algorithm, so when one hotel raises price, adjacent rivals respond instantly. Price adjustments moved from once or twice per week to hourly, even minute-by-minute.

**2. Consumer Distrust and Regulatory Scrutiny**

Dynamic pricing is perceived as "discriminatory pricing," creating fatigue. For example, quoting the same room at $180 for customer A and $320 for customer B is the fastest path to eroding trust. EU regulators are already expanding investigations into "personalized pricing," and the U.S. FTC is raising its attention too. Under this regulatory pressure, companies limit discount spreads for transparency—making profit improvement difficult.

**3. The Power of Online Price Comparison**

As metasearch platforms like Google Hotel Search, Booking.com, and Airbnb evolved, consumers learned competitor prices within three seconds. Historically, they would "book at this price," but now they abandon it saying "that hotel is cheaper." From the company's perspective, demand elasticity when raising price has surged, and the margin-improvement effect is evaporating.

**4. The Double Squeeze on Margins**

**MAR**'s net profit margin remains high at 35.0%, but this combines booking fee revenue and system efficiency gains. Costs to operate, maintain, and improve dynamic pricing systems are rising steeply. Back-office investments in data infrastructure, AI model training, cybersecurity, and compliance review are unavoidable. As a result, price volatility rises while net profit stagnates.

## Why **MAR** and **RCL** Remain Strong

**Global Scale Cost Advantage**: Marriott commands a portfolio of 1.3 million rooms; Royal Caribbean operates 27 cruise ships. They can spread the fixed costs of revenue management systems and overwhelm smaller competitors in data volume. As of October 1, 2026, **MAR** showed revenue growth of 11.1% (**RCL** 6.5%), still driving volume growth.

**Brand Loyalty and Ancillary Revenue**: Dynamic pricing doesn't move only room rates. It fine-tunes ancillary revenue—resort fees, early check-in, upgrades, spa and food packages—with digital levers more precisely. Large chains have greater ability to calibrate these triggers.

## Conclusion

As dynamic pricing technology becomes universal, a paradox emerges: volatility increases while genuine revenue improvement becomes harder. **MAR** and **RCL** weather this surge through scale and technology leadership, but consumer trust and regulatory risk remain variables. Investors should scrutinize not simply "revenue grows" but "can margins sustain?"
