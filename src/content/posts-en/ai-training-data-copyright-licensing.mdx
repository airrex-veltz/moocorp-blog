## Mandatory Copyright Licensing Debates Shake AI Development Economics

Discussions on mandatory royalty payments to creators for copyrighted works used in generative AI model training are advancing at the national and industry level. When this becomes reality, large technology companies' model development costs will surge, while competition for securing high-quality training data is expected to intensify further. This forces a fundamental reassessment of current large-scale data collection strategies.

## Structural Context of Mandatory Copyright Licensing

Generative AI models are trained on vast amounts of text, images, and voice data across the internet. This training data includes large quantities of copyrighted works owned by individuals and institutions, such as news articles, books, music, and artwork. Over recent years, content creators and publishers have filed legal suits claiming their works are being used without consent. In the United States, Europe, South Korea, and other countries, legislative discussions reflecting this are intensifying. Once mandatory royalty payments are introduced, the licensing fees technology companies must pay for model training will inevitably increase sharply from the current zero.

## Why Model Development Economics Are Shaking Now: Four Factors

**1. Risk of Skyrocketing Training Costs**: Large model training currently minimizes development costs by collecting data at virtually no cost. If even an average royalty of around 1 cent per work is applied, when multiplied across tens of billions to trillions of training instances, unit development costs more than double. This extends the model iteration cycle and raises barriers to market entry for new companies.

**2. Intensifying Concentration of Data Ownership**: When mandatory royalty policies become reality, companies that possess sufficient proprietary content and user-generated platform data gain absolute advantages. Platform companies like **META** and **GOOGL** can freely leverage social media and search-generated data, but startups with high external data dependency become relatively weaker. Ultimately, wealth concentration accelerates.

**3. Constraints on Open Model Ecosystems**: The tradition of open-source foundations and academic communities releasing free models is shaken. If royalties are charged even for training open-source models, public release and maintenance become economically burdensome. This produces a result contrary to technology democratization.

**4. Regulatory Uncertainty Across Countries**: The EU, US, UK, and South Korea are each pursuing different legislative directions. Given the nature of global models that can use data from any country, whichever regulation becomes reality will impact overall development costs. This uncertainty itself impedes investment and technology planning.

## Yet Major Technology Companies' Response Strategies: Structural Strengths and Weaknesses

**GOOGL and MSFT's Data Control**: **GOOGL** (closing price $354.97 on September 21, 2026) has strong proprietary data ownership through search, YouTube, and Android. **MSFT** ($501.61) leads model development through its partnership with OpenAI and can also leverage enterprise cloud customer data. Both companies have structures that allow them to lower dependence on external licensing.

**META's Independent Model Path**: **META** ($741.25) has pursued a strategy to develop and deploy LLaMA-based models in-house to avoid licensing costs. It has effectively accelerated market entry through social platform data and open-source-based models. However, if mandatory royalty policies are applied to open-source models as well, this path could also face constraints.

**NVDA as an Outsider to Cost Discussions**: **NVDA** ($227.38), as an AI chip manufacturer, is distanced from training data licensing costs. Rather, even if copyright royalty mandates raise technology companies' development costs, demand for training infrastructure (GPUs) could remain unchanged or potentially increase.

## Conclusion

Mandatory copyright licensing simultaneously reshapes both the cost structure and competitive landscape of generative AI development. The gap between companies with abundant proprietary data assets and those without is expected to widen, and barriers to model development entry will likely rise. In the short term, regulatory uncertainty could serve as a variable affecting technology investment and model release timelines.
