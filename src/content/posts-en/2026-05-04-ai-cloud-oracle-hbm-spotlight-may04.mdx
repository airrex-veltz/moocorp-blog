## Key Trends and Catalysts

### Cloud Infrastructure Investment Boom

Infrastructure spending is surging as companies migrate AI workloads to the cloud. Oracle's strong rally is interpreted as signaling expanded large contracts for its Gen2 Cloud platform. Amid intensifying competition among AWS, Azure, and GCP, Oracle is succeeding in cloud transformation by leveraging its legacy database customer base.

### AI Chips and HBM Demand Cycle

NVIDIA fell 0.56% due to short-term profit-taking, but high-bandwidth memory (HBM) demand remains robust. Blackwell architecture shipments are set to ramp up in Q2 2026, with the HBM supply chain drawing attention amid rising AI server demand.

<Callout type="catalyst" title="HBM Supply Expansion">
SK Hynix and Samsung plan to double their HBM3E production capacity in 2026 compared to the prior year. SK Hynix maintains its number one HBM market share through an exclusive supply agreement with NVIDIA, while Samsung is pursuing aggressive investment aimed at expanding market share.
</Callout>

### Accelerating Enterprise AI Adoption

Adoption of Microsoft Azure AI and Google Cloud Vertex AI is increasing. Salesforce is integrating AI into CRM through Einstein GPT, strengthening its subscription model, with AI feature ARR growth expected to stand out in its Q1 results scheduled for mid-May.

<Callout type="data">
Microsoft's Copilot subscription expansion and Azure AI revenue are expected to grow 40% year-over-year. In the enterprise AI market, Microsoft is securing a competitive edge through OpenAI integration.
</Callout>

## Key Stock Analysis

### Oracle: Cloud Infrastructure's Comeback

**Current Price $171.83 | Market Cap $494B | +6.47%**

Oracle is demonstrating a successful transition from legacy databases to the cloud. Growth in its Gen2 Cloud platform and expectations for major contract announcements have driven the stock higher. While at an ecosystem disadvantage compared to AWS and Azure, Oracle's cloud migration strategy leveraging its existing customer base is proving effective.

### NVIDIA: Is the Correction an Opportunity?

**Current Price $198.45 | Market Cap $4.82T | -0.56%**

NVIDIA's near-term correction is interpreted as profit-taking by the AI chip market leader. Given the ramp-up of Blackwell architecture shipments and persistent HBM demand, the correction could be a buying opportunity. However, a lofty P/E ratio (75x) and China export controls present risk factors.

### Microsoft and Salesforce: Enterprise AI Competition

**Microsoft Current Price $414.44 | Market Cap $3.08T | +1.63%**
**Salesforce Current Price $183.82 | Market Cap $150B | +4.13%**

Microsoft is solidifying its position as the enterprise AI leader through Azure and OpenAI integration. Salesforce is pursuing differentiation by integrating AI into CRM via Einstein GPT. Both companies have stable revenue structures based on subscription models, but competition from Microsoft Dynamics remains a challenge for Salesforce.

## Korean-Listed Stocks: Core to AI Memory Supply Chain

### SK Hynix (000660) and Samsung Electronics (005930)

Korean companies are playing an increasingly important role in the AI memory supply chain. SK Hynix is strengthening its position as the number one HBM market share holder, backed by an exclusive supply agreement with NVIDIA. Samsung is pursuing aggressive investment aimed at expanding HBM market share and securing foundry AI chip orders.

**Key Catalysts**: Sustained HBM price appreciation, rising AI server demand
**Risks**: Weak commodity DRAM pricing, slowing Chinese economy

### NAVER (035420) and Kakao (035720)

As a Korean local cloud platform, NAVER is expanding cloud AI services based on HyperCLOVA X (Korean-language LLM). Kakao is advancing integration of its Kakao i platform and KakaoTalk AI chatbot. Local companies' cloud transition and AI chatbot subscription models are growth drivers, but overcoming the technology gap versus U.S. cloud providers and scale economy limitations remain challenges.

## Sector Risks

<Callout type="risk">
Major AI stocks trade near historically high P/E multiples, creating valuation pressure. Delayed rate cuts could reduce cloud spending, and regulatory headwinds from the EU AI Act and U.S. antitrust investigations pose risks. The cost competitiveness of Chinese AI models (DeepSeek, Baidu) also merits long-term attention.
</Callout>
