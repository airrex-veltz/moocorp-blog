## Gene Editing: From Regulation to Funding

As the regulatory environment for gene editing technology becomes clearer, the pace of biotech venture financing is accelerating. Investors are turning their attention back to technologies freed from decades of "regulatory constraints."

## Technology Barriers to Entry and the Role of Regulation

Gene editing technology has become more accessible to startups than pharmaceutical companies, as the cost of tools like CRISPR-Cas9 has fallen and accuracy has improved. However, lower technical barriers to entry are separate from regulatory barriers.

This was the main challenge over the past decade. The U.S. FDA and European EMA did not specify approval pathways for gene editing therapies. It was unclear what data had to be submitted or what criteria determined the transition from animal testing to human trials. This uncertainty delayed venture capital decision-making.

## Why Capital Is Flowing Now: Four Factors

**1. Specification of regulatory frameworks**: The FDA and EMA have published or are publishing specific guidelines for gene editing therapies. Being able to understand what must be proven for approval has itself become the foundation of business planning. As uncertainty decreases, due diligence periods shorten.

**2. Visibility of clinical progress**: Regulatory clarity signals that "clinical entry is possible." Startups can now engage in Pre-IND meetings with regulators and show the results to investors. It becomes an inter-institutional agreement rather than a promise on paper.

**3. Shift in investor sentiment**: Psychology is a major factor in the biotech venture ecosystem. Progressing from early-stage funding to later rounds requires confidence that "the path is open." The moment regulators begin speaking clearly, letters of intent (LOIs) from major funds emerge.

**4. Continuous cost reduction in technology**: Costs for measurement, analysis, and cell culture continue to decline. From a founder's perspective, more clinical data can be generated with less capital. Investors also want to see more progress for the same dollar amount.

## Persistent Unresolved Issues: Structural Weaknesses

**Characteristics of long-term investment**: Gene editing therapies take 10 years to develop and 5–7 years in clinical trials. Regulation can change again during this period. Investors need sufficient capital to endure this decades-long cycle.

**Off-target effects and safety**: No matter how sophisticated the technology, the possibility of affecting DNA outside the target site remains. If one patient experiences an unexpected adverse effect, the entire industry halts for months. Even with clear regulation, there is no countermeasure for "unforeseen risks."

**Intellectual property saturation**: Litigation over who owns CRISPR patents continues. The IP barrier that new startups must cross remains high.

## Conclusion

The clarification of the regulatory environment has opened a window of "predictability" rather than overcome technical limitations. Capital flows not by chasing technology, but by answering the question "When can we get our money back?" Gene editing has become better able to provide clearer answers to that question, and the biotech venture funding cycle is responding accordingly.
