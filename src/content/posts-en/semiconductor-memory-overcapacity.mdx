## Memory Overcapacity and Semiconductor Price Collapse: HBM Competition and Profitability Divergence

The semiconductor market in the second half of 2026 displays a stark dual structure. The mainstream memory (DRAM, NAND) segment is experiencing price collapse due to oversupply, while high-bandwidth memory (HBM) for AI accelerators remains in a supply shortage state. This divergence is expected to significantly split the profitability of major semiconductor players in 2027.

Stock price movements reflect this situation intuitively. NVDA recorded the largest decline at -21.29% in one month and -21.98% in three months, while TSMC also showed declines of -12.76% in one month and -12.30% in three months. In contrast, QCOM declined only -7.00% in one month and maintained a slight gain of +2.44% on a three-month basis. This is not simply a deterioration of market conditions but rather selective weakness linked to each company's portfolio composition.

Profitability metrics reveal even more distinct differences. NVDA's net profit margin of 62.5% remains the industry's highest, and a revenue growth rate of 103.2% reflects ultra-high growth. However, a PER of 17.8 represents a significantly lower multiple compared to companies with similar growth levels. TSMC maintains a solid trajectory with a net profit margin of 51.1% and revenue growth of 50.9%, while receiving a relatively higher valuation at a PER of 23.7. QCOM's net profit margin has declined to 31.6%, while revenue is contracting at -8.4%, suggesting a business structure with relatively high dependence on mainstream memory.

HBM competition is a key variable that will determine 2027 revenue scenarios. NVDA, as a GPU designer, has strong internal HBM procurement capabilities, while TSMC is leveraging its foundry position to secure high-value chip orders. Meanwhile, in a weak mainstream memory demand environment, companies unable to ride the cycle must endure pricing pressure. QCOM's declining revenue trend demonstrates how vulnerable a portfolio with high PC and mobile memory dependence is in the current cycle.

It is likely that this divergence will intensify through mid-2027. While HBM prices remain on a gradual adjustment trajectory, mainstream memory is expected to continue facing supply adjustment pressures. Companies like NVDA and TSMC, focused on AI and high-value chips, are well-positioned to defend profitability, while those reliant on diversified memory portfolios appear unlikely to find revenue growth momentum. The selective weakness in stock prices can be interpreted as the market's early reaction to these microscopic business differences.
