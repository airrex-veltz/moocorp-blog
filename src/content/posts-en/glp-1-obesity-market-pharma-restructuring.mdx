## Obesity Drug Success: Big Pharma's 'Survival Game'

The rapid expansion of obesity treatment drugs is fundamentally reshaking the structure of the global pharmaceutical industry. As GLP-1 receptor agonists transform the medical paradigm, pharmaceutical companies that previously specialized in blood glucose control face an entirely different competitive landscape. With revenue restructuring and large-scale mergers and acquisitions (M&A) occurring in sequence, investors' biggest concern is who will ultimately dominate this market.

## The GLP-1 Disruption of the Pharmaceutical Industry Landscape

GLP-1 drugs rapidly expanded in diabetes treatment after 2022, and after proving their efficacy in obesity treatment, opened a market of an entirely different scale. The global obese population is estimated at approximately 800 million, with over 100 million estimated to be eligible for pharmaceutical treatment. Market research firms forecast the 2030 GLP-1 market at KRW 5 trillion annually.

In this process, the existing landscape of diabetes and cardiovascular drugs is rapidly changing. **Eli Lilly (LLY)** leads the diabetes market with Mounjaro, **Pfizer (PFE)** and **Merck (MRK)** are rapidly expanding their obesity drug candidate pipeline, and **Johnson & Johnson (JNJ)** is acquiring multiple drug candidates to strengthen its relatively weak portfolio in this area.

## Why Portfolio Restructuring Is Urgent Now: Four Factors

**1. The Imperative to Capture the Obesity Market**: With GLP-1's efficacy in obesity treatment confirmed, an opportunity far larger than the diabetes market has emerged. Losing this would mean losing a core growth engine for the next decade. This is why each pharmaceutical company is pursuing equity investment, licensing agreements, and in-house development simultaneously.

**2. The Threat of Cannibalization of Existing Drugs**: Cardiovascular drugs, antihypertensives, and certain diabetes drugs are being replaced by GLP-1's comprehensive effects (weight loss + blood glucose + blood pressure improvement). Pfizer's cardiovascular portfolio and Johnson & Johnson's diabetes drug line are particularly exposed to this potential risk.

**3. The Urgency of Closing Competitive Technology Gaps**: As Eli Lilly and industry leaders proactively expand their product lineups, trailing companies must fill the gap through technology acquisitions. Merck's biotech acquisitions and Pfizer's new drug candidate licensing are evidence of this.

**4. Intensifying Insurance Constraints and Price Negotiations**: The high price of GLP-1 drugs ($1,000–$1,500 per month) and limited insurance coverage are becoming bottlenecks to market entry. Overcoming this requires developing generics or lower-cost competing drugs, which demands separate acquisitions or partnerships.

## Why Big Pharma Still Has the Advantage: Structural Strengths and Weaknesses

**Strengths: Capital and Distribution Networks**
Major pharmaceutical companies possess enormous R&D capital and global sales networks. Eli Lilly, Pfizer, Johnson & Johnson, and Merck all have the financial capacity to invest tens of billions of dollars in GLP-1 drug development and acquisitions, and once they secure a product, they can immediately leverage their existing sales channels within the healthcare system.

**Weaknesses: Technology Gap as Latecomers**
However, in the GLP-1 space, development lags behind a few leading companies. In-house development could take several more years, so paying premium prices in the current acquisition competition is unavoidable. Additionally, decision-making speed at large pharmaceutical companies is typically slower than that of agile biotech firms.

## Summary

GLP-1's success is not merely the emergence of a new drug, but an event reshaping the entire value chain of the pharmaceutical industry. Now, as major players like Eli Lilly, Pfizer, Johnson & Johnson, and Merck simultaneously restructure their portfolios, the industry is at a critical inflection point. While these companies, armed with capital and distribution networks, are expected to ultimately seize market dominance, if they fail to quickly close the technology gap, they could suffer declining market share instead.
