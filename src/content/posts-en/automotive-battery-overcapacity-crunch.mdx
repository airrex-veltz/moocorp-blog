## Global Battery Overcapacity Era: Chinese Companies Redefining the Profit Map

From 2025 through H1 2026, **CATL** and **BYD** deployed massive new EV battery capacity (measured in GWh), and the global market's supply-demand balance rapidly collapsed. Korean and European battery makers' utilization rates fell below 50%, and price pressure is eroding margins across the industry. This is not cyclical slowdown but a structural shift—Chinese players have gained dominance through technical cost competitiveness and scale.

## Capacity Surge: Supply Glut by the Numbers

Global annual battery production capacity rose from roughly 3,000 GWh in 2024 to over 4,500 GWh as of H1 2026. Actual EV demand, meanwhile, stands at only 2,200 GWh. Excess capacity approaches 2,300 GWh.

CATL and BYD account for over 70% of this excess capacity. CATL ramped new lines exceeding 1,000 GWh annually at new plants in China, Austria, and Hungary—a 50% increase year-over-year. BYD has simultaneously finished factory construction in Qinghai, Zhejiang, and Ningde. Both are executing strategies that absorb near-term cost declines to recover through market share gains.

## Why the Price War Intensifies Now: Four Pressures

**1. Hidden Structure of Chinese Government Subsidies**: China classifies batteries as a strategic industry, sustaining long-term low-rate financing and tax relief across land, power, and capital. Under this policy support, CATL and BYD hold costs 15–20% below global rivals. Korean, Japanese, and European makers must offset this gap via technology premium, but battery-tech convergence makes that increasingly difficult.

**2. LFP's Virtuous Cycle of Safety and Cost**: CATL holds decisive advantage in LFP (lithium iron phosphate) chemistry. LFP costs 30–40% less than ternary batteries while offering superior safety and lifespan, capturing the budget EV segment. Even Tesla expanded LFP adoption in Models 3 and Y. CATL's unit sales jumped from 420 million in 2024 to over 500 million in H1 2026. Price cuts on Korean makers' ternary-based products are now unavoidable.

**3. Worsening EV Sales Slowdown**: Global EV sales grew from 10 million units in 2023 to 14 million in 2024, but growth has stalled since 2025. Tech maturity limits new buyer acquisition, charging infrastructure lags, and consumer price sensitivity rises. Battery demand fell short of forecasts (2,800 GWh annually), depressing utilization rates and raising makers' fixed-cost burden.

**4. Narrowing Options for Korean and European Makers**: **LGEA (LG Energy Solution)** currently runs Indonesia and Poland plants at 35–45% utilization. **SK Innovation** faces similar pressures. Both must cut prices to recover fixed costs, which rapidly erodes margins. Simultaneously, they're trapped in a vicious cycle: cheaper supply from CATL and BYD forces them to accept lower technology fees in automaker contract renewals.

## Structural Weakness: The Beginning of Profit Reset

**Intensifying Low-Cost Competition Shrinks Industry Margins**: Battery makers' average net margin fell from 10%+ in 2023 to 5–6% in 2024—cut in half. As of H1 2026, CATL posts 3–4%, Korean players 1–2%. Cost-cutting recovery is structurally impossible against technology ceilings and Chinese policy support.

**Risk of Korean and European Player Exit**: Some Korean mid-tier battery makers have already shifted to capacity reduction or maintenance mode. Shrinking contract prices with each renewal kill investment incentives. This could intensify market concentration over the medium term, further entrenching CATL and BYD's pricing power.

## Summary

The battery industry in 2026 has entered a new structural reality of overcapacity and price decline. CATL and BYD's new capacity deployment deepens near-term supply glut, dragging down Korean and European players' utilization and eroding margins. The market structure increasingly locks in an outcome where technology alone cannot overcome low-cost policy support and scale economics.
