## Lithium Oversupply Systematically Pressures EV Profitability

Global battery raw material supply is overwhelming demand, causing EV production profit margins to erode incrementally. Lithium spot prices have fallen from $88 per gallon in 2022 to $15–18 recently, representing a decline of over 80%—a signal of structural industry reorganization, not merely temporary adjustment.

## Rapid Expansion of the Lithium Supply Market

Global lithium production capacity is projected to increase nearly threefold from 2024 levels through 2028. Major mines in Australia, Chile, and Argentina have simultaneously ramped up production, and Chinese processing facilities continue to bring new capacity online. Meanwhile, EV sales growth has slowed to an average of 15–20% annually over the past two years, and some markets are even showing stagnation. As the gap between supply and demand curves widens, the direction of prices becomes self-evident.

## Why EV Margins Are Tightening Now: Four Pressure Factors

**1. Structural Collapse in Battery Pack Raw Material Costs**: Lithium accounts for 8–12% of total raw material costs in a battery pack. When lithium prices fall 30–40% per unit, the entire battery pack's cost drops 3–5%. OEMs face pressure to immediately return margin gains through supply chain renegotiation processes. Particularly when nickel and cobalt prices also decline in tandem, battery cost savings accumulate.

**2. Price Competition from Emerging EV Players**: Chinese newcomer OEMs (BYD, SAIC subsidiaries, etc.) and new Indian market entrants are penetrating the market with low-cost strategies. In developed markets, pure EV companies like **Tesla** have responded with price cuts, leading to a vicious cycle where traditional automakers must also lower EV lineup margins.

**3. Imminent Scale-Up of Battery Recycling**: EU Battery Regulation (2023) and US Inflation Reduction Act requirements are set to strengthen through 2027–2029. This will increase recycled lithium's market supply, further pressuring primary ore demand. Returns on investment for existing mine development will deteriorate further.

**4. Slowing Energy Storage System (ESS) Demand**: Solar and wind subsidies are declining, and power grid stabilization investments are becoming more selective, causing large-capacity battery storage demand growth to lag expectations. Plans to diversify lithium demand sources are partially unraveling, creating a paradox: EV sector dependency is rising even as overall demand growth slows.

## Yet Technical Leadership Remains: Structural Strengths and Weaknesses

**OEM Economies of Scale**: Large manufacturers like Hyundai Motor, Volkswagen, and BMW can achieve deeper cost reductions through their negotiating power with suppliers. However, this means eroding supplier margins, so mid-size battery makers face structural pressure they cannot avoid.

**Acceleration of Technology Differentiation**: As LFP (lithium iron phosphate) batteries proliferate, low-lithium-content designs are becoming mainstream, reducing per-unit-energy-density lithium requirements. However, this transition requires reinvestment in technology development, and cash-strapped companies face falling-behind risk.

## Conclusion

Lithium oversupply is a long-term structural change, not a short-term tactic. While improved EV cost competitiveness is positive, when all players face simultaneous margin compression, market restructuring—through mergers and acquisitions, production cutbacks, and regional exit—becomes inevitable. Companies unable to adapt to low lithium prices have few options.
