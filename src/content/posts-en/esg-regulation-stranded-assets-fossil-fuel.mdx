## Regulation Declares Stranded Assets

Mandatory ESG regulations are systematically eroding the value of fossil fuel assets. As the EU's climate finance disclosure rules, North American securities regulators' carbon disclosure standards, and Australian pension funds' ESG portfolio shifts proceed simultaneously, coal and oil-related assets are being reclassified as "investments without a future." This is not an isolated corporate problem but a **structural shift in which the entire financial market's risk weighting is changing**.

## Regulatory Tightening and the Definition of Stranded Assets

Stranded assets are assets that have lost value due to technological change, regulation, or market shifts. Fossil fuel assets now fall into this category because regulation has become mandatory rather than optional. The EU has made carbon emission disclosure compulsory through its Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD), and North American securities regulators have codified Scope 1 and 2 emission disclosure for listed companies. Australia is more direct: pension funds are required to reduce fossil fuel exposure in their portfolios. The stronger the regulation, the higher the funding costs for fossil fuel energy companies and the lower the approval rate for new projects. Asset write-downs are an inevitable consequence of this chain reaction.

## Why Stranded Assets Are Accelerating Now: Four Factors

**1. Simultaneity of regulatory spread**: Regulation does not stop at one region. When the EU sets strict standards, international capital begins to conform to them, and then North America and Australia also tighten theirs competitively. This simultaneity blocks escape routes for fossil fuel companies. Even if they try to move assets to markets with weaker regulation, advanced financial institutions have begun monitoring those transactions under Financed Emissions rules.

**2. Scale of portfolio reallocation**: As regulation tightened, asset managers increased ESG funds, which exclude fossil fuels. Meanwhile, public pension funds and insurers are undergoing large-scale portfolio reallocation as they face mandatory reduction of fossil fuel exposure. This is not mere stock replacement but means the **buyer base for fossil assets is disappearing altogether**.

**3. New capital avoidance**: Financial institutions have begun restricting lending to the fossil fuel industry. They move preemptively to avoid net losses before regulation mandates it. Long-term projects like new well development and coal mine expansion face funding constraints, and refinancing existing operations becomes difficult. Fossil fuel companies are pushed into a position where they must sustain operations on operating cash flow alone.

**4. The downward spiral of valuation**: As asset write-downs are recognized, corporate debt ratios worsen. This leads to credit downgrades and rising borrowing costs. Energy companies must cut dividends or sell assets to raise cash. With fewer buyers, asset prices fall further. In this downward spiral, valuation multiples for fossil fuel companies can diverge from energy transition companies by a factor of 2 or more.

## Can It Be Reversed?: Structural Strengths and Weaknesses

**Structural weakness**: Fossil assets are not "outdated technology" but "assets destined to disappear" within the regulatory framework. While automakers can transition to electric vehicles, existing oil fields and coal mines cannot undergo technological conversion. The assets themselves persist, but regulation erases their value. Unlike energy transition companies, there is no new market for cutting-edge technology to pioneer. This is the essence of stranded assets.

**Structural strength**: Fossil fuels still account for the majority of global energy. Despite regulation, demand remains and cash flows persist. If companies do not give up, asset value recovery is not impossible. There is room for revaluation if regulation is controlled. However, this would require a reversal of the regulatory tide, which appears unlikely given current momentum.

## Conclusion

Regulatory tightening is systematically eroding the value of fossil fuel assets. This goes beyond stock price adjustments. As the entire financial market's risk weighting shifts, the capital allocation structure itself is being reorganized. The valuation gap between energy transition and traditional energy companies will widen as regulation strengthens.
