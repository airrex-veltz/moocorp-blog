Snap, which operates Snapchat, shocked the market on May 6, 2026, by announcing Q2 guidance. The company's executives disclosed that Q2 daily active users (DAU) would decline by 1 million compared to the prior quarter. This stems from the termination of its partnership with Perplexity AI and reduced advertising spending from the Middle East. The stock currently trades at $6.11, down over 40% from its 52-week high of $10.41.

## Fundamentals

Snap's fundamentals expose two simultaneous weaknesses: slowing growth and the absence of profitability. In Q1 results, DAU grew 3% year-over-year, slightly exceeding expectations, but executives' Q2 DAU guidance of a 1 million decline has put growth momentum on pause. The decline reflects damage from the termination of the Perplexity AI partnership, which hurt ad revenue, combined with reduced advertising spend from the Middle East, which accounts for approximately 15% of total revenue.

On profitability, the company remains in the red, with no P/E ratio to report. This stands as a clear disadvantage compared to rivals Meta and Pinterest. Pinterest, though facing stalled growth, at least remains profitable; Snap faces both growth stagnation and losses simultaneously, making it a higher-risk proposition for investors.

## Implications

Snap is a textbook high-risk turnaround play. The DAU decline guidance is a clear near-term bearish signal and unsuitable for conservative investors. However, if the Memories feature actually drives DAU reversal in Q3 and beyond and the stock reprices to the $3-4 range, it could become a speculative value bet.

Two critical watch points merit attention. First, whether the Memories feature's actual retention benefit is validated in Q3 DAU figures. Second, whether management can articulate a concrete roadmap to profitability. If neither materializes, Snap is far more likely to be a value trap than a buying opportunity.

Comparison with Pinterest illuminates the situation. Pinterest has stalled growth but remains profitable. Snap, by contrast, faces both growth stagnation and losses, resulting in misaligned risk-to-reward compensation. For investors interested in this name, it is prudent to set clear loss limits and allocate only a minimal portfolio weight.
