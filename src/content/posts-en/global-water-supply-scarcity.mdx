## In an age of water scarcity, water utilities' real price hikes align with government investment

As drought lengthens and climate change becomes visible, water utilities have assumed a new position. A transformation from simple infrastructure operators to brokers of scarce resources is now underway. A structure is forming simultaneously where government pours money into water infrastructure and corporations gain pricing power.

## Water Utilities and Water Resources ETF Divergence

**AWK** (American Water Works Company, Inc.) is North America's largest water utility company. As of the closing price on 2026-09-17, it was $138.24, rising 10.96% over three months. This company, with a market cap of $2.75 billion, maintains high profitability with a net profit margin of 21.3%, and an annual revenue growth rate of 6.2%. The PER of 23.9 has formed a valuation premium.

Meanwhile, **PHO** (Invesco Water Resources ETF) tracks a broad range of water resource companies and technologies. At the closing price on the same day of $68.45, the one-month change is -3.39%, showing a downtrend. While the three-month gain of 2.70% shows recent recovery, the PER of 24.2 remains at the level of individual companies. This divergence is significant. North American mainstream water utilities are strong, while the water resources sector overall is mixed.

## Why Now: Four Pressures and Drivers

**1. Materialization of climate change and water scarcity**: Drought in North America's west and south is becoming chronic. With demand fixed and supply constrained, water's scarcity is beginning to be reflected in actual prices. This is a fundamental shift from the historical treatment of water as a public good. The more severe the supply shortage, the higher the value of corporations efficiently operating existing infrastructure.

**2. Acceleration of government infrastructure investment**: Advanced country governments are investing in replacing aging water supply systems, upgrading water purification technology, and building rainwater storage infrastructure. This increases construction contracts and operational outsourcing for water utilities. In particular, private water companies like AWK can expand revenues through government subsidies and long-term contract renewals.

**3. Regulatory easing and price increases for water utilities**: As water supply has been elevated to a national security issue, regulatory authorities are shifting toward recognizing reasonable profits for water companies. The more severe the drought in a region, the higher the likelihood that the state Public Utilities Commission will approve rate increase applications. This makes net profit margins of 21.3% for large companies like AWK even more resilient.

**4. ESG investment and divergence in the global water theme**: Water-related ETFs (PHO) encompass a broad range of sectors from water treatment technology companies to pump manufacturers to water analysis software firms. Among these, technology companies have high sensitivity to macro interest rates, and legacy technologies face obsolescence risk. Conversely, water monopoly companies like AWK show a different trajectory with predictable cash flows and stable dividend yields.

## Between Structural Strengths and Weaknesses

**Strength: Deepening natural monopoly**. Water utilities hold regional monopoly rights. Water infrastructure requires enormous upfront investment, and multiple supply chains are inefficient. The more severe the drought, the fewer alternative suppliers exist, so corporations' bargaining power strengthens. AWK's three-month gain reflects this.

**Weakness: Political risk in public utilities**. Water rate increases become politicized as rising living costs for ordinary citizens. State legislatures can reject rate increases by water companies and strengthen acquisition regulations (M&A). PHO's decline shows how such uncertainty extends beyond individual companies to affect the entire sector.

## Conclusion

Water supply crisis is a benefit for water utilities and a weighty choice for investors. As drought deepens, companies like AWK strengthen pricing power and grow through government investment. However, the nature of public goods and political oversight set upper limits, so permanent strength cannot be expected. Water-themed investment is a long-term allocation asset, but regulatory and climate data must be continuously monitored.
