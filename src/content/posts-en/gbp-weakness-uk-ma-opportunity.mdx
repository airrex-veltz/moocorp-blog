## Why Sterling Weakness Treats British Companies as a 'Bargain Sale'

As sterling continues to weaken, the acquisition price of British companies on a dollar and euro basis is plummeting. Foreign acquirers are seizing this window to pursue large-scale deals in finance, utilities, and retail sectors. The mechanism that accelerates M&A is that asset prices have fallen but business value remains intact.

## Relative Undervaluation of British Equities

**EWU** (iShares MSCI United Kingdom ETF) tracks approximately 350 London-listed companies. As of September 11, 2026 close, it stands at $47.94, near the lower end of the six-month range of $42.97–$49.39. The one-month decline of -0.89% reflects persistent weakness in the UK equity market. However, the three-month gain of +3.68% suggests selective interest in undervalued assets. With a P/E of 16.9, well below European averages, the ETF presents an attractive entry point for foreign acquirers.

## Why British M&A Is Concentrated Now: Four Factors

**1. Exchange Rate Effect Lowers Real Prices**: Sterling weakness directly reduces the valuation of British assets on a dollar or euro basis. For example, if sterling weakens from $1.20 to $1.25 per pound, a dollar-based acquirer can buy the same pound price more cheaply than before. This creates a 'spread' situation where asset quality is unchanged but price falls.

**2. Time-Limited Window to Harvest Undervalued Assets**: The undervalued state at P/E 16.9 can be corrected at any time. Large foreign acquirers see this window as narrow and move quickly. This is why whole-portfolio acquisitions have increased during this period, rather than individual stock purchases.

**3. Structural Shift in Finance and Utilities Sectors**: British finance faces regulatory risks and restructuring pressures following Brexit. Amid this chaos, major European banks seek to acquire British assets at reduced prices and consolidate them. The utilities sector has strategic value due to rising needs for energy transition and infrastructure investment, but rising funding costs from sterling weakness are fueling forced sales.

**4. Digital Transformation in Retail and Consumer Sectors**: British offline retail faces competitive pressure, yet its online capabilities and brand assets remain intact. Large retail groups are keen to acquire legacy retailers at depressed prices and reorganize them as e-commerce platforms.

## Still EWU: Structural Strengths and Weaknesses

**Strength — Cushion from Undervaluation**: Currency weakness is temporary. Over the long term, if sterling recovers or the UK economy stabilizes, valuations of companies held in EWU could rise sharply. Especially high-dividend sectors like finance and utilities provide dividend income that acts as a buffer against currency weakness.

**Weakness — Uncertainty After Acquisition**: The rise in foreign takeovers means fewer British companies will remain independent. Investors face selling before acquisition premiums are paid or exposure to unpredictability in consolidation. Also, if sterling weakness persists, UK-based acquirers' foreign borrowing costs rise, making M&A financing harder.

## Summary

Sterling weakness makes British companies relatively cheaper for acquirers, and large transactions in finance, utilities, and retail sectors concentrate during this period. EWU shareholders should keep in mind the tension between acquisition expectations and currency recovery expectations, following dual signals from dividend income and market price.
