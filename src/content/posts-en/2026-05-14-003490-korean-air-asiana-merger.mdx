Korean Air is completing its acquisition of Asiana Airlines and preparing to leap to become Northeast Asia's largest carrier. Amid post-COVID international demand recovery and strong cargo performance driving improved results, the company will transform into a mega-carrier capable of transporting 70 million passengers annually upon completion of the integration in 2026. While the integration is expected to generate cost savings of KRW 500 billion or more through synergies, the increase in debt ratio and fuel price volatility are key points to monitor in the medium to long term.

## Company Overview

Korean Air is the No. 1 domestic carrier, with passenger and cargo transportation as its main business. With a market capitalization of approximately KRW 3.5 trillion, it is a member of SkyTeam Alliance and maintains a global network. Over the past 30 days, its stock price has shown an upward trend amid expectations for Asiana acquisition approval and news of international demand recovery.

## Fundamentals

Korean Air's business structure consists of 55% passenger, 30% cargo, and 15% aircraft maintenance and other operations. During the COVID-19 pandemic, the cargo division buoyed results, and as of 2025, international seat capacity has recovered to 90% of pre-pandemic levels. After the Asiana integration, the company will have the capacity to transport 70 million passengers annually, with further strengthened hub competitiveness through Terminal 2 expansion at Incheon Airport and slot acquisition.

China routes are planned to expand to 150-plus flights per week in 2026, a volume exceeding pre-pandemic levels. The cargo division maintains an operating margin of 15% thanks to stabilized freight rates, buffering volatility in the passenger division.

## Catalyst and Risk

## Implications

Korean Air faces an opportunity to grow into Northeast Asia's largest carrier through the Asiana integration. International demand recovery and cargo division stability support near-term performance, but post-integration debt ratio increase and fuel price volatility could affect medium and long-term financial stability. The realization speed of integration synergies and the China route recovery trajectory appear to be key variables for future stock price movements.
