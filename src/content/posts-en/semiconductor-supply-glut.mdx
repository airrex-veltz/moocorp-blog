## Semiconductor Oversupply After AI Boom Collapses Chipmaker Profitability

As the AI infrastructure investment boom cools, semiconductor supply oversupply intensifies, and major chipmakers' pricing power is rapidly weakening. The shift from excess demand to oversupply goes beyond mere supply-demand adjustment, leading to widespread profitability deterioration across the industry.

## Major Chipmakers' Performance Deterioration

**NVDA** (Nvidia)'s stock price stood at $125.53 as of 2026-11-20, down 39.72% over three months. Even Nvidia, the absolute leader in the AI chip market, has not escaped pricing pressure. However, its net profit margin of 62.5% (as of 2026-11-20) and revenue growth rate of 103.2% (YoY) remain at elevated levels, demonstrating a strength gap.

**INTC** (Intel)'s situation is far more severe. Its stock price fell to $51.28 over the same period, down 41.23% over three months, with a net profit margin of −20.1% (as of 2026-11-20), falling into losses. Structural weakening is accelerating as intensified competition in the CPU market combines with failure to capture replacement demand for AI chips.

**TSMC** (Taiwan Semiconductor) is in the position of providing foundry services to both, directly experiencing postponed orders from customers and pressure to reduce unit prices.

## Why Is Pricing Power Collapsing Now: Four Factors

**1. Accelerating demand normalization**: Generative AI infrastructure construction demand that peaked in 2024–2025 is winding down, with new orders plummeting. The transition to substitute demand (automotive, IoT) is also not proceeding as quickly as expected.

**2. Supply chain reversal**: Completely exiting the severe shortage of 2023, the industry entered a clear supply surplus phase from mid-2026 onward. As all preemptive capacity expansion investments come online, excess capacity is accumulating.

**3. Emergence of substitute technologies**: The premium for general-purpose processors is disappearing. As customers switch to specialized chips (ASICs), Nvidia's differentiation is also being compressed into price.

**4. Strengthened customer bargaining power**: Large cloud service providers (CSPs) can now leverage chip order volumes as a weapon in unit price negotiations. The balance of power has reversed from supplier to buyer.

## Structural Strengths and Weaknesses

**Weakness: Surging fixed cost burden**. Excess capacity built through preemptive expansion becomes a drag during oversupply phases. As capacity utilization drops, unit costs rise and prices fall.

**Strength: Innovation speed and differentiated technology**. Nvidia's CUDA ecosystem and TSMC's advanced process nodes remain competitive barriers, but in an oversupply phase, even this difference ultimately translates into price cuts.

## Summary

The semiconductor industry has become a game of 'who avoids the worst.' Whether enduring through financial strength like Nvidia, dispersing risk through customer diversification like TSMC, or collapsing from adaptation failure like Intel. How deep this cycle will be and when it will end remains unclear.
