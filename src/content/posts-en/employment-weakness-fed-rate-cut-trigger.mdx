## When Employment Weakness Pushes Aside Inflation

In the Fed's interest rate decisions, inflation and employment always compete. But as labor market signals have weakened recently, the balance has shifted sharply in one direction. When unemployment rises and wage pressure eases, inflation concerns diminish sharply. This shift is leading the Fed toward rate cuts, and various asset classes are showing contrasting movements.

## The Signal Employment Data Tells

Over recent months, U.S. nonfarm employment has underperformed expectations. Unemployment has risen from the 3.5% range last year to the mid-4% range this year, while new job openings continue a downward trend. This represents a completely different phase from the "hot" labor market of 2021–2023. At that time, one reason inflation was elevated was precisely because the job market was overheating, and companies continued raising wages. Now that dynamic has reversed.

## Why Rate Cuts Are Accelerating Now: 4 Mechanisms

**1. Diminishing Inflation Concerns**: When employment weakens, consumption slows, and businesses feel less pressure to raise prices. Job insecurity dampens consumer confidence, leading to reduced demand. Confident that inflation has peaked, the Fed gains room to lower rates.

**2. Weakening Phillips Curve**: The era when high unemployment didn't necessarily mean lower wages is now past. With employment falling, companies have less incentive to offer hiring bonuses or engage in wage competition. As a result, the traditional relationship—weak jobs equal weak wages—has been restored, strengthening the Fed's case for rate cuts.

**3. Dual Mandate Rebalancing**: The Fed holds two objectives: price stability and maximum employment. While it has focused on raising rates due to elevated inflation, weakening employment now reminds it of its second mandate. As inflation declines to manageable levels, the employment goal comes back into focus, triggering the rate-cut signal.

**4. Virtuous Cycle of Market Sentiment and Forward Guidance**: As markets price in rate cuts ahead of time, long-term rates decline preemptively. Observing these market moves, the Fed adjusts the degree of monetary tightening, shifting from pausing hikes to beginning cuts. Employment weakness signals serve as the trigger for this inflection point.

## Structural Responses of Three Assets: Dollar, Gold, Treasuries

**Dollar Index (`DXY`) Weakness**: Rate cuts reduce the relative appeal of the dollar. As U.S. rates fall, capital flows to foreign assets, and the dollar weakens. Each time employment weakness news emerges, DXY faces near-term downward pressure.

**Gold (`GLD`) Strength**: Historically, gold yields nothing in a low-rate environment, but it's a haven for uncertainty. As rates decline, the opportunity cost falls, and when economic weakness signals are mixed in as now, gold's safe-asset value shines.

**Long-Term Treasuries (`TLT`) Strength**: In the early stages of rate cuts, long-term Treasuries are the biggest beneficiary. The value of currently held bonds rises, and future coupon income enters a more attractive environment.

## Summary: The Gravity of Employment Signals

The moment employment weakness appears, the Fed's decision-making tilts in one direction. Once the inflection point where employment wins in the inflation trade-off arrives, rate cuts become nearly inevitable. Understanding this mechanism is key to macro asset allocation.
