## Shipping Rate War: The Return of Brick-and-Mortar

Global e-commerce platforms are entering into aggressive shipping rate competition, compressing retail margins. Major retailers, including **Walmart (WMT)** and **Alibaba (BABA)**, are now beginning to rediscover brick-and-mortar channels as 'strategic assets'.

## Declining Shipping Prices and Margin Erosion

As shipping competition becomes industry standard practice, pressure is mounting from both customer acquisition and logistics unit costs. North American e-commerce average shipping rates declined 37%, from $4.50 in 2024 to $2.80 in 2026, with Asia-Pacific showing even steeper drops. Walmart lowered industry benchmarks by offering next-day delivery as standard service, while Alibaba's logistics network reduced domestic budget shipping to CNY 1.50, intensifying global competition. As a result, B2C e-commerce net profit margins fell 46%, from 5.2% in 2023 to 2.8% in 2026.

## Why Margins Are Compressing Now: Four Factors

**1. Scale Competition Reaches Peak**: Alibaba processes over 200 million shipments annually, Walmart handles 120 million, driving competition in fixed-cost reduction. Smaller platforms cannot match this scale and are exiting the market or becoming acquisition targets.

**2. Consumer Expectations Shift Upward**: Free shipping and ultra-fast delivery have shifted from optional to baseline expectations. Platforms face a vicious cycle of either absorbing shipping costs into product prices or offsetting them with ad revenue.

**3. Global Logistics Infrastructure Oversupply**: E-commerce logistics capacity has consistently exceeded consumption growth since 2025. Oversaturation in warehouses, transport vehicles, and last-mile labor has structurally depressed unit costs.

**4. Walmart's Brick-and-Mortar Counterattack**: Walmart leverages 4,700 stores across the US as shipping hubs, achieving simultaneous reductions in delivery costs and improvements in speed. Pure e-commerce firms face a competitive gap they cannot replicate.

## Redefining Brick-and-Mortar: Structural Strengths and Weaknesses

**Online Channel Limitations Deepen**: Pure e-commerce firms structurally struggle to achieve economies of scale in shipping competition. Rising customer acquisition costs accelerate overall profitability deterioration. Even Alibaba, recognizing domestic market saturation, is pursuing international brick-and-mortar expansion.

**The Logic of Brick-and-Mortar's Resurgence**: Integrated retail platforms (Walmart, Costco, Amazon Fresh) have secured structural advantages: (1) existing stores serving as low-cost fulfillment hubs, (2) immediate fulfillment for customer purchases (BOPIS), (3) means to monetize premium services. Through integrated online-offline margin structures, they can exit pure shipping competition.

## Conclusion

As shipping competition intensifies, pure e-commerce relying solely on online channels faces an existential crisis. Conversely, integrated retail models leveraging brick-and-mortar infrastructure are becoming the only path to margin defense. If Walmart's model of 'achieving both speed and margins' becomes the global standard, a reorganization of the retail ecosystem will be unavoidable.
