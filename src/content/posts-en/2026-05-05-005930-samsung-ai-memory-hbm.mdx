Samsung Electronics is ramping up mass production of HBM (high-bandwidth memory), a critical component for AI infrastructure, signaling passage through the memory cycle trough. The HBM market is projected to reach $30 billion in 2026, with Samsung targeting market share expansion and normalized operating margins.

- HBM3E mass production to capture NVIDIA, AMD AI server demand
- Memory price recovery, operating margin target 15-18%
- HBM market share gap vs SK Hynix (20% vs 50%) poses key risk
</KeyPoints>

## Company Overview

Samsung Electronics is a top 20 global company by market cap with diversified operations spanning memory, foundry, and mobile within the global semiconductor market. 2025 annual revenue reached approximately KRW 280 trillion, with memory as the core revenue driver. Over the past 30 days, the stock has fluctuated between memory upcycle expectations and HBM competitiveness concerns.

## Fundamentals

Samsung's memory division has shown recovery from Q4 2024 onwards, with operating margins returning to double digits. 2026 Q1 memory operating margins are projected at 15% or higher, driven by HBM3E mass production acceleration and rising generic DRAM prices. The company plans total capex of KRW 50 trillion in 2026, allocating a substantial portion to expanding HBM production lines.

## Catalysts and Risks

<Callout type="catalyst" title="HBM3E Mass Production Ramp">
Samsung is expanding HBM3E mass production in H1 2026 to capture demand for NVIDIA H200 and AMD MI300 series AI accelerators. Combined with memory upcycle entry, profitability improvements are expected.
</Callout>

<Callout type="risk">
SK Hynix maintains overwhelming leadership with 50% HBM market share, while Samsung remains at 20%. HBM quality validation delays and continued foundry losses pose additional headwinds. China geopolitical risks and memory cycle volatility remain key variables.
</Callout>

## Implications

The key question for Samsung Electronics is whether it can overcome its position as a latecomer in the HBM market and expand share. With memory upcycle and HBM production expansion aligned, an earnings turnaround is now possible, but resolving the technology gap with SK Hynix will determine long-term competitiveness. The Q1 earnings announcement scheduled for mid-May will be closely watched for HBM revenue contribution and memory operating margin improvement.
