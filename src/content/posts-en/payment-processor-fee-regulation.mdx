## The Golden Age of Card Networks Faces Threat

As discussions on payment fee regulation spread globally, the ultra-high-profit structure of credit card networks faces mounting pressure to change. The economics of these network effects are straightforward: the fees paid by merchants (interchange fees) are central to total revenue, yet regulators in major countries are seeking to impose legal limits on them.

**Card Networks: Platforms Powered by Fees**

**Visa** (**V**) traded at $369.93 on closing on 2026-09-17, with a net profit margin of 50.8% and revenue growth of 14.4% YoY. **Mastercard** (**MA**, closed at $565.73) exhibits a similar structure with a net profit margin of 46.3% and growth of 14.1%. The source of these high profit margins is evident: platform business. Merchant fees collected per transaction constitute the bulk of revenue, while system operating costs increase only linearly with transaction volume, resulting in high margins.

**American Express** (**AXP**, closed at $311.17) differs. With a net profit margin of 16.1% and growth of 12.8%, it operates at roughly one-third the profitability level of its two competitors. This is because it issues its own cards and bears credit underwriting and delinquency risk. However, in exchange, transaction values center on premium cardholders, generating inherently higher profitability per transaction.

## Regulation Has Begun Cutting Fees

The EU already constrained card fees to tiered caps in 2015, restricting both credit and debit cards to specific thresholds. The United States also regulated debit card fees under the Dodd-Frank Act in 2010, with recent discussions now extending to credit cards. China, India, and Southeast Asia are pursuing similar policies.

## Why Networks Are Under Pressure Now: Four Factors

**1. Synchronized Global Regulatory Acceleration**: Following the EU, the likelihood of fee regulation has risen in the US and APAC. As countries move under the banner of consumer and merchant protection, networks must abandon their existing revenue structures. Recovery from such a high-margin architecture is difficult.

**2. The Dilemma of Rising Transaction Volume versus Declining Unit Fees**: While V and MA maintain revenue growth around 14%, if a substantial portion stems from increased transaction count rather than higher per-transaction fees, this would be offset by falling fee rates. Should regulation substantially compress fee rates, transaction volume growth alone cannot sustain current profitability levels.

**3. Proliferation of Alternative Payment Channels**: Digital wallets (Apple Pay, Google Pay), QR code payments, and payment storage solutions are rapidly gaining market share. These typically charge lower or near-zero fees per transaction. This signals effective erosion of card networks' monopolistic position.

**4. Strategy Adjustment Costs from Heterogeneous Regulation Across Countries**: As V and MA operate in over 160 countries, they must adapt systems and settlement structures to comply with each nation's regulations. Operational complexity rises sharply, and compliance costs accumulate exponentially.

## Why They Remain Payment Networks: Structural Strengths and Weaknesses

**The Defensive Power of Network Effects**: V and MA still command billions of cardholders and millions of merchant partners. Displacing this incumbency would require rebuilding the entire alternative payment infrastructure, so even with regulation, they do not forfeit substantial market share in the near term.

**The Fundamental Weakness: The Profit Structure Itself**: High net margins are inherent regulatory targets. When regulation actually takes effect, net margins are expected to decline materially. Recovery through transaction volume growth alone would require years.

AXP may face relatively smaller regulatory impact, given its already-moderate profit margins (16.1%). Conversely, deteriorating profitability at V and MA could allow competitive dynamics to rebalance.

## Summary

Credit card fee regulation is no longer theoretical—it is underway. As the EU example demonstrates, transactions continue after regulation, but network revenue structurally declines. V and MA can partially offset losses through transaction growth, but the era of ultra-high net margins is ending. Country-specific regulatory adaptation and revenue diversification (blockchain, digital asset settlement, and others) will become survival strategies.
