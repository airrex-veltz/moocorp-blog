## Fundamentals

Apple's financial metrics rank at the top tier among large technology companies. The ROE of 151.9% results from aggressive share buybacks combined with high profitability. ROA of 31.2% and net margin of 27.15% reflect Apple's premium pricing policy and ecosystem lock-in effects. TTM EPS stands at $8.27, and while the dividend yield is low at 0.38%, the company continues to return cash to shareholders through stable cash flow.

The P/B ratio of 34.0 reflects intangible assets and brand value. Beta of 1.07 indicates volatility similar to the market average. Over the past seven days, multiple news reports on the Intel chip manufacturing contract have increased investor interest.

## Risks

<Callout type="risk">
Intel Foundry's reliability and initial yields have not been verified. There is a risk of production disruptions during supply chain transition.
</Callout>

<Callout type="risk">
Smartphone replacement cycles have lengthened, slowing iPhone revenue growth. High dependence on the Chinese market creates significant vulnerability to geopolitical tension.
</Callout>

EU antitrust regulations and App Store fee restrictions could negatively impact services revenue. At P/E of 35x, valuation is elevated relative to growth rates, making valuation adjustment inevitable if earnings fall short of expectations.

## Implications

Apple's Intel Foundry contract represents a strategic choice that goes beyond supply chain diversification, reshaping the semiconductor industry landscape. The intent to reduce concentration risk at TSMC and establish production capacity within the United States is clear. However, Intel Foundry's unverified reliability and elevated valuation warrant caution. The key points to watch are whether the 52-week high is breached and the next earnings announcement.
