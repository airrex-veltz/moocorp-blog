Supply constraints in high-performance AI chips are fundamentally reshaping cloud infrastructure investment structures. Advanced processors needed for model training and inference depend on a small number of manufacturers, with supply quantities remaining fixed. As AI demand rises steeply against this inelastic supply curve, chip availability has become a direct constraint on cloud capacity provisioning.

Under this constraint, cloud providers have little choice but to enter a preemption competition. With chips limited, whichever competing platform secures sufficient capacity first is likely to absorb customers. As a result, each provider faces pressure to forecast future demand and proactively invest in chips and infrastructure. This becomes a driver for increasing capex even amid uncertainty.

This competitive structure in turn passes cost pressure to customers. The capital costs of infrastructure deployed aggressively for preemption must ultimately be reflected in unit service pricing. Customers must absorb these rising costs to build AI capabilities, which prompts more enterprises to evaluate adopting cloud AI services. As larger demand materializes, providers' future capacity estimates are adjusted further upward.

As this cycle repeats, the capex spiral steepens. Supply bottleneck → preemption competition → rising customer costs → expanded demand estimates → additional capex investment → higher unit service costs. While each actor at every stage behaves rationally in this structure, the overall system is locked on an upward-spiraling trajectory of cost increases. Until chip supply catches up with demand, this cycle is likely to accelerate.
