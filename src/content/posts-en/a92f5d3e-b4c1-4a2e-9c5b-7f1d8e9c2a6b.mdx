## Australia-Chile Mine Concentration Is Reorganizing Battery Supply Chains

Lithium mine mergers and acquisitions are accelerating, shaking up the battery industry's raw material sourcing structure. As a handful of major companies dominate mines, emerging battery makers lose out on cost competitiveness, while alternative lithium source technologies such as geothermal brines and recycled lithium are surging.

## The Changing Landscape of Lithium Extraction

Most of the world's lithium is produced in just a handful of countries: Australia, Chile, China, and Argentina. Among these, Australia and Chile account for the overwhelming majority. The trend of their mines being consolidated by large mining companies is distinct. Each time an individual mine is absorbed under a large corporation, raw material supply routes simplify, and pricing negotiation structures are reorganized around major mining companies. Small and mid-sized mining firms and newcomers lose competitiveness, while battery manufacturers become trapped in a structure dependent on limited suppliers.

## Why Is Concentration Intensifying Now: Four Factors

**1. Operating Cost Advantage of Large Mines:** Developing and operating mines requires enormous upfront investment. Major companies operating multiple integrated mines can lower unit costs far below those of individual small-scale operations. As cost competitiveness translates directly into market dominance, consolidation becomes a natural outcome.

**2. Depletion Concerns and Preemption Competition:** As lithium shortage scenarios from rising EV demand repeat, major companies accelerate efforts to preempt mines. The strategy to proactively manage future supply risks through mine acquisitions now fuels consolidation.

**3. Emergence of Unconventional Source Technology:** As Australia-Chile concentration intensifies, pressure to find alternative pathways grows. Technologies to extract lithium from geothermal brines or recover it from spent batteries are becoming economical. Unlike large-scale mines, these can be operated in a geographically distributed manner, making them accessible to emerging companies.

**4. Emerging Battery Maker Cost Pressure:** As Chinese and South Korean battery companies chasing Tesla grow in number, raw material sourcing becomes a competitive weapon. Newcomers unable to source at the same price from major mines have no choice but to invest in unconventional sources or recycling routes.

## Yet Paradoxically, Opportunities Open: The Rise of Alternative Sources

**Economic Improvement of Unconventional Sources:** Recycled lithium recovery technology and geothermal brine extraction methods are becoming increasingly economical. As Australia-Chile mines grow more expensive, these alternative routes become relatively more attractive.

**Differentiation Opportunity for Emerging Firms:** Emerging battery makers with low dependence on majors can, by investing early in unconventional sources, build independent supply chains. This creates a structure less vulnerable to Australia-Chile price volatility in the long term.

**But the Preemption Trap:** Major mining companies are also investing in unconventional sources. The most economical technologies are most likely to be preempted by major players first.

## Takeaway

As the battery industry booms, lithium mine consolidation is accelerating. While this creates near-term cost pressure on emerging battery makers, it paradoxically sparks development of alternative lithium source technologies such as geothermal brines and recycled lithium. Battery supply chains appear poised to evolve into a dual-structure where major mines and alternative sources coexist.
