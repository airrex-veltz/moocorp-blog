As lithium prices fell more than 90% from their 2022 peak by 2024, the global battery value chain entered an unexpected phase of restructuring. This extreme price collapse goes beyond a mere market adjustment, forcing structural changes in strategic relationships between mining companies and automotive and energy manufacturers.

## Lithium Market Overcapacity Reality

The International Energy Agency (IEA) found that lithium supply exceeded demand by more than 35% in 2024. Annual supply surpassed 1.2 million tonnes due to large new mining operations in Australia, Chile, and China, but demand growth fell short of expectations due to slowing electric vehicle sales and battery technology efficiency gains.

Spot prices per tonne collapsed from $87,000 in 2022 to an average of $7,500–$8,500 in 2024. In particular, large-scale exports of cheap Chinese lithium have locked in the global price floor. This has severely pressured mining companies' profitability, leading to production shutdowns and project delays. Major lithium producers like Albemarle (ALB) and SQM (SQM) repeated quarterly production cuts throughout 2024, and their share prices hit 52-week lows.

## Major Players' Survival Strategies

**Albemarle (ALB)** is the world's leading lithium supplier, controlling two major pillars: the Greenbushes mine in Australia and the Salar de Atacama salt flat in Chile. As quarterly losses accumulated in 2024, management changes and production cuts followed. The company is attempting to restructure its cost base to around $3,500 per tonne, a level that minimizes margins over spot prices. In price negotiations with long-term contract customers—particularly Tesla and LG Energy Solution—ALB prioritized loss-mitigation discussions.

**SQM** (Chilean chemical conglomerate) has diversified revenue streams through nitrogen and potassium fertilizer divisions alongside lithium, providing better cash flow support than ALB. However, it faces strategic impasse due to pressure from the Chilean government to raise lithium mining royalties (from the current 6–8% to a proposed 20% or more). SQM is deferring production capacity expansion while reconsidering partnerships within Chile.

**Tesla (TSL)** is accelerating vertical integration through in-house battery cell production (the 4680 cell project) and lithium mining investments (pursuing a stake in Arizona's Thacker Pass mine). In an oversupplied market, Tesla is pushing hard to eliminate price escalation clauses or convert them to spot-price indexing in long-term contracts. While this pressures suppliers like ALB and SQM, it reflects Tesla's strategy to defend electric vehicle margins through reduced battery costs.

**NIO** (Chinese EV maker) is indirectly hedging lithium price volatility through long-term supply agreements with CATL, but its battery procurement bargaining power is weakening amid intense domestic overproduction competition. NIO is pursuing battery swapping to separate battery ownership—a long-term strategy to mitigate battery raw material risks.

## Sectoral Impact: Mining, Energy, and Automotive

**Mining sector**: Price pressure is being exerted not just on lithium but across all battery raw materials, including nickel and cobalt. Chinese battery makers' expansion of LFP (lithium iron phosphate) batteries is reducing lithium demand, and as Chinese manufacturers maintain higher margins in the low-price environment, global competition is intensifying. Mining companies in Australia, Chile, and Argentina are reassessing project viability across all stages.

**Energy sector**: There is a downward trend in normalizing price expectations for 2025–2026. As the cost of deploying battery energy storage systems (BESS) moderates, energy companies' financial planning may improve. However, competition for long-term contract volumes is making it difficult for energy firms to predict lithium procurement costs accurately.

**Automotive sector**: Supply chain vertical integration is accelerating as leaders like Tesla and NIO prioritize battery cost reduction. Traditional automakers (Toyota, Volkswagen) are also concentrating investments on in-house battery production capability. This directly pressures battery makers' (CATL, LG Energy Solution, SK On) profitability, ultimately reducing lithium suppliers' customer base.

## Structural Shift in Long-Term Contracts and Integration

During the supply shortage of 2022–2023, spot-price-based short-term contracts and premium price clauses were prevalent. From 2024 onward, buyers (automakers and battery makers) are enforcing spot-price indexing clauses (with price caps) in 3–5 year long-term contracts. This increases unpredictability for lithium suppliers.

From Q4 2024, Tesla formalized its "self-supply" strategy through direct lithium mining investments. This goes beyond cost reduction—it aims to secure dominant positioning in the battery supply chain. LG Energy Solution is also negotiating to acquire a stake in Australia's Greenbushes mine, while CATL is concentrating capital on securing salt flat resources within China. Such vertical integration directly threatens independent lithium suppliers' (ALB, SQM) market share.

## Margin Pressure and Signals of Industry Restructuring

Lithium mining's break-even point has fallen to $4,000–$5,000 per tonne. Low-cost salt flat methods (Chile, Argentina, China) maintain profitability, but high-cost spodumene mining (Australia, Spain) is entering shutdown phases. This results in global lithium supply reconcentrating around China and Chile.

Battery makers' margins are also deteriorating. As automakers intensify demands for lower battery prices, battery manufacturers find that raw material price declines are not translating into their own profits. CATL has disclosed quarterly profit declines in 2024, and LG Energy Solution is accumulating operating losses. Only automakers are monopolizing the benefits of reduced battery costs.

## Added Complexity from Regulatory Environment

The Australian government is considering restrictions on strategic mineral exports, while the Chilean government is pursuing royalty increases and stricter environmental regulations. China maintains strategic advantage by continuing to lead the global market with low-cost supply. The US and EU are promoting global supply chain diversification through domestic lithium mining development and battery localization policies. While this intensifies short-term oversupply, it is expected to reshape regional supply ecosystems within five years.

## Outlook: Market Redefinition for 2026–2027

Oversupply is expected to persist until the first half of 2025, with gradual adjustment likely beginning in the second half. In the interim, independent suppliers like ALB and SQM must accept significant production cuts, while battery and automotive makers accelerate vertical integration. Lithium prices are likely to find equilibrium in the $8,000–$12,000 per tonne range, implying limited margins for mining companies.

Tesla's battery self-sufficiency, NIO's battery swapping expansion, and China's strengthened battery industry policy are expected to reshape the global battery supply chain around three axes (China, US, and EU). In this process, independent lithium suppliers' influence will diminish, and battery makers and automakers are likely to transition to a regime of jointly managing raw material costs within collaborative contract frameworks. As a result, from 2027 onward, the lithium market is expected to evolve beyond today's "transparent commoditization" phase toward a "long-term contracts plus strategic equity participation" model.
