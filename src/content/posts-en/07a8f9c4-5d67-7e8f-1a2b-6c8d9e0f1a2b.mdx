## Peru's Strike Puts EV Battery Costs Under the Knife

Labor disputes at Peru's copper mines are driving up global EV battery costs. As mining production shrinks by roughly one-fifth year-over-year, a bottleneck in worldwide copper supply has emerged, forcing battery makers to absorb rising raw material costs.

## Geopolitical Risk in Copper Mining

Peru is the world's second-largest copper producer. **SCCO** (Southern Copper Corporation) and **FCX** (Freeport-McMoRan) are major mining operators in Peru, and recent strikes have cut production by 2% year-over-year. As of September 25, 2026, SCCO closed at $203.92, down 4.60% over one month, while FCX fell to $72.31, down 8.47%. Both companies appear to be underestimating strike risk.

## Why Battery Costs Are Rising Now: Four Factors

**1. Copper Supply Inelasticity**: Copper is the raw material for cathodes, a critical component in EV batteries. When Peru's strikes create a supply bottleneck, global prices react immediately. Mine expansion takes years, so near-term supply is fixed.

**2. Margin Pressure on Battery Makers**: Samsung SDI closed at KRW 528,000 on September 28, 2026, down 16.06% over three months, and posted a net loss of KRW 584.9 billion in 2025. SK Innovation closed at KRW 158,900 on the same day (up 42.90% over one month) but shows a negative net margin of −1.3%. Rising costs squeeze already weak margins.

**3. Price Sensitivity in the EV Market**: Battery costs account for 40–50% of an electric vehicle's sales price. Raw material increases shift either to consumer costs or erode maker profits.

**4. Intensifying Competition with Chinese and U.S. Battery Makers**: Korea's share in the global battery market already faces pressure. Peru-driven cost increases further erode price competitiveness against Chinese rivals.

## Structural Weakness, No Near-Term Fix

Samsung SDI's 2025 revenue reached KRW 1.33 trillion; SK Innovation, KRW 8.03 trillion. Both need time to overhaul cost structures. Peru's strike may resolve or worsen in weeks, but prolonged battery cost increases will structurally squeeze Korean makers' margins.

## Summary

Peru's copper strike has exposed a global supply chain weakness. Rising EV battery costs directly undermine Korean makers' competitiveness, and near-term cost cuts are unlikely.
