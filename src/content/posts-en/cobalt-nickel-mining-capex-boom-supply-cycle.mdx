## Global Battery Transformation, Entry into Cobalt and Nickel Mining Investment Cycle

The surge in demand for electric vehicles and renewable energy storage devices has triggered a supply war for battery core raw materials such as cobalt and nickel. The scale and timing of capital deployment by global mining companies moving in this cycle will serve as a compass for raw material prices over the next three years.

## Performance Differentiation of Three Mining Companies—Growth Rate Gap by Investment Level

**Glencore (GLNCY)** closed at $15.55 on September 15, 2026, up 6.86% over the past month. With a year-over-year revenue growth rate of 48.6%, it demonstrates the most aggressive investment pace among the three companies. **Rio Tinto (RIO)** closed at $97.26 with a revenue growth rate of 15.5% and a net profit margin of 19.6%, maintaining a mid-tempo approach. **Vale (VALE)** closed at $14.47 with a revenue growth rate of 6.4%, showing a relatively gradual growth pace, though it has recovered 6.16% on a one-month basis.

What these numbers tell us is clear. GLNCY's high growth rate suggests that new mining development and increased production investment in existing mines are concentrated. Meanwhile, VALE appears to be undergoing an adjustment phase within a portfolio structure with high nickel dependency.

## Why is Cobalt and Nickel Mining Investment Surging Now: Four Factors

**1. Irreversible shift in battery demand structure**
Electric vehicle sales and energy storage system (ESS) deployment entered exponential growth after 2025. Demand to shift battery chemistry from LFP to high energy density NCA and NCM is also accelerating cobalt and nickel consumption. This represents not a simple business cycle but a permanent rise in demand base over a ten-year horizon.

**2. Realization of supply gap**
Global battery-grade cobalt supply is concentrated in the Democratic Republic of Congo, while nickel is dominated by Indonesia's mining base. The lack of new mining development investment over the past five years from 2020 to 2025 has led to a concrete supply gap emerging from 2026 onward. GLNCY's 48.6% revenue growth rate is the market's urgent signal for filling this gap.

**3. Capital intensity and first-mover advantage**
New mining development requires 5–10 years of lead time and billions of dollars in initial investment. Mining companies with projects already under way at this point will reap the benefits first from supply expansion in 2028–2030. GLNCY's high growth rate reflects this first-mover positioning.

**4. Difference in supply cycle maturity**
Each company's portfolio composition differs. GLNCY and RIO hold diversified mining operations across multiple raw materials and regions, enabling risk mitigation, while VALE has high dependency on nickel and iron ore. If a supply surplus signal emerges first in the nickel market, there is a risk that VALE's growth rate lag could become structural.

## However, Structural Pitfalls of the Mining Investment Cycle

**The paradox of profitability and growth rate**: RIO has the highest net profit margin at 19.6%, but its growth rate is mid-level. This means it is placing emphasis on stable cash generation from existing mining operations. GLNCY's high growth rate sacrifices near-term profitability to invest in new projects. When supply expands and mineral prices decline, the profitability of this strategy could come under pressure.

**Geopolitical supply disruption risk**: Political instability in the Democratic Republic of Congo and policy changes regarding export restrictions in Indonesia can disrupt the supply chain at any time. In particular, external shocks before new mines reach production can maximize price volatility.

## Conclusion

As the cobalt and nickel mining investment cycle enters full operation, the growth trajectories of the three companies are clearly diverging. GLNCY's extreme growth rate of 48.6% makes it the company responding most aggressively to supply expansion pressures, RIO maintains a stability-oriented stance, and VALE is adapting to structural change. Over the next one to two years, as supply expansion and price adjustment become apparent, this divergence will clearly manifest in the stock performance of each company.
