## Global Tech Companies' Return to India — The Beginning of Weakening China Dependency

Led by **AAPL**, global electronics companies are intensively building production bases in India. The move to disperse smartphone, semiconductor, and electronics components production outside of China is now a necessity rather than a strategy.

## Why India: The Geopolitics of Supply Chain Restructuring

China's dominance in global electronics manufacturing has lasted over 40 years. As wages, logistics, and political risks rose simultaneously, countries began seeking alternatives. India is the only viable option in terms of population, labor force, and government incentives.

**AAPL** is officially expanding the proportion of India production for key products like iPhones and AirPods. Local component suppliers like **SSNLF** (Sterlite Technologies) are direct beneficiaries of technology transfer and investment expansion. Localization efforts are accelerating not only in electronics assembly but also in semiconductor wafers, copper components, and optical materials.

## Why India Now: Four Factors

**1. Geopolitical risk mitigation**: US-China tensions and Taiwan Strait concerns have made supply chain concentration a political issue. Companies need not just cost savings but "production facilities that governments cannot take away." India is a strategic ally of the United States and has made clear its policy to distance itself from China.

**2. Rising risks within China**: Wage increases, strengthened environmental regulations, semiconductor export controls—the production costs in China have risen sharply over the past five years. Simultaneously, technology leakage disputes involving Chinese companies remain unresolved. The choice has shifted from "coexistence with China" to "gradual transition to India."

**3. India's aggressive government incentives**: India provides direct subsidies to semiconductor and electronics companies through the "Make in India" and "PLI (Production Linked Incentive)" policies. Using policy levers such as corporate tax cuts and raised import tariffs, India is attracting global companies.

**4. Economic efficiency of supply chain diversification**: Single-location production is no longer economically viable. Operating production bases in both China and India simultaneously reduces China risk exposure while maintaining price competitiveness. Low-cost smartphone and appliance production for export is particularly advantageous under India's wage structure.

## Why India Nevertheless: Structural Strengths and Weaknesses

**Strengths**: India's labor force is virtually unlimited. As smartphone manufacturing experience accumulates, the development of mid-skilled labor is accelerating. The domestic electronics market (1.3 billion population) also becomes a reason for production. The improved technical level of component suppliers like **SSNLF** also expedites global companies' investment decisions.

**Weaknesses**: India's logistics infrastructure still lags behind China's. Bottlenecks remain in port processing capacity, semiconductor back-end production experience, and skilled engineer shortage. Policy volatility is also significant. Local content ratio requirements could suddenly increase, or industrial policies could change, creating uncertainty that complicates investment planning.

## Summary

Global electronics production is now moving away from the single-China-hub era. **AAPL**'s expansion of India production is a signal of corporate strategy, and component suppliers and semiconductor manufacturers in India are expected to rapidly improve their competitiveness. However, given the infrastructure gap between India and China, India production is unlikely to completely replace China. Instead, a transitional period where "both production increases but China's share gradually decreases" is expected to continue over the next five years.
