## China's Solar Manufacturing Expansion Reshapes Global Pricing Structure

As China continues investing in solar panel manufacturing capacity, the global supply balance is collapsing. The oversupply goes beyond simply lowering prices—it is fundamentally changing how renewable energy project profitability is calculated. Project-level returns (IRR) are particularly affected, impacting capital raising and feasibility assessments.

## China's Manufacturing Capacity Expansion Trend

China's solar panel production capacity has surged over the past decade. Backed by carbon neutrality targets and energy independence policies, the Chinese government has supported manufacturing firms' capacity expansion. China's share of global production has risen steadily, with Chinese companies now dominating the majority of global panel supply.

Alongside increased volume, manufacturing processes have undergone significant refinement and automation. This has reduced production costs, strengthening the price competitiveness of Chinese manufacturers that have achieved cost advantages. In this structure, companies without cost advantages face pressure to adapt.

## Why Global Panel Oversupply is Intensifying Now: Four Factors

**1. Sustained Government Policy and Capital Inflow in China**

The Chinese government has classified the solar industry as strategic and continues to supply capital and policy incentives. Local governments and state capital have also participated in manufacturing expansion. While China's domestic market has grown rapidly, capacity additions have outpaced domestic demand growth. Excess production naturally flows to global markets. As long as this mechanism persists, China's production growth trajectory is unlikely to slow.

**2. Global Demand Growth Cannot Keep Pace with Supply Expansion**

Global renewable energy demand is increasing. Carbon reduction commitments and power supply pressures are indeed driving solar investment demand. However, demand growth has lagged behind China's manufacturing capacity expansion pace. As this gap widens, oversupply becomes entrenched. In particular, as adoption rates in developed countries rise, demand growth shows signs of slowing.

**3. Manufacturing Technology Standardization and Rising Competition**

While solar panels initially had high technological barriers, core technologies have been standardized and disclosed over the past decade. The number of manufacturers in China has grown significantly, and technology gaps have narrowed. Easier market entry for new players has intensified competition. This directly drives price competition deeper. Vicious cycles form as each manufacturer aggressively cuts prices to gain market share.

**4. Low Panel Prices Eroding Project Profitability**

As panel prices decline, individual project costs fall. On the surface this appears positive, but from an industry structure perspective it is complex. Overall project profitability depends not only on panel prices but also on inverters, installation, land lease, financing costs, and other factors. Panel price declines do not proportionally lower all these elements. Consequently, project IRRs fall short of expectations. When investors' minimum required returns become difficult to meet, project initiation itself is delayed.

## Structural Weaknesses and Some Strengths in the Global Renewable Energy Industry

**Weakness: Margin Pressure and Declining Financial Access**

Panel price declines directly pressure margins for panel manufacturers, particularly those producing outside China. Simultaneously, deteriorating project profitability makes capital raising more difficult. Financial institutions and investors withhold funding if expected returns are insufficient. This slows renewable energy project development. Smaller developers and startups are most vulnerable to this pressure.

**Strength: Technology Evolution and Long-Term Demand Trend**

Price declines lower market barriers to entry. Projects previously justified only at large scale are now viable at smaller scales. Additionally, solar technology continues to evolve. Efficiency improvements, longer lifespans, and installation innovations mean better products at comparable price points. Meanwhile, global decarbonization and energy transition are structural trends. Short-term price declines may even stimulate renewable energy industry growth.

## Conclusion

China's solar panel manufacturing capacity expansion is driving global panel price declines. While this appears to promote renewable energy adoption, it is actually reshaping the industry's profitability structure. Deteriorating project returns alter capital flows and may force some operators to exit. Only entities with technological innovation capacity and cost competitiveness survive this process, concentrating industry structure further. The future of the global renewable energy industry will be determined not by simple price competition but by the interplay of technology and capital power.
