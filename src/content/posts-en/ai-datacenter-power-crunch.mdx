## AI Demand Surge Strains Power Grid

The explosive demand for high-performance GPU servers is exposing the power design limitations of existing data centers. As individual AI chip modules consume far more power than previously anticipated, power infrastructure upgrades are accelerating rapidly. This signals a simultaneous boom for semiconductors, power utilities, and networking equipment makers.

## Roots of the Data Center Power Crisis

**NVIDIA** is the dominant force in the AI chip market. As of October 16, 2026, the stock closed at $214.13, with a market cap of $5.17 trillion. Revenue growth of 105.9% shows how strongly the market values GPU demand. Net profit margin of 63.7% reflects best-in-class pricing power.

But the problem is not the **NVDA** chips themselves but the infrastructure that operates them. Latest-generation GPUs like the H100, H200, and B200 each consume over 500 watts of power. High-density server racks require tens of megawatts of power. Existing data centers lack the electrical distribution design to handle this.

## Four Changes Triggered by Power Shortage

**1. Utility Supply Capability Reconfiguration**: Power companies have begun expanding transmission lines near data center hubs and modernizing substations. **NextEra Energy** (NEE) is a direct beneficiary of this trend. As of October 16, 2026, the stock closed at $79.80, with revenue growth of 12.4% and net profit margin of 32.4%. While growth is steady due to its solid business model, the surge in data center demand leaves room for acceleration.

**2. Downstream Opportunities for Chip Makers**: Not just **NVIDIA** but also networking infrastructure manufacturers benefit. **Broadcom** (AVGO) supplies data center power management, network interfacing, and high-speed serial connectivity chips. As of October 16, 2026, the stock closed at $328.75, with revenue growth of 85.5% and net profit margin of 42.9%.

**3. Surge in Power Network Equipment Orders**: Orders for high-capacity distribution equipment, cooling systems, and UPS manufacturers are increasing. Data center operators are reconsidering new construction site selection rather than merely improving existing facilities.

**4. Regulatory Environment Changes**: As regional power supply constraints tighten, some U.S. states have made data center capacity permits more stringent or added conditions. This creates a structure that guarantees long-term contracts and sustained revenue.

## Why Each Stock Deserves Attention

**NVIDIA's Strength**: Revenue growth of 105.9% and net profit margin of 63.7% indicate the market views GPU supply as hard to disrupt. As AI infrastructure expansion accelerates, chip demand will continue to grow.

**Broadcom's Opportunity**: With diverse serial, power management, and networking technologies, it achieved revenue growth of 85.5%. While the P/E ratio of 42.0 is high, data center infrastructure replacement cycles could sustain growth.

**NextEra's Stability**: As power supply constraints deepen, the importance of major utilities like **NEE** grows. While 12.4% growth is modest, undervalued energy infrastructure investment is supported by regulatory revenue guarantees and long-term demand.

## Summary

As AI chip demand hits the limits of power infrastructure, semiconductors, networking, and energy sectors move in concert. **NVIDIA**'s dominant chip share, **Broadcom**'s infrastructure diversification, and **NextEra Energy**'s power grid expansion each follow different trajectories. In the near term, chip supply, and in the medium term, network equipment and power grid modernization, are expected to become sequential bottlenecks.
