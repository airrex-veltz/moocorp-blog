## Interest Rate Decline Upvalues Existing Construction Contracts

Korean construction companies are re-evaluating project profitability in a changing interest rate environment. Falling financing costs and rising embedded value of existing fixed-rate contracts are the paths improving profit structures.

## Interest Rate Sensitivity of Construction Project Profitability

Construction firms finance large, long-term projects with multibillion-won borrowings. Contract prices are mostly fixed, so financing costs determine final margins. In fixed-price contracts, rate declines translate directly to upside profit. Conversely, rate increases raise funding costs and erode margins.

A construction company's profit structure follows: Contract Price (fixed) - Raw Materials/Labor (variable) - Financing Costs (rate-linked) = Project Profit. In large infrastructure projects, construction spans 3-5 years, so financing cost fluctuations from rate changes accumulate after contract signing.

## Why Builder Margins Are Improving Now: Four Drivers

**1. Rising Real Value of Fixed-Rate Contracts**: Already-won fixed-price contracts were evaluated for viability at contract-time rates. When rates fall, the assumed financing costs at signing exceed reality. For example, a project signed at 3% rates in early 2026 likely factored in borrowing costs of 3.5-4%. If current rates are now 2%, new borrowing is around 2.5%, so existing contract margins improve by 0.5-1.5 percentage points in real terms.

**2. Declining Interest Expense on New Borrowing**: Projects requiring additional funds can borrow at lower rates. Large builders like **LX International**, **GS Construction**, and **GS** benefit from strong credit ratings and preferred rates—advantages that magnify as rate environments decline.

**3. Improved Funding Cost Environment**: When signing new contracts in a positive rate outlook, builders can evaluate project viability with lower financing costs than before. This creates room to book higher margins with unchanged costs. In highly competitive fields like government infrastructure, margin improvement is directly tied to competitive positioning.

**4. Debt Service Burden Relief**: Interest payment obligations on long-term borrowings decline. Improved financial liquidity can accelerate new project starts and completion of existing ones.

## Yet Structural Constraints Remain for Builders

**Fixed Contracts' Double Edge**: Rate decline favors existing contracts, but if rate increases loom, new bids must rise. High economic volatility can shrink bidding opportunities themselves.

**Inflexible Capital Sourcing Structure**: Borrowing terms differ between government and private projects, with substantial rate differentials by credit rating. Rate decline benefits don't apply uniformly across all projects.

## Summary

Declining interest rate environments raise real margins on fixed-price projects construction firms already secured. However, this benefit extends only to existing contract portfolios; new bidding environments are more heavily influenced by macro conditions and bid competition. Assessing builder profitability requires watching both financing cost changes on in-progress projects and margin trends in new bids.
