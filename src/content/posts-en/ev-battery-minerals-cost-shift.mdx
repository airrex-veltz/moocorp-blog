## Battery Raw Material Regulations Are Shaking the EV Industry

Europe's Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) and the US Inflation Reduction Act (IRA) are being implemented simultaneously, driving sharp increases in battery material costs. This represents a structural change leading to higher EV prices and compressed manufacturer margins.

## Regulations Creating Cost Pressures

Lithium, cobalt, and nickel—the lifeblood of the EV industry—are critical battery minerals. Yet in recent months, cost pressures on mining companies are becoming visible.

**TSLA** was trading at 354.72 USD as of October 16, 2026, down 4.02% over the past three months. In the same period, **ALB** (Albemarle) dropped to 95.48 USD, a sharp 18.95% decline over three months. By market capitalization, TSLA stands at 1.40 trillion dollars, while ALB is at 11.3 billion dollars, yet these two companies' fortunes are intertwined by regulation.

## Why Raw Material Costs Are Rising Now: Four Factors

**1. EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) Expansion**: Starting in 2026, the EU began pricing carbon emissions from mining and refining stages. Mining governments and mining companies must either absorb additional costs or reduce production. These costs are passed onto international market prices.

**2. US Inflation Reduction Act (IRA) Local Content Requirements**: The IRA provides tax benefits only to EVs fully assembled in North America. Simultaneously, it restricts battery minerals to a certain percentage from US or free trade agreement sources. This excludes low-cost suppliers from Africa and China, forcing concentration among suppliers meeting higher environmental standards.

**3. Mining Companies' Margin Maintenance Strategy**: Mineral suppliers like ALB do not passively accept cost increases. Despite achieving 31.1% sales growth (YoY), the stock fell 18.95% because the market views profit gains as being offset by raw cost pressures. When spot prices are high, mining companies tend to adjust production volumes to maintain price levels.

**4. Supply Uncertainty and Dual-Sourcing Costs**: EV makers must now avoid single suppliers and develop diversified sourcing strategies. This increases logistics, inventory, and contract management costs. Even high-volume producers like TSLA are no exception.

## Nevertheless / However: Structural Duality

**Manufacturer margin compression appears inevitable.** TSLA's net profit margin of 3.7% is already extremely thin. As raw materials comprise a substantial portion of battery costs, rising material prices cut into margins. The 25.5% sales growth (YoY) must be supported by unit price increases, yet rising consumer prices lead to demand softening.

**Yet the regulations are not entirely negative.** ALB's 31.1% sales growth signals that a premium is attached to developed-country minerals to meet regulatory compliance. Suppliers with advanced refining technology, high environmental standards, and strong ESG scores can now defend their price points. Medium-term, thin-margin miners may exit the market, potentially deepening monopolistic concentration among larger players.

## Summary

Battery raw material regulations go beyond momentary price spikes, bringing structural industry restructuring. Manufacturers like TSLA must navigate the narrowing path between rising costs and price competitiveness, while mineral suppliers like ALB have entered an era where regulatory compliance capability determines survival. The implementation timeline of regulatory details and the speed of global supply chain realignment will determine this sector's profitability through 2027–2028.
