## Iron Ore Collapse Compresses Australian Mining Capex 30% Over 18 Months

China's structural steel overcapacity deepens, pushing iron ore near seven-year lows. Sustained weakness scenarios could force major Australian miners **RIO** and **BHP** to cut capex and dividends 30% over 18 months. The world's #1-2 mining firms face this pressure zone.

## Current Status of Australian Iron Ore Miners

**RIO** (Rio Tinto): Oct 9, 2026 close $94.78. 1-month -4.64%, 3-month +7.80%. Net margin 19.6%, PER 12.8. **BHP** (BHP Group): same date $86.74. 1-month -0.70%. Net margin 16.7%, PER 22.5 (higher valuation than RIO). YoY revenue growth: RIO 15.5%, BHP 18.3%—solid but forward headwinds are real.

## Why Cash Flow Faces Pressure Now: Four Factors

**1. Structural China Steel Overcapacity**: China is core production economy yet faces endemic steel overcapacity. Construction and auto demand weakness cuts ore import appetite. Supply excess directly suppresses raw material prices.

**2. Iron Ore Supply Inelasticity**: Australian, Brazilian, Indian giants expanded capacity over ten years; supply now high and stuck. Even if price drops, they can't cut production quickly.

**3. Capex Cutdown Necessity**: Mining firms operate finite-life assets. Price weakness forces deferring new mining projects and large M&A. RIO and BHP likely signal capex revisions next quarter.

**4. Dividend Compression Scenario**: Net margins stay solid (RIO 19.6%, BHP 16.7%), but 30% earnings fall makes dividend payout maintenance impossible. Cash returns to shareholders fall automatically.

## Why Still RIO and BHP: Structural Assets and Resilience

**Strengths**: RIO's PER 12.8 trades below sector average; revaluation room exists on price recovery. Both hold world-scale iron ore, copper, coal assets. Supply resumes fast when prices rebound. Cash buffers let dividend cuts be minimal.

**Weaknesses**: Steel overcapacity resolution takes months-plus. Cash flow deterioration looms several quarters. FMG (Fortescue), more iron ore-concentrated, may compete harder. Recovery timing uncertainty shakes confidence.

## Summary

China's structural adjustment could force Australian miners to cut capex and dividends 30%+ over coming quarters. RIO and BHP have low valuation and cash cushions, but price volatility will be high during the pause. Market focus shifts to each firm's capex guidance revision and dividend reset announcements.
