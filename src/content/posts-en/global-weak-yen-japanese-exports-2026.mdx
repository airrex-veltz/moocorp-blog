# Deepening Japanese Yen Weakness and Price Competitiveness Recovery in Export Manufacturing

Persistent yen weakness over recent months has created an important opportunity for Japanese export manufacturers to recover global price competitiveness. Currency weakness is not merely a monetary phenomenon but a development directly linked to corporate performance and reshaping of global market share, and as of the fourth quarter of 2026, the contours of this structural change are becoming clearer.

## Reshaping Export Competitiveness Through Yen Weakness

A weak yen automatically reduces the dollar-denominated export prices of Japanese companies. With the same production costs, overseas customers can now purchase at lower dollar prices, causing price competitiveness in developed markets to surge. This effect is particularly pronounced in high-value-added manufacturing sectors such as semiconductors, automobiles, and consumer electronics. As of October 5, 2026, stock price movements of major Japanese exporters reflect this phenomenon.

## Automotive Industry: Testing Toyota's Resilience

Toyota Motor (TM), the world's largest automaker, is the most direct beneficiary of yen weakness. As of the close on October 5, 2026, it is recording 184.12 USD, with a one-month change of -7.88% showing near-term weakness. However, the three-month change of +2.61% demonstrates resilience, and the current price is in the mid-range considering the recent six-month range of 166.10~219.85 USD.

Toyota's fundamentals remain sound. A market cap of 220.0B USD, a low valuation with PER of 8.2, net profit margin of 8.6%, and most importantly YoY revenue growth of 10.4%, signal genuine export recovery. The logic is that in a weak yen environment, even if dollar-denominated export prices fall, increased transaction volume can actually grow absolute revenue. This represents the process of Toyota's price competitiveness being re-evaluated by automotive buyers worldwide.

## Consumer Electronics Global Competition: Sony's Mixed Signals

Sony (6758.T) is exposed to global markets through its diverse consumer electronics and entertainment business. As of the close on October 5, 2026, at 3,723.00 JPY, it showed slight weakness with a one-month change of -7.54%, but posted significant strength with a three-month change of +15.10%. This suggests that yen weakness benefit logic is operating on a three-month timeframe.

However, Sony's fundamentals send mixed signals. A market cap of 22.00T yen (approximately 220.0B USD), a PER of 20.2 above market average, and a net profit margin of -1.8% indicate recent profitability deterioration. In contrast, YoY revenue growth stands at 8.2%, among the top tier. This suggests revenue is expanding but net profit is under pressure through cost management and business restructuring. The price competitiveness benefit from yen weakness is not fully translating into margin improvement.

## Investment Sector Potential: SoftBank's Positioning

SoftBank (9984.T), an investment and telecommunications company rather than a pure manufacturer, benefits from yen weakness from a different angle. As of the close on October 5, 2026, at 6,497.00 JPY, it recorded a one-month change of +23.58% and three-month change of +8.38%. This is a distinct uptrend unlike manufacturing companies.

SoftBank's appeal lies in its commanding net profit margin of 61.6%. This is because investment gains and financial revenue are core operations, and in a weak yen environment, the dollar-denominated performance of global portfolio companies can appear relatively stronger. A PER of 7.2 is undervalued, and YoY revenue growth of 10.9% shows solid expansion. In yen weakness scenarios, SoftBank holds a two-sided advantage where rising global asset values reinforce domestic telecommunications business profitability simultaneously.

## Relative Position Against Korean Competitors: Hyundai Motor

Hyundai Motor (005380.KS) serves as a comparative benchmark as a major competitor to Japanese automakers. As of the close on October 6, 2026, at 348,500 KRW, it showed weakness similar to Toyota with a one-month change of -7.80%, but recorded a far more severe decline with a three-month change of -41.13%.

Hyundai Motor's fundamentals are weaker than Japanese competitors. A net profit margin of 4.3% is half Toyota's level, and YoY revenue growth of 1.9% is nearly flat. While 2025 revenue of KRW 186.3 trillion and operating profit of KRW 11.5 trillion remain substantial, relative growth lags. As Japanese companies strengthen price competitiveness from yen weakness benefits, the relatively stable Korean won leaves Hyundai Motor at risk of global market share pressure. The three-month steep decline is interpreted as a move that pre-reflects these structural weaknesses.

## Investment Implications: Selective Benefits, Caution on Near-Term Volatility

First, Toyota is the purest beneficiary of yen weakness. Undervalued valuation (PER 8.2), solid profit margin (8.6%), and rising revenue growth rate (10.4%) move consistently together. The near-term -7.88% decline has room to be viewed as overshooting.

Second, Sony sends mixed signals. Three-month strength and revenue growth are positive, but negative net margins show the need to improve cost structure. A watchful stance is warranted until yen benefits fully convert to margin improvement.

Third, SoftBank is worth considering if aiming for dual benefits from global portfolio companies and domestic telecommunications business. It is already showing strength at +23.58% one-month and undervalued valuation (PER 7.2) leaves room for further appreciation.

Fourth, Korean automaker Hyundai Motor faces structural weakness. Strengthened price competitiveness of Japanese competitors directly means market share pressure for Hyundai Motor, and the three-month -41.13% decline already significantly reflects these concerns.

The yen weakness phenomenon is likely to reshape the global manufacturing competitive landscape from the fourth quarter of 2026 through 2027. However, given significant near-term stock price volatility, positions should be evaluated by reviewing fundamental metrics (growth rate, profit margin, valuation) together.
