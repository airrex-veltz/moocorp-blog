## Technology Barriers in Ultra-Pure Chemicals Determine Korean Chemical Firms' Pricing Power

The supply structure of ultra-pure chemicals demanded by semiconductor manufacturing and battery production processes dominates Korean chemical firms' margins. These materials require ultra-refined technology tolerating no trace impurities, and whether this translates to pricing power or margin-eroding raw material and yield pressure determines operating profit's fate.

## Semiconductor and Battery Manufacturing Demand Ultra-Pure Hydrofluoric Acid, Determining Margins

**LG Chemical** (051910) and **SK Innovation** (096770) are South Korea's main suppliers of ultra-pure chemicals including semiconductor etchants, hydrofluoric acid, and battery electrolytes. LG Chemical's 2025 revenue was KRW 45.9 trillion, SK Innovation's KRW 80.3 trillion—large in scale but struggling to generate profits. LG Chemical recorded a net loss of KRW -977.1 billion in 2025, while SK Innovation's profit margin stands at -1.3%. Revenue growth (LG 19.0%, SK Innovation 49.9%) fails to translate to profit growth—a structure reflecting specialty chemical characteristics.

## Why Ultra-Pure Chemical Margins Face Pressure Now: Four Drivers

**1. High Technical Barriers Yet Extreme Customer Concentration**: Ultra-pure hydrofluoric acid tolerates no trace impurities, requiring extremely advanced purification technology. This excludes new entrants but simultaneously forces semiconductor and battery makers to severely limit suppliers. Major customers like Samsung Electronics, SK Hynix, and LG Energy Solution prioritize supply stability over diversification, so existing supplier contracts ossify. Result: high technical barriers transform into volume competition rather than price increases.

**2. Climate Regulation and Purification Process Yield Pressure**: Hydrofluoric acid faces the strictest environmental regulations of any chemical. Emission limits, rising disposal costs, and strengthened recovery standards continuously tighten, but yield improvement in purification is difficult. Raw material input grows or waste disposal costs rise to produce one ton, raising costs. Given industry structure where passing price increases to customers is difficult, this directly cuts margins.

**3. Accelerating Battery Supply Creating Electrolyte Competition**: Post-2025, rapidly surging EV battery production creates soaring electrolyte demand. SK Innovation's 49.9% revenue growth reflects expanded battery value chain. Yet widespread initial margin sacrifice occurs to capture new customers and expand volume. As competitor entry and price competition among incumbents converge, profit growth cannot keep pace with revenue growth.

**4. Global Supply Chain Uncertainty in Raw Material Price Volatility**: International prices for ultra-pure hydrofluoric acid feedstocks—fluorite and fluorine compounds—are unstable. China's environmental controls, rare earth and mineral tariffs, and geopolitical tensions create sharp feedstock volatility. Customers strongly tend to externalize this, forcing suppliers to absorb pass-through.

## Why They Remain Technology Firms: Structural Strengths and Weaknesses

**Technology Monopoly and Customer Lock-In**: Ultra-advanced purification capability and quality assurance guarantee customer production stability. Once designated as supplier, switching costs are huge, justifying unit price increases.

**Yet Why Margins Collapse**: Customer value chain power is overwhelming. Semiconductor and battery giants enforce quarterly contract prices on chemical firms and allocate yield gains from technical improvement as price cuts. Large capital investment scales also pressure margin sacrifice to hit cost reduction targets.

## Summary

Ultra-pure chemical technology barriers are high, but customer concentration, regulatory tightening, supply chain volatility, and new market competition neutralize pricing power. **LG Chemical** and **SK Innovation** recent losses and margin pressure stem not from technical shortage but from industry oversupply and absolute customer bargaining power. Medium-term margins will depend on customer capex cycles and new supplier entry.
