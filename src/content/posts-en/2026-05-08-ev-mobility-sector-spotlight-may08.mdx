The electric vehicle and battery sector is at an important inflection point. While market share expansion continues, the landscape has shifted to prioritize profitability and technological differentiation. As of 2026, the global EV market has grown to an annual level of 17.5 million units, but intensifying price competition and rising raw material costs are pressuring manufacturers' profitability.

<KeyPoints title="Key Takeaways">
- Global EV market projected at 17.5 million units in 2026, penetration rate 18–20%
- Advantage to well-capitalized, technologically strong players like Tesla, Hyundai Motor/Kia, and LG Energy Solution
- Accelerating competition in battery technology innovation (LFP, NCMA, solid-state batteries)
- Expansion of North American factory investments benefiting from US IRA policy
- Short-term volatility expected, but long-term growth trend remains intact
</KeyPoints>

## Sector Overview

The electric vehicle and clean mobility sector is transitioning from early growth to mass adoption and profitability—a new set of challenges. The global EV market is projected to grow from approximately 14 million units in 2025 to 17.5–18 million units in 2026, while the battery market, valued at USD 12 billion, is growing at a compound annual rate of 22%.

## Key Holdings — US Market

**Tesla (TSLA)** is maintaining its market leadership through expanded Cybertruck production and plans to launch a lower-priced model in 2027. With a market cap of around USD 60 billion, its Full Self-Driving technology and energy storage business have emerged as new growth drivers, expanding 50% year-over-year.

<Callout type="catalyst" title="Tesla Catalysts">
Cybertruck production target of 250,000 units in 2026 and the FSD v13 launch are likely to determine near-term stock momentum.
</Callout>

**Rivian (RIVN)** is expanding into lower price points with its R2 platform launch in H2 2026 in the premium electric truck market. It has a contract with Amazon for 100,000 electric delivery vans, but burning over USD 1 billion in cash per quarter makes the path to profitability an immediate challenge.

**Ford** and **GM** are expanding their lineups with the F-150 Lightning, Mustang Mach-E, and Ultium platform vehicles, but face dual pressure: persistent losses in their EV divisions alongside declining demand for traditional combustion vehicles.

## Key Holdings — Korea Market

## Trends and Catalysts

Competition in battery technology innovation is accelerating. For low-cost models, Chinese player CATL dominates LFP batteries, while Korean and Japanese companies show strength in NMC/NCMA for high-performance models. Tesla is expanding in-house production of 4680 cylindrical cells, while Toyota and Samsung SDI are stepping up R&D with a target of commercializing solid-state batteries in 2027–2028.

The US IRA law provides USD 7,500 tax credits for EVs and batteries produced in North America, stimulating factory investment by Korean and Japanese companies. Chinese battery component restrictions are set to tighten gradually from 2026, driving supply chain restructuring.

<Callout type="data" title="Near-Term Catalysts">
Full capacity operations at Hyundai Motor/Kia's North American factories, the IONIQ 7 launch, and LG Energy Solution's Q2 results are expected to provide near-term momentum.
</Callout>

Progress is also underway in autonomous driving and robotaxi. Tesla's FSD beta is expanding, while Waymo and Cruise have entered commercialization—though regulatory and safety concerns persist.

## Risks and Outlook

China dominates over 50% of global EV sales but is seeing demand growth slow due to economic softness. Domestic players like BYD, Geely, and Li Auto control the market, making it difficult for foreign companies to break in.

<Callout type="risk">
Delayed rate cuts could dampen EV demand, while lithium and nickel price volatility, battery fire recall costs, and EV subsidy reductions pose key risks.
</Callout>

Long-term, EV penetration is projected to reach 30–40% by 2030, and battery costs are expected to fall a further 10–15% from 2025. However, in the near term, valuation pressure and intensifying competition will continue to squeeze margins.

Well-capitalized, technology-strong players like Tesla, Hyundai Motor/Kia, and LG Energy Solution are positioned advantageously, while cash management remains the survival question for new entrants like Rivian. The sector remains attractive for long-term investors, but the moment calls for readiness for near-term volatility.
