The explosive growth in AI model training demand is straining the cooling capacity of datacenter infrastructure. As GPU-intensive workloads proliferate, electricity consumption and cooling water demand surge simultaneously, while regional utility capacity constraints and environmental and water resource regulations have emerged as new axes of location competition. This extends beyond simple infrastructure cost increases, affecting corporate regional strategies and long-term operational structures.

## AI Chip Demand and Infrastructure Bottleneck Mismatch

**NVDA** stands at the center of this demand. As of November 12, 2026 close, trading at $166.19, despite a one-month decline of -24.90%, revenue growth maintains a year-over-year rate of 105.9%. The high net profit margin of 63.7% demonstrates the intensity of AI chip demand. However, the recent stock price adjustment suggests that investors are increasingly evaluating supply expansion constraints—particularly infrastructure bottlenecks—more seriously.

## Rising Capital Intensity for Cloud Operators

**MSFT** is positioned to directly capture this demand through Azure. As of November 12, 2026, trading at $523.00 with a market cap of $3.89 trillion, it is achieving 18.8% revenue growth. The net profit margin of 41.2% remains solid, but rising capital expenditures from datacenter cooling infrastructure expansion could threaten future profit margin maintenance. This is because the deployment of high-power GPU clusters is hitting the limits of existing cooling systems, shortening equipment replacement cycles.

## Dual Constraints of Power Supply and Freshwater Regulation

Rising datacenter power consumption collides with regional grid supply capacity. As GPU density increases, cooling power also increases exponentially, yet many regions lack new generation capacity. Cases are multiplying where negotiations with local utilities result in premium charges or delays in expansion approvals themselves. Simultaneously, the surge in freshwater extraction for cooling is generating community resistance and strengthening environmental regulations. In water-stressed regions, new datacenter permits are being restricted or cooling water recycling technologies are being mandated, sharply constraining corporate site selection decisions.

## Structuring of Policy Risk

Multiple local governments are moving to suspend new datacenter permits or tighten conditions. Under pressure to meet carbon neutrality targets, the regulatory stance toward "high-power consumption industries" will be difficult to ease over time. This causes corporations to lose strategic flexibility in site selection and increases dependence on secured capacity.

## Restructuring of Profit Structure

NVDA's high net profit margin (63.7%) means it retains pricing power even amid supply shortages. Large operators like MSFT possess the capital and government influence necessary for new site development. Conversely, for smaller cloud providers or new datacenter operators, securing a location itself could become a survival issue.

**CRM**, while not a direct operator, trades at $217.57 as of November 12, 2026, recording 10.8% revenue growth and may face indirect impacts from rising datacenter expansion costs among its customer base. As customers absorb higher cloud costs, spending decisions on software services like CRM could become more cautious.

Going forward, the keys to datacenter competition will lie in low-power design, freshwater conservation technology, and building community trust. Companies that overcome infrastructure bottlenecks quickly will secure competitive advantage in the AI era.
