## Blockbuster Patents Expire, Reshaping the Pharma Industry

The 2020s mark a 'patent cliff' era for global pharmaceutical companies. As major drug patents expire in waves, original drug makers face simultaneous pressure on revenue and margins. Conversely, generic drug manufacturers are seizing an opportunity to reassess profitability through low-cost market entry.

## Slowing Growth for Brand-Name Makers, Headwinds for Generics

**JNJ** (Johnson & Johnson) trades at $253.79 as of October 19, 2026, yet revenue growth lags at 5.9% annually. Net profit margin remains healthy at 16.2%, but growth momentum has clearly stalled. **PFE** (Pfizer Inc.) faces even tougher conditions. Closing at $29.44 on October 19, 2026, with revenue growth of just 2.6%, the company exemplifies industry stagnation. Net profit margin stands at only 6.8%.

Generic leader **TEVA**, meanwhile, closed at $41.31 on October 19, 2026, bearing thin margins of 4.1% while managing revenue decline of -0.8% amid market share restructuring.

## Four Drivers Behind Current Industry Movement

**1. Blockbuster Patent Expiration**: Major drug patents expire sequentially, kicking off generic competition in earnest. Once patent protection ends, generic makers can enter at lower prices, and market share rapidly shifts to low-cost offerings. Original drug makers' excess profits evaporate instantly.

**2. Rapid Price Collapse Upon Generic Entry**: Generic drugs launch below brand-name prices, and when multiple generics compete, prices tumble further. Market share transitions to generics within a relatively short window. Brand-name makers face a revenue cliff.

**3. Rising R&D Cost Burden on Original Drug Makers**: To withstand patent cliffs, original drug makers concentrate investment in new drug development. Yet approval timelines and costs keep climbing, while success rates hover below 10%. PFE's 2.6% growth and JNJ's 5.9% growth reflect this structural squeeze.

**4. Generic Makers' Portfolio Diversification and Economies of Scale**: TEVA anticipates patent expiration on dozens of drugs and has built production capacity in advance. By spreading fixed costs across diverse generic offerings, the company pursues economies of scale. Margins are thin, but bulk volume preserves profit—a volume-based strategy.

## Structural Tensions Worth Noting

**Brand-Name Makers' High Net Margins and Cash Cows**: JNJ's 16.2% net profit margin still towers over generic makers' 4.1%—four times higher. Cash and brand value accumulated during periods of patent monopoly underpin R&D and M&A. Patent cliffs leave time for portfolio transition spanning years.

**Generic Makers' Low-Margin Trap**: TEVA's P/E of 68.7 dwarfs JNJ (39.4) and PFE (39.7). The market doubts generic makers' long-term profitability. Should patent expirations flood the market with generic drugs, price competition intensifies, and smaller players risk elimination. TEVA is no exception.

## Takeaway

Pharma's patent cliff is not mere price erosion but a restructuring of margin dynamics. Brand-name makers must defend margins while surrendering growth; generic makers must grow volume amid cutthroat competition. Both camps struggle to sustain yesterday's business model.
