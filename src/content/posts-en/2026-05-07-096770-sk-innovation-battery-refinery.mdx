## Company Overview

SK Innovation is a holding company overseeing SK On (battery), SK Earthon (separator materials), and SK Trading International (energy trading). With market cap KRW 10.2 trillion, it combines traditional refining operations with battery startup investments. Current price is KRW 142,000, up 1.8% from the previous close but 15.7% below the 52-week high of KRW 168,500.

## Fundamentals

2024 consolidated revenue reached KRW 68 trillion, up 3% year-over-year, but operating profit fell 62% to KRW 845 billion, driven primarily by SK On's massive losses. The refining division processes 840,000 barrels per day—Korea's third-largest capacity—but achieved an operating margin of just 1.2%. Singapore refining spreads show signs of $3 per barrel improvement, though Brent crude remains in a $75–85 range with high uncertainty.

SK On recorded KRW 2.9 trillion in 1Q 2025 revenue and an operating loss of KRW 280 billion. Cumulative losses stand at approximately KRW 6 trillion. The Georgia plant operates at 21.5 GWh annual capacity and targets 35 GWh in 2026. It supplies batteries to North American automakers including Ford and Hyundai and benefits from IRA tax credits.

## Catalysts and Risks

<Callout type="catalyst" title="SK On IPO and IRA Benefits">
A potential SK On IPO relaunch in H2 2026~2027 looks likely, with a target market cap of KRW 15 trillion. Success would resolve SK Innovation's holding company discount and improve its financial structure. The Georgia plant secures competitiveness through IRA tax credits while raising utilization.
</Callout>

<Callout type="risk">
SK On's breakeven timing remains opaque. Chinese competitors including CATL and BYD are intensifying price competition, and massive capital requirements from plant expansion weigh heavily. The refining division faces crude price volatility and refining spread uncertainty.
</Callout>

## Takeaway

SK Innovation investing hinges on two competing scenarios. First, SK On achieves an IPO and reaches breakeven via IRA benefits. Second, refining margins recover as traditional operations offset battery losses. H2 2026 IPO timing and Georgia plant utilization ramp are near-term watch points. Resolution of the holding company discount is also worth monitoring. Given a debt-to-equity ratio of 230% and massive investment burdens, a cautious approach is warranted.
