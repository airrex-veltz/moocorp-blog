## NVIDIA's Absolute Dominance Shakes: GPU Performance Gap Narrows to Historic Minimum

As of end-2025, **NVIDIA**'s GPU performance gap has been the narrowest since 2020. AMD's MI325X and Google TPU v5e have caught up to the H100 at 70-80% levels, and enterprise customer technical committees have publicly strengthened demands for multi-source adoption. Supply chain stability and license cost reduction have emerged as new decision-making variables.

## New Competitive Landscape for AI Chips

**NVIDIA** dominated the GPU market for over a decade, but competitive dynamics have shifted rapidly in the past two years. As **AMD**'s EPYC-based accelerators, Tesla's custom chips, and Meta's MTIA development proceed simultaneously, consensus has formed that dependence on a single supplier is risky. With **TSM** increasing orders from competing chips, manufacturing capacity is also being decentralized.

## Why Is Competition Intensifying Now: 4 Key Factors

**1. Actual Reduction in Performance Gap**: AMD MI325X is showing efficiency levels equal to or better than H100 in certain inference tasks. Google TPU is reaching similar levels in specific workloads. While a gap of 2x or more would not have been impossible 5-10 years ago, now there is room to choose optimal solutions for each workload.

**2. Large Enterprises' Supply Chain Diversification Demands**: Major AI infrastructure investors like Meta, Google, and Microsoft have surfaced the risk of NVIDIA monopoly. During contract negotiations, clauses for "verification of competing chip options" have become standardized, and in fact some batches are now being allocated to AMD or custom chips.

**3. Rising License and Regulatory Uncertainty**: As U.S. export controls and China isolation policies persist, distrust in NVIDIA architecture licensing has grown. With ARM licensing having a history of disputes, more companies are turning to independent designs.

**4. Softened Learning Curve and Maturing Software Ecosystem**: General-purpose frameworks like PyTorch and TensorFlow have reduced CUDA dependency, reducing chip compatibility issues. Previous migration costs of 3-5 years have now shortened to 6 months to 1 year.

## Why NVIDIA Remains Strong: Structural Advantages

**Developer Ecosystem and CUDA Legacy**: CUDA remains the default currency of AI software. Even nascent startups test NVIDIA first, and requiring "CUDA experience" in engineer job postings is standard. This inertia will not break within 3-5 years.

**Continued Design Superiority**: NVIDIA is proactively publishing H200, B200, and Blackwell architectures while pressuring competitors' roadmaps. It still leads in memory bandwidth and power efficiency per teraflop.

## Summary

The winner in the AI chip market is becoming the survivor, not the monopolist. **NVIDIA**'s absolute dominance is wavering, but 3-5 years of technological superiority is secured. A multipolar ecosystem is expected to form, with **AMD** competing on price and **TSM** leveraging manufacturing monopoly to divide the ecosystem.
