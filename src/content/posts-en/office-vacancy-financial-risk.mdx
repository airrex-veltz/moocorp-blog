As remote work spreads, office demand has structurally contracted, and the deterioration of the commercial mortgage-backed securities (CMBS) market threatens the soundness of financial institutions. Within this vicious cycle, major U.S. banks like **JPM**, **BAC**, and **C** face profit margin pressure caught between regulatory capital requirements and rising credit costs.

## The Structure of the Office Vacancy Crisis

Four years after the pandemic, office vacancy rates in major financial hubs like New York, San Francisco, and Boston have become chronic at over 15%. As remote work and hybrid employment have taken hold, companies have reduced their required office space, and this is not a temporary business cycle downturn but a structural change that will persist for decades.

The problem concentrates in financial institutions. Major banks and insurance companies invested substantial capital in Commercial Mortgage-Backed Securities (CMBS) issued from the 1980s until just before the 2020 pandemic. These securities were valued under the assumption they were backed by sound rental income, but the surge in office vacancy rates demolished that premise. Landlords' cash flows have deteriorated, and the inability to meet loan repayment schedules is becoming more common.

## Why Are Banks' Non-Performing Loans Increasing Now? Four Factors

**1. Rising vacancy erodes rents**: Property owners lower rents or face non-renewals to retain tenants. Declines are especially sharp in high-cost areas like Manhattan. As debt repayment capacity weakens, the underlying asset value of CMBS also deteriorates.

**2. High rates block refinancing**: Property owners with low-rate loans cannot refinance in the current high-rate environment as their loans mature. They end up unable to complete refinancing within the deadline and face default risk.

**3. CMBS deterioration erodes bank assets**: The deterioration of CMBS securities held or invested in by banks accumulates accounting losses. This reduces equity and worsens the regulatory capital ratio.

**4. Tightening capital requirements force loan contraction**: The Federal Reserve monitors banks' capital adequacy trends. If capital is severely eroded, banks must reduce new lending and divest assets. This contracts credit supply across the entire economy.

## Why Banks Can Still Endure: Structural Reality

**Exposure limits some losses**: Megabanks like **JPMorgan Chase** and **Bank of America** have large equity bases and diversified revenue streams, giving them capacity to absorb some CMBS losses. Global banks like **Citigroup** also have relatively lower concentration in U.S. office exposure.

**Long-term demand for urban redevelopment**: Projects to convert abandoned office buildings into residential, hotel, and lifestyle uses are underway. While this provides little near-term help, it enables some asset recovery in 3–5 years.

## Summary

The establishment of remote work is an irreversible change. If office vacancy rates remain elevated over the long term, CMBS market deterioration will intensify, eroding financial institutions' capital and constraining credit supply. While large banks' relative strength protects them, structural improvement in the industry will take years.
