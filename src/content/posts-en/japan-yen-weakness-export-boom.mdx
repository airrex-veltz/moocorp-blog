## Yen Weakness Drives Dollar Earnings of Japanese Exporters to Five-Year Peak

As the Japanese yen weakens further to 160+ per dollar, the dollar earnings conversions for export-heavy Japanese companies like **Toyota**, **Honda**, and **Sony** are increasing significantly. Yen weakness extends beyond short-term currency gains to reshape the structural profitability of manufacturing.

## Recent Stock Performance and Results Trends of Three Exporters

As of October 23, 2026, **TM** (Toyota) closed at $196.88, rising 8.09% over the past three months. Revenue growth year-over-year reached 10.4%. **HMC** (Honda) closed at $34.17, with a three-month gain of 17.30% and revenue growth of 13.5%. **SONY** (Sony Group) closed at $26.09, rising 17.05% over three months with revenue growth of 8.2%. All three companies recorded double-digit stock gains over the past three months with solid revenue growth.

## Why Yen Weakness Now Drives Export Earnings: Four Factors

**1. Currency effects and forex gains:** Japanese companies earn revenues in dollars, euros, and other foreign currencies from overseas sales. The weaker the yen, the larger the yen amount when converting the same foreign currency revenue. The current weakness beyond 160 per dollar represents a decline of more than 50 yen from two years ago. This creates an effect of increasing the dollar earnings of export products like automobiles and electronics when converted to yen.

**2. Improved production costs and competitiveness:** Yen weakness relatively lowers production costs within Japan. It is equivalent to global-level wages and material costs declining in yen terms. Particularly for high-value products like automotive components and semiconductors, this cost advantage can be maximized.

**3. Central bank policy divergence:** The Bank of Japan is holding back on tightening beyond current levels, while the Federal Reserve is in the process of rate cuts. The larger the interest rate differential, the more sustained the yen weakness pressure. This policy divergence is likely to continue dampening exchange rate stability and maintaining dollar strength.

**4. Market's anticipatory reaction:** The stock market has already reflected future earnings improvement. HMC and SONY's three-month gains of over 17% suggest investors expect sustained yen weakness. Stock price patterns where prices reflect expectations ahead of actual earnings reports.

## Still, Structural Weaknesses to Watch

**Risk of yen rebound:** It is difficult to predict when policy direction might change. If the BOJ suddenly raises rates or geopolitical crisis increases safe-haven demand, the yen could rapidly strengthen. Then currency gains disappear instantly.

**Trend of expanded local production:** Over the long term, Japanese companies are increasing production in local markets (U.S., Europe, Southeast Asia) to reduce trade conflict and supply chain risks. This limits the benefits of yen weakness. The fact that HMC and SONY have negative net profit margins of -0.8% and -1.8%, respectively, also suggests a gap between short-term currency gains and structural profitability.

## Summary

Japanese yen weakness significantly boosts the dollar earnings of export companies, reflected in the recent stock price and revenue growth of Toyota, Honda, and Sony. However, this effect depends entirely on currency policy and exchange rates, making it structurally fragile.
