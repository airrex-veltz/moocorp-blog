## Medical Device Price Controls Signal Healthcare Companies' Profit Structure Restructuring

As healthcare authorities in major countries intensify medical device price regulations, large medical device companies are fundamentally restructuring their profit structures. While revenue continues to grow despite regulatory pressure, a strategy to sustain profit margins has become essential.

## Current Status: Profit Divergence Amid Growth

**Medtronic (MDT)** is valued at a market capitalization of USD 117.8 billion as of September 19, 2026. In the past six months, its stock price has fluctuated between USD 72.65 and USD 95.41, with revenue growth of 13.7% year-over-year. However, with a PER of 22.7, net profit margin remained at just 13.9%.

**Abbott Laboratories (ABT)** is the industry's largest player with a market capitalization of USD 177.6 billion. During the same period, its stock price ranged from USD 81.39 to USD 117.34, achieving revenue growth of 13.0%, but net profit margin fell to just 11.7%. With a PER of 33.2, which is higher than MDT's, the market's expectations premium appears to be reflected.

**Stryker Corporation (SYK)** has a market capitalization of USD 105.5 billion. Over the past six months, its stock price ranged from USD 267 to USD 352; however, among the three operators, it had the lowest revenue growth rate at 9.4%. In contrast, it boasts the highest net profit margin at 14.4% and maintains market valuation with a PER of 28.5.

## Why Profit Structures Are Shaking Now: Four Factors

**1. Global Expansion of Price Regulation**
Advanced countries including the United States, Europe, and Japan are tightening medical device price standards as a measure to contain healthcare costs. As each country adjusts its reimbursement policies, per-unit transaction prices inevitably decline. While companies must fill volume through new product launches, there are limits to offsetting this through economies of scale.

**2. Realization of Margin Pressure**
ABT's net profit margin of 11.7% lags behind its revenue growth of 13.0%. This indicates that rising manufacturing and distribution costs under price controls cannot be solved by cost reduction alone. MDT and SYK face similar dilemmas.

**3. Portfolio Shift Toward High-Margin Products**
Because price regulation begins with low-cost products, companies are naturally shifting their portfolios toward premium medical devices, AI diagnostic devices, and customized solutions. This increases market fragmentation and customer segmentation costs.

**4. R&D Cost Efficiency Competition**
Under strengthened regulation, new product development timelines lengthen while expected returns diminish. Despite significant R&D investments by MDT and ABT, simultaneously achieving revenue growth and margin improvement has become difficult.

## Why They Survive Anyway: Structural Strengths and Weaknesses

**Strengths: Economies of Scale and Ecosystem Control**
MDT, ABT, and SYK each hold near-oligopoly positions in specific medical areas (surgical instruments, diagnostics, and orthopedics). Even with regulation, the narrow choice among few suppliers means pricing power does not completely disappear. Accumulated patents and clinical data also prevent new competitors from entering the market.

**Weaknesses: Persistence of Regulatory Risk**
Price controls are likely to intensify in the future. As healthcare cost burdens grow across countries, regulation becomes unavoidable. A strategy focusing solely on high-margin products for profitability improvement may result in ceding the low-cost market to Chinese companies.

## Summary

Medical device companies finding it difficult to grow profits despite rising revenues signals that regulation's impact has become clear. MDT's PER of 22.7, ABT's 33.2, and SYK's 28.5 each reflect different market confidence levels, and the success or failure of future profit restructuring will be key to differentiating stock prices by ticker.
