## Double Squeeze: Airline Demand Recovery and Fuel Cost Pressure

As international travel demand has recovered since the pandemic, major airlines are recording double-digit annual revenue growth. However, over the same period, oil price volatility has intensified, and fuel cost pressures are hampering earnings improvement. This dual structure operates with varying intensity across airlines, and notable differences are evident in how investors assess valuations.

### Demand Recovery Signals and Earnings Growth

Key indicators comparing last year to this year suggest robust demand recovery in travel. Delta Air Lines (DAL) revenue increased 21.1% year-over-year, while United Airlines (UAL) recorded growth of 17.4%. SkyWest (SKYW), which operates domestic and regional routes, showed relatively lower growth of 6.5%, reflecting market characteristics where long-haul international route recovery is outpacing the slower normalization of domestic service.

Despite revenue growth, net profit margins vary significantly among carriers. Delta maintains a 4.6% net margin, while United shows 5.5% and SkyWest 9.8%, reflecting clear differences based on route mix and operational efficiency. SkyWest's elevated net margin as a regional carrier suggests lower exposure to international currency risk and potentially lower fuel hedging costs.

### Oil Price Volatility and Intensifying Fuel Cost Pressure

In the airline industry, fuel represents a major variable operating cost. Stock price movements over the past three months illustrate how heavily this pressure weighs on the market.

**Delta**: Recorded a three-month decline of -7.96% and one-month decline of -6.82%. A P/E of 15.7 is the highest among the three, indicating the market assigns a premium valuation reflecting Delta's brand value and customer loyalty. However, the sustained stock decline during this period suggests skepticism about growth prospects despite the elevated P/E. Revenue growth of 21.1% is strong, but the 4.6% net margin indicates substantial fuel cost absorption.

**United**: Experienced the steepest decline. A three-month drop of -16.62% and one-month decline of -14.53% represent the most severe among the three carriers. Conversely, a P/E of 9.3 is the lowest, showing the market applies a highly conservative valuation. The sharp stock decline despite 17.4% revenue growth suggests market concern that fuel cost increases are offsetting earnings growth.

**SkyWest**: Showed moderate declines of -3.50% over three months and -7.83% over one month. Its domestic-focused operations and elevated 9.8% net margin serve as relative strengths. A P/E of 9.5 is similar to United's, but in an environment of lower revenue growth, strong profit generation tends to receive higher valuation appreciation.

### Fuel Cost Exposure Analysis by Airline

The impact of fuel cost pressure on each carrier varies based on route composition, aircraft age, and hedging strategy.

For Delta, its extensive international network makes high fuel consumption unavoidable. The disparity between 21.1% revenue growth and merely 4.6% net margin indicates that crude price increases are not being fully passed through via pricing. Particularly on international routes, currency volatility compounds the actual fuel burden.

United's steepest three-month stock decline suggests the market views its fuel cost exposure as the greatest concern. The 9.3 P/E, among the lowest, reflects judgment that structural cost pressures make near-term earnings growth unlikely and structural relief improbable. However, its 5.5% net margin exceeds Delta's, indicating relatively better operational efficiency.

As a regional carrier, SkyWest's limited exposure to long-haul international routes translates to relatively lower currency hedging costs. Domestic-focused operations produce more predictable fuel consumption patterns and facilitate easier price pass-through. The 9.8% net margin clearly demonstrates these structural advantages.

### Long-Term Outlook and Investment Implications

The demand recovery trend remains strong, yet accelerating recovery carries the risk of a vicious cycle where surging fuel demand drives oil prices higher. Greater volatility in fuel derivatives markets constrains the actual earnings improvement airlines can achieve.

Current stock declines reflect the market's negative assessment of this dual structure. United's case is particularly noteworthy, with the steepest decline and lowest valuation signaling investor risk aversion. Delta maintains a relatively elevated P/E reflecting some brand premium, yet if fuel pressures persist, this premium faces reassessment risk.

SkyWest's domestic-focused operating model provides relative stability in the current environment, but 6.5% revenue growth falls well short of overall market recovery pace. This hints at structural limitations that could cause it to miss growth opportunities when international route recovery accelerates.

Ultimately, earnings recovery in the airline industry will hinge significantly on fuel cost stabilization. How well each carrier absorbs fuel pressures through technological innovation, operational efficiency gains, and disciplined hedging strategies will be the key determinant of investment appeal across the sector.
