## Rising Healthcare Costs Propel Healthcare Firm Profitability

In an aging society, rising healthcare costs are inevitable. Within this structural shift, pharmaceutical, device, and healthcare IT firms are securing pricing power while simultaneously improving profitability.

## Healthcare Cost Trends and Major Firm Positioning

The U.S. healthcare industry is entering a dual-pressure dynamic: aging populations and rising utilization. **JNJ** (closing USD 261.44 on 2026-10-09) maintains 21.5% net margin while logging 6.6% revenue growth. **UNH**, an integrated insurer and healthcare services firm, trades at USD 379.30 (2026-10-09) with 3.1% net margin and 0.4% revenue growth—stagnation. **TMO**, a medical technology and equipment maker, trades at USD 658.02 (2026-10-09) and leads with 10.5% revenue growth.

## Four Drivers of Rising Healthcare Costs

**1. Demographic aging and chronic disease prevalence**: Growing share of 65+ population drives surging utilization of treatment for diabetes, cardiovascular disease, cancer. This immediately increases demand for services and drugs.

**2. Rising costs from medical technology advancement**: Precision diagnosis, biotech therapy, personalized medicine—advanced tech adoption is accelerating. These innovations command premium pricing.

**3. Limited substitutes and pricing power**: When treatment options narrow, firms' pricing freedom expands. Breakthrough drugs or devices enjoy patent protection with limited competitive pressure.

**4. Cost pass-through via insurance premiums**: Rising medical costs feed insurance premium increases, which carriers pass to subscribers. In this dynamic, pharma and device makers can claim cost-increase justification in negotiations with government and insurers.

## By Firm: Winners and Burden-Bearers

**JNJ strength**: Integrated pharma and device business makes it a dual beneficiary of cost inflation. 21.5% net margin far exceeds industry average; simultaneous stable growth (6.6%) and high profitability. PER 30.3 is elevated but can be justified on profit basis.

**UNH weakness**: As a primary insurer, UNH bears cost pressure first. 0.4% revenue growth and 3.1% net margin reflect cost burden. PER 24.4 is the lowest of the three, appearing attractive from valuation.

**TMO opportunity and risk**: 10.5% revenue growth reflects direct benefit from cost inflation. But PER 35.4 is quite high, suggesting the market has priced in substantial growth already.

## Summary

Rising healthcare cost pressure is inevitable, not choice. As long as demographic aging and utilization growth persist, pharma and device firms' pricing power and profitability should improve. Insurers and healthcare services, meanwhile, face cost headwinds.
