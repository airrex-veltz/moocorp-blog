## Automation in Food Service: An Unavoidable Response to Rising Minimum Wages

As minimum wage increases and labor shortages accelerate, food service companies are making massive investments in order automation and operational efficiency improvements. This is not simply a matter of technology adoption but a survival strategy in response to structural cost pressures.

## Starbucks and McDonald's: Different Speeds of Change

**SBUX** closed at $93.65 on September 24, 2026, with a three-month decline of 8.70%. The company's net profit margin stands at 5.2%, which is very low, and revenue growth is -1.4% year-over-year. **MCD**, by contrast, closed at $237.02 the same day, with a three-month decline of 9.77% but a net profit margin of 31.7%. MCD maintains positive revenue growth of 3.7%.

The difference in profit structure between the two companies reflects their distinct automation strategies. SBUX's low net profit margin reflects a high labor cost structure, while MCD's high net profit margin demonstrates the leverage of a franchise model. This difference determines the urgency of automation investment.

## Why Food Service Turns to Automation Now: Four Factors

**1. Cumulative pressure from minimum wage increases**: Minimum wages rise annually in major markets, including Korea. For retail-oriented businesses like SBUX with high labor cost ratios, passing wage increases to customers in pricing is difficult. Automation is the only way to reduce per-location staffing requirements and improve fixed cost structures.

**2. Chronic labor shortages**: Low-wage, low-skill positions face severe supply constraints. Automating repetitive tasks such as order taking, beverage preparation, and payment processing allows remaining staff to focus on training and customer service. This becomes a competitive advantage in the battle for labor.

**3. Matured technology and falling prices**: Kiosks, automated drink dispensers, and integrated POS systems have become affordable even for small locations. Initial investment can now be recovered through labor cost savings within two to three years.

**4. Integration with the digital ordering ecosystem**: As delivery apps and mobile ordering advance, on-site automation becomes more integrated with these platforms. Local optimization has evolved into systemwide efficiency gains.

## Why Competition Continues: Structural Strengths and Weaknesses

**Limits of premium positioning**: SBUX's -1.4% revenue growth suggests the limits of price increases. To maintain high profitability, the company must lower costs without degrading customer experience. Automation is essential, but human touch cannot be abandoned.

**Flexibility of the franchise model**: With a 31.7% net profit margin, MCD can pursue automation investment more comfortably. By recommending automation equipment adoption to franchisees and setting standards, the company can lead the industry. However, franchisee burden increases.

**Risk of capital intensity**: Automation equipment means higher fixed costs. This creates vulnerability to demand fluctuations. Particularly for new market entry or economic downturns, unrecoverable losses pose a real risk.

## Conclusion

Automation in food service has become not a choice but a necessity. Recent weakness in SBUX and MCD share prices reflects uncertainty in this transition. Companies that lower costs through automation while maintaining customer experience will survive.
