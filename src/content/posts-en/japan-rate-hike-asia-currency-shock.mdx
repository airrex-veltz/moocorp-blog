## Japan's Rate Hike Squeezes Asia's Growth

As the Bank of Japan (BOJ) signals rate increases, yen strength is shaking the currency regimes of Asia's major export nations—South Korea, Taiwan, and others. Within a short period, Asia's equities and currencies have weakened simultaneously; yet paradoxically, regional economies face deeper structural pressure: growth slowdown.

## Asia's Stock Market Cascade Decline

**EWJ** (iShares MSCI Japan ETF) closed at $86.90 as of November 23, 2026, posting a one-month change of -7.65% and a three-month change of -9.13%. Over the same period, **JPY** (Lazard Japanese Equity ETF) stood at $35.73 (as of November 23, 2026), showing a one-month decline of -6.04% and a three-month decline of -6.81%. Hong Kong's large multinational **0001.HK** (CK Hutchison Holdings) traded at 64.80 HKD (as of November 23, 2026) with modest one-month movement (+0.70%), but posted a three-month decline of -6.35%. This weakness is not limited to Japanese equities but reflects broad momentum weakness across Asia.

## Why the Weakness Now: Four Factors

**1. Eroded Export Competitiveness From Yen Strength**: The BOJ's rate hike signals attract international capital into yen, raising its exchange rate. Conversely, the South Korean won, Taiwan dollar, and Hong Kong dollar weaken proportionally. While yen strength reduces Japanese goods' price competitiveness, the larger problem is that South Korea's and Taiwan's exports—semiconductors and automobiles—lose price advantage in markets where they compete directly with Japanese products.

**2. Interest Rate Differential and Emerging Market Capital Outflow**: If the BOJ raises rates, it may move above the U.S. Federal Reserve (Fed) rate. Unlike the past, when Asia's emerging markets had rates higher than Japan, this reversal can now occur—accelerating capital movement from Asia to developed nations. South Korea's, Taiwan's, and Hong Kong's equity markets and currencies face capital outflow pressure.

**3. Deteriorating Profitability for Multinational Firms**: **0001.HK** posted revenue growth of -1.9% (YoY, as of November 23, 2026). Yen strength causes losses when the group converts local-currency earnings to dollars at the group level. With net margin stuck at 13.6% while revenue declines, this signals simultaneous export weakness and currency pressure.

**4. Growth Slowdown and Rate Hike Feedback Loop**: When Asia's economies lack room to proactively raise rates but Japan does, capital outflow and currency weakness from the interest rate differential compound. Weak currency raises import prices, intensifying inflation pressure and further suppressing already-weak growth (0001.HK revenue growth of -1.9%).

## Structural Weaknesses

Asia's economies reveal fundamental vulnerabilities facing Japan's rate path. First, South Korea and Taiwan are highly export-dependent, directly exposed to currency movements. Second, with constrained rate-raising room, sustained yen strength widens the interest differential, reversing capital flow. Third, multinationals like 0001.HK lose paths to profitability improvement amid currency pressure and growth slowdown. Asia's three-month equity moves (EWJ -9.13%, JPY -6.81%) are not simple correction but price discovery of structural weakness.

## Conclusion

Japan's rate increase transmits currency shock and growth pressure across Asia's economy via yen strength. Broad Asia equity weakness and multinational revenue slump like 0001.HK show this mechanism already operates. Note that the fundamental structure—growth slowdown—is shifting, not short-term currency swings.
