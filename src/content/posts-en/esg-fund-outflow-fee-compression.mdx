Long-term underperformance of investment funds based on ESG (Environmental, Social, Governance) criteria is eroding asset managers' profit base. As investor redemptions rise amid unmet return expectations, asset managers' assets under management (AUM) shrink, triggering fee competition—a vicious cycle.

## Underperformance to Asset Flight

Over five years, ESG fund returns have lagged comparable general index funds and non-ESG-screened funds. This reflects both insufficient diversification from high ESG asset exclusion (screening) and misalignment among ESG rating agencies. Investors unable to achieve expected returns have begun withdrawing funds, unleashing a wave of redemptions across the sector.

**BlackRock** (**BLK**), the industry's largest asset manager, has led the ESG fund market. As of 2026-10-23, BLK stock was 1,118.25 dollars, up 5.82% in three months. YoY revenue growth reached 19.1%, but this reflects the entire asset management portfolio. Asset flight specific to ESG funds may hide beneath aggregate figures.

**Blackstone** (**BX**)'s situation is clearer. Stock closed 117.97 dollars on 2026-10-23, down 10.13% over three months. Notably, YoY revenue growth registered -0.5%—a negative. This signals that AUM shrinkage directly impacts asset manager overall profitability.

## Four Pressure Factors in the Vicious Cycle

**First, erosion of investor trust**: Persistent underperformance spreads skepticism—"ESG is only cost, not benefit." This blocks new inflows and accelerates existing fund withdrawal. Mounting redemption pressure forces funds to liquidate assets to ensure liquidity, further worsening fund performance.

**Second, direct fee base compression from AUM decline**: Asset manager fees typically comprise a fixed percentage of assets under management. Shrinking AUM means declining fee revenue. Small-scale ESG funds lacking economies of scale struggle to cover operating costs.

**Third, vicious fee-cutting competition**: As asset scale shrinks, managers cut fees to remain competitive. **Charles Schwab** (**SCHW**) closed 90.58 dollars on 2026-10-23, down 12.80% over three months, reflecting worsening profitability in asset management.

**Fourth, erosion of manager profit margins**: Revenue decline from fee cuts makes covering fixed costs (staff, systems, compliance) difficult, triggering a further cycle constraining research and innovation investment.

## Structural Weakness That Even Strongmen Can't Overcome

ESG funds' structural weaknesses are clear: high asset exclusion thresholds, rating agency misalignment, long-term underperformance. These cannot be overcome by short-term tactics.

BLK, with 2026-10-23 market cap 183.7 billion dollars, 24.2% net margin, and 25.7 PER, remains industry strongest. Scale and diverse portfolio can absorb some single-sector weakness. Yet this doesn't solve ESG fund structural problems. Individual fund underperformance erodes investor trust, accelerates AUM flight, and ultimately pressures industry-wide margins.

The cycle of poor ESG fund returns → rising redemptions → AUM shrinkage → fee pressure → margin erosion forces asset managers toward not merely business unit challenges but strategic transformation. Breaking the cycle requires either fund performance improvement or fundamental business model restructuring.
