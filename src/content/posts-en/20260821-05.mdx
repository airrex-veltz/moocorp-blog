## Geopolitical Mineral Grab: Western Africa Strategy Makes Mine Operators Rich

As the US and Europe move to secure African cobalt, copper, and lithium, they are creating "one-time opportunities" for mining companies. Under the pretext of countering China and securing supply chains, Western investments and preferential treatment are sending stock prices and business opportunities for Australian mining companies and African mine operators soaring.

## Mineral Procurement: Now a Security War

Battery electric vehicles (EVs) and renewable energy are impossible without minerals. One battery requiring 1 ton of lithium requires a long supply chain from mine to refinery. As of 2024, 70% of global lithium production and over 60% of cobalt come from mines controlled by China or involving Chinese companies. This has become an intolerable weakness for the US and EU.

## Why Is This Competition Heating Up Now: 4 Factors

**1. Rapid EV Market Growth**: Global battery electric vehicle sales are projected to grow from 14 million units in 2023 to over 40 million units by 2030. This drives mineral demand up more than threefold. As government EV subsidies accelerate demand, mining companies are scaling up production.

**2. Rise of "Friendly Supply Chain" Strategy**: The US began offering tax incentives for minerals from "trusted" countries like the US, Canada, and Australia through the Inflation Reduction Act (IRA). The EU is following suit and negotiating bilateral agreements with African nations. This becomes a rationale for circumventing Chinese minerals while justifying price increases.

**3. Africa's Geopolitical Importance**: The Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) accounts for 70% of global cobalt production. Zambia and Botswana are also rich in copper. As the West strengthens ties with these nations, efforts to replace existing Chinese investment are accelerating.

**4. Strengthening Climate Policy**: The EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) and US carbon neutrality goals serve as rationales for excluding carbon-intensive Chinese minerals. As African nations face deepening fiscal crises, opportunities for Western companies to get involved have expanded.

## Mining Companies' Structural Strengths and Weaknesses

**Strengths**: Global players like Australia-based **Rio Tinto** (RIO) and **Glencore** (GLEN) have sufficient capacity to manage ESG regulations and geopolitical risks. African nations, too, facing pressure to reduce Chinese dependence, have gained opportunities to improve contract terms with Western companies.

**Weaknesses**: Africa's political instability, infrastructure deficits, and strengthening environmental regulations cause project delays and cost overruns. Low-carbon mining technologies like hydrogen and electric smelting remain costly. If mineral prices fall, these innovation investments will erode profitability.

## Conclusion

The West's "friendly supply chain" strategy offers short-term investment opportunities and price increases for mining companies. Yet whether this competition will create permanent advantage or lead to mineral oversupply remains unclear. True supply chain independence will become reality when geopolitical advantage meets technological innovation.
