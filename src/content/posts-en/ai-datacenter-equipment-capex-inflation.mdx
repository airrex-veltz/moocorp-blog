## After Chip Shortage Ends, Infrastructure Costs Cut Into Margins

The chip shortage era is over. As NVIDIA normalizes supply and AI demand reaches saturation, the problem is no longer **chip prices but the cost of systems built around those chips**. Surging data center construction and operating costs are squeezing cloud companies' margins.

## Normalized Chip Supply vs Exploding Infrastructure Costs

**NVIDIA (NVDA)** remains strong. As of October 5, 2026 close, the stock traded at $238.90 with revenue growth of 105.9% year-over-year. Net profit margin stands at 63.7%, among the industry's highest. Yet the one-month gain of +4.69% trails the three-month gain of +21.45%, a signal that growth momentum has peaked.

Rival **AMD** surged 38.53% in one month but sends a different message. As of October 5, 2026 close at $631.91, its PER of 161.2 far exceeds NVIDIA's 30.2. With year-over-year revenue growth of just 50.1%, such extreme valuation suggests the market is **pricing in intensifying price competition**.

Cloud giant **Alphabet (GOOGL)** faces different headwinds. As of October 5, 2026 close at $346.63, the three-month decline was -5.50%. With a PER of 17.4, it appears undervalued, yet year-over-year revenue growth of 24.2% is only half NVIDIA's. Net profit margin remains healthy at 54.8%, but concerns over surging infrastructure investment and shrinking future margins are weighing on the stock.

## Why Semiconductors Rise While Cloud Falls: Four Factors

**1. Normalized chip supply has unleashed price competition**: NVIDIA's 105.9% growth represents **filling the shortage gap**, not an explosion in global AI infrastructure spending. Demand growth is moderating, and suppliers now compete on **costs, not prices**. AMD's PER of 161.2 reflects the market's expectation of falling chip prices.

**2. Data center construction and operating costs keep rising**: While chip supply has normalized, the servers, power, cooling, and network infrastructure to run those chips remain severely constrained. Unpredictable infrastructure cost spikes are crushing cloud companies' CapEx budgets. GOOGL's three-month decline of -5.50% and depressed PER reveal the **limits of infrastructure expansion**.

**3. Profitability shift from cloud to chips**: NVIDIA's net margin of 63.7% versus GOOGL's 54.8% represents an 8.9 percentage point gap. In the past, data center businesses commanded far higher margins. Now **chipmakers are capturing more profit than chip users**. Power in the supply chain has shifted.

**4. Rising marginal costs of infrastructure expansion**: Data center saturation, power grid bottlenecks, land scarcity—as marginal costs climb, cloud companies are **losing economies of scale**. If GOOGL's CapEx expansion accelerates beyond its 24.2% revenue growth, margin compression is certain.

## NVIDIA Holds Strong, Alphabet Must Endure: Structural Strengths and Weaknesses

**NVIDIA's strengths**: A net margin of 63.7% lets it absorb near-term price competition. A PER of 30.2 remains **reasonable relative to growth**. For 105.9% revenue growth to persist, NVIDIA must open new markets—inference chips, edge computing—segments where NVIDIA controls the industry.

**GOOGL's weaknesses**: As cloud competition intensifies and price pressure mounts, cloud companies have less room to absorb rising infrastructure costs. A PER of 17.4 reflects **market skepticism about future profitability**. Even if AI demand keeps growing, the infrastructure costs to meet it could consume all margin gains.

## The Bottom Line

AI chip scarcity is solved. The new constraint is infrastructure cost. NVIDIA absorbs it through high margins, but Alphabet has limited room to raise prices to customers. Semiconductors look poised to hold strength, while cloud companies will quietly focus on margin defense.
