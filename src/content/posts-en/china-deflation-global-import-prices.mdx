A paradoxical situation is intensifying in which global import price weakness constrains advanced economies' monetary policy. China's deflation from weak domestic demand is translating into expanded exports, pressing down global commodity prices. Recent asset allocation shifts in financial markets are making this mechanism starkly apparent.

## Strengthened Export Competitiveness Amid China's Weakness

**FXI** (iShares China Large-Cap ETF) traded at $34.95 as of November 6, 2026, down 4.22% over three months yet up 4.48% over one month. A PER of 9.6—extremely low—reflects the structural weakness of China's stock market and market pessimism. This signals that Chinese firms' low-price export strategy is not translating into improved profitability.

Despite weak domestic conditions, China is strengthening price competitiveness to avoid losing overseas demand. In a deflationary environment, firms are trapped in a vicious cycle of lowering prices to compete on volume, systematically pressing down prices of global import goods.

## The Significance of Bond Strength and Gold Weakness

**TLT** (iShares 20-Year U.S. Treasury ETF) traded at $81.92 as of November 6, 2026, up 5.20% over one month. The sharp rise in bond prices means the market is pricing in rate cuts preemptively. Global import prices are showing weakness, easing inflation pressure in advanced economies.

Paradoxically, **GLD** (SPDR Gold ETF) traded at $372.52 as of November 6, 2026, down 1.61% over one month and 7.46% over three months. Gold, traditionally an inflation hedge and sought for yield normalization when uncertainty declines, weakens. This is evidence that the market is assessing "inflation concerns are fading, and rate cut prospects are rising." The gap between the six-month low of $354.67 and high of $436.04 also reveals the magnitude of this shift.

## Four Structural Weaknesses

**First, China's excess production capacity.** Needing to maintain existing industrial structure, China must sustain exports even at low prices. This exerts persistent downward pressure on the global supply chain.

**Second, insufficient global effective demand.** Advanced economy consumers' real purchasing power is stagnant, and creating new demand is difficult. Rising volume of low-cost goods risks market saturation.

**Third, central banks' shrinking policy space.** As inflation moderates, rate-cut pressure grows, yet financial stability and long-term structural issues remain. TLT strength reflects this dilemma.

**Fourth, instability in global asset allocation.** GLD weakness reflects expectations that rate cuts will erode cash value, but China's weakness and export-decline risks could actually lift safe-haven demand. This tension widens volatility.

## Collision Between Structural Strengths and Weaknesses

China's low-cost exports bring temporary benefit to advanced economy consumers and suppress global inflation, appearing positive on the surface. TLT's 5.20% one-month gain means this "inflation moderation" is reflected in actual asset prices.

Yet this structure leads to deteriorating Chinese firm profitability (PER 9.6), wage stagnation in advanced economies, and weak global demand. Temporary price stability translates to economic growth weakness. Gold weakness sharply lays bare this contradiction: inflation concerns have faded, but so have growth expectations.

## Points to Watch Going Forward

This vicious cycle is unlikely to reverse without structural reform in China. Export-dependent low-cost competition will continue pressing down global import prices, leaving advanced economy central banks trapped in an environment of low inflation but stalled growth. The TLT and GLD movements—signaling "price stability without growth"—point to deepening structural weakness in global asset markets. FXI's low valuation also suggests this outlook is already sufficiently priced in.
