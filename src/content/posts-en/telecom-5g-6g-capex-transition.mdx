## Introduction Headline — 6G Pre-Investment Launch During 5G Depreciation Cycle

Base station investment follows a typical infrastructure depreciation cycle of over 10 years. Yet between 2024 and 2026, while 5G network buildout has not yet completed even half its depreciation, major telecom carriers have already begun 6G preliminary research and experimental frequency allocation efforts. This signals that the technology cycle, which previously spanned more than 10 years between generations, has compressed to 5-7 years. As a result, with 5G debt still remaining, 6G debt overlaps, pushing telecom companies' capital expenditure (capex) burden to historic levels. This has become a variable affecting dividends, M&A, and stock prices.

## Telecom Capex Pressure and Growth Rate Divergence

**VZ** (Verizon Communications Inc.) closed at $46.36 on November 13, 2026, with revenue growth of only 1.5%. Net profit margin is healthy at 11.8%, but at a PER of 11.8, the market values growth prospects conservatively. By contrast, **TMUS** (T-Mobile US, Inc.) closed at $169.95 (as of November 13, 2026) with revenue growth of 7.4% and net profit margin of 12.0%, showing the highest growth among peers. Its PER of 16.4 is more than double that of other competitors. **T** (AT&T Inc.) at $22.18 (November 13, 2026) is performing well with 5.3% revenue growth, but shows recent weakness with one-month fluctuation of -5.62% and three-month fluctuation of -9.11%. **CMCSA** (Comcast Corporation) at $25.16 (November 13, 2026) is the only one with negative revenue growth (-6.1%), and its net profit margin of 9.2% is the lowest.

## Why is Capex Burden Intensifying Now: Four Factors

**1. Economics of shortened technology cycles**: In the past, transitions from analog to 2G, 3G, 4G, and 5G occurred at intervals of 7-10 years each. However, as wireless capacity competition accelerates, next-generation planning is already underway during 5G network buildout. Each generation requires significant portions of existing infrastructure to be rebuilt, so new investment and old-generation depreciation debt overlap. Telecom companies are forced to make next-generation capex decisions before depreciation of existing assets is complete.

**2. Competitive pressure and the necessity for pre-emptive investment**: TMUS's high growth rate is the fruit of first-mover advantage gained through the most aggressive 5G investment. As a counterweight, that company also carries the highest capex burden. VZ's 1.5% growth is the result of a conservative investment strategy, but it carries the risk of falling behind in competition. Each company cannot postpone 6G investment to avoid lagging.

**3. Regulatory environment and frequency allocation costs**: U.S. FCC spectrum auctions appear as billions of dollars in additional costs annually. Failure to secure frequencies in mmWave, C-band, and lower-band competition risks inevitable competitive decline. The 6G first-mover competition unfolds by similar logic. This acts as an unexpected expenditure beyond planned capex.

**4. Conflict between shareholder expectations and financial soundness**: Dividend growth and capex expansion are difficult to reconcile. CMCSA's -6.1% growth and PER of 8.1 undervaluation shows the market doubts that company's flexibility in capex adjustment. VZ also signals the same in its PER discount. Conversely, TMUS's premium reflects the market's approval of its management strategy of focusing on growth investment.

## Structural Strengths and Weaknesses of Each Company

**Capital advantage differentiation**: TMUS enjoys lower capital procurement costs due to its PER premium over peers. High growth rates mean greater capacity to withstand additional debt burden. VZ has the largest absolute asset base by market cap (192.5B), but limited growth constrains it from aggressively expanding capex. Both T and CMCSA are disadvantaged in both dimensions.

**Multi-layered business structure**: CMCSA, which also runs cable operations, shoulders fixed-line capex as well. This is reflected in its negative growth. TMUS, centered on wireless business, can allocate capex more concentratedly. T and VZ are wireless-focused, but VZ possesses a more mature portfolio.

**Dividend policy flexibility**: VZ and CMCSA, with low PERs, have little room to cut dividends. Rather, maintaining dividends is key to stock price defense. T and TMUS are relatively favorable to growth reinvestment. TMUS's high PER reflects that investors buying it prioritize growth over dividends.

## Summary

The shortened technology generation transition cycle from 5G to 6G is deepening the differential capex response capabilities among telecom carriers. TMUS has the capacity to pursue both growth and capex, while VZ, T, and CMCSA each show signals of capex weakness for different reasons. In the short term, companies with growth capacity seem positioned to gain first-mover advantages, but over the medium term, excessive capex is likely to pressure all companies' dividends and profitability. Investors should closely track each company's capex guidance and cash flow plans.
