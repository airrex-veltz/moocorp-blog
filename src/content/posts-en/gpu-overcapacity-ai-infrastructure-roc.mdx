## GPU Oversupply Simultaneously Pressures Semiconductor Margins and Data Center Investment Returns

A competitive battle between chip makers and cloud companies over AI infrastructure investment is backfiring. Procurement driven by reluctance to surrender technological leadership has created oversupply, and falling prices now threaten both chip and infrastructure profitability. This creates a structure where semiconductor and cloud margins face simultaneous pressure.

## Market Signals Told by Stock Price and Growth Rate

**NVIDIA**'s one-month stock price decline is -19.02%, **AMD** at -27.65% (as of November 6, 2026). The big three cloud providers are also weak. **GOOGL** -18.23%, **MSFT** +1.53%, **AMZN** +2.06%, with chip makers showing sharper declines.

What stands out is **NVIDIA**'s revenue growth rate (105.9%) and net profit margin (63.7%). Despite high growth and strong profitability, the stock fell—indicating investors are already pricing in future margin deterioration. **AMD** faces worse conditions. Revenue growth of 44.7%, net profit margin of 16.8%, and P/E ratio of 95.9—performance lags behind the high valuation.

## Why Oversupply Now: Four Pressuring Factors

**1. Intensifying price competition between semiconductor manufacturers**

As long as **NVIDIA** maintains market dominance, customers (especially cloud companies) leverage competitors as leverage. **AMD** seeks market share through price cuts. The result: declining average chip selling prices and shrinking supplier margins. Even with **NVIDIA**'s high net profit margin, when faced with reduced transaction volumes or pressure to cut prices, the margin rate itself cannot be protected.

**2. Simultaneous large-scale GPU investment competition among cloud majors**

**MSFT** (revenue growth 18.8%), **AMZN** (12.1%), **GOOGL** (24.7%) have simultaneously expanded data center GPU capacity. As each company pursues proprietary chip (or custom chip) development to differentiate AI services, total investment reached historic highs. This created oversupply in the market, leading to declining GPU utilization rates for individual cloud providers.

**3. Chip price decline directly impacts semiconductor net profit**

As chip prices fall, even high-margin players like **NVIDIA** cannot escape unit price declines. **AMD** faces a more severe situation—with already-low net profit margins (16.8%), survival depends on price cuts. This traps them in a "paradoxical growth" where they can maintain total revenue through scale but see cumulative net profit decline.

**4. Deteriorating data center investment profitability**

Cloud companies likely expected high returns when making GPU investment decisions. Price declines extend the payback period for those investments. GPUs and infrastructure recorded as fixed assets have predetermined depreciation periods—failure to achieve expected returns requires recognizing impairment. Companies like **AMZN**, with already-slowing growth rates (12.1%), now face pressure to reassess investment efficiency.

## Structural Strengths and Weaknesses

**Strengths**: **NVIDIA**'s net profit margin (63.7%) and **GOOGL**'s sustained growth (24.7%) reflect technological superiority and brand value. Customer lock-in effects remain strong.

**Weaknesses**: **AMD**'s P/E ratio (95.9) signals that market expectations are high yet net profit margins (16.8%) struggle to meet them. **AMZN**'s slow growth (12.1%) reveals data center investment saturation.

## Summary

GPU oversupply is not a temporary phenomenon. The structural overcapacity created by chip competition and cloud procurement competition simultaneously pressures both price and margins. Both semiconductor and cloud companies now face an adjustment period where they must accept near-term profit compression.
