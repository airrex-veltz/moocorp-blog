## The Impossible Coexistence of Fiscal Deficits and Inflation Resurgence

The U.S. federal government's fiscal deficit surpassed $2 trillion in the first half of 2026. Simultaneously, the Fed is preparing for rate cuts. These two signals fundamentally collide. High government spending pulls prices upward, yet high interest rates are required to contain inflation. Faced with this contradiction, the market now confronts the limits of a rate-cutting cycle.

## Fiscal Deficit of $2 Trillion: The Beginning of Cascading Effects

In the first half of 2026, the U.S. federal deficit increased by more than 25% compared to the same period the previous year, exceeding $2 trillion. The main drivers are declining corporate income tax revenues and expanded defense and infrastructure spending. Simultaneously, core inflation (Core PCE), previously stable, is showing renewed acceleration signals through rising consumer goods prices and service costs. With the unemployment rate exceeding 4%, the labor market is also weakening, which provides grounds for Fed rate cuts, yet rising government borrowing costs exert upward pressure on market interest rates.

## Why Policy Conflicts Are Intensifying Now: 4 Factors

**1. Surging Government Fiscal Demand**: To cover the high deficit, the federal government must increase bond issuance. This creates a net supply effect that pushes bond yields higher. Even as the Fed wants to cut rates, fiscal demand props up long-term rates. The yield on 10-year Treasury notes repeatedly becomes stuck in the mid-4% range.

**2. Reignition of Wage-Price Inflation Spiral**: Even as employment weakens, wages in high-income sectors (technology, healthcare, finance) are rising at 3–4% levels. This structure supports consumption while simultaneously reigniting service-sector inflation. Food and energy prices remain sensitive to supply shocks.

**3. The Fed's Policy Dilemma**: Cutting rates invites concern about further inflation, while maintaining rates risks the weakened labor market slipping into recession. Talk of "stagflation" may be exaggerated, but falling Fed credibility is real. Markets are raising volatility over uncertainty about the interest rate path.

**4. Signals of Reversed Global Capital Flows**: Rising U.S. government borrowing costs reduce capital inflows from emerging markets. As dollar strength momentum weakens, international portfolio rebalancing pressures intensify. U.S. equities lose relative appeal, and the risk of intensified bond selloff grows.

## Structural Weaknesses: The Inevitability of Policy Conflicts

**The Vicious Cycle of Inflation-Rate Tradeoffs**: Unless the fiscal situation improves, the government must continue issuing bonds. This keeps nominal rates under sustained upward pressure. Real rates (nominal rates minus expected inflation) can actually decline, weakening the Fed's tightening signals in the market.

**Political Economy Deadlock**: Cuts to infrastructure and defense spending are politically difficult, while tax increases face resistance due to recession concerns. Therefore, structural deficit reduction appears unlikely, and the deficit is likely to grow faster than nominal GDP.

## Summary

The $2 trillion fiscal deficit is not a mere number. It symbolizes a fundamental imbalance that monetary policy alone cannot solve. It signals that Fed rate cuts may not prevent inflation reignition, and market volatility reflects concern about how far this policy uncertainty will extend. The second half of 2026 will be a period requiring reassessment of policy direction.
