## Iron Ore Supercycle Recovery and Mining Companies' Profitability Rebound

With infrastructure investment resuming in India and China, iron ore demand is entering a rebound phase. Recent results from the three largest global iron ore mining companies clearly reflect this shift.

## Global Three Players' Current Position

**BHP** (closing price $86.09 as of 2026-09-21) is expanding fastest among the three with 18.3% YoY revenue growth. It maintains a 16.7% net profit margin and a P/E ratio of 22.2, reflecting high-growth valuations.

**RIO** (closing price $97.04) boasts the highest profitability among the three with a 19.6% net profit margin. Despite 15.5% YoY revenue growth, it trades at the lowest valuation with a P/E ratio of 12.9. High efficiency and low multiples coexist.

**VALE** (closing price $14.15) shows a relatively weaker performance. With 6.4% YoY revenue growth and 4.8% net profit margin, it lags behind the other two. Its P/E ratio of 27.9 is high, but growth and profitability do not support this valuation.

## Four Factors Driving Iron Ore Demand

**1. Acceleration of India's Infrastructure Investment**: India is injecting billions of dollars into highway, rail, and port expansion within 2026. Steel consumption is a direct result of this investment, and iron ore is a key raw material for steel.

**2. Spillover Effects of China's Stimulus**: To escape the real estate crisis, the Chinese government is resuming construction and manufacturing stimulus measures, driving steel demand recovery. Rising demand from East Asia's largest steel consumer directly impacts global iron ore prices.

**3. Improvement in Mining Companies' Cash Flow**: BHP and RIO's high net profit margins (16.7–19.6%) demonstrate mining companies' cash generation capability. This translates into shareholder dividends, new mine development investments, and debt reduction, strengthening structural stability within the industry.

**4. Supply Bottleneck in Iron Ore**: Strengthened environmental regulations and production constraints at major mines prevent global iron ore supply from keeping pace with demand recovery. Supply shortages support prices, and the high-price environment maintains mining companies' profit margins.

## Structural Weaknesses to Watch

**Profitability Gap**: RIO's net profit margin of 19.6% is nearly four times VALE's 4.8%. This means that even with identical demand recovery, earnings diverge significantly due to profitability differences.

**VALE's Growth Stagnation**: VALE's 6.4% revenue growth rate lags far behind BHP's 18.3% and RIO's 15.5%. This suggests constraints on the Brazilian miner's production capacity or market share expansion.

**One-Month Stock Price Weakness**: All three stocks show negative one-month returns (BHP -6.05%, RIO -5.02%, VALE -0.56%). The market may have already digested some recovery expectations or is pricing in additional concerns.

## Conclusion

Infrastructure investment resumption in India and China drives iron ore demand rebound, a mechanism that directly translates to mining companies' high profit margins. BHP and RIO show strong growth and profitability, while VALE exhibits weak growth momentum. If demand rebound persists, the mining industry's cash generation will accelerate. However, given current negative stock returns, the market may have already priced in part of this or is reassessing additional risks.
