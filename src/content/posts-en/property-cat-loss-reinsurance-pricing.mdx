## Property-Casualty Insurer Stocks Rose, But Reinsurance Price Surge Looks Hard to Absorb

Over the past month, **ALL** (Allstate) and **CB** (Chubb) have each gained 16.37% and 10.91%. As of November 3, 2026, ALL trades at $261.50 and CB at $366.54. Yet behind this rise lies structural crisis. Natural disaster losses are climbing steadily, driving reinsurance costs—the tool these companies use to transfer external risk—through the roof. Premiums cannot easily cover this. Specifically, as reinsurance costs rise, profit margins compress, while higher premiums drive customer defection.

## The Changing Profit Structure of Property-Casualty Insurers

ALL posts a net profit margin of 19.0% and revenue growth of 11.8% (YoY), while CB has a net profit margin of 18.1% and revenue growth of 0.3% (YoY). Both maintain high net margins, but growth rates diverge sharply. ALL appears to be growing faster than CB, but this likely reflects premium increases rather than genuine operational improvement. CB's near-zero revenue growth suggests intensifying competitive pressure and customer attrition in the property-casualty market.

## Four Factors Squeezing Property-Casualty Insurer Profits

**1. Long-term Rise in Natural Disaster Losses**: As years pass, extreme weather events grow more frequent, and insurance claim volumes rise. When a single catastrophic event occurs, property-casualty insurers must absorb massive losses. Reinsurance is the most effective tool for transferring this risk externally.

**2. Rising Reinsurance Costs Driving Up Input Costs**: As natural disaster losses grow, expected losses from the reinsurer's perspective also grow. Thus, the reinsurance premiums property-casualty insurers purchase climb year after year. This means direct cost increases for insurers.

**3. Customer Resistance to Premium Increases**: Property-casualty insurers want to pass rising reinsurance costs to customers, but excessive increases trigger defection. Customers with relatively weak finances turn to cheaper products or defer purchases. CB's near-zero revenue growth reflects this dilemma.

**4. Intensifying Competition and Margin Pressure**: New insurers and fintech insurance startups are entering the market, sharpening price competition. Established large insurers must also restrain price hikes to defend market share. This makes it impossible to fully offset reinsurance cost increases through premium raises.

## Strengths and Weaknesses at a Crossroads

**Strengths**: ALL and CB still maintain high net profit margins around 19%, and backed by strong capital bases and long operating experience, they can weather financial shocks. Their large asset bases also provide absorption capacity for major disasters.

**Weaknesses**: Yet ALL trades at a P/E of just 5.3, and CB at only 13.0. This signals the market is skeptical of both companies' future earnings growth. If the structural squeeze from premium-raise limits and continuously rising reinsurance costs remains unresolved, earnings growth will be constrained. CB's near-zero revenue growth shows this difficulty is already materializing.

## Summary

The stock price gains for property-casualty insurers likely reflect short-term market sentiment and valuation rebound. As long as structural pressure from rising reinsurance costs persists and cost increases cannot be offset by premium increases, earnings growth for property-casualty insurers will remain constrained.
