## Lithium Supply Bottlenecks Are Squeezing EV Maker Margins

Over the past month, the global lithium market has sent warning signals. **LIT** (Global X Lithium & Battery Tech ETF), which tracks battery technology companies, stood at $64.05 as of October 16, 2026, down 9.66% in a month. Major mining company **VALE** fell to $13.06 during the same period, declining 9.74%. Against this backdrop, it is crucial to examine how the cost structures of automakers are being restructured.

## Mineral Market Weakness and Structural Imbalance in Lithium Supply

The weakness in lithium prices stems not from simple demand contraction. With Australia and Chile holding an absolute majority of global mining operations, supply has become geographically concentrated. Dependence on China for refining capacity is deepening. When supply chains are so concentrated, price volatility increases, and environmental regulation or political crisis in a single region immediately translates into rising costs.

Diversified miners like VALE maintain stable earnings from iron ore, but the lithium segment's net margin stands at only 4.8% as of October 16, 2026. While revenue growth reaches just 6.4%, investments in lithium refining capacity are not translating into profits. This signals that even mining companies are experiencing margin pressure in a soft pricing environment.

## Why EV Makers Are Getting Pulled into a Cost War Now: Four Factors

**1. End of Rising Lithium Prices**: Large-scale investment in lithium mines took place during 2024–2025 based on battery demand forecasts. However, with EV sales growth proving more gradual than expected, lithium entered a period of oversupply. As capital costs from new mining projects flowed into production costs, lithium prices shifted from stabilization to decline.

**2. China's Overwhelming Refining Dominance**: Processing lithium is more capital-intensive than mining it. With China controlling battery cell production capacity, suppliers in Australia and Chile lost bargaining power. Miners lack pricing authority, and when automakers shorten battery supply contracts or reduce volumes, mining profitability collapses.

**3. Automaker Battery Vertical Integration**: Leading players including Tesla (**TSLA**) are bringing battery production in-house. As of October 16, 2026, TSLA's revenue growth stands at 25.5%, but net margin is only 3.7%. To pursue high growth, they must secure more lithium, yet without their own mines, TSLA lacks cost competitiveness. They must rely on economies of scale.

**4. Survival Pressure on Budget Automakers**: Ford (**F**) shows a net margin of -3.9% and revenue growth of -3.8% as of October 16, 2026. Investment in EV transition has grown, but sales have actually contracted. When lithium prices rise even slightly in such circumstances, traditional automakers fall out of the race not on ideas or innovation but on raw cost competition. Ford has almost no hedging tools against lithium price swings.

## Leaders Hold Their Ground While Laggards Fall Behind

**Tesla's Scale Advantage**: TSLA's market capitalization stands at $1.40 trillion as of October 16, 2026, allowing it to meet mineral supply risks with financial firepower. Maintaining 25.5% revenue growth while continuously lowering battery costs, TSLA could see margin expansion if lithium supply stabilizes at current price levels.

**Vale's Weakening Profitability**: While VALE maintains stability through mineral diversification, the lithium segment's 4.8% net margin falls short of expectations. As returns on investment decline, the company may defer additional investments, which could lead to lithium shortages after 2027. Within this vicious cycle, VALE must endure near-term stock weakness.

**Ford's Cost Squeeze**: With F recording net losses (-3.9%), battery costs remain its greatest vulnerability. Unless lithium prices stabilize, Ford's EV push could result not in earnings improvement but in accumulated losses. The company lacks even standard valuation metrics like P/E, reflecting weak stock pricing.

## Conclusion

Lithium supply bottlenecks and mineral price volatility will be a key variable reshaping the EV industry through 2026–2027. Tesla, with scale and capital-raising power, can turn this phase into an opportunity. But traditional automakers like Ford, already recording losses, face the reality that any further cost rise becomes an existential business crisis. Mining companies face the same pressure: margin compression until price stabilization becomes unavoidable.
