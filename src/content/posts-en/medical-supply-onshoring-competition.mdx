## Onshoring Wave Crushes Global Medical Companies' Margins

Following the pandemic, the U.S. and European governments have mandated domestic production of key medical supplies, and the global medical device industry now faces an oversupply surge. Growth rates may be rising, but profit margins are plummeting.

## What Is Onshoring?

The pandemic's experience of supply chain disruption due to shortages of masks, vaccines, and medical equipment prompted governments to reshape policy. The U.S. Inflation Reduction Act (IRA) and Europe's supply chain autonomy initiatives mandate domestic production. The result is new factory construction and rising costs.

## Why Are Profit Margins Declining Now: Four Factors

**1. Overcapacity drives prices down**: Onshoring has dramatically increased supply capacity, but demand has returned to normal levels. Price declines in competing goods have begun.

**2. New entrants intensify competition**: Small manufacturers backed by domestic government support are entering the medical supply market. The pricing power of established giants—**JNJ** (closing price $253.91 as of Oct. 14, 2026), **MDT** ($94.23), and **ABT** ($102.97)—is weakening.

**3. Dismantling of multi-tier global supply chains**: The Asia-centric low-cost outsourcing model runs counter to domestic production requirements. Manufacturers bear the transition costs.

**4. Medical supplies becoming low-margin**: As competition intensifies, these products become commoditized. ABT's net profit margin is 11.7% (as of Oct. 14, 2026), the lowest in the industry, while MDT stands at only 13.9%. JNJ maintains better margins at 21.5%, but its revenue growth rate of 6.6% is the lowest among the three.

## Strengths and Weaknesses: A Structure Where Even the Strong Struggle

**Survival possible with technology, but**: Large pharmaceutical firms' R&D capabilities and economies of scale remain strong. They cannot win through price competition—only through innovation can they preserve margins.

**But near-term profitability deterioration is unavoidable**: Onshoring investment recovery, production diversification, and rising labor costs have converged. Growth may be possible, but depressed profitability appears likely to persist for years.

## Summary

While medical supply onshoring began with good intentions—pandemic preparedness—it is fundamentally reshaping the industry's profitability by restructuring the global supply chain. Even if growth rates may rise, profit margins face sustained downward pressure.
