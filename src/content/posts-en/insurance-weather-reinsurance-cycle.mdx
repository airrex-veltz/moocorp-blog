## Extreme Weather Reinsurance Crisis Erodes P&C Insurer Profitability

Extreme weather is driving reinsurance rates sharply higher, eroding the cost structure of property and casualty insurers. As prices surge in the reinsurance market—where insurers transfer risks to other insurers—major U.S. P&C insurers face a choice: accept diminished profitability or raise premiums.

## The Structure Behind Skyrocketing Reinsurance Rates

P&C insurer profitability depends on underwriting profit—premiums minus claims and operating expenses. Since insurers cannot bear all risks directly, they transfer excess losses to reinsurers. Reinsurance costs are a direct expense for insurers, and as extreme weather events become more frequent, reinsurance suppliers have sharply raised their rates.

**PGR** traded at $207.31 as of September 30, 2026, down 7.94% over the past three months. **ALL** stood at $221.86 on the same date, with a steeper three-month decline of 8.36%. Both stocks reflect market signals.

## Why P&C Insurer Stocks Are Under Pressure: Four Headwinds

**1. Rapid Accumulation of Extreme Weather Losses**: The frequency and severity of extreme weather events exceed predictions. Hurricane, wildfire, and flood damages are outpacing reserves and premium collection. When actual losses deviate from normal scenarios, reinsurance costs rise exponentially.

**2. Supply Constraints Among Reinsurers**: Major reinsurance firms have depleted capital due to expanded climate losses. In a constrained supply market, pricing power rests with reinsurers. P&C insurers must either accept higher prices or reduce their risk exposure.

**3. Limits to Premium Increases**: Consumer resistance and regulatory constraints prevent unlimited premium hikes. Insurance regulators in U.S. states scrutinize rate increases. Aggressive hikes also risk customer defection in competitive markets.

**4. Capital Adequacy Requirements**: P&C insurers must maintain sufficient capital relative to expected losses. As climate losses grow and reinsurance costs rise, required capital increases. This shrinks capacity for dividends and share buybacks.

## Yet Company Responses Differ: Structural Distinctions

**ALL's Valuation Paradox and Strong Growth**: As of September 30, 2026, ALL trades at a P/E of 4.5, well below PGR's 10.5. Net profit margin stands at 19.0% for ALL versus 12.8% for PGR. Revenue growth also favors ALL at 11.8% versus PGR's 7.3%. Yet ALL's steeper decline of 8.36% suggests the market views its future reinsurance burden as more severe.

**The Importance of Scale and Diversification**: PGR has long maintained a more auto-insurance-focused model. ALL has a more diversified property insurance and underwriting portfolio. Auto insurance is relatively less exposed to climate-driven losses like floods. This portfolio mix may influence future reinsurance rate increases for each company.

**Data and Forecasting Capability**: Large P&C insurers hold decades of loss data and machine learning models. With these tools, they can price risk more accurately and adjust reinsurance strategy. Smaller competitors face a disadvantage from this capability gap.

## Takeaway

The surge in reinsurance rates due to extreme weather is a shock to the entire P&C industry. However, because companies differ in their response capabilities and portfolio composition, the impact on losses will vary by firm. Current stock prices reflect this differential effect in real time.
