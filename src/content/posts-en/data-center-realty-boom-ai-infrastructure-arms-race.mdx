## The Hidden Cost of the GPU Revolution: Why Data Center Real Estate Is Gaining Attention

As demand for GPU chips required to train generative AI models surges globally, the construction of mega data centers is accelerating worldwide. This is not merely a challenge for technology companies—it is creating a structural investment opportunity in real assets: real estate, power grids, and cooling infrastructure.

**NVDA** achieved a 105.9% year-over-year revenue growth rate (as of October 16, 2026), while **MSFT** and **AMZN** continue cloud infrastructure investments with annual revenue growth rates of 17.7% and 19.6%, respectively. As these three companies compete to build and expand massive data centers, the prices of physical real estate, power supply, and cooling systems—all critical hardware assets—are rising in tandem.

## Data Center REITs and the Role of Cloud Companies

Mega data centers require billions of dollars in capital investment as infrastructure assets. **PLD** (Prologis, Inc.), a global REIT holding large-scale industrial and logistics real estate, recorded a net profit margin of 43.1% and revenue growth of 10.2% as of October 16, 2026, with a market capitalization of KRW 12.22 trillion. Cloud companies are increasingly entering into long-term lease agreements with real estate firms like PLD, reducing their capital burden and establishing a structure focused on data center operations.

## Four Drivers of the AI Infrastructure Competition

**1. GPU Shortage and Sustained Chip Demand**: NVDA's 105.9% revenue growth demonstrates how rapidly AI model training is expanding. More GPUs require larger data centers. This trend is expected to continue for the foreseeable future.

**2. Power Supply Crisis and Energy Infrastructure Investment**: Mega data centers consume the power output of one or two nuclear plants. Governments and companies worldwide are investing in securing renewable energy sources and expanding transmission and distribution infrastructure, which directly affects real estate selection. This is why land prices are rising in areas with superior power infrastructure.

**3. Evolution and Rising Costs of Cooling Technology**: Data center cooling accounts for a substantial portion of operating costs. As new technologies such as liquid cooling and AI-driven thermal management become necessary, the costs of renovating and expanding existing facilities have increased. This sometimes makes building from scratch on new land more cost-effective than improving existing equipment.

**4. Capital Competition Among Cloud Companies**: **MSFT** (market capitalization of KRW 3.93 quadrillion), **AMZN** (KRW 2.70 quadrillion), and **NVDA** (KRW 5.17 quadrillion) are investing tens of billions of dollars annually in data center expansion. As competition intensifies among these firms, land acquisition and rapid construction become decisive factors in profitability.

## Structural Strengths and Weaknesses

**Structural Strengths**: The 43.1% net profit margin of real estate companies like PLD indicates that data center lease rates are high and maintenance costs are controllable. Given that AI infrastructure demand is expected to persist for the next 5 to 10 years, securing large tenants ensures long-term profitability.

**Structural Weaknesses**: Regional imbalances in power supply limit where data centers can be constructed. In regions with strong environmental regulations, large-scale data center development may be delayed. Additionally, because the scale of initial construction and infrastructure investment reaches tens of billions of dollars, it is difficult to respond immediately to changes in market demand.

## Summary

Behind AI innovation lies a massive investment flow in physical assets. As GPU chip demand surges, power grid constraints tighten, cooling technology advances, and competition among cloud companies intensifies, data center real estate is likely to become a key pillar of infrastructure investment in the years ahead.
