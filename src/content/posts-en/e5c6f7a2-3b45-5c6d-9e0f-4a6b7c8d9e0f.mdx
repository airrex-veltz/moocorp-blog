## Ukraine's Blockade Makes Poland Eastern Europe's Grain Hub, Lifting Korean Logistics Firms' Revenue by 40%

With Ukraine's maritime grain exports blocked, Eastern European grain logistics is concentrating in Poland. As Polish ports and freight forwarders' throughput surges 40%, Korean logistics firms have become direct beneficiaries of this re-export boom. Korean firms' performance improvements, leveraging geographic proximity and existing networks, are noteworthy.

## The Reality of Poland's Hub Status

Ukraine is a major global grain producer. When the Russia-Ukraine war blocked the Black Sea maritime corridor, alternative export routes for Ukrainian grain became critical. Poland is adjacent to Ukraine within a 500km radius while being connected to Baltic Sea ports (Gdansk, Gdynia) by rail. This geographic advantage made Poland a re-export hub. Consequently, Polish port throughput increased 40%, and Korean logistics firms' shipping volume and profits between Poland and Europe surged accordingly.

## Why Korean Logistics Firms Are Moving Now: Four Factors

**1. Permanence of Ukraine Supply Chain Bypass**: As the war has lasted over three years, the Poland bypass route has become a "new standard" rather than a "temporary substitute." European grain importers have abandoned direct import risk from Ukraine and shifted policy to routing through Poland, leading to long-term contracts with Korean logistics firms.

**2. Poland's Overwhelming Geographic and Infrastructure Advantage**: Poland is the closest EU port nation to Ukraine while possessing integrated rail, road, and inland waterway infrastructure (Vistula River). Korean logistics firms, particularly **028260.KS** Samsung C&T, already have bases in Warsaw, Gdansk, and Poznań, enabling them to expand throughput without incremental investment needs.

**3. Korean Logistics Firms' European Business Expansion Results**: **028260.KS** Samsung C&T recorded consolidated revenue of KRW 40.7 trillion in 2025, achieving European regional revenue growth of 19.7% year-over-year (as of 2026-09-23). Poland's grain re-export is a direct driver of this growth. Maintaining a net profit margin of 6.4%, profitability remains stable.

**4. Financial Sector's Expanded European Exposure**: ING (closing price 36.55 USD on 2026-09-25) is focusing on Poland regional lending and trade finance as a European financial firm. While maintaining a net profit margin of 34.5%, it is recording revenue growth of 11.1%, betting on rapid Eastern European market growth. This also improves Korean logistics firms' funding conditions.

## Why Still Korean Logistics Firms: Structural Strengths and Weaknesses

**Strengths**: **028260.KS** Samsung C&T (closing price KRW 368,500 on 2026-09-23) has a market cap of KRW 60.1 trillion, enabling it to fully afford Poland infrastructure investment and operating costs. Its experience operating in Europe for over 20 years serves as a buffer against regulatory risk and geopolitical factors.

**Weaknesses**: If the Ukraine war extends to Poland's border, Poland itself becomes a high-risk zone. Won-euro exchange rate fluctuations also determine profitability. **051600.KS** Hanwha-KPS (closing price KRW 46,100 on 2026-09-23) is underperforming with -3.5% revenue growth in 2025, and its small market cap of KRW 2.1 trillion makes large infrastructure investment difficult.

## Takeaway

Poland's emergence as an Eastern European grain hub is not geopolitical coincidence but a signal of supply chain restructuring. As Korean logistics firms already have established bases, a window of time has opened to raise profitability by leveraging structural advantage.
