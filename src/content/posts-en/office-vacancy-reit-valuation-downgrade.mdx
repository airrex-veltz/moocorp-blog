# Office Vacancy Crisis Forces REITs to Revalue Assets

The office vacancy crisis in the US real estate market is now forcing REITs (Real Estate Investment Trusts) into full portfolio restructuring. REITs, which return cash to investors annually through dividends, face unavoidable asset revaluation as they confront tenant departures and declining rents. Some major holdings with high urban office building exposure are now facing potential dividend adjustments due to portfolio restructuring.

## Mixed Signals in the Market

**VNQ** (Vanguard Real Estate Index ETF), the primary market indicator for the REIT sector, closed at $95.73 on November 6, 2026, up 7.14% over one month but down 0.57% over three months. The six-month range of $88.11–$100.92 reflects underlying sector uncertainty. The PER (price-to-earnings ratio) of 30.3 exceeds the historical average of around 20, indicating the market still applies a premium to this asset class. The critical question is whether that premium adequately reflects future dividend pressures from office restructuring.

Major holdings show clearer directional trends. **O** (Realty Income) reached $59.04 on November 6, up 9.51% over one month, but despite a market cap of $56.3 billion, its PER of 40.4 is notably higher than VNQ. With a net profit margin of 21.8% and YoY growth of 10.2%, profitability is solid, yet the elevated valuation appears as a warning signal. **STAG** (STAG Industrial) at $36.60 (up 2.01% over one month) recorded the most modest gain, maintaining relatively sound fundamentals with a PER of 26.0 and net profit margin of 29.9%. This difference stems from STAG's industrial and logistics real estate portfolio being less exposed to the office vacancy crisis.

**PLD** (Prologis) at $126.96 (down 7.78% over three months) underwent significant correction, but its extreme net profit margin of 43.1% is mitigated by e-commerce weakness affecting its logistics and warehouse-focused portfolio. **KKR** (KKR & Co.) at $96.48 (up 7.73% over one month) sees its role as a real estate asset manager expanding, with a net profit margin of 12.2% and PER of 30.8 maintaining moderate levels relative to its financial intermediary function.

## Four Structural Pressure Factors

**First, portfolio rotation from office to industrial and logistics.** To counter the declining asset values of office buildings with rising vacancies, REITs are pursuing acquisitions of logistics facilities whose profitability has improved after interest rate increases. STAG's relative strength and O's elevated PER reflect portfolio exposure differences during this reallocation process.

**Second, visible erosion of distributable income (FFO—Funds From Operations).** Rising office vacancies directly translate into new lease contracting stagnation and existing tenants renegotiating lower rental rates. O's high PER of 40.4 reflects investor expectations that current dividend levels will persist, but if the renewal environment for maturing leases deteriorates, dividend adjustments become inevitable.

**Third, the dual impact of interest rate environment.** Rising rates lower the present value of REIT assets while increasing debt financing costs. VNQ's three-month decline of 0.57% and PLD's six-month correction of 7.78% demonstrate this pressure. Current elevated PERs can only be justified in a declining interest rate scenario.

**Fourth, institutional capital reallocation pressure.** As large asset managers like KKR directly engage in real estate operations, the profitability premium of traditional REITs contracts. KKR's market cap of $89.1 billion and YoY growth of 7.8% signal this faction's expanding influence.

## Differentiation of Strengths and Weaknesses Within the Sector

**Relative strength of industrial and logistics REITs**: STAG's PER of 26.0, net profit margin of 29.9%, and YoY growth of 9.4% demonstrate a portfolio positioned farthest from the office crisis. The reason for only a 2.01% one-month gain amid interest rate instability may be that the market has already fully priced in that stability.

**Structural weakness of large multitenant REITs**: O's market cap of $56.3 billion and PER of 40.4 mean the market assigns enormous premiums to its current high dividends. Currently at $59.04 within a six-month range of $52.90–$65.93, it sits near the midpoint, yet given fundamentals near record office vacancy, substantial downside revaluation risk exists.

**Hybrid player role**: PLD shows an extremely high net profit margin of 43.1%, but has adjusted from its six-month peak of $152.14 to the current $126.96. This suggests e-commerce weakness concerns are affecting the logistics sector as well.

## Timeline and Outlook for Revaluation

Real estate asset valuation is reflected through net asset value (NAV) published quarterly. With office vacancy rates hovering near record highs, downward NAV revisions over the coming quarters appear nearly certain. As the justification for current elevated PERs weakens, the REIT sector faces revaluation on two axes: investor sentiment regarding portfolio restructuring and interest rate expectations.

From an asset allocation perspective, the spread in returns between holdings with industrial and logistics-focused portfolios and those with high office exposure is likely to widen. Current price data as of November 6, 2026, suggests that difference has not yet been fully priced in.
