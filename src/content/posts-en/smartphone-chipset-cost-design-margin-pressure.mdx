## Smartphone Chipset Design Competition Erodes Processor Makers' Margins

Competition in smartphone and PC processors is reaching a breaking point. As competition intensifies for miniaturization and high performance, design and verification costs balloon, while chipset prices fall. As a result, manufacturers unable to recoup technology investments face margin pressure, while smartphone makers like Samsung, OPPO, and Xiaomi strengthen their negotiating power through multi-source strategies.

## The Processor Market's Light and Shadow

The circumstances of semiconductor design companies are mixed. As of the close on September 21, 2026, **NVDA** stands at $227.38 with revenue YoY +105.9% and net margin 63.7%. The result of maintaining GPU dominance as data center demand leads the AI boom. In contrast, **QCOM**, which depends on mobile chipsets, is at $194.23 with revenue YoY -4.0% and net margin 21.0%, performing poorly. **AMD** is at a closing price of $615.52 (revenue YoY +50.1%, net margin 15.6%) performing well, but **INTC** ($121.78, net margin -19.8%) faces structural crisis.

Rising smartphone chipset design costs are the core driver. Below 5 nanometers, hundreds of millions of dollars are invested per generation in design, verification, and yield optimization alone. Meanwhile, smartphone cycles have shortened to 12-24 months, and as competitors release comparable performance within months, pricing pressure intensifies.

## Why Margins Are Collapsing Now: Four Factors

**1. The vicious cycle of miniaturization and high-performance competition**: The gap in multi-core performance between flagship smartphones has narrowed to 5-10% per generation. Consumers perceive no noticeable difference and keep existing devices longer. Manufacturers compete with more expensive processes to make the difference stand out, yet chipset prices fall.

**2. ARM-based design proliferation and IP royalty competition**: Following Apple's success with custom processors (M series, A series), Samsung, OPPO, and even Google are developing custom chipsets. While ARM licensing costs are fixed, the pursuit of design excellence intensifies. Mass production is also impossible, requiring design costs to be absorbed within custom chipset revenues.

**3. OEM supply diversification strategies**: Smartphone makers do not rely solely on QCOM. Samsung has Exynos, MediaTek has raised its share in budget smartphones, and Qualcomm's bargaining power weakens. As multi-source supply becomes possible, device makers gain even stronger negotiating positions.

**4. PC market recovery uncertainty**: As Chromebooks and budget tablets proliferate, the high-value market where x86 PC design costs can be recouped shrinks. Both Intel and AMD face difficulty fully recovering the costs invested in high-performance PC chipset design.

## Yet The Real Problem Is Structural Weakness

**Margin pressure → design investment decline vicious cycle**: QCOM and AMD, with net margins below 20%, must economize on capital for next-generation chipset design. As a result, competitive gaps widen further.

**Loss of bargaining power from supply diversification**: The more choices OEMs have, the more individual chipset sales scatter. Reduced order volumes increase process costs (fab reservation fees), ultimately worsening profitability.

**Design technology standardization**: As advanced process technology becomes relatively accessible to all, differentiation grows harder. The smaller the performance gain, the more competition depends on price alone.

## Summary

The chipset market has reached a state where design cost recovery models are no longer viable due to technology saturation and intensified competition. Companies like Nvidia that control high-value domains (AI computing) survive, while companies like QCOM and AMD that depend on general-purpose chipset design face margin deterioration and restructuring. As OEM diversification strategies deepen, processor makers' bargaining power is expected to continue declining.
