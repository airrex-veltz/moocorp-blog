## GLP-1 Patent Expiry Shakes Big Pharma

GLP-1 obesity drug patents are expiring soon, hitting innovation-pharma with margin pressure. **NVO** (Novo Nordisk) is down 22.25% in three months.

## GLP-1 Market's Structural Shift

Obesity and diabetes treatment transformed in the early 2020s with GLP-1 receptor agonists. Novo's Ozempic and Wegovy, Lilly's Mounjaro created multibillion-dollar markets. But core patents expire 2025–2027, as generic entry looms. **NVO** closed at USD 37.53 (2026-10-06), down sharply from USD 51.46 year-to-date. **LLY** at USD 1,157.49 held relative stability but still logged 4.66% three-month decline.

## Why GLP-1 Firms Falter Now: Four Pressure Factors

**1. Generic entry and price collapse imminent**: Patent expiry lets low-cost generics flood the market, destroying branded drug pricing power. **NVO** shows 35.4% net margin but only 2.1% revenue growth—confined to existing patients, growth ceiling reached. **LLY**'s 47.7% revenue growth signals strategy to offset through new indications and market share capture.

**2. Pharma's differential response capacity**: **LLY** (USD 1.07T market cap, 6x **NVO**'s USD 168.6B) diversified its pipeline. High PER 40.1 reflects market confidence in forward growth. **AMGN** (USD 402.60) alone posted three-month +10.04%, unique strength. 22.9% net margin shows relative stability.

**3. Market saturation and indication limits**: GLP-1 expanded beyond obesity/diabetes into cardiovascular and kidney disease. But the absolute prescribable population is finite. Market segmentation from staggered patent expiry by firm sharpens competition.

**4. Competitor entry and healthcare cost control**: **JNJ** (USD 254.78) is attempting market entry with its own products, but brand advantage still holds; 6.6% growth lags incumbents. Government and insurer cost-control policies limit premium-drug price increases.

## Strength and Weakness Among Giants

**Strength**: **LLY** and **AMGN** have deeper pipelines; GLP-1 dependence is lower. **LLY**'s 47.7% growth likely comes from non-GLP-1 products. **NVO**'s 35.4% net margin provides near-term buffer, but portfolio concentration is the risk.

**Weakness**: **NVO**'s biggest danger is portfolio concentration. GLP-1 dominates revenue; post-patent, company performance could crater sharply. **JNJ**'s 6.6% growth and 21.5% margin reflect general pharma saturation. Market poised to divide sharply—winners and losers emerging fast.

## Summary

GLP-1 patent expiry is not just a drug-price issue but signals pharma industry reorganization. Firms with innovation depth and capital scale (LLY) adapt; single-product dependencies (NVO) face structural risk. **NVO**'s three-month drop versus **LLY**'s relative stability shows exactly this divergence.
