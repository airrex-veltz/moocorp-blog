## Cloud Market's Monopoly Structure Cracks, Multi-Cloud Era Accelerates

AWS's enterprise cloud market, held in a single hand, faces a historical turning point. As customers' supply diversification strategies shift from simple risk avoidance to a structural choice, the cloud landscape begins to tilt. Established players like Oracle and IBM show visible results, while AWS itself loses ground to encroachment on its dominance.

## Market Leaders' Conflicting Signals

**AMZN** maintains absolute superiority in scale. As of September 24, 2026 close, it recorded $249.38, showing 19.6% revenue growth and considerable pulling power in the cloud market. However, the 1-month decline of -4.47% hints at a recent weakening of market sentiment.

**ORCL** shows surprising growth momentum. As of September 24, 2026 close, it recorded $139.54, with 29.6% revenue growth nearly 1.5 times that of Amazon. Its cloud strategy based on databases and enterprise software is gaining persuasiveness among customers.

**IBM** reflects a modest situation. As of September 24, 2026 close it recorded $227.06, but with 1.1% revenue growth, it falls significantly below industry average. Despite hybrid cloud positioning, it lags behind the massive currents of change.

## Four Factors Accelerating Multi-Cloud

**1. Cloud Cost Optimization Urgency**: Amid prolonged economic weakness, enterprises have begun rejecting acceptance of pricing power created by single-vendor dependency. Creating competitive dynamics among multiple clouds and raising one's own bargaining power has now become a mandatory choice. The mechanism of placing each workload on the most cost-efficient platform is settling as a standard in enterprise architecture.

**2. Technology Standardization and Improved Compatibility**: The development of open standards like Kubernetes and OpenStack has dramatically increased cloud portability. The risk of vendor lock-in has now fallen sharply, and customers can more freely compare options. On this technical foundation, players like Oracle have gained space to exercise expertise-based competitiveness.

**3. Enterprise Supply Chain Diversification Strategy Expansion**: As geopolitical uncertainty and supply chain risks have become management agenda, companies view IT infrastructure as an extension of this. The judgment that single-region, single-vendor dependency is a strategic vulnerability has become widespread. While this can be an opportunity for global players like Oracle and IBM, it simultaneously works as a mechanism to disperse AWS's influence.

**4. Emerging Players and Niche Solutions' Competitiveness**: Challengers like Microsoft Azure and Google Cloud have created clear advantages in specific workloads (AI, data analytics, specialized industries). Companies are now changing their decision-making approach from "doing everything in one place" to "finding the optimal combination."

## Diverging Paths of Winners and Losers

The cloud market restructuring is a process of reorganizing vertical monopoly structures into horizontal ecosystems. Amazon still holds superiority in absolute scale, but situations where that superiority automatically translates to improved profitability are intensifying. As price competition intensifies, margin pressure becomes unavoidable. Meanwhile, Oracle's growth momentum suggests it has successfully grafted its existing strengths in databases and enterprise integration onto cloud transformation. However, this advantage can itself be eroded depending on the pace of technology standardization.

Despite its position in hybrid cloud, IBM is not gaining growth momentum. Enterprise trust and legacy system integration capabilities remain, but it lags in preference among cloud-native customer segments.

## Summary

The weakening of enterprise cloud market monopoly structure is an irreversible trend. Amazon's dominance remains substantial in absolute terms, but its relative influence will systematically diminish. Now the key to success has shifted from scale to expertise and efficiency in specific customer segments and workloads. Investors should evaluate not just market share but also margin rates, customer satisfaction, and consistency of technology roadmap.
