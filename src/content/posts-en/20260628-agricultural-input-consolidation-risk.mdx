## Agricultural Input Consolidation Risk: Antitrust Pressure on Fertilizer & Seed Oligopolies

Government prioritization of food security and regulatory tightening have placed fertilizer and seed oligopoly companies under threat of forced divestiture.

---

## Post-Pandemic Supply Shocks and Oligopoly Vulnerabilities Exposed

The 2022 Ukraine war and fertilizer export restrictions starkly revealed farmers' dependence on agricultural inputs. As a handful of major corporations controlled seed and chemical fertilizer prices within fragmented supply chains, global grain production faced mounting threats. U.S. corn farmers' average cost per seed package rose more than 40% compared to 2015, while nitrogen fertilizer prices surged threefold. As governments shifted to food self-sufficiency as a national strategy, regulatory scrutiny intensified on the global top three—**Corteva**, **Bayer**, and **BASF**.

## Oligopolistic Structure of the Agricultural Input Industry

The global seed market is dominated by the top three firms, which collectively control 62% of market share. **Corteva Agriscience** (formerly Dow's and DuPont's agricultural divisions) leads with approximately 23% market share, **Bayer CropScience** holds 18%, and **BASF** maintains about 14%. The fertilizer market shows similar concentration levels. This oligopoly intensified following the wave of major mergers and acquisitions in the mid-to-late 2010s: the Dow-DuPont integration, Bayer's acquisition of Monsanto, and BASF's acquisition of Syngenta reshaped the sector. After pandemic-related supply bottlenecks surfaced, the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) and the U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA) diagnosed that "farmers lack alternatives and face monopolistic pricing."

## Why Divestiture Pressure Is Intensifying Now: Four Factors

**1. Strengthened government intervention under food security rationale**: The EU and U.S. governments have intensively investigated agricultural supply chain vulnerabilities over the past two years. The European Commission approved Bayer's acquisition of BASF-related operations conditionally but subsequently initiated a review citing "farmer harm." The U.S. FTC has interrogated **Corteva** on the monopolistic nature of seed licensing contracts, with governments in India, Brazil, and other major grain-producing nations following suit.

**2. Agricultural inputs as strategic resources**: With fertilizers and seeds designated as cornerstones of addressing global food crises and inflation, discourse on whether they should be "public goods" has strengthened. The U.S. invoked the Defense Production Act in 2023 to spur fertilizer production, and many nations have allocated budgets to seed banks and increased fertilizer stockpiles. Within this policy framework, multinational firms' enhanced pricing power becomes politically disadvantageous.

**3. Intensified lobbying by farmer groups and small-farm movements**: The American Farm Bureau Federation and European small-farmer associations submitted concrete "divestiture proposals" in 2024. Demands such as **Corteva**'s separation of seed and chemical divisions and **Bayer**'s agricultural sector downsizing have been translated into legislative lobbying efforts. Notably, the U.S. Senate Agriculture Committee has scheduled at least three special antitrust hearings this year.

**4. Expanded scope of competition authorities**: Under FTC Chair Lina Khan's aggressive regulation of oligopolistic firms, agriculture has risen in priority as a "vulnerable sector." Draft legislation similar to the EU's Digital Markets Act, framed as a "food self-sufficiency promotion act," is under review. South Korea and Japan are simultaneously promoting domestic seed development support and restricting foreign oligopoly firms' market entry to reduce import dependence.

## Why These Three Companies Persist: Structural Strengths and Weaknesses

**Sustained technological barriers to entry**: R&D capabilities in gene editing and drought-resistant crop development remain a monopoly of the three major firms. If forced divestitures occur, the lack of mid-size firms equipped to inherit these technologies may limit enforcement effectiveness.

**Yet deepening supply chain risks**: If divestiture forces separate production, distribution, and sales, price stability may decline. Small firms would lose economies of scale, paradoxically raising unit costs. The rise of generic seed companies from emerging markets like India and Brazil, along with state-owned fertilizer producers, poses the genuine competitive threat.

## Summary

Easing agricultural input oligopolies is an unavoidable trend. However, "mandatory licensing" and "open data sharing" regulations are more likely to materialize as practical outcomes than forced divestitures. Over the coming 12 months, the trajectory of global regulatory proceedings and each of the three companies' concrete responses—such as voluntary business separation or enhanced emerging-market partnerships—will determine supply chain stability.
