## Thai Real Estate Crisis Shakes Southeast Asian Financial Credit System

Structural oversupply in the Thai housing market and deterioration in borrowers' repayment capacity are driving sharp increases in financial sector NPL ratios. Credit risk emerging from here is spreading to regional financial institutions such as Singapore, creating a structure where Korean financial firms advancing into Asia and credit-based rental companies will be directly affected.

## Deepening Picture of Thai Financial Deterioration

Thai bank sector loan NPL ratios have risen to around 7%. This significantly exceeds the average for regional financial institutions. The combination of sharp residential real estate price declines and rising household debt has rapidly eroded the repayment capacity of existing borrowers who took out low-interest loans. Financial supervisory bodies have already issued directives to strengthen capital ratios, and major Thai and Singaporean banks' capital adequacy ratios are declining toward international baseline levels.

## Why Korean Financial Companies and Rental Firms Are Affected Now: Four Factors

**1. Credit risk transmission across regional financial institutions**: The expansion of NPLs at Thai banks is immediately reflected in the inter-bank lending market. Singaporean financial institutions also carry exposure to Thai banking sector assets, so the proportion of risky assets rises, and as a result, trust in the regional financial system declines. If this intensifies, interest rates among regional financial institutions spike sharply.

**2. Expanding Asia emerging market credit premium**: As the Thai situation is recognized not as an isolated issue but as regional systemic risk, financial firms in neighboring countries such as Singapore and the Philippines face rising financing costs in international capital markets. Operations conducted by Korean financial holding companies such as Shinhan Financial Group in Asia automatically attract a risk premium.

**3. Household credit contraction and weak consumption**: Sharp Thai housing price declines reverse the wealth effect to negative, and elevated NPL ratios strengthen banks' loan screening standards. Reduced consumption and tightened credit supply also dampen demand in the rental market. Lotte Rental faces indirect and direct exposure to rental business share in the region and Thai credit risk.

**4. Pressure on Korean financial firms' international capital ratios**: If NPLs related to Thailand increase in the loan portfolio held through Asian subsidiaries by Shinhan Financial Group and others, capital ratios decline on a consolidated basis. There is a high likelihood that financial regulatory authorities will demand additional capital provisions or reductions in risk assets.

## Can Korean Financial Firms Still Withstand the Shock: Capital Strength vs. Exposure

**Shinhan Financial Group's support**: On a closing price basis of KRW 110,800 on 2026-09-28, it maintains a net profit margin of 26.2%. The scale of KRW 5.1 trillion in net income for 2025 indicates sufficient capital to absorb short-term credit shocks from a single country like Thailand. However, this is the existing capital state, and the risk of violating capital ratio thresholds does not disappear if additional losses accumulate going forward.

**Lotte Rental's vulnerability**: With a net profit margin of 4.0% and 2025 net income around KRW 126.7 billion, its support is much weaker than Shinhan Financial Group. The rental business model directly depends on borrower creditworthiness. Deterioration in Thai household credit immediately translates to increased delinquencies and defaults.

## Summary

The Thai real estate crisis leads to capital ratio deterioration and credit risk chains across regional financial institutions. Korean financial firms are affected proportionally to their Asia business scale. In particular, the credit costs of rental firms with low profitability and slow capital accumulation will likely visibly deteriorate from the latter half of this year through early next year.
