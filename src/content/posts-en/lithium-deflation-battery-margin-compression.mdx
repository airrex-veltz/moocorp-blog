## Lithium Price Collapse Pressures Battery Makers' Net Margins in Reverse

Lithium import prices collapsed more than 50% throughout 2024, yet this has paradoxically become a burden for battery and electric vehicle manufacturers. As raw material cost savings are passed entirely to lower selling prices, profitability is actually deteriorating rather than improving.

## Battery and EV Company Performance Deteriorates Amid Lithium Plunge

**SQM** (Chile's largest lithium producer) traded at 57.52 USD on 2026-11-06, with a three-month change of -19.52%. In the same period, **ALB** (a major U.S. lithium producer) fell from 99.25 USD, registering a three-month decline of -24.10%. Despite supply glut driving down prices, both companies reported rising revenue — SQM's sales growth rate (YoY) reached 136.7%. However, this is also a signal of cost pressure.

Electric vehicle makers face the same challenge. **TSLA** closed at 358.22 USD on 2026-11-06 with a net profit margin of only 3.1%, while revenue growth stagnated at -0.2%. **LI** faces a more severe situation with a net profit margin of -4.4% (operating at a loss) and YoY revenue contraction of -15.1%. Although the one-month stock price rose 14.95%, this is only a short-term rebound and does not signal fundamental profitability improvement.

## Why This Phenomenon Is Intensifying Now: Four Pressure Factors

**1. Vicious cycle of oversupply**: SQM's 136.7% revenue growth rate demonstrates that global lithium production significantly exceeds demand. Price collapse is not a short-term phenomenon but a signal that structural oversupply is becoming entrenched.

**2. Battery manufacturer margin squeeze**: Raw material costs have fallen, but battery makers cannot retain these savings as profits. Instead, they face automakers' demands for cost reductions and must pass savings on entirely.

**3. Automaker pressure for price cuts**: **TSLA**'s 3.1% net profit margin and **LI**'s -4.4% loss illustrate how intense price competition in electric vehicles has become. Companies immediately pass material cost savings into lower selling prices to defend market share.

**4. Stagnant consumer purchasing power**: Even if lithium prices drop, electric vehicle demand does not revive in tandem. Within this vicious cycle, downward price pressure persists, leaving little actual profitability.

## Why This Structure Continues: Structural Weaknesses

**ALB's premium valuation**: A PER of 23.9 is elevated among lithium producers. This suggests investors still believe in long-term lithium demand growth, yet near-term profitability is deteriorating.

**The margin paradox trap**: It might seem that falling raw material costs should increase company profits. However, within competitive structures, this gain is not fully preserved. Battery makers pass lithium savings to automakers, who in turn pass them to consumers.

## Summary

The lithium price collapse is a signal of energy transition while simultaneously indicating deteriorating profitability across the entire industry. In a market structure where cost savings do not translate into improved net profits, battery and EV makers are forced to pursue only economies of scale, which in turn generates further downward price pressure.
