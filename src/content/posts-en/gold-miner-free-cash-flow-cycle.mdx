## Gold Mining Industry Profitability at Peak Gold Prices

To diagnose shareholder return capacity for gold mining companies when gold prices hover near historic highs, it is necessary first to understand the profitability structure unique to the gold mining industry.

Recent financial metrics from Newmont (NEM) clearly show where the industry stands today. What do the closing price of USD 109.09 on November 2, 2026, a net profit margin of 34.7%, and a P/E of 12.5 mean?

### The Meaning of High Net Profit Margins

A net profit margin of 34.7% is a level rarely seen in manufacturing or technology industries. This reflects the fact that while sales have increased due to rising gold prices, fixed costs of mine operations have not become proportionally heavier. The profitability of gold miners is heavily dependent on the gap between 'gold production volume' and 'all-in sustaining cost (AISC) per unit of gold'. The current high gold price has created an environment that maximizes this gap, resulting in a net profit margin of 34.7%.

### What P/E 12.5 Suggests

Simultaneously, a P/E of 12.5 signals that the market does not view this high profit level as sustainable. In an industry where profitability is highly dependent on gold prices, investors judge that the current scale of earnings cannot be expected to continue in the future. If gold prices adjust, net profit margins can collapse sharply.

### 15.2% Growth Rate and Capital Allocation

A revenue growth rate of 15.2% carries signals of both nominal increases from rising gold prices and actual expansion in mining output. The fact that the world's largest gold mining company by market cap at USD 111.8 billion is growing at this pace means it is allocating capital toward project expansion in a high gold price environment.

### Shareholder Returns: Capacity and Limits

In the gold mining industry, the gold price cycle determines capital allocation policy. When gold prices are at their peak, companies face two choices.

First, return cash to shareholders through dividends or share buybacks,
second, invest in mine development and acquisitions to increase future production.

The high net profit margin of 34.7% certainly suggests capacity for shareholder returns. Yet the undervaluation implied by a P/E of 12.5 also means that the market views this capacity as temporary. Once gold prices enter a downcycle, the same company's net profit margin could fall below current levels, and with it, the sustainable levels of dividends and capital expenditures would shrink substantially.

### The Reality of Mine Profitability Structure

To fairly judge a gold mining company's actual shareholder return capacity, one must consider all-in sustaining cost (AISC), gold production volume, and actual free cash flow (FCF) together. Under the assumption that gold prices maintain or increase from current levels, there would be ample capacity. However, in a scenario where gold prices fall to the breakeven level, the majority of value creation that underpins the current high net profit margin would evaporate.

### Conclusion: At the Peak of the Cycle

Newmont's high net profit margin is a snapshot of how favorable the current gold price level is. However, the low valuation implied by a P/E of 12.5 means that investors recognize the temporary nature of this situation. The lesson in evaluating shareholder return capacity is that sustainable allocation policy for the next cycle must be designed based on the cash accumulated at the peak of the gold cycle.
