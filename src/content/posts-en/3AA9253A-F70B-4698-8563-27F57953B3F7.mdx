## When AI Chip Boom Arrives, China Tightens Rare-Earth Refining Controls

As AI semiconductor demand surges, the global supply chain risk in rare-earth refining for packaging materials has come to the surface. China handles approximately 85% of global rare-earth refining, and this bottleneck directly threatens the scalability of the semiconductor industry.

## Rare-Earth Elements and the Hidden Role of Semiconductor Packaging Materials

Rare-earth elements (REE) consist of 17 elements including neodymium, praseodymium, and dysprosium. They are key additives that enhance thermal conductivity and reliability of packaging materials in the semiconductor assembly process. Particularly, high-performance GPUs and AI accelerators generate significant heat, driving surging demand for packaging materials containing rare-earth elements. Global rare-earth refining volume increased from approximately 130,000 tons in 2019 to approximately 155,000 tons in 2025, yet China's share remains above 85%.

## Why Supply Bottlenecks Are Worsening Now: Four Factors

**1. China's Own Demand Surge**: Following China's semiconductor self-sufficiency strategy (targeting 50% domestic content rate by 2030), major foundries like SMIC and Huawei Semiconductor have significantly increased their consumption of rare-earth packaging materials. This shrinks exportable volumes.

**2. Tightening Environmental Regulations**: The cost of handling radioactive waste (thorium and uranium) generated during rare-earth refining has surged. From 2026, China is applying "green refining" standards to its refining industry, leading to reduced operating rates at existing refineries.

**3. Geopolitical Power Reconfiguration**: The U.S., Japan, and Australia are moving toward rare-earth self-sufficiency, but building refining facilities takes 3–5 years alone. Refinement quality and price competitiveness still lag behind China.

**4. Triple Demand Shock**: AI data centers (NVIDIA, AMD GPUs), EV batteries (permanent magnets), and defense industries (missiles, radar) all compete for rare-earth elements. Semiconductor packaging alone accounts for 15–18% of total demand, while other industries are also consuming supplies.

## Supply Risks Remain Real: Structural Weaknesses

**Difficulty in Expanding China's Refining Capacity**: Environmental approval processes for new or expanded refining plants add another 1–2 years, and production costs are rising to align with international standards.

**Long Development Cycles for Substitutes**: Developing rare-earth-free packaging materials requires a minimum of 2–3 years of R&D and validation. This is slower than the semiconductor product cycle (12–18 months), creating a timing gap.

**Limitations in Inventory Build-Up**: Semiconductor manufacturers are unable to accumulate long-term inventory due to rising cost concerns, compounded by worries over Chinese export restrictions.

## Summary

As global AI chip demand surges, the structure in which one country monopolizes 85% of rare-earth refining remains unchanged. Supply bottlenecks may constrain semiconductor assembly schedules in 2026–2027, and technology companies and foundries are already considering alternative packaging materials or investments in their own refining.
