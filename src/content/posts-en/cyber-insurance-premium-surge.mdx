## Cyber Insurance Premium Surge Suppresses SME Investment Paradox

As cyber security incidents increase, insurance premiums rise steeply. A paradox emerges where exactly when companies must invest more, insurance premium burdens cause digital transformation investments to be crowded out.

## Insurance Company Growth Signals and Profitability Strengthening

Three major global insurers show profitability improvement. **CB** (Chubb) at close USD 350.18 (as of 2026-10-14) maintains 18.1% net profit margin with 6.1% revenue growth. **ALL** (Allstate) at close USD 236.17 shows 19.0% net profit margin, highest profitability, with 11.8% revenue growth, top level among three. **HIG** (Hartford) at close USD 131.99 recorded 14.9% net profit margin and 8.1% revenue growth.

## Why Premiums Rise Now: Four Factors

**1. Ransomware and data breach surge**: As cyber attacks increase in corporate environments, insurers face rising claim counts and amounts. One major breach can inflict multi-million dollar losses on companies.

**2. Insurance company loss compensation pressure**: With claims increasing, insurers have no choice but to raise risk premiums. High net profit margins stem from premium increases.

**3. Increase concentrated on SMEs**: Large enterprises can lower risk through own security investment but SMEs cannot. Insurers apply higher increase rates to high-risk SMEs.

**4. Investment capital reallocation pressure**: As insurance burden grows, companies prioritize premium payments over digital security infrastructure investment. A vicious cycle of delayed actual cyber defense system construction begins.

## Structural Strengths and Limitations

**Insurer profitability**: CB, ALL, HIG all maintain stable net profit margins with excellent financial health. Short-term revenue can be generated from premium increases.

**System's fundamental limitation**: Insuring losses is symptom treatment, not cause treatment. If companies delay real security investment, accident probability itself doesn't decrease. This threatens insurer long-term profitability.

## Summary

Premium surges support insurer revenue short-term, yet suppress companies' real security investment while increasing cyber risk industry-wide. Breaking this vicious cycle requires not just insurance premiums but corporate voluntary investment incentive structures.
