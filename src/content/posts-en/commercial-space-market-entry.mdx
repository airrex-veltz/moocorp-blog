## Defense Contractors' Space Business Diversification, but Stock Prices Decline

**BA** (Boeing) and **LMT** (Lockheed Martin) have primarily relied on defense contracts since the Cold War. However, expansion in commercial space transportation and satellite businesses is presenting new revenue opportunities for these companies. At the same time, both companies' stock prices are weak. BA closed at $196.87 on 2026-09-24, down 6.73% over one month, while LMT declined 6.57% over one month from $523.85.

## The Intersection of Defense Contractors and the Space Industry

Traditionally, BA and LMT have conducted cyclical businesses dependent on government defense contracts. However, the growth of the space economy has increased the necessity for these companies to develop revenue sources beyond defense. As of 2026-09-24, BA recorded a market cap of 155.6 trillion dollars and revenue growth rate of 8.0%, while LMT showed a market cap of 120.9 trillion dollars with revenue growth rate of 10.5%. LMT's high profit margin (net margin of 8.2%) is typical of dependence on a small number of large contracts, while BA's low net margin (2.6%) reflects the recovery process following the 737 MAX crisis.

## Why Are They Rushing to Enter the Space Market Now? Four Factors

**1. Acceleration of Commercial Space Demand**: Civilian space activities such as satellite communications, space tourism, and orbital asset development are growing rapidly. Unlike during the Cold War, space is no longer the exclusive domain of nation-states.

**2. Cyclicality and Uncertainty of Defense Budgets**: Government defense spending is sensitive to political and economic conditions. Diversification is a management strategy to buffer against such volatility.

**3. Pressure for Vertical Integration**: Companies with integrated capabilities across launch vehicles, satellites, and ground stations are gaining advantage in the space value chain. Large companies like BA and LMT are leveraging experience and capital from existing defense businesses to expand into space.

**4. Emergence of New Competitors**: The success of private space companies like SpaceX puts pressure on existing defense contractors to adapt. Falling behind in technological innovation and cost efficiency could result in loss of long-term competitiveness.

## Why They Remain Defense Giants: Structural Strengths and Constraints

**Technical Assets and Regulatory Trust**: BA and LMT possess technologies and supply chains accumulated over decades in the aerospace industry. Experience with government contracts translates into capability to navigate regulatory complexity.

**Financial Strength**: The market caps of both companies (BA 155.6 trillion dollars, LMT 120.9 trillion dollars) represent sufficient resources to invest in space business R&D and acquisitions.

**However, Lack of Agility**: The high P/E ratios of BA at 70.8 and LMT at 19.3 (particularly BA) reflect investors' expectations for profit margin recovery. It remains uncertain how quickly a transition to space business is possible given the influence of existing defense contracts.

## Summary

While defense contractors' diversification into space business is a long-term trend, the near-term weakness in stock prices reflects high valuation multiples and delayed profit margin improvement despite revenue growth. Investment during industry transformation is a combination of technological capability, capital, and execution ability; volatility is likely to persist until the harmonization of these three elements translates back into investor confidence.
