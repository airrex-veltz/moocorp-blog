## GLP-1 Price Collapse Scenario: Market-Dominating Drug's Success Strangles Itself

Ozempic's explosive success paradoxically triggers obesity market-wide price collapse within two years. Market share concentration, generic/biosimilar fast entry, and payer pressure compound to slash profitability 50%+.

## GLP-1 Winners and Losers, Time-Gap Between

Oct 9, 2026: **LLY** $1,179.27, YoY revenue growth 47.7%. **NVO** $38.64, revenue growth 2.1%. **PFE** $28.28, growth 2.6%.

LLY's high growth reflects Ozempic's (semaglutide) dominance. But this success inevitably triggers next-stage competition. Rivaling pharmas invest heavily in competing products; patent expiration timelines accelerate generic entry.

## Why Drug Prices Fall Now: Four Price War Signals

**1. Accelerating Competitor Launch**: Ozempic success prioritized GLP-1 receptor agonist development company-wide. NVO competitors, LLY's Mounjaro (tirzepatide), new entrants already completed market launch. First-mover price premium evaporates rapidly.

**2. Shortened Generic Timing**: Obesity demand exploded globally; composition and use-patent invalidity suits now carry economic value. Generics bypass patent expiration, making earlier market incursion common. Insurance negotiations already signal entry.

**3. Biosimilar Competition Heating**: GLP-1 drugs aren't large-molecule biologics but manufacturing difficulty remains high, enabling similar-quality biosimilar development. Approval cost exceeds generics but market size justifies faster entry.

**4. Payer and Government Price Pressure**: Obesity became chronic disease coverage topic. Ozempic profit visibility triggers payer demands for price cuts. Uninsured markets price much lower vs. developed markets—pricing compression locks in globally.

## Why the Three Firms Diverge: Structural Reassessment

**LLY's Dilemma—Growth Then Price Reckoning**: PER 39.6 is very high. Market expects LLY's 47.7% revenue growth to sustain. Price collapse scenarios slash new product margins sharply. LLY stock faces largest negative revisions.

**NVO's Trap—Innovation and Stagnation Boundary**: PER 9.4 trails pharma peers significantly. Revenue growth 2.1% signals Ozempic dominance paradox. NVO's stock is already cut in half from Ozempic's success. Downside room is tighter. Yet price pressure cuts deeper into drug margins.

**PFE's Peripheral Position—Multi-Crisis Impact**: Net margin 6.8% (lowest of three). PFE's obesity position is weak vs. rivals. Pharma's profit base thins most. Revenue growth 2.6% flags failed growth investment credibility.

## Summary

Obesity drug price war begins now; acceleration ramps within two years. LLY faces high-expectation risk, NVO already-absorbed losses, PFE margin squeeze hardest. 50% price collapse is backed by insurance data and generic entry schedules, not speculation.
