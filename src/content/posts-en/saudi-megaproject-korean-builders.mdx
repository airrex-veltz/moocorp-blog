## Introduction

Saudi Arabia's Vision 2030 megaproject acceleration is forming structures that will drive Korean construction and engineering firms' long-term contracts and international revenue expansion over the next 5–10 years.

NEOM (new city), Riyadh urban development, and the Red Sea Project (tourism zone) have all entered active construction phases. These are long-lifecycle projects spanning design, construction, and operation over decades. Korean major construction and engineering firms are entering as main contractors and subcontractors, expanding from simple civil engineering contracts to integrated project execution encompassing design, supervision, and operations.

## Global Infrastructure Market Growth Signal

Expanding global infrastructure markets support these opportunities. GE posted 15.9% annual revenue growth, closing 285.64 USD (2026-10-23), signaling sustained strong demand in energy and equipment supply. Bank of America trades 50.56 USD (2026-10-23) with 28.1% net margin, revealing strong profitability in large-scale project financing. This signals that capital-intensive ventures like Middle East megaprojects attract global financial ecosystem attention.

## Four Drivers Propelling Korean Builders

**1. Multi-year contract stability**: NEOM Phase 1 alone spans 3–5 years forward. Project duration ensures stable cash flow and revenue base independent of domestic real estate cycles.

**2. Technology premium realization**: Ultra-high-rise construction, smart cities, offshore building demand advanced technology. Korean builders' cost competitiveness combined with technical prowess provides advantage over US, European, and Chinese competitors.

**3. Expanding international revenue share**: Despite weak domestic real estate, Middle East project wins can drive revenue growth. Corporate earnings gain geographic diversification.

**4. Engineering and design subsidiary monetization**: Beyond construction execution, design, supervision, and operations participation diversifies revenue recognition paths. Multiple affiliates participate in single projects; overall profit margins expand.

## Structural Strengths and Weaknesses

**Strengths**: Korean builders possess global project execution experience and cost management capability. Track records completing large complex facilities within budget and schedule build trust with Middle East project developers.

**Weaknesses**: Rising global competition is concerning. US, European, and Chinese builders also enter Saudi projects; large rivals partnered with international financial institutions possess superior financing capability. Geopolitical variables—Middle East political instability, expanding sanctions—risk delaying project schedules.

## Summary

Saudi Vision 2030's full ramp-up presents Korean builders with multi-decade monetization opportunity. For investors prioritizing mid-to-long-term stability over short-term earnings, it signals positive direction. Yet rigorous risk management for environmental changes and political hazards during construction is imperative.
