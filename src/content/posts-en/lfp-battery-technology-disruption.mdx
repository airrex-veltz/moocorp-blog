## Low-cost battery technology is eroding the value of premium cells

LFP (lithium iron phosphate) battery technology maturation and expanding cost advantage are reshaping the EV industry. As premium chemistry cell (NCA·NCM) premiums collapse rapidly, global automakers are responding by diversifying their technology portfolios. Having passed the inflection point of battery technology standardization, the automotive industry's profit structure is fundamentally shifting.

## LFP Economics and Safety: The Arrival of Low-Cost Standardization

**BYD** (closing price USD 72.97 on 2026-09-18, reference date 2026-09-18) maintains overwhelming cost competitiveness in LFP cell production. Its net profit margin of 44.3% demonstrates the power of standardized low-cost production systems. However, a 0.0% revenue growth rate may signal market saturation.

Meanwhile, **Rivian (RIVN)** is sticking to premium NCA technology-based vehicles. At a closing price of USD 14.99 on 2026-09-18, it shows 27.2% revenue growth, but its net loss margin of -54.9% reflects high technology transition costs and lack of economies of scale. As pressure to switch to low-cost LFP cells increases, the cost of maintaining technology identity grows heavier.

## Why Battery Technology Is Bifurcating Now: Four Factors

**1. Cost Curve Inversion**: As LFP production technology entered large-scale automation, costs surpassed NCA·NCM. BYD's 44.3% net profit margin represents the economic realization of this cost advantage. In same-performance price competition, premium technology can no longer be justified.

**2. Building Trust in Safety Improvements**: LFP has lower hydrogen venting and explosion risk than NCA·NCM. As long-term driving and module recycling data accumulate, consumer skepticism toward the psychological premium (long range·fast charging) grows.

**3. Changes in Government Subsidy Policy**: Countries are relaxing battery energy density requirements in decarbonization standards. The "regulatory compliance necessity" that was the strongest sales argument for expensive premium technology is fading.

**4. Counter-Growth in Mid-to-Low-Cost EV Market**: While Rivian shows 27.2% revenue growth, it remains in the high-end segment. Meanwhile, demand for LFP-based vehicles in the USD 15-20 million range is expanding to developed countries, pressuring the premium segment's growth rate.

## Why Premium Technology Doesn't Disappear: Structural Strengths and Weaknesses

**Strengths**: In ultra-premium segments (luxury cars·sports cars), NCA technology's energy density and fast charging remain required. **XP** (closing price USD 20.01 on 2026-09-18, P/E 10.2, net profit margin 28.5%), such as finance- and valuation-based investors, see this segmentation as opportunity. Technology bifurcation itself becomes a market opportunity.

**Weaknesses**: With a net loss margin of -54.9%, Rivian has no room for technology transition. As the shift toward low-cost LFP sourcing becomes inevitable, the differentiation of its technology fades. Until achieving economies of scale, maintaining both technologies simultaneously is unsustainable.

## Conclusion

Battery technology does not converge to a single optimum. LFP's low-cost standardization has secured absolute dominance in basic mobility (B·C segments), while NCA·NCM remain only in ultra-premium and extreme-performance segments. Automakers have entered an era where they must pursue both technology paths simultaneously.
