## Intensifying Competition in the AI Chip Market

**Analysis Date**: 2026-10-30 | **Tickers**: NVDA, AMD, INTC | **Sector**: Semiconductor, AI

---

## Nvidia's Unshakeable Wall, Yet Cracks Are Forming

As the generative AI boom shows no signs of cooling, the semiconductor market landscape is being shaken. Competitors challenging **NVIDIA**'s GPU throne are one by one revealing their capabilities, and enterprise customers are showing signs of no longer wanting to depend on a single supplier. Within a new competitive structure pursuing both price and performance simultaneously, semiconductor companies' fortunes are being determined.

## Market Leader's Financial Performance vs. Challengers' Growth Rates

As of the October 28, 2026 closing price, **NVDA** stood at $215.38 with a market cap of $5.14 trillion, a YoY revenue growth rate of 105.9%, and a net profit margin of 63.7%. Nvidia's profit margin overwhelms the industry average, and its two-year revenue growth remains in the triple-digit range. Meanwhile, challenger **AMD** closed at $578.44 on the same day with a market cap of $92.3 billion, a revenue growth rate of 50.1%, and a net profit margin of 15.6%. **INTC** showed a closing price of $95.53 and a market cap of $48.28 billion with a revenue growth rate of 25.4%, but its net profit margin of -19.8% remains in the red.

## Why Semiconductor Competition Is Intensifying Now: Four Factors

**1. Gap Between Data Center Demand and Supply Shortage**: Although Nvidia is increasing supply, the AI chip demand from large cloud companies like Meta, Microsoft, and Amazon still outpaces supply. This provides competitors with an opportunity to enter the market. Data center customers realize that "someone must" buy Nvidia chips, but they have begun wanting diversification across their entire infrastructure.

**2. Competitiveness of AMD's EPYC and MI Series**: Building on its experience surpassing Intel in the server CPU market, AMD has constructed an aggressive product portfolio in the GPU domain. High-performance accelerators like the MI300X demonstrate cost-effectiveness for specific workloads, drawing price-sensitive enterprise customers.

**3. Intel's Attempted Comeback**: Intel has pursued the GPU business for over 20 years without success. However, it recently has been rebuilding its position through the expansion of foundry operations and renewed GPU investment, allowing it to simultaneously control semiconductor production and design. Although currently in a loss position, a lead in process technology could become a long-term asset.

**4. Diversification of AI Models and Open Source Proliferation**: As Meta's open models like Llama proliferate, AI training and inference have become possible outside the CUDA ecosystem standardized by Nvidia. This gives competitors like AMD and Intel a more substantial opportunity to close the technology gap compared to before.

## Why Nvidia Still Dominates: Intersection of Structural Strengths and Weaknesses

**Nvidia's Staying Power**: The depth of the CUDA ecosystem is not merely hardware but an accumulation of software and algorithms. A 63.7% net profit margin demonstrates the ability to convert this depth into price. From the customer perspective, because "switching costs" are highest, replacing Nvidia is not easy even when competitors offer equivalent performance.

**Competitors' Gaps**: AMD is expanding market share with 50.1% revenue growth, but a PER of 144.2 suggests it is trading at a very high valuation relative to current earnings. If results fall short of expectations, there is a risk of sharp declines. Intel remains in net loss, and the path to profitability recovery is unclear in an environment where semiconductor price competition is intensifying.

**Beginning of Price Pressure**: As Nvidia increases supply, average selling prices (ASP) may gradually decline. This could erode net profit margins, and at that moment competitors' relative positions will improve.

## Summary

The AI chip market is evolving from Nvidia monopoly to a "multi-layer competitive structure." Enterprise customers already sensing the risks of single-supplier dependence are preparing multi-sourcing strategies, and AMD and Intel are each seeking their competitiveness between technology and cost. Nvidia's dominance is likely to persist for some time, but 2027 onward is expected to bring much greater market elasticity than today.
