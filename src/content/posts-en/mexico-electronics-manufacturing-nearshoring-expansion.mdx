## Mexico: The Biggest Beneficiary of China Avoidance

US-Mexico proximity and USMCA (US-Mexico-Canada Agreement) tariff advantages are driving electronics manufacturing to Mexico. As semiconductor and electronic components firms reduce China dependency, North American supply chains are undergoing fundamental restructuring.

## Nearshoring Within North America

**Intel (INTC)** is expanding its chip manufacturing capacity in Mexico following investments in Arizona and New Mexico. At a closing price of $122.60 on 2026-09-23, while net profit margin stands at -19.8%, revenue growth is tracking at 25.4%. The impact of manufacturing infrastructure investments is beginning to show in financial statements.

**Broadcom (AVGO)** is dispersing communications and data center chip production across Mexico and Costa Rica, achieving 85.5% revenue growth at a closing price of $354.99 on 2026-09-23. High-margin product demand growth and supply chain diversification are occurring simultaneously.

## Why Mexico Now: Four Reasons

**1. Geopolitical Proximity**: Mexico is only days away by road and rail from the US. Compared to China-dependent supply chains, it dramatically reduces clearance, transportation costs, and time. This difference has been reassessed as a strategic advantage following the chip shortage.

**2. USMCA Tariff Benefits**: Preferential tariff treatment applies when the North American content threshold for raw materials is met. This represents a structural advantage distinct from the high tariffs on Chinese imports.

**3. China Avoidance**: Circuit designers and packaging firms are accelerating Mexico investments amid US tech restrictions and supply chain disruption concerns. **Qualcomm (QCOM)** keeps design in the US and Taiwan but is moving final assembly to Mexico and Malaysia (closing price of $197.24 on 2026-09-23).

**4. Cost Competitiveness**: Labor costs are one-third the US level while maintaining developed infrastructure and logistics. **Flex (FLEX)**, an EMS (Electronics Manufacturing Services) provider, is increasing Mexico factory utilization while maintaining 20.6% revenue growth (closing price of $112.30 on 2026-09-23).

## Structural Challenges Remain

**Analog Devices (ADI)** achieved 39.6% revenue growth in analog and sensor chips (closing price of $385.23 on 2026-09-23), but advanced process chips for AI and high-performance computing still show high dependence on Taiwan and Japan. Upgrading Mexico manufacturing capacity requires technology transfer and time.

Even impressive growth rates are primarily driven by manufacturing cost reduction, which can translate to price pressure in intensifying competition.

## Summary

US-Mexico proximity and the tariff framework are accelerating supply chain localization, which is improving cost structures for North American semiconductor and electronics firms. However, achieving technology upgrades and completely replacing China's supply chain will likely require considerable time.
