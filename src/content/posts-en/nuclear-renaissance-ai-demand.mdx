## AI Data Centers Reassess Nuclear Power

Global tech giants are turning to nuclear power to supply the massive electricity needed for large-scale artificial intelligence training and inference. As the gap widens between carbon neutrality goals and actual power supply demands, regulators are accelerating approval of small modular reactors (SMRs). Companies focused on uranium and nuclear fuel face a historic growth opportunity, yet paradoxically, their stock prices have shown weakness in the short term.

## Shifting Ground for Nuclear: Data Center Power Demand Reshapes the Industry

As of 2026, AI data center power consumption has become the fastest-growing energy demand competing in the global power grid. Technology giants require new power supplies on the order of tens of gigawatts annually, and they are turning to nuclear power for baseload electricity that cannot be met by wind and solar alone. This approach differs fundamentally from the 2010s energy transition logic toward renewables. Existing nuclear plants that were once being retired are now being reassessed as a "practical means of decarbonization."

**UEC** (Uranium Energy Corp.) closed at $7.34 as of October 23, 2026, declining 22.49% over one month and 25.93% over three months. Market capitalization stands at $3.6 billion. **CCJ** (Cameco Corporation) closed at $78.61 the same day, down 10.79% over one month and 12.02% over three months, but remains the largest uranium company by market capitalization at $34.2 billion. While net profit margin is 10.2%, revenue declined 7.2% year-over-year. Both companies face apparent supply shortages yet see weak near-term stock performance.

In contrast, **NEE** (NextEra Energy, Inc.) closed at $82.11 on October 23, 2026, up 8.58% over one month. With a market capitalization of $172.2 billion, it is one of the largest U.S. power utilities and maintains an exceptionally high net profit margin of 40.4%. **DUK** (Duke Energy Corporation) closed at $124.97, up 10.37% over one month. As the Southeast's largest power grid operator with a market capitalization of $97.4 billion, PER of 18.8, and solid net profit margin of 16.0%, it maintains strong fundamentals. These companies, already owning existing nuclear plants, are positioned to meet new AI demand with their current assets.

## Why Nuclear Energy Matters Now: Four Pressures and Drivers

**1. Non-negotiable power demands from AI tech giants:** Technology giants like OpenAI, Microsoft, Google, and Meta require power supplies on the order of tens to hundreds of megawatts annually for GPT training and inference infrastructure. This scale is more than double that of typical data centers and cannot be met with backup power or intermittent supply.

**2. Intermittency limitations of renewable energy:** Wind and solar cannot guarantee baseload power. AI computing cannot stop during nights or windless periods. Nuclear power provides stable baseload electricity and is the only low-carbon energy source that can supplement renewables.

**3. Regulatory relaxation for small modular reactors (SMRs):** The U.S. Department of Energy and NRC are accelerating deployment of modular reactors under 300 megawatts instead of traditional large reactors. Tech giants are also exploring installation of SMRs on their own sites. The reduction in regulatory uncertainty itself builds industry confidence.

**4. Long-term power purchase agreements (PPAs):** Microsoft and Google have already signed or are negotiating contracts to purchase power from existing U.S. nuclear plants for 10-20 year periods. This creates certainty around uranium demand and forms the foundation for rising uranium prices.

## The Short-Term Weakness Paradox: Why Supply Shortage Drives Uranium Stocks Down

**Supply shortage from uranium mines is already priced in:** The reason CCJ and UEC have both declined over the past three months is that the supply shortage scenario has been partially reflected in prices, but this has not yet converted to actual purchase orders.

**Advantage of utilities (NEE, DUK) lies in cash flow:** Large power companies that own existing nuclear plants can monetize AI demand immediately. They simply need to sell baseload power. Uranium mining companies, in contrast, must wait 2-3 years for improved profitability.

**Selective supply recontract:** If long-term shortage is certain, uranium mines could now sign long-term contracts and cash in on improved economics. Otherwise, they remain exposed only to spot market price volatility.

## Summary: Structural Strength vs. Mixed Signals

Long-term supply-demand conditions for nuclear energy are improving, but short-term capital allocation has concentrated on mega-cap utilities like NEE and DUK that show visible profits. Uranium mining companies like UEC and CCJ are beneficiaries of a boom yet also carry the pricing risk of a middle tier. For AI data center power demand to truly translate into uranium supply shortage, it must be backed by long-term contract announcements from tech companies and actual production start-ups.
