## Data Center Power Trading Structural Shift: Overheated PPA Market Drives Rising Electricity Costs

As explosive growth in cloud computing dramatically increases data center power demand, tech companies have attempted to control costs through long-term renewable energy power purchase agreements (PPAs), but the market has become overheated and contract terms are rapidly deteriorating.

## Why Has PPA Become Essential?

Long-term power purchase agreements (PPAs) are contracts of ten years or more between renewable energy developers and tech companies. In exchange for securing power at a fixed long-term rate or discounted price, companies bear the capital costs of power plant construction or guarantee long-term purchases.

For companies, these contracts offer three key benefits. First, they reduce the risk of power outages. Second, they satisfy ESG requirements and carbon-neutral commitments. Third, long-term fixed pricing enables operational predictability. Consequently, PPA demand, which began with large-scale enterprises, has now expanded to mid-sized companies and startups.

## Why Contract Structures Are Breaking Down Now: Four Pressure Factors

**1. Deteriorating Profitability From Excessive Competition Among Renewable Energy Developers**

As investment pours into the renewable energy sector, the number of developers has surged. In the past, building a power plant made it easy to find long-term purchasers, but now developers compete with each other to win orders from tech companies. While initial contract prices have declined, developers' profitability has worsened. To compensate, they have begun extending contract periods or demanding greater capital participation from companies.

**2. Explosive AI Demand Creates Acute Power Supply Shortages**

With surging demand from AI, cloud computing, and machine learning workloads, data center power demand has overwhelmed regional supply. Multiple companies compete in bidding wars for power plants in high-demand regions. Ultimately, only companies offering the most favorable terms secure contracts, while others must accept remote power sources or unfavorable conditions.

**3. Weakened Bargaining Power From Traditional Energy Companies' Market Entry**

Existing energy companies have entered the renewable energy business en masse. They possess advantages in regulatory experience, transmission infrastructure, and capital. The pool of partner options available to tech companies has shrunk, and the bargaining power of smaller enterprises has weakened further.

**4. Premium Costs From Strengthened ESG and Carbon-Neutral Regulations**

Government mandates for higher renewable energy percentages, carbon taxes, and strict permitting procedures add regulatory costs to PPA contract prices. Developers pass these increased costs through to contract pricing.

## Corporate Destinies: Scale and Foresight Determine Outcomes

**Vulnerability: Weakened Position for Small and Emerging Tech Companies**

Smaller companies lose out in PPA bidding. The capacity already contracted by larger companies intensifies supply competition, leaving fewer attractive contract options. Instead of long-term fixed rates, small companies must accept variable pricing or commit to longer periods, losing flexibility to adapt to technological change once locked into a contract.

**Strength: Large Companies' Strategic Advantage Through Early Contracting and Self-Sufficiency**

Large corporations that have already secured many PPAs enjoy advantages in acquiring additional capacity. They raise energy self-sufficiency through constructing their own power plants, building microgrids, and deploying battery storage technology, and they sometimes secure preferential contracts through regulatory lobbying and local government cooperation.

## Summary

PPA has become a necessity rather than a choice, but the market has lost its initial openness. Supply shortages, intensifying competition, and rising costs create a vicious cycle that continuously increases the power share of data center operating costs. Large enterprises endure through long-term contracts, but others face margin pressure. Without breakthroughs in power technology innovation and regulatory improvement, barriers to entry will only rise.
