## Chrome Third-Party Cookie Elimination Undermines Ad Industry Targeting Foundation

As Google Chrome confirms its timeline for complete third-party cookie elimination, the ad industry's 30-year targeting infrastructure is shaken to its foundation. Platform companies will rely exclusively on proprietary data, while independent advertisers and small publishers lose nearly all ability to track user identity.

## Third-Party Cookies and How the Ad Ecosystem Works

Third-party cookies were the key tool for tracking user behavior in the ad industry. When a user visited publisher A and then publishers B and C, cookies identified them as the same individual, enabling advertisers to execute cross-site targeting. This mechanism allowed advertisers to build interest-based audiences and improve ad efficiency.

Chrome commands an overwhelming majority of the global web browser market, and its mobile version comes preinstalled on Android devices. When Chrome blocks cookies, the ad ecosystem rapidly loses this tracking capability. The market began phased elimination in 2024, and the industry expects complete elimination by late 2026 or early 2027.

## Why Ad Platforms and CRM Companies Are Moving Now: Four Factors

**1. Platform Data Monopoly Strengthens**

**Google** (**GOOGL**) and **Meta** (**META**) are in the most favorable position from third-party cookie elimination. Both companies own large-scale platforms (Gmail, YouTube, Facebook, Instagram) where users operate while logged in. Without third-party cookies, their in-platform user behavior data remains nearly infinite.

**2. Industry-Wide Collapse in Cross-Site Targeting Capability**

Participants in the ad ecosystem beyond Google and Meta—digital publishers, ad networks, independent advertisers—depended on third-party cookies. When cookies are eliminated, targeting becomes possible only within a single publisher. Ad tech companies whose original purpose was cross-site tracking lose their foundation for existence. **Meta**'s year-over-year revenue growth rate stands at 28.0% (as of 2026-10-16), but this already reflects adjustments using proprietary platform data and AI models.

**3. Shrinking Target Selection for Small Advertisers**

When cross-site tracking becomes impossible, advertisers must concentrate on platform-native networks like Google Search Ads and Meta feed ads. This means revenue concentration for **Google** and **Meta**. Simultaneously, independent advertisers lose nearly all means to reach third-party publishers.

**4. Surging Demand for CRM Solutions to Integrate First-Party Data**

In a world without cookies, advertisers must transition to first-party data (proprietary CRM and registered customer information). **Salesforce** (**CRM**), a leader in enterprise CRM platforms, is strengthening customer data integration and ad targeting linkage capabilities. Advertisers' attempts to move their CRM data to ad platforms fuel CRM companies' service expansion. **Salesforce**'s year-over-year revenue growth rate stands at 10.8% (as of 2026-10-16), below general B2B SaaS market growth (15-20%), but demand increases in the data connectivity space are visible.

## A Still-Risky Structure: Platform Concentration and Weakening of Independent Players

**Structural Problem of Monopoly Deepening**: **Google**'s PER of 17.1 (as of 2026-10-16) and **Meta**'s PER of 26.7 show that the market already reflects these two companies' ad market dominance in valuation. Third-party cookie elimination is expected to further strengthen this dominance, which will narrow competition in the ad ecosystem and lead to regulatory controversy.

**Limited Position for CRM Companies**: **Salesforce** can strengthen its "intermediary" role in the ad targeting ecosystem, but final ad delivery still occurs only on **Google** and **Meta** platforms. Therefore, CRM companies' revenue growth depends significantly on the goodwill of ad platforms.

## Conclusion

Third-party cookie elimination significantly shrinks ad industry targeting foundations. Companies owning proprietary platform data like **Google** and **Meta** benefit, while small advertisers and independent publishers rapidly lose targeting capability. Advertisers are forced to depend on first-party data based on CRM, increasing demand for solutions like **Salesforce**, but final ad execution concentrates on **Google** and **Meta**.
