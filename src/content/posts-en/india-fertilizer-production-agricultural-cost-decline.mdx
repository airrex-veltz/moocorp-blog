## India's Fertilizer Production Expansion and Improved Profitability in Global Agricultural Companies

As the Indian government accelerates its fertilizer industry development, structural changes in the global agricultural market are accelerating. Expanding domestic fertilizer production capacity is not just affecting the Indian market—it is reshaping the global fertilizer supply structure, creating a positive cycle that leads to **reduced input costs and improved profitability for agricultural companies**.

### Expansion of India's Fertilizer Supply Capacity

Fertilizer is a critical input for all agricultural production, including grains, cocoa, and palm oil. India's fertilizer industry development policy aims to improve domestic agricultural productivity, but its spillover effects extend to global markets. As India expands domestic fertilizer production, **global fertilizer supply increases**, inevitably creating downward pressure on international fertilizer prices.

Declining fertilizer prices mean reduced raw material costs for agricultural companies. The nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium fertilizers consumed by grain producers, the nutrient management of cocoa plantations, and the yield maintenance of palm oil farms—all depend on fertilizer costs. **The lower fertilizer prices fall, the more substantially agricultural production companies' actual profitability improves**.

### Fertilizer Company Price Pressure vs. Agricultural Company Opportunities

Based on data as of September 23, 2026, this structural change is already reflected in the market. **Fertilizer companies are facing price pressure.** Mosaic (MOS), for example, saw revenue decline **-6.0%** year-over-year, while net margin worsened to **-5.2%**. This signals that global fertilizer supply increase and price decline are already underway.

In contrast, **agricultural companies are entering an opportunity phase.**

**Bunge (BG)** saw revenue surge **+88.3%** year-over-year. As an agricultural collection, processing, and distribution company, reduced fertilizer costs translate directly into improved profitability. However, a net margin of just **1.1%** suggests high commodity volatility, but the absolute value of revenue growth clearly demonstrates the effect of reduced input costs.

**ADM (Archer-Daniels-Midland, ADM)**, a global grain-focused agricultural company, recorded revenue growth of **+7.2%** and net margin of **2.2%**. As a direct beneficiary of declining fertilizer prices, it continues steady growth.

**CF Industries (CF)**, a fertilizer manufacturer, recorded a **+18.69%** stock price increase over three months (reference price 120.64 USD). This suggests the market believes the fertilizer price decline is temporary, or that global fertilizer demand remains sufficiently robust despite India's expanded supply. More notably, CF's **net margin of 27.1%** indicates that profitability among leading fertilizer industry companies remains healthy. Additionally, **PER of 8.9** indicates an undervalued state, suggesting recovery potential in a fertilizer supply stabilization scenario.

### Profitability Improvement Mechanism in Agricultural Companies

The pathway through which India's expanded fertilizer production improves global agricultural companies' profitability is as follows:

**First, direct cost reduction.** Grain, cocoa, and palm oil production companies can lower their fertilizer purchase prices. In particular, large agricultural companies can capture greater gains from price declines through long-term contracts or futures trading.

**Second, accelerated profitability improvement.** As Bunge's revenue growth rate (**+88.3%**) demonstrates, increased sales volume becomes possible on a lower cost base. As agricultural competitiveness increases, expansion of market share in global export markets is also expected.

**Third, stabilization of long-term profitability.** As fertilizer price volatility declines, agricultural companies' earnings predictability improves. This translates into rising investor confidence.

### Investment Perspective Assessment

At present, **global fertilizer supply expansion is a structural tailwind for agricultural companies**. Companies like Bunge and ADM are recording revenue growth, and BG's three-month stock price increase (**+1.19%**) also reflects relatively stable earnings expectations.

While fertilizer companies (particularly MOS) face price pressure, CF Industries maintains **high net margin (**27.1%**) and undervaluation (**PER 8.9**), positioning it with recovery potential when supply normalizes. If India's fertilizer supply expansion passes the worst oversupply phase and prices stabilize, investment opportunities may emerge for both profitable fertilizer companies like CF and benefiting agricultural companies.

Ultimately, this market structure represents an **agricultural company profitability improvement cycle**, which becomes an important foundation for establishing medium-term investment strategy.
