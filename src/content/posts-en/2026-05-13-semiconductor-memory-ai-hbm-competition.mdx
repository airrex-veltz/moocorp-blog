The generative AI boom is reshaping the semiconductor ecosystem. As Nvidia's H100/B100 shipment volume surges, high-bandwidth memory (HBM) demand has overwhelmed supply, intensifying technological competition between SK Hynix and Samsung Electronics. Meanwhile, TSMC is solidifying its dominance in the 2nm process, while Samsung Electronics' foundry division faces challenges in securing external customers.

## Sector Overview

The semiconductor sector is maintaining strong momentum in the first half of 2026 driven by expanded AI infrastructure investment. In the US market, Nvidia, TSMC, Broadcom, and ASML lead by market capitalization, while Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix dominate memory markets in Korea. Nvidia's data center GPU revenue accounts for 88% of total revenue, while TSMC's 3nm/5nm process utilization is at maximum capacity.

## Key Trends

Demand for high-bandwidth memory (HBM) for AI accelerators far exceeds supply, allowing SK Hynix to establish market leadership. As Nvidia's H100/H200/B100 shipment volume surges, SK Hynix has secured over 50% of the HBM market, while Samsung Electronics, delayed in HBM3E mass production, aims to pass Nvidia validation tests in the second half of 2026.

The memory pricing cycle has also turned upward. DRAM contract prices have risen 15–20% versus Q1, and NAND prices have recovered as DDR5 transition accelerates and data center SSD demand rises. Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix are expected to raise second-quarter guidance following Q1 earnings improvements.

In the foundry market, TSMC has established dominance in 2nm process technology and locked in major customers including Apple, Nvidia, and AMD. Meanwhile, Samsung Electronics is improving yields on its 2nm GAA process but faces challenges in securing external customers, relying instead on internal volumes.

## Catalysts and Risks

<Callout type="catalyst" title="Near-Term Catalysts">
Nvidia's Q1 FY26 earnings announcement on May 22 (consensus revenue USD 26 billion) and AMD, Intel, and Nvidia's new product roadmaps unveiled at Computex 2026 on June 2–5. SK Hynix's HBM4 sample shipment is also expected in mid-June.
</Callout>

<Callout type="risk">
Valuation concerns are mounting. Nvidia trades at FY26 P/E 45x and ASML at 38x, with downside risk if growth slows. Geopolitical risk with China persists, and US-China semiconductor competition is strengthening export restrictions on sub-7nm equipment.
</Callout>

Medium-term risks include sustainability of the AI capital expenditure cycle and weakening legacy memory demand from extended PC/smartphone replacement cycles. Taiwan risk and currency volatility are also key variables.

## Implications

The semiconductor sector is expected to remain strong through 2026 driven by structural growth in AI demand and HBM/high-performance memory supply constraints. Near-term direction will be set by Nvidia earnings and the Computex event.

Key catalysts by company include SK Hynix's HBM leadership benefits, Samsung Electronics' turnaround potential, TSMC's foundry dominance, and ASML's equipment monopoly. However, valuation concerns and geopolitical risk warrant cautious positioning.

Key metrics to watch include Nvidia earnings announcements, weekly DRAM contract price trends, TSMC monthly revenue, and technical support levels for the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index.
