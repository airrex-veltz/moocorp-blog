As the enterprise software market enters maturity, SaaS companies are facing new pressures. With new customer acquisition becoming increasingly difficult, the model of relying on revenue expansion from existing users is showing its limits, leading to a decline in average selling price (ASP).

## SaaS Market Saturation Signals

Recent earnings from **Salesforce (CRM)** and **Adobe (ADBE)** illustrate this well. As of October 2, 2026, CRM closed at $234.69, down 8.50% over one month, with revenue growth of 10.8% year-over-year. ADBE closed at $237.69 on the same date, declining 15.05% over one month, showing steeper losses. Both companies posted positive three-month cumulative returns (CRM +41.93%, ADBE +9.00%), but the sharp decline over the past month indicates the market is questioning near-term profitability.

## Four Drivers of Average Selling Price Decline

**1. Contraction of New Customer Pool**: The large pool of new customers accessible during the initial SaaS boom has largely saturated. Competition for remaining prospective customers is intensifying, driving up customer acquisition costs (CAC). A vicious cycle is deepening in which companies have no choice but to lower adoption pricing to capture market share.

**2. Rapidly Intensifying Competitive Landscape**: The proliferation of cloud platforms and democratization of development tools have significantly lowered barriers to entry for new SaaS companies. With differentiation difficult on features alone, companies are being pushed into price competition. Active pursuit of niche markets is accelerating erosion of market share among incumbents.

**3. Increased Risk of Customer Churn**: Market saturation requires companies to pay greater attention to maintaining customer satisfaction. However, investment cuts driven by price competition slow product improvement. This increases the likelihood of customer churn and creates a vicious cycle requiring more new customer acquisition to offset it.

**4. Deceleration of Upsell and Expansion Revenue**: Early adopters are purchasing additional licenses and premium features at a slower pace than expected. Rising customer price sensitivity and constrained IT budgets in the macro environment reflect this.

## Nonetheless, SaaS's Structural Strengths

SaaS companies' profit structures remain solid. CRM's net profit margin of 22.0% and ADBE's 28.1% (as of October 2, 2026) maintain high levels, and customer lifetime value (LTV) driven by contractual lock-in is among the highest in the industry.

The weakness is concern about valuation reassessment. CRM's P/E ratio of 21.5 signals overvaluation when reflecting slowing growth, and ADBE's P/E of 13.3, while low relative to historical levels, faces heightened downside risk if persistent growth deceleration continues.

## Conclusion

SaaS companies must move away from a new customer acquisition-focused strategy. Maximizing LTV of existing customers, expanding into adjacent markets, and integrating new technologies like generative AI are key. Maintaining profitability amid declining average selling prices will be the challenge ahead.
