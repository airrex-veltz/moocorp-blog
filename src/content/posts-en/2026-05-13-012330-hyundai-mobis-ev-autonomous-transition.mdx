The automotive industry is undergoing a massive transition toward electric vehicles and autonomous driving. In this paradigm shift, Hyundai Mobis—as a key component supplier for Hyundai Motor Group—stands to benefit. With rapidly growing electrification component sales and Level 3-4 autonomous driving technology in place, the company attracts attention as a stock combining growth and dividend appeal.

<KeyPoints>
- Hyundai Motor Group's electrification acceleration drives rising sales of core components including e-Powertrain and inverters
- 2024 electrification component sales of KRW 5 trillion; target 20% share expansion by 2027
- Level 3-4 autonomous driving technology secured; deployed in Genesis GV80 with in-house lidar development
- Stable dividend yield of 4-5% with payout ratio maintained at 30-40%
- Risks include high Hyundai dependence and margin pressure on electrification components
</KeyPoints>

## Company Overview

Hyundai Mobis is a major automotive parts supplier with a market cap of approximately KRW 25 trillion and a core Tier-1 supplier to Hyundai Motor Group. Its main businesses are modular products (chassis, cockpit), after-sales components, and electronic components. Hyundai and Kia account for roughly 80% of sales. In the global market, it competes with Bosch, Continental, and Denso while securing stable cash flow through after-sales parts operations. Over the past 30 days, the stock has maintained steady momentum supported by expectations for electrification component growth and dividend appeal.

## Fundamentals

In 2024, Hyundai Mobis posted revenue of approximately KRW 42 trillion, growing 3% year-over-year. The core growth driver is expanding electrification component sales, which reached roughly KRW 5 trillion (12% of total) in 2024. The company is targeting expansion of this share to 20% by 2027, increasing supply of core components including e-Powertrain, inverters, and battery packs.

Operating margin stands at 7-8%, in line with the automotive parts industry average. Stable cash flow from after-sales parts operations (8-10% operating margin) underpins overall profitability. R&D investment at 6-7% of annual revenue is concentrated on autonomous driving and electrification technology development.

## Catalysts and Risks

<Callout type="catalyst" title="Electrification Component Growth">
Hyundai Motor Group's Georgia EV factory in North America becomes operational in 2025, expanding component supply. The plan targets raising electrification component sales to KRW 8-9 trillion by 2027.
</Callout>

<Callout type="catalyst" title="Autonomous Driving Technology">
Level 3-4 autonomous driving sensors and controllers developed in-house and deployed in the Genesis GV80. In-house lidar and radar module development reduces external dependency and creates a foundation for margin improvement.
</Callout>

<Callout type="risk">
High Hyundai dependence at roughly 80% means direct impact if Hyundai and Kia sales falter. Global Hyundai sales declined 2% in 2024, with particular concern over China market weakness.
</Callout>

<Callout type="risk">
Electrification components face margin pressure—new investment costs are substantial but scale economies remain elusive. Technology gaps versus global players Bosch and ZF, plus vertical integration competition from Chinese rivals Huawei and BYD, present additional headwinds.
</Callout>

## Takeaway

Hyundai Mobis is positioned to benefit from the automotive industry's long-term shift toward electrification and autonomous driving, leveraging its role within Hyundai Motor Group. Electrification component sales are rising rapidly and in-house autonomous driving technology strengthens future competitiveness. A stable 4-5% dividend yield compares favorably to the KOSPI average of 2-3%, making the stock suitable for investors seeking both growth and dividend income.

However, high Hyundai dependence, margin pressure on electrification components, and intensifying global competition warrant caution. Key watch points will be Hyundai Motor Group's EV sales trajectory, North America factory operations, and electrification component margin improvement.
