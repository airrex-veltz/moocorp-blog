## India's Wage Rise Is Reshaping the Global Manufacturing Landscape

India's status as a low-cost manufacturing hub has reached a turning point. As wages rise due to skilled labor shortages, the gap with China narrows, accelerating multinational corporations' supply chain reconfiguration decisions.

## India's Manufacturing: Simultaneous Growth and Cost Pressures

For the past decade, global supply chains were concentrated in China alone. India was "second choice." But the changes of recent years are different. India's government policies to promote manufacturing investment—particularly in semiconductors and electronics—are producing tangible results, and multinational companies are concretely pursuing China+1 strategies to diversify production bases. This has led to increases in India's factory counts and workforce numbers. In that process, shortages of skilled technicians and workers have intensified.

The expansion of manufacturing facilities in India has created labor supply imbalances. Particularly, skilled workers needed for electronics and auto parts production have failed to keep up with demand. As a result, firms face pressure to secure workforce even at higher wages. Simultaneously, occupational shifts among Indian workers—such as exits from manufacturing toward high-growth retail and service sectors—are intensifying labor shortages.

## Why Wages Are Rising Now: Four Structural Factors

**1. China's Preemptive Labor Cost Rise**: China has not been a low-wage country for some time. Labor costs have risen consistently with urbanization and industrial upgrading, and skilled labor rates in major cities are already quite high by developing-country standards. This has accelerated firms' search for alternatives.

**2. Global Supply Chain Hedging Strategy**: Governments in major economies—the United States, Europe, India, and Southeast Asia—are steering manufacturing investment toward domestic or aligned nations through tax and regulatory incentives to reduce China dependence. So-called "nearshoring" and "friendshoring" are no longer mere buzzwords but are materializing in actual capital allocation. India has become a major alternative hub due to geopolitical advantage and demographic scale.

**3. Deepening Indian Government Policy Support**: Investment from the Indian government in manufacturing employment expansion, skills training programs, and special zones is visibly increasing. However, as training infrastructure and supply fail to keep pace with rising demand, market wages are exceeding the government's policy intent.

**4. Localization Pressure**: Indian national and regional governments are intensifying requirements for foreign manufacturers to increase local content ratios. This expands local supply chain and mid-tier technician demand, ultimately driving wage growth.

## Structural Strengths and Vulnerabilities That Remain

**India's Persisting Competitiveness**: Absolute wage levels remain lower than China's, and demographic scale guarantees long-term supply potential. Moreover, relatively abundant English-speaking workforce facilitates multinational integration.

**Structural Vulnerabilities**: Building skilled labor supply takes time. Uncertainties in infrastructure—power and logistics—and governance—regulatory inconsistency across regions and labor relations—remain elevated. If these issues do not improve quickly, wage growth could erode cost advantages.

## Summary

India's manufacturing wage growth is not mere inflation but a critical signal of global supply chain reconfiguration. As the gap with China narrows, corporate calculus shifts. Beyond cost, political risk, time, and quality weigh more heavily. The relative appeal of other candidates—Southeast Asia and Mexico—rises correspondingly.
