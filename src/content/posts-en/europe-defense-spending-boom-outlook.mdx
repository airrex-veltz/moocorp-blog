## NATO Defense Budget Surge and War Prolongation Accelerate Defense Sector Profitability

Russia-Ukraine conflict prolongation, coupled with NATO member countries' defense budgets reaching historic highs, is driving simultaneous improvements in U.S. major defense contractors' earnings and stock price appreciation. This structural demand increase is solidifying into multi-year contracts that will sustain beyond the near term for years ahead.

## Recent Trends in Defense Industry ETF and Key Companies

**ITA** (iShares U.S. Aerospace & Defense ETF) closed at $71.89 as of November 9, 2026, up 4.36% over one month but down 14.28% cumulatively over three months. The six-month range spans $65.79 to $85.43, reflecting considerable volatility. A P/E of 31.8 remains elevated relative to historical averages, indicating the market is pricing in future growth.

**RTX** Corporation trades at $196.76 (as of November 9, 2026) with a one-month gain of 5.80% and a market cap of $265.5 billion, maintaining its status as the industry leader. Notable is its revenue growth of 11.1% year-over-year and net profit margin of 8.8%, demonstrating solid profitability. While the P/E of 32.0 is elevated, both growth and profitability metrics exceed industry averages.

**LMT** (Lockheed Martin) trades at its highest absolute price of $587.62 (as of November 9, 2026), with a one-month gain of 15.31% significantly outpacing RTX. With a market cap of $135.7 billion and P/E of 20.9—lower valuation than RTX—it posts revenue growth of 11.3% and net profit margin of 8.2%, maintaining solid growth.

## Why the Defense Sector Is Entering a Monetization Phase Now: Four Drivers

**1. NATO Defense Budget Surge and Europe's Strategic Rearmament**: Following the Ukraine war, NATO member countries are allocating defense budgets at the largest scale in 30 years. Germany, Poland, and the Baltic states are substantially expanding defense spending as a percentage of GDP, leading to direct orders from U.S. defense contractors and NATO standard weapons adoption. This trend is expected to persist for years until regional defense gaps are closed.

**2. War Prolongation and Sustained Demand**: Contrary to initial expectations, the war has entered a stalemate phase, with uninterrupted demand for consumable military supplies—ammunition, missiles, air defense systems—and equipment replacement. What was once a single-time purchase has shifted to an annual recurring order structure, improving revenue predictability and bolstering investor confidence.

**3. Intensifying Supply Constraints and Margin Improvement**: Shortages in strategic materials—semiconductors, titanium, rare earths—are strengthening defense contractors' bargaining position. Supply risk premiums and pre-agreed price increases on long-term contracts are now possible, driving net profit margins above historical levels. RTX's 8.8% net margin and LMT's 8.2% reflect this.

**4. Surge in Multi-Year Defense Contracts (Multi-Year Development & Production Agreements)**: European defense ministries are locking in 2025-2030 multi-year purchase commitments, fixing defense contractors' future revenues in advance. This raises cash flow certainty in company valuations, explaining why even elevated P/E ratios maintain institutional investor demand.

## Structural Weaknesses and Risks to Watch

**Valuation Limits—Is the Future Already Priced In?**: P/E ratios of 31–32 exceed normal market ranges. Should NATO defense pledges face political reversal or resolution prospects surge unexpectedly, stock prices face downside risk. Notably, ITA's cumulative 14.28% three-month decline suggests some investors are already adjusting.

**Geopolitical Variables and Supply Chain Risk**: War escalation, China-Taiwan-like scenarios, or Middle East tensions could void or modify long-term contracts. Supply constraints are also not permanent, so margin improvement sustainability cannot be guaranteed.

**Intensified Competition from European Players like BAE Systems**: UK BAE Systems, Airbus Defence and Space, and other regional players are benefiting from the same demand surge, intensifying competition for orders with U.S. firms.

## Summary

The defense sector's near-term rally rests on a strong foundation of structural demand growth, yet elevated valuations already embed considerable expectations. RTX's 11.1% revenue growth and LMT's 15.31% one-month gain reflect market optimism, but volatility risks coexist. The outlook hinges significantly on the sustainability of European defense policy and geopolitical variables.
