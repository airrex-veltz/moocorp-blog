## Container Shipping Oversupply: Maersk and CMA CGM's New Capacity Accelerates 2026 Rate Decline

**In the first half of 2026, the container shipping market faces a widening gap between recovering orderbook levels and weakening actual demand.** As major carriers including Maersk and CMA CGM continue deploying new vessels, overlapping channel inventory normalization is expected to sustain rate pressure for the next 2-3 years.

## New Supply Meets Lackluster Demand

Over the past two years, the shipping industry, which posted record-high rates following the post-pandemic surge, significantly increased new vessel orders. Container ship orders from 2023-2025 reached historic highs, with these vessels beginning full deployment this year. Maersk has ordered approximately 80 dual-fuel methanol vessels, while CMA CGM is also pursuing a major fleet renewal program.

Meanwhile, actual cargo demand is falling short of expectations. Global container trade volumes for January-May 2026 grew just 2-3% year-over-year, with lingering concerns about economic slowdown. In particular, weaker Chinese export performance means Asia-Europe route capacity utilization is failing to sustain above 90%.

## Why Rates Are Falling Now: Four Pressure Factors

**1. Simultaneous deployment of large new vessels**: Maersk's GE-class vessels (approximately 23,756 TEU) and CMA CGM's mega-ships are concentrating their deployment in 2026-2027. The replacement of smaller vessels with larger ships signals genuine supply oversupply. Annual new capacity injection of 2-2.5 million TEU enters the market while scrapping removes only about 500,000 TEU annually.

**2. Prolonged channel inventory normalization**: The extensive supply chain disruptions during the pandemic created accumulated safety stock that has not normalized even through mid-2026. Dominant analysis suggests shipping demand is running approximately 15-20% below actual consumption levels as retailers and manufacturers work through excess inventory.

**3. East Asia export weakness persists**: Mid-sized carriers including **ZIM** (Zim Integrated Shipping) are also feeling the impact of weakening Chinese exports. As growth in emerging manufacturing hubs like Vietnam and India remains modest, the "recovery" phase of the historical rate cycle faces prolonged delays.

**4. Intensifying competition among carriers**: Major operators like **MAE** (Maersk), **CMA** (CMA CGM), and **GLEQ** (large capacity carrier including Golar LNG registered vessels) are proactively cutting spot rates to maintain load factors. The Shanghai-Rotterdam route spot rate fell 27% from $2,200/TEU at the start of 2026 to $1,600/TEU in May.

## Shipping Still Exposes Structural Vulnerabilities

**Prolonged capacity oversupply**: Historically, shipping supply-demand imbalances take 3-5 years to resolve. The current oversupply stems from clear structural factors—depressed orderbook demand and insufficient scrapping—making the adjustment period likely to extend further.

**Sharp rise in environmental compliance costs**: 2030 IMO sulfur oxide (SOx) regulations and carbon neutrality targets are expected to raise new vessel construction costs by 30-40%. Yet the current low-rate cycle makes it difficult to pass these costs on to freight rates. Smaller carriers consequently face mounting insolvency risk.

## Summary

**From 2026 through 2028, the container shipping market will likely remain under sustained rate pressure amid structural overcapacity.** While major carriers like Maersk and CMA CGM can defend cash flow through economies of scale and long-term contracts, mid-sized operators like ZIM face elevated margin pressure. Rate weakness appears inevitable until clear demand recovery signals emerge.
