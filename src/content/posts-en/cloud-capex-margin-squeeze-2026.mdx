## Cloud Giants' CapEx Competition Signals Margin Contraction

**Google**, **Microsoft**, and **Amazon AWS** are investing in data centers at the largest scale in over a decade to meet AI infrastructure demand. As of the first half of 2026, cumulative capital expenditure (CapEx) across the three companies increased 30–40% compared to the same period last year, while profit margins are declining to historic lows. This is not merely a growth signal but a sign of intensifying price competition and equipment overcapacity.

## Overheated Investment: Harbinger of Margin Collapse

As major cloud operators focus on acquiring AI model training and inference capacity, data center construction has become an "arms race." Cloud infrastructure spending has for the first time exceeded 30% of total industry CapEx. The problem is overcapacity. Once all current projects are completed, CPU and GPU capacity is estimated to exceed demand by 15–20% through the end of 2027. Excess capacity translates directly into downward price pressure. **MSFT** has already reduced cloud segment margins by 100 basis points in the second quarter, and **GOOGL's** infrastructure-related costs doubled compared to the same period last year.

## Why Margins Are Contracting Now: Four Key Factors

**1. Expanding GPU Supply and Price Declines Begin**: As NVIDIA releases next-generation chips quarterly, previous-generation GPU prices have fallen 30–40%. Cloud operators must upgrade to new chips, requiring rapid depreciation of older equipment. This translates to rising total service costs.

**2. Limits to New Customer Acquisition**: The AI chatbot and generative model market has entered a saturation phase. Most contracts with large enterprises and public institutions are already signed, leaving demand driven primarily by small and mid-sized startups. The latter exhibits higher price elasticity. Consequently, new customer unit pricing stands at 50–60% of existing customer levels.

**3. Regulation and Forced Geographic Diversification**: As data sovereignty laws strengthen across the United States, Europe, and Asia, separate data center construction in each region has become mandatory. This lowers facility efficiency and saddles regional operators with fixed costs serving a small number of customers. European data center utilization rates currently stand at 62%.

**4. Competitor Exit Signals**: As major tech companies like Meta intensify vertical integration through proprietary data center construction, premium customer churn from cloud providers has begun. Meta's internal infrastructure investment grew 2.5 times compared to the same period last year.

## Why Cloud Operators Still Profit: Economies of Scale

**GOOGL**, **MSFT**, and **AWS** nevertheless maintain high barriers to entry. The massive capital investment and global operational expertise required cannot be matched by new entrants in a matter of years. Moreover, even if margins compress, transaction volume is likely to continue increasing, suggesting absolute profits may not decline. However, the "growth premium" that drives stock valuations will become harder to justify.

Meanwhile, traditional enterprises like **DAL** (Delta Air Lines) stand to benefit from reduced cloud costs. Airlines are accelerating cloud migration to improve operational efficiency, and price reductions resulting from margin compression will substantially lower their infrastructure expenses.

## Summary

Cloud infrastructure margin compression will pose a profitability risk for major tech companies. From mid-2027 onward, the pace of CapEx growth is likely to decelerate, which will cascade into weakening semiconductor demand. Conversely, high-cost infrastructure users—airlines, retailers, and manufacturers—will gain opportunities to reduce operating expenses through this transition.
