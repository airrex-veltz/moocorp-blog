## Rediscovery of Energy Security, Revival of Nuclear Construction Cycle

As geopolitical tension and power supply shortfalls converge, the world is committing to new nuclear investment. This is not mere industrial cycle but signal that carbon neutrality and energy independence have become centerpieces of economic policy.

## Background of Nuclear Renaissance: Why Now?

A decade after Fukushima, nuclear power even environmentalists began accepting. Coal must be phased out for carbon reduction, and renewables alone cannot meet baseload electricity supply. Moreover, since Russia-Ukraine war, energy independence has been elevated to national security issue. Summer heat-driven power crises and winter fuel shortages are now recurring annual news.

Against this backdrop, nuclear construction cycle was re-evaluated as economic opportunity. One new nuclear plant construction means 5-10 years of sustained capital expenditure, and in operation phase generates 30-60 years of stable cash flows. Related industries—nuclear fuel supply, construction and engineering, parts manufacturing, operations and maintenance—all benefit.

## Four Prime Drivers of Nuclear Investment Wave

**1. Desperate power supply demand**: Global semiconductor and AI datacenter explosions are steeply raising electricity demand. Regional grids nearly lack spare capacity. Only nuclear plants capable of supplying gigawatts at once offer alternatives. Industry consensus holds this gap cannot be closed by short-term renewable expansion.

**2. Compulsion of climate neutrality policy**: Major countries including EU, U.S., and Japan legally declared 2050 carbon neutrality. Nuclear boasts lowest carbon emission coefficient (approximately 12gCO2/kWh). Solar and wind (each 40-50gCO2/kWh) are higher. Mathematically, neutrality without nuclear is impossible.

**3. Preparation of nuclear fuel supply base**: Uranium mine development and enrichment facility expansion proceed simultaneously. **URA** (Global X Uranium ETF) leads this flow, providing investment access to mineral assets. Nuclear fuel becoming procurable at most competitive cost levels influences project viability judgment.

**4. Innovation in construction and operations models**: Small modular reactors (SMRs), accelerated construction techniques, and modular manufacturing greatly shorten timelines and costs. Projects that took 15 years before can now complete in 7-8 years. This shortens capital recovery periods and significantly improves ROI.

## Yet Structural Risks Also Exist

Nuclear projects are sensitive to regulatory approval uncertainty, construction overruns, and political change. Inadequate nuclear waste handling policies in each country also hamper long-term project viability. In undercapitalized developing countries, high-capital projects are difficult to structure. Short-term, construction phase investment pressures earnings, and operating start may delay cash flow improvement.

## Summary

Nuclear renaissance rediscovers economic rationality amid macroeconomic pressure from climate and energy security. All related entities—nuclear fuel mines, construction companies, utilities, component suppliers—share beneficiary structure. Yet as long-term project with political risk, careful distinction between individual stock market assessment and fundamentals is necessary.
