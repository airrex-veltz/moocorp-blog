## Fed Rate Cuts and the Beginning of the Credit Vicious Cycle

As the Federal Reserve enters a rate-cutting cycle, the consumer credit market is rapidly expanding. Lower borrowing costs stimulate credit card usage and personal loans, but underneath lies a ticking time bomb of accumulated household debt. **SPY** (S&P 500 ETF) recorded a 3-month change of -4.65%, closing at $735.29 on November 6, 2026, reflecting market concerns about economic deterioration. Meanwhile, credit risk in the financial sector is quickly surfacing.

## The Dual Nature of Credit Stimulus

Rate cuts appear favorable to consumers on the surface. As the real cost of credit card purchases and personal loans declines, it immediately drives increased consumption. However, this structure encourages households to rely on credit for savings capacity, becoming a factor in advanced economies' household debt indicators rising to record levels relative to GDP. **Capital One Financial (COF)** closed at $201.87 on November 6, 2026, with a net profit margin of 22.2% and revenue growth of 6.2%, benefiting from increased credit demand in the current cycle. However, this has limitations as a leading indicator in assessing whether loan portfolio risk provisioning is sufficient.

## Why Is Credit Risk Intensifying Now? Four Factors

**1. Limits of the rate-cutting cycle**: If the Fed's rate reductions are limited or cease prematurely, borrowers adapted to a low-rate environment will simultaneously face debt repayment pressure. The moment interest rates show signs of rising again, credit card delinquency rates and personal loan defaults are likely to surge.

**2. Vulnerability of household debt structure**: Credit card debt and personal loans are the fastest-growing items in a low-rate environment. These are unsecured or have low collateral value, making them highly sensitive to rate increases and reacting sharply to income shocks.

**3. Insufficient credit loss provisioning by financial institutions**: The current favorable earnings (COF's P/E of 12.3, net profit margin of 22.2%) are heavily dependent on economic support. If credit deterioration materializes, additional loss provisions could cause earnings to plummet.

**4. Signals of broad economic vulnerability**: **SPY's** P/E of 23.9 remains at elevated levels, and some sectors (such as **LPL**, closing at $2.54 on November 6, 2026, with revenue growth of -9.8% and net profit margin of -2.3%) are already showing profitability deterioration. This suggests structural problems that rate cuts alone cannot resolve.

## The Imbalance Between Strengths and Weaknesses

Financial institutions benefit in the short term from increased credit demand. **COF's** earnings and a 1-month stock price rise of +1.24% demonstrate this. However, this is a typical signal at the economic peak. Additional borrowing at already-high household debt levels can quickly convert to systemic defaults when interest rate or income shocks occur. The ability to absorb shocks that ensures financial stability is gradually diminishing, and if credit deterioration accelerates, there is significant risk that financial institutions' capital adequacy levels will prove insufficient.

## Conclusion

2027 is likely to be an inflection point in the credit cycle. As the economic stimulus effects of rate cuts reach their limits, credit deterioration resulting from accumulated household debt is expected to intensify. Investors should watch for the divergence between the persistence of credit stimulus structures and the realistic limits of household repayment capacity.
