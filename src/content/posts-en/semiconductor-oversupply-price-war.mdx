# Semiconductor Oversupply and Price Pressure: How Long Will It Last?

In 2026, the global semiconductor industry faces oversupply pressure. DRAM and NAND flash prices are declining steadily, squeezing margins at memory chip makers and cascading effects to foundries and chip designers. New fab completions and yield improvements by incumbents add further supply to the market, suggesting the current downturn will persist.

## Supply Outpaces Demand

The cyclical nature of the semiconductor industry triggered massive capital spending by companies following the recovery that began in 2024. Memory semiconductors particularly experienced strong demand driven by AI infrastructure needs and customers rebuilding inventory, but these forces have weakened by 2026. Simultaneously, global leaders like Samsung and SK Hynix are ramping new production lines, and existing fabs are achieving yield improvements at scale, adding further capacity. Supply capacity now significantly exceeds current end-market demand levels.

## Price Pressure and Margin Compression

DRAM average selling price (ASP) and NAND flash prices have shown clear downward trends entering 2026. Micron (MU) saw its share price fall 23.56% over the past three months (as of November 16, 2026), and 18.19% over the past month, reflecting market concern about memory price weakness. Interestingly, Micron's revenue growth rate stands at 379.3% (YoY) with a net profit margin of 63.8%, reflecting recovery from the prior year's trough and a sales mix weighted toward higher-margin customers. However, if the downward price trend continues, significant margin erosion is expected in coming quarters.

Samsung Electronics (SSNLF) faces similar pressures. While posting a net profit margin of 35.8% and revenue growth of 126.8% (YoY), semiconductor price declines are dulling margin improvement momentum.

## Cascading Effects on Foundries and Chip Designers

Memory price weakness spreads across the industry. TSMC (TSM) has already reacted with share price declines of 9.49% over one month and 9.42% over three months, anticipating memory weakness. Customers' cost-cutting efforts appear as reduced foundry demand, raising concerns that current strong orders may shift into 2027. TSMC's PER of 23.1, net profit margin of 51.1%, and revenue growth of 50.9% still indicate sound fundamentals.

Chip designer Qualcomm (QCOM) posted negative revenue growth of 8.4% (YoY), suggesting end-market weakness now reaches the design stage. With a one-month share price decline of 2.09%, it is holding up relatively better, but prolonged semiconductor demand weakness will likely impose additional downward pressure.

## Timeline to Cyclical Recovery

Current oversupply is unlikely to clear quickly. New capacity is continuing to come online and yields keep improving, while end-market recovery remains uncertain. Historically, semiconductor price downturns have lasted 12 to 18 months, and the current cycle is expected to follow a similar pattern. During this period, memory makers' margins will remain compressed, and foundries and chip designers must absorb weaker demand from cost-conscious customers.

A complete demand collapse, however, is unlikely. The underlying demand trajectory for AI data center infrastructure and next-generation mobile devices remains intact, and as prices adjust to sustainable levels, cost-sensitive customers may return to purchasing. Until that moment arrives, the industry must endure the downturn while pursuing cost optimization and a strategic shift toward higher-value products.
