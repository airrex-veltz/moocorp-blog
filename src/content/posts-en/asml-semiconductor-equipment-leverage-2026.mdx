## ASML's Equipment Monopoly and Semiconductor Industry Infrastructure Crisis

As **ASML** has become virtually the sole supplier in the extreme ultraviolet (EUV) lithography equipment market essential to semiconductor manufacturing, this Dutch company has become a bottleneck in the global semiconductor chain. With technological barriers, geopolitical regulations, and non-linear demand dynamics converging, ASML's position strengthens while risk concentrates across the entire industry.

## ASML and the Structure of Semiconductor Equipment

Semiconductor advanced processes require highly precise lithography technology at extreme ultraviolet (EUV) levels and below. ASML is the only company to have commercialized this technology, and it is impossible for leading foundries like Intel, TSMC, and Samsung to pursue sub-7-nanometer processes without ASML equipment. With price tags in the hundreds of billions of won per unit and lead times of 2-3 years, semiconductor manufacturers' capital expenditure decisions are tied to ASML's production schedule.

## Why ASML's Position Is Strengthening: 4 Key Factors

**1. Extreme Technological Barriers**: The core of lithography systems lies in large-scale precision lens design, optical engineering, and extreme ultraviolet light source control. In this field, only ASML globally possesses more than a decade of accumulated experience and thousands of specialized engineers. The structure does not allow new competitors to emerge, so ASML's demand monopoly is structurally entrenched.

**2. Absolute Dependence of Semiconductor Manufacturers**: In the semiconductor industry where falling behind in advanced process competition means losing market share, failing to secure ASML equipment is tantamount to elimination from the race. Manufacturers therefore plan their expansion strategies around ASML's supply schedule. Supply delays immediately translate into global chip shortage risks, giving ASML significant negotiating power.

**3. Intensifying Geopolitical Regulations**: Following U.S. requests, the Dutch government has been controlling ASML's exports of high-performance equipment (such as EUV) to Chinese customers. This politicizes ASML equipment exports as a tool of statecraft and grants advantages to U.S. and allied semiconductor companies in geopolitical competition. Paradoxically, as each country strengthens its chip self-sufficiency, global dependence on ASML increases.

**4. Non-Linear Capital Expenditure Cycles**: The semiconductor industry repeatedly expands and contracts capital expenditures dramatically within business cycles. During a single expansion phase, dozens of ASML units are ordered simultaneously, while during contractions they drop to nearly zero. This sharp demand volatility disrupts ASML's production planning and supply chains but simultaneously prevents other suppliers from entering the market.

## Why It Still Has to Be ASML: Structural Strengths and Weaknesses

**Strengths**: ASML's greatest strength lies in the irreplaceability of its technology and global recognition. No matter how expensive, customers have no choice but to purchase when there are no substitutes. Additionally, after equipment sales, ASML secures long-term revenue streams through maintenance, upgrades, and software updates.

**Weaknesses**: ASML faces regulatory and political pressure as a target precisely because of its monopoly position. As China intensifies efforts toward chip self-sufficiency, movements by countries to reduce ASML dependence also accelerate. Additionally, ASML is sensitive to semiconductor industry cyclical fluctuations, and revenues can plummet when customer orders decline.

## Summary

ASML's semiconductor equipment monopoly is solid in the near term due to technological superiority and barriers to entry, but geopolitical regulations and countries' efforts to move away from ASML are already underway. As it becomes increasingly clear how dependent the entire semiconductor industry is on this one company, responses from governments, businesses, and allied factions will accelerate.
