Global interest rate hikes are directly threatening the economic viability of solar and wind projects. Renewable energy businesses with high initial capital intensity are extremely sensitive to borrowing rates.

## Structural Vulnerabilities in Renewable Energy Project Finance

Renewable energy projects consume most of their investment during the construction phase. This requires a "project finance" structure composed of bond issuances, bank loans, and developer equity. This means that borrowing is the most critical variable determining project profitability.

A typical solar power plant generates fixed returns for 20 to 25 years after completion. This means annual cash flows are locked in by contract. However, when financing costs (interest rates, credit spreads, insurance premiums) rise, those contracted revenues remain unchanged while costs increase.

## Four Pathways of Rising Financing Costs

**1. Global Interest Rate Hikes and Rising Base Borrowing Costs**

Central banks' tightening cycles raise prime rates. Renewable energy projects typically start with floating-rate loans that are then locked in through bond issuance, but rising rates make this process itself more expensive. Developers must raise long-term financing at higher interest rates.

**2. Widening Credit Spreads and Rising Risk Premiums**

As financial uncertainty grows, banks and bond investors demand "excess risk compensation." Particularly for projects in developing countries or regions with high policy risk, spreads spike dramatically. Even identical projects see significantly lower returns purely due to financing conditions.

**3. Policy Uncertainty and Declining Renewable Support**

When government subsidies (solar FITs, wind power purchase agreements) shrink or face uncertainty, project cash flows weaken. When cash flows weaken, financial institutions tighten borrowing conditions. They raise collateral ratios or increase interest rates.

**4. Exchange Rate Volatility and Foreign Currency Borrowing Costs**

As the dollar strengthens, local currency borrowing becomes more expensive. International project finance is predominantly raised in dollars, requiring hedging of the risk of converting local currency revenues to dollars. Rising hedging costs erode business margins.

## Structural Weaknesses and Strengths

**Weaknesses**: The core issue is the mismatch between locked-in long-term contract revenues and rising financing costs. Completed power plants cannot be shut down, and electricity sale prices cannot be raised. Rising financing costs translate directly into net profit erosion.

The higher the debt-to-investment ratio in the initial phase, the more severe this pressure. When developers' equity returns plummet, new project commencements slow.

**Strengths**: Renewable generation costs (solar panel and wind turbine prices) continue to decline. This means the cost per unit of generation itself is improving. Additionally, global energy demand is increasing, and carbon neutrality goals have solidified as policy frameworks.

Over the long term, interest rate hike cycles will end. When financial conditions normalize, borrowing costs will stabilize. The current high-rate environment is likely a temporary phenomenon.

## Conclusion

Rising financing costs directly impact renewable energy projects' structural weaknesses—fixed revenues and high debt dependence. Near-term project commencements may contract. However, the fundamental trends of improving generation technology and rising energy demand remain unchanged. How this sector restructures as financial conditions normalize will be the key point to watch.
