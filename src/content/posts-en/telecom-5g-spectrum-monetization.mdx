## 5G Spectrum Auctions: Tightening Pressure on Telecom Profitability

The U.S. telecom industry is pouring tens of billions of dollars into securing frequency bands. 5G spectrum auctions serve as both a gateway to next-generation communication technology and a signal that the time gap until revenue realization has widened.

## Telecom Current Status: Imbalance Between Spending and Revenue

**VZ** (Verizon) shares closed at $45.87 on September 30, 2026, down 8.30% over one month. **T** (AT&T) also declined 5.76% during the same period. The context for the two companies differs. VZ's revenue growth rate declined 0.7% year-over-year, while T's grew 2.3%. However, looking at VZ's net profit margin of 11.6% and T's 16.9%, the weight of capital expenditure burden from spectrum investments is reflected directly in net profit. With VZ's market capitalization of $19.08 billion and T's $16.70 billion, investment acquisition appears to be a priority over profit generation for both companies.

## Why Are Telecom Share Prices Under Pressure Now: Four Factors

**1. Increasing Trend in Spectrum Auction Costs**: Over the past decade, the U.S. FCC has auctioned multi-layered frequencies including mmWave, C-band, and mid-band. Bidding heated up at each auction, with winning prices soaring. Telecom companies had to secure frequencies at high prices to maintain competitiveness. This represents a short-term cash outflow, and 3 to 5 years are needed until 5G monetization.

**2. Infrastructure Construction Delays and High Costs**: 5G base stations require higher bandwidth and shorter range than 4G. Achieving broad coverage requires increasing base station density, which creates cumulative costs in land leasing, backhaul fiber optics, and network equipment. The time gap between auction and construction start acts as an unfavorable market variable.

**3. Unclear Monetization Pathways**: Early 5G pricing showed little difference from 4G. Enterprise revenue streams such as IoT, edge computing, and network slicing have yet to gain significant scale. With consumers not perceiving a 5G premium, telecom companies struggle to quickly recover their high investments.

**4. Intensifying Competition and Industry Structure**: The U.S. telecom market centers on three major operators (VZ, T, DISH). While there are attempts at diversification into fixed-line, wireless, and streaming, all remain capital-intensive industries. As spectrum investment accumulates, capital structure debt ratios rise, intensifying pressure on dividends.

## Why This Structure Persists: Telecom Companies' Structural Strengths and Weaknesses

**Strengths:**
- **High Barriers to Entry**: Spectrum auctions, infrastructure investment, and regulatory barriers make it nearly impossible for new entrants. VZ and T maintain near-monopolistic market positions. In other words, the long-term environment is set for recovering high investment costs.
- **Technology Advantage Persistence**: The earlier 5G infrastructure is built, the more favorable the position for acquiring high-value locked-in customers.

**Weaknesses:**
- **Capital-Intensive Vicious Cycle**: With spectrum auctions repeating annually, new investment demands emerge before infrastructure completion. Deteriorating capital efficiency lowers shareholder returns.
- **Uncertainty in Profitability Recovery**: If premium services (Standalone 5G, Network Slicing, Private Network) only acquire B2B customers in limited amounts, revenue scenarios relative to investment remain unclear.

## Summary

While 5G spectrum is an essential competitive element, its cost erodes short-term profitability. Both VZ and T must balance this dilemma. Over the next 1 to 2 years, revenue and profit trends will serve as a test case showing whether spectrum investment converts into actual revenue sources.
