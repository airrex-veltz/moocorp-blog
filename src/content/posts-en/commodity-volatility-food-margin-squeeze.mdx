## The Cost Inflation Vicious Cycle Crushing the Food Industry

The global food manufacturing sector is caught in unpredictable commodity price volatility. The simultaneous stock price declines of major food companies over the past month signal not merely market correction, but structural deterioration in profitability.

As of September 29, 2026, General Mills (GIS) fell 18.60%, Kellogg successor KHC declined 7.30%, and Campbell fell 13.51%. Tyson Foods (TSN) fared slightly better at 7.12% decline, but this is relative outperformance. Their net profit margins reveal the severity of the problem.

**Current State of Margin Collapse**

Kellogg (KHC) carries a net profit margin of -13.6%, operating at a loss. General Mills stands at -4.9%, while Tyson Foods barely manages 1.0%. Only Campbell maintains positive profitability at 4.1%, yet its revenue growth rate of -7.9% ranks worst in the industry. This reflects the limits of pricing power.

**Cost-Driven Earnings Deterioration**

Deepening volatility in agricultural and livestock product prices directly threatens food companies' cost structures. When grain, vegetable oil, and feed prices surge, firms cannot respond immediately. Their fixed contracted supply prices limit flexibility, and consumers resist price increases.

General Mills' largest one-month decline of 18.60% is no coincidence. Grain price volatility—critical to cereal manufacturing—hits hardest. KHC's diversified product portfolio (sauces, dairy, snacks) proved unable to absorb cost pressures, manifesting in its -13.6% net margin.

**The Challenge of Margin Recovery**

Escape from this spiral is difficult. All firms show negative revenue growth (KHC -1.4%, GIS -2.8%, CPB -7.9%, TSN -0.1%), confirming that price increases trigger volume declines. Consumers respond to higher prices by reducing purchases or switching to budget brands.

Campbell's 7.9% revenue decline particularly signals that price hikes triggered severe demand destruction. Even in staple categories like canned soups, consumers now have wider alternatives.

**Key Takeaway for Investors**

Unless commodity prices stabilize at current levels, these firms face a lengthy margin recovery. Near-term, restructuring and portfolio optimization offer the only cost-relief avenue. Yet this approach carries its own risk: weakened price competitiveness.

Notably, Tyson Foods maintains a 1.0% net margin despite direct exposure to livestock feed price shocks—a sign it may retain selective pricing power or efficiency upside. KHC's negative profitability underscores the urgent need for portfolio restructuring.

Ultimately, food stock investment during this period demands simultaneous evaluation of climate risk and cost management capability. Companies failing to articulate concrete strategies for escaping the volatility spiral face continued share price pressure.
