## U.S. Office Vacancy Hits Record High as Data Center REITs Surge: The 'Bifurcation' of Real Estate

**Analysis Date**: 2026-09-09 | **Tickers**: PLD, O | **Sector**: Real Estate, REITs

---

## Offices in Decline, Data Centers Booming: Real Estate's 'Bifurcation'

While U.S. office vacancy rates reach historic highs, AI infrastructure data center REITs are tracing the steepest growth trajectory in history. This is an extreme bifurcation unfolding within the same real estate sector.

## Two Front-Line Players in Real Estate

**PLD (Prologis)** is America's largest industrial and data center–focused REIT. **O (Realty Income)** is a diversified REIT spanning retail, office, and industrial assets.

Office vacancy breached roughly 20%—a historic peak—as early as 2024, and as of 2026 continues to rise. Conversely, AI data center supply cannot keep pace with demand, pushing premium rents ever higher. As the supply-demand structures of these two asset classes remain in perfect opposition and this dynamic persists, pressure for selectivity within the REIT sector has intensified to an extreme.

## Why the Bifurcation Now: Four Drivers

**1. Structural entrenchment of remote work**: Hybrid and remote policies sustained for nearly seven years since the pandemic have made it difficult to return office demand to its original trajectory. As companies have permanently reduced office footprints, office space has entered 'structural oversupply.'

**2. Explosive demand for AI and cloud infrastructure**: The proliferation of generative AI, edge computing, and surging cloud services have driven demand for data center power, cooling, and space to roughly 20–30% annual growth. With scarcely any idle supply, rents continue climbing.

**3. Economic unviability of office reuse**: Attempts to convert vacant office buildings into residential or other uses are blocked by high conversion costs, stringent regulations, and weak returns. Demolition incentives are lacking, leaving zombie buildings to accumulate.

**4. Intensifying location premium for data centers**: The variance in location value is extreme depending on power grid capacity, fiber optic access, and cooling water availability. Prime locations command sustained premium rents, which drive data center REIT profitability.

## Strengths and Weaknesses: Why Still REITs

**PLD's strength—data center dominance**: Starting from industrial real estate, it has raised data center assets to over 60% of its portfolio. Debt levels are low, and dividend growth rates hold at 6–7% annually. However, the risk of data center market saturation may increase post-2027.

**O's weakness and diversification**: A broad asset portfolio (retail 40%, office 15%, industrial 25%, etc.) offers the stability of diversified investment, but cannot escape structural weakness from office exposure. Dividend growth rates have fallen to the low 3% annual range.

## Conclusion

This real estate bifurcation stems not from business cycles but from 'structural change.' Data center demand will remain strong for at least five more years, while office recovery is uncertain. The importance of sector selectivity is unprecedented.
