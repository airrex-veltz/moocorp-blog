## The Subsidy Paradox: Supply Chain Independence Breeds Overcapacity

The U.S. CHIPS and IRA Act and the EU Chips Act have unleashed billions of dollars into semiconductor manufacturing, yet global fab utilization rates have fallen below 50%. The goal of supply chain independence has instead created a vicious cycle of inefficiency.

## The Structure of the Subsidy Bubble

Since 2022, the U.S. and Europe have deployed massive government funding toward semiconductor self-sufficiency. **INTC** is investing over $20 billion in new fab construction within the U.S., **ASML**'s EUV equipment demand has surged through European foundry projects, and **TSMC** has demanded advance payments due to supply pressures from its Arizona facility expansion.

Yet subsidy dependence has distorted market signals. Government support replaced profitability as the justification for investment, and capacity expansions proceeded without profitability validation.

## Why Fab Utilization Has Collapsed Today: Four Factors

**1. Simultaneous Overcapacity Entry**: The subsidy race prompted the U.S., Europe, and even Japan to build fabs simultaneously. Market demand remained constant while supply alone increased. The global fab footprint added in 2023-2025 reached record levels, while PC, smartphone, and server chip demand stagnated.

**2. Geopolitical Demand Slowdown**: The artificial intelligence chip boom has centered on fabless designers like NVIDIA, and advanced-node capacity below 5nm remains concentrated at TSMC. What the U.S. and Europe expanded was largely mature-node capacity at 28nm and above—where operational capacity was already sufficient.

**3. Fiscal Deficits and Subsidy Retrenchment Signals**: U.S. federal subsidy scrutiny has tightened, and potential EU fiscal austerity has halted late-stage expansion projects. Even committed funding has faced implementation delays. Unprofitable fabs have sat idle after completion.

**4. Technology Convergence Limits**: INTC's process technology lags TSMC by two to three years, and new European foundries like INTEL's Germany facility operate at roughly 40% utilization due to ramp issues. U.S. and European fabs face 40% higher per-unit production costs than Asian counterparts due to labor, regulation, and energy expenses.

## Why These Names Still Matter: The Cross of Structural Weakness and Strength

**Deepening Vulnerabilities**: **INTC** faces mounting debt from capital overextension and capacity reduction under geopolitical uncertainty. ASML has seen foundry customers defer or cancel long-term supply contracts as their own fab investment slows.

**Structural Advantages Remain**: Yet government subsidies, while creating near-term overcapacity, represent long-term supply-security investment as a cost basis. INTC retains x86 architectural dominance and data-center customer relationships. ASML maintains EUV monopoly status. TSMC commands premium pricing through advanced-node market share.

## Conclusion

Government subsidies reduced supply chain risk but externalized the cost of overcapacity through investment divorced from market discipline. Utilization recovery may take five years or more, during which the gap between competitive leaders like ASML and TSMC versus followers like INTC is likely to widen.
