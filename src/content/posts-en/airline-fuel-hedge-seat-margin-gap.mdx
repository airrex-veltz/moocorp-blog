## Airline Fuel Hedging and Revenue-Per-Seat Margin Gap

Even in the same global crude oil price environment, airline net profit margins diverge sharply due to differences in fuel hedging strategy and seat operational efficiency. **Delta Air Lines (DAL)** and **United Airlines (UAL)** clearly illustrate this.

## Profitability Gap Between The Two Airlines

As of November 5, 2026, **DAL**'s net profit margin is 4.6% and **UAL**'s is 5.5%. A 1.0 percentage point difference is far from negligible at airline scale. Revenue growth rate also lags: **DAL** at 21.1% versus **UAL** at 17.4%. Yet the market views the two companies in opposite ways. **DAL**'s P/E ratio is 17.5 while **UAL**'s is 10.3. Market capitalization shows a similar pattern: **DAL** at $57.6B substantially leads **UAL**'s $36.5B. While **UAL** currently has the higher profit margin, the market values **DAL** more highly for future growth—a clear sign of differing growth expectations.

## Why Profit Margins Differ at the Same Oil Price: Four Factors

**1. Difference in Realized Fuel Hedging Costs**: The amount of fuel an airline has secured in advance affects its realized costs even when both face the same spot crude price on any given date. Airlines with aggressive forward contracts or extensive long-term fixed hedges suffer less from short-term crude spikes but bear counterparty credit risk and opportunity cost. Conservative airlines remain more sensitive to short-term crude swings but maintain flexibility.

**2. Revenue-Per-Seat-Mile (RASM) Operational Efficiency**: Even when paying the same fuel costs, airlines that extract more revenue per seat enjoy higher profit margins. Route network optimization, pricing algorithms, load factors, and premium seat mix determine RASM. If **DAL** and **UAL** structure their hub cities and international route mix differently, seat revenue diverges even at the same crude price.

**3. Recent Stock Volatility Implications**: Over the past three months, **DAL** declined 6.28% while **UAL** fell 14.72%. **UAL**'s steeper drop suggests the market views its hedging strategy or RASM outlook more negatively. Over a six-month range, **UAL** (88.34–138.77 USD) shows greater volatility than **DAL** (67.40–95.18 USD).

**4. The Flip Side of Growth Momentum**: **DAL**'s 21.1% revenue growth outpacing **UAL**'s 17.4% also explains profitability divergence in the same fuel environment. Expanding revenue faster while maintaining margins signals more efficient hedging structure and RASM management than competitors.

## Structural Strengths and Weaknesses

**DAL's Strength**: High growth rate (21.1%) and market confidence (P/E 17.5) signal a structure nimbler in responding to future crude spikes or seat yield improvements.

**UAL's Strength**: Its current net margin advantage (5.5%) provides near-term room for dividends or shareholder returns. However, its low P/E of 10.3 indicates the market does not expect that advantage to persist.

## Summary

Fuel hedging ratios and RASM operational efficiency heavily shape airline net profit margins. The **DAL**–**UAL** gap reflects structural management differences that spot crude alone cannot explain. The market's higher P/E for **DAL** reflects its assessment of competitive hedging and RASM strategy advantage, visible in revenue growth rate and recent volatility.
