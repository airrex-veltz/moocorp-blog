## How Ad-Supported Tiers Reshape the Digital Advertising Market's Bidding Structure

As video streaming erodes television audiences, advertisers can no longer rely solely on traditional media spending. Major streaming platforms' expansion of ad-supported tiers is redefining the advertising market itself into a new competitive landscape.

## Ad-Supported Tiers Become a New Revenue Engine

**Netflix** (**NFLX**) and **Amazon Prime Video** (**AMZN**) initially adhered to subscription-only models, but as subscription growth signals stalled, they accelerated ad-supported tiers. Both companies are experiencing simultaneous expansion of ad-tier subscribers from existing premium tiers and new user acquisition. Netflix maintains a revenue growth rate of 13.4% year-over-year (as of 2026-09-16), while Amazon records 19.6% YoY growth across its entire business. These figures signal that ad-tier adoption is contributing to overall revenue gains.

## Why Ad-Supported Tiers Expand Now: Four Factors

**1. Advertiser budget reallocation pressure**: As traditional TV ad spending declines and online video advertising effectiveness is proven, advertisers accelerate their bid shift toward streaming platforms. The programmatic buying options offered by ad-supported tiers give advertisers precise targeting and measurement capabilities.

**2. Streaming operators' need for revenue diversification**: When subscription fees alone cannot cover original content production costs, advertising revenue becomes a source for content investment. As ad-tier subscribers grow, the pool of active users accessible to advertisers expands, boosting the ad-tier's own appeal.

**3. The pricing resistance paradox**: Consumer resistance to streaming price increases makes ad-supported tiers lower barriers to entry. As lower-priced options broaden the subscriber base, platforms become larger media properties from an advertiser's perspective.

**4. Maturation of programmatic advertising infrastructure**: Real-time bidding (RTB), audience segmentation, and cross-device tracking technologies have become sophisticated enough to enable streaming platforms to act as digital ad exchanges. This simultaneously raises advertiser CPM expectations and platform supply pricing.

## Why Streaming Platforms Still Matter: Structural Strengths and Risks

**Combining first-party content and user data**: Netflix and Amazon are both original content producers and advertising media operators. This guarantees advertisers the ability to run ads within premium content environments and enables precise targeting based on subscriber behavior data.

**The double-edged sword of ad fatigue**: Maximizing ad-tier revenue requires increasing ad density. However, excessive ad exposure can drive churn among budget-conscious subscribers, creating a vicious cycle that shrinks the ad inventory itself.

## Takeaway

Ad-supported tiers serve streaming operators as a buffer to reduce subscription dependency, while for advertisers they provide a premium venue for performance measurement. In the near term, they will be a driver of revenue growth; longer-term, intensifying competitive bidding between platforms for advertiser budgets remains likely.
