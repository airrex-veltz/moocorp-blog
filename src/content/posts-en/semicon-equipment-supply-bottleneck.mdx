## EUV Bottleneck Controls Foundry Expansion

Technological competition in the semiconductor industry ultimately boils down to competition in advanced processes, and advanced processes are tied to lithography equipment. **ASML** is the sole supplier of extreme ultraviolet (EUV) lithography. ASML's supply constraints now determine the speed of advanced process expansion across the entire foundry sector.

## ASML's Market Position and Business Performance

**ASML** is a core infrastructure company for implementing advanced semiconductor processes. It is the only company to commercialize extreme technologies like EUV lithography, and its customers are limited to foundries and memory manufacturers like **TSMC** and Samsung. Based on ASML's closing price of USD 1,641.52 (2026-10-16), the company reported YoY revenue growth of 55.2% in the recent quarter and maintains an extremely healthy financial position with a net profit margin of 34.1%. This reflects strong customer demand for foundry investment. Yet the supply constraint persists.

## Why Supply Is a Bottleneck Now: Four Factors

**1. Extreme Technical Complexity of EUV Equipment**: EUV lithography uses extreme ultraviolet light instead of visible light to draw fine patterns below 13.5 nanometers. A single machine costs tens of billions of won, and takes 5 to 10 years from design through manufacturing, assembly, and validation. Only dozens of units can be produced in a year. ASML cannot overcome this constraint through technical improvement, and increasing production requires long-term investment in factory expansion and supply chain sophistication.

**2. Surging Demand for Advanced Foundry Processes**: **TSMC** recorded YoY revenue growth of 50.9% at a closing price of USD 412.61 as of 2026-10-16. This reflects surging orders for advanced processes (5 nanometers and below) driven by increased AI chip demand. In the same period, TSMC's net profit margin of 51.1% signifies strong pricing power. However, production capacity itself has a ceiling set by ASML's EUV supply. Even if demand reaches 100, if equipment is supplied at only 30 units at a time, production fabs can only be sized at 30 units.

**3. Geopolitical Export Restrictions and Supply Chain Constraints**: ASML is a Dutch company, and EUV technology is subject to export controls by the U.S. and Netherlands. In particular, exports to China are blocked at the source. Furthermore, ASML's supply chain itself is highly concentrated. Few companies supply critical components for semiconductor manufacturing equipment (lasers, mirrors, vacuum systems), and their production capacity is also limited. If some components are delayed, ASML's entire production falls behind.

**4. Concentrated Capital Investment by Foundry Customers**: TSMC and Samsung are investing trillions of won annually in EUV fab expansion. **TSMC**'s net profit margin of 51.1% shows sufficient investment capacity, but this investment pace must also align with ASML's supply pace. As a result, ASML's annual supply volume determines the advanced process expansion schedule across the entire foundry sector. Fab establishment plans are revised, and process transfer schedules are delayed.

## Structural Strengths and Weaknesses

**TSMC's Profitability and Cutting-Edge Process Monopoly**: TSMC's net profit margin of 51.1% stems from high technological barriers. Even with limited EUV supply, existing customers (Apple, NVIDIA, AMD) cannot leave TSMC, and TSMC can prioritize supply volumes to selected customers. This is a near-term strength. However, it also buys time to prevent new competitors from entering, as Samsung and Intel are also investing in EUV fab construction.

**Deteriorating Process Access for Smaller Foundries and Design Houses**: Companies like **SMTC**, a smaller semiconductor firm, find it difficult to access advanced processes (5 nanometers and below). SMTC maintains a high multiple with a PER of 90.5 at a closing price of USD 136.73 as of 2026-10-16, and its YoY revenue growth of 32.7% is significantly lower than TSMC or ASML. With a net profit margin of 13.1%, its investment capacity is also limited. If EUV supply constraints persist, the position of foundries monopolizing advanced process production will strengthen, and the technology gap for smaller players will widen further.

## Summary

ASML's EUV supply constraint is a structural bottleneck mixing technological complexity, extreme manufacturing difficulty, and geopolitical regulation. This controls the pace of foundry expansion and deepens process differentiation in the semiconductor industry. In the near term, supply-demand imbalance will persist, and under this imbalance, only strong customers will maintain competitiveness.
