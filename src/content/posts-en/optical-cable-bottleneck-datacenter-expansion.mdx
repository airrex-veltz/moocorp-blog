## AI Data Centers Have Chips and Power. The Next Bottleneck Is Optical Cable.

For the past two years, NVIDIA GPU shortages and power grid expansion were the main constraints on AI infrastructure growth. Both are now easing. The remaining chokepoint is optical communication cable. As fiber-optic components—from submarine cables to internal data center wiring—face supply crisis, cable manufacturers' profit margins are heading toward historic highs.

## Optical Cable Becomes the Main Act

Fiber-optic cable is the unsung star of AI data center construction. The intra-network connecting thousands of GPUs, submarine cables spanning continents, and interconnects between cloud providers all depend on optical communication.

**Corning (GLW)** is the world's largest supplier of fiber-optic and optical-electronic components. On October 13, 2026, it closed at $148.54, up 3.44% in the past month. This year's revenue growth stands around 16.6%. **Prysmian (PRYMY)**, a submarine cable specialist, closed at $67.82 the same day, with revenue growth of 22.6%, outpacing GLW.

Both companies are growing rapidly, but valuations diverge. GLW's P/E is 64.4, very high, while PRYMY's is 22.8, relatively rational. This suggests the market values GLW's market dominance more highly but underestimates PRYMY's growth potential.

## Why Optical Cable Is a Bottleneck Now: Four Drivers

**1. Structural Rigidity in Supply Chains**: Fiber-optic manufacturing requires over a decade of accumulated technology and massive capital. New entrants cannot enter, and capacity expansion by existing players takes months or more. Unlike chip shortages, optical cables can't be "solved" simply by making more.

**2. Explosive Growth in AI Investment**: Meta, Microsoft, Google and others are pouring trillion-dollar annual sums into AI infrastructure. As each builds massive data centers simultaneously, optical cable demand has exceeded normal levels by more than twofold. Supply cannot keep pace.

**3. Soaring Profit Margins**: Supply scarcity becomes a price-increase opportunity. GLW's profit margin is 11.2%, PRYMY's 6.6%—both significantly higher than the same periods in the past. In excess-demand conditions, supplier bargaining power is maximized.

**4. Geopolitical Diversification Necessity**: Amid U.S.-China technology tensions, Western data center operators are mandating non-Chinese optical cables. This grants exclusive positioning to Western suppliers like GLW and PRYMY.

## Opportunity and Limits: Structural Assessment

**GLW's Position**: With a market cap of $121.0 billion, it dominates the sector and has demonstrated high efficiency with 11.2% profit margin. As long as it maintains leadership in the fiber-optic and optical-component markets, medium- to long-term profitability looks strong.

**PRYMY's Opportunity**: Growing at 22.6% revenue growth—faster than GLW—while relatively undervalued. Submarine cable demand is expected to grow further in the AI era, with substantial room for margin improvement.

**Shared Risk**: The profit boom may not be permanent. If supply shortages ease or demand shifts with new technology, today's price premium will disappear.

## Summary

In AI data center competition, chips and power are no longer major constraints. Optical cable has become the new bottleneck, and suppliers like GLW and PRYMY have entered a historically profitable period. However, this could prove a short-term phenomenon, so differentiation by company's long-term competitiveness is essential.
