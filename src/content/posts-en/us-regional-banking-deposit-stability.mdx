## Recovery from Silicon Valley Bank Shock: Regional Bank Deposits Stabilize After 18 Months

Eighteen months after Silicon Valley Bank's collapse, regional bank deposit outflows have subsided and customer confidence is recovering. While financial system vigilance has eased, structural vulnerabilities among mid-sized banks remain.

## SVB Aftermath and Regional Banks' Survival

US regional banks such as **WAL** (Western Alliance), **ZION** (Zions Bancorporation), and **FITB** (Fifth Third Bancorp) experienced deposit surges, lending contractions, and stock price stagnation immediately after SVB's collapse in March 2023. Mid-sized banks in particular (assets between $100 billion and $500 billion) were exposed to structures similar to SVB—the pitfall of holding long-term fixed-rate securities and concentrated exposure to IT and startups.

However, data from the past year signals recovery. The Federal Reserve's emergency liquidity support (Bank Term Funding Program) and the gradual moderation of rate hike policy have calmed sentiment. WAL and FITB recorded net deposit inflows in first-half results, while ZION reduced loan loss provisions through improved credit portfolio quality.

## Why Stability Signals Are Emerging Now: Four Headwinds and Catalysts

**1. The Dual Effect of Interest Rate Normalization**: As the Fed's rate-hiking cycle ended and shifted to a cutting bias, unrealized losses on long-term securities began to narrow. Simultaneously, the deterioration of net interest margins from existing fixed-rate deposits also eased. Consequently, bank capital stress declined, and fears of deposit outflows diminished.

**2. Competitive Dynamics and Deposit Concentration**: While the trend of customers within the FDIC insurance limit ($250,000) shifting from smaller to larger banks persists, as confidence concerns ease, funds are beginning to return to mid-sized banks. Corporate clients in particular, which prioritize deposit stability, have created an 18-month window of quiet market conditions ideal for rebuilding trust.

**3. Asset Quality Improvement and Lower Loan Loss Provisions**: Deposit-focused institutions like ZION and FITB revealed unexpected reductions in credit loss provisions in earnings reports. As the economic adjustment period has passed, borrower repayment capacity has stabilized, and new delinquency rates have converged to historical averages. This signals not merely statistical recovery but improvement in cash flow fundamentals.

**4. Policy Infrastructure Strengthening and Market Sentiment Reversal**: The Treasury and Federal Reserve tightened "too big to fail" criteria after the SVB crisis, and regional banks intentionally increased liquidity hoarding and regulatory scrutiny. Markets recognized this shift, and headlines related to "banking crisis" in financial news dropped sharply. Stabilized sentiment alone accelerates deposit return flows.

## Why Latent Vulnerabilities Remain: Structural Weaknesses

**Interest Rate Duration Risk Persists**: While the Fed has cut rates by 0.5% through end-2025, if economic slowdown intensifies, an era of ultra-low rates could reemerge. If so, unrealized losses on long-term securities would expand again. WAL and ZION still hold over 30% of assets in long-term fixed securities.

**Credit Cycle Peak Uncertainty**: While unemployment has recovered to the 4% level and consumption remains steady, the impact of political uncertainty and global tensions on fiscal policy remains unclear. For FITB and ZION, where small and medium enterprise lending dominates, a reversal in the credit cycle could suddenly expose vulnerabilities.

## Conclusion

Eighteen months after SVB's collapse, regional bank deposit stabilization is real, but structural risks have not entirely disappeared. If interest rates rise again or the economy contracts sharply, mid-sized banks like WAL, ZION, and FITB could face renewed liquidity pressures. Thus, the current "recovery" should be viewed as a stabilization phase.
