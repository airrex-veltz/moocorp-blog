## AI Boom Intensifies Data Center Site Competition, Power Infrastructure Becomes Bottleneck

As competition in generative AI training and inference infrastructure accelerates capital expenditure (capex) growth at Amazon, Microsoft, and Google, securing data center sites and power supply have emerged as new constraints. With the big three tech companies simultaneously investing heavily in infrastructure, private infrastructure companies are benefiting while facing supply shortage pressures.

## Big Tech Capex Growth Intensifies Data Center Competition

**Microsoft (MSFT)** reported 18.8% revenue growth in 2026 and is trading at $523.88 (as of November 13, 2026), while **Google (GOOGL)** leads with faster 24.7% growth at $295.77 (same reference date). Both companies are pouring massive investments into AI model training and cloud infrastructure expansion. **Amazon (AMZN)** is also recording 12.1% revenue growth at $266.66 (as of November 13, 2026) while accelerating AWS data center expansion. This competition is expanding beyond the software level to the physical infrastructure realm.

## Why Data Centers Are Now a Bottleneck: Four Key Factors

**1. Land Acquisition Challenges**: GPU clusters required for AI model training have high power consumption structures, simultaneously demanding large physical spaces and facilities with cooling capabilities. Suitable data center sites in the United States and Europe are already near saturation, with auction prices skyrocketing.

**2. Power Supply Infrastructure Shortage**: New data centers requiring hundreds of megawatts of power cannot be fully supplied by existing grids alone. While power grid expansion in each country and region takes years to complete, capex competition continues unabated.

**3. Extended Construction Timelines**: From regulatory approvals through environmental assessments to community consultations, new data center construction from groundbreaking to operation takes 2-3 years or more. As this misaligns with the rapid pace of AI technology development, the lead time gap widens.

**4. Recruitment and Operational Staffing Shortages**: Large-scale data center operations require specialized engineers and technical personnel, but the global talent pool for such expertise is limited.

## Why Opportunities Still Exist: The Role of Infrastructure Companies

**Hosting Infrastructure Company Benefits**: Companies like **Equinix (EQIX)**, which already own data centers worldwide, are direct beneficiaries of this competition. Despite a stock price of $1,053.79 (as of November 13, 2026), maintaining a high P/E ratio of 63.3 signals that the market is pricing in continued demand growth. The 10.4% year-over-year revenue growth rate is conservative, but profitability gains are possible through high-margin structures.

**Big Tech Self-Construction Constraints**: Meanwhile, MSFT, GOOGL, and AMZN are pursuing their own construction rather than relying on third-party infrastructure, which partially constrains the growth of private infrastructure companies.

## Summary

In a situation where AI capex competition is severely pressuring data center sites and power supply, existing infrastructure companies gain opportunities while needing to watch out for big tech's expanded self-construction. In the long term, power grid expansion and site acquisition competition will be key variables determining profitability for these companies.
