## 1. Simultaneous Weakness Signal in Korean Material Companies

The sharp decline in Korean steel and chemical companies' stock prices in Q3 this year cannot be explained by ordinary economic slowdown. POSCO Holdings fell 39.16% over three months (as of September 23, 2026), and LG Chem also fell 40.26%. Hyundai Steel's situation is more severe. Despite 2025 operating income of KRW 219.2 billion, its net profit margin is -0.1%, essentially break-even (as of September 23, 2026).

This simultaneity suggests a change in **global raw material price structure**, beyond the deteriorating operational performance of individual companies.

## 2. Mechanism of Chinese Oversupply

China's steel and aluminum production capacity accounts for over 60% of global output. In 2025–2026, China's oversupply means that manufacturers who control pricing power in the international spot market are driving prices down.

- **Supply advantage = pricing power**: When Chinese manufacturers undercut prices through cost competitiveness, Korean, American, and Japanese manufacturers must either reduce margins or forfeit orders.
- **Korean companies' choices**: (1) Competing at the same price worsens margins, (2) Rejecting orders reduces sales. Both damage net income.

Hyundai Steel's net profit margin falling to -0.1% demonstrates the reality of this pressure. With 2025 revenue of KRW 22.7 trillion, the company barely broke even as rising costs or price cuts eliminated most profit.

## 3. Deteriorating Performance at Korean Steel and Chemical Companies

POSCO Holdings' net profit margin of 1.9% (2025 basis) is historically low for the industry. A company with market capitalization of KRW 23.6 trillion achieving only KRW 504.4 billion in 2025 net income reflects the severity of price pressure.

LG Chem is even more serious. Despite 2025 revenue of KRW 45.9 trillion, the company posted a **net loss of KRW 977.1 billion**. A net profit margin of -3.2% indicates market collapse that cost structure improvements alone cannot recover.

All three companies show clear margin pressure signals:
- POSCO: net profit margin 1.9%
- Hyundai Steel: net profit margin -0.1% (break-even)
- LG Chem: net profit margin -3.2% (loss)

## 4. Global Steelmakers Face the Same Pain

This is not a Korea-only problem. American steelmaker Cleveland-Cliffs (CLF) has a net profit margin of -4.6% (as of September 25, 2026), deteriorating more severely than Korea's. In other words, **Chinese oversupply is eroding the profit structure of the entire global steel industry**.

## 5. Who Benefits

Looking at the other side, this cost pressure lowers raw material expenses for construction companies, utilities, and shipbuilders. As steel and aluminum prices fall:
- **Major construction companies**: Lower costs for concrete reinforcement and frame materials → improved profit margins
- **Utilities**: Reduced material costs for transmission lines and transformers
- **Shipbuilders**: Lower ship construction costs

Construction cost reduction of 12% improves construction companies' operating profit margins.

## 6. The Investor's Choice

For shareholders of POSCO, Hyundai Steel, and LG Chem, current price pressure means **near-term profit deterioration**. The 39–40% three-month stock decline reflects this deterioration.

However, the question of how long this situation will persist is separate. Whether Chinese oversupply is structural or the result of temporary fiscal stimulus will determine the timing of recovery. For now, **the reality of margin deterioration is confirmed by the numbers**, and investment decisions rest on each company's potential to improve cost structures and demand recovery scenarios.
