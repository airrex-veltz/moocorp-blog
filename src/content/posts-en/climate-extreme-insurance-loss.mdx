## How Extreme Weather Inflates Insurance Losses and Shifts Burden to Consumers

Global extreme weather events are eroding the profitability of the insurance industry. As the frequency of major natural disasters such as hurricanes, wildfires, and floods increases, insurance companies face surging claims losses, and a vicious cycle is forming as they raise premiums to cover these losses. Ultimately, consumer household burdens continue to mount.

## Insurance Losses Pressured by Extreme Weather

Looking at **PRU** (Prudential Financial) financial performance, this pressure is evident. As of 2026-09-11, the closing price was $119.24, up 13.24% over the past three months but down 1.73% over the past month. This reflects near-term profitability headwinds facing the entire industry.

PRU has a market capitalization of USD 41.1 billion and trades at a P/E ratio of 10.8. Yet its net profit margin is only 6.0%, revealing structural weaknesses in the insurance industry. This means claims increases from extreme weather are eroding profits. While revenue growth is a positive 14.1% (YoY), most of that growth is being offset by losses.

## Why Are Insurance Losses Expanding Now: Four Factors

**1. Increasing Frequency and Severity of Extreme Climate Events**: Over recent decades, the frequency of major natural disasters has rapidly increased with climate change. Insurance companies' expected loss models (actuarial models) are failing to reflect reality. A single major storm can now trigger claims in the billions of dollars.

**2. Sharp Rise in Claims Costs**: Rising construction materials and labor costs push up disaster recovery expenses. A storm of the same size today commands far more expensive compensation than a decade ago. Insurance companies increasingly find their provisioned reserves insufficient.

**3. Surging Reinsurance Costs**: Prices for reinsurance that insurers purchase to distribute losses are soaring. As extreme weather losses rise, reinsurers also value risk more highly and raise premiums. This cost increase again pressures insurers' net profits.

**4. Premium Hikes Feed Consumer Burden**: To offset losses, insurers are repeatedly raising premiums charged to customers. Significant increases have been documented across homeowners, auto, and commercial insurance. Consumers have limited choice. Insurance is essential to basic living.

## Why PRU Endures—And Its Vulnerabilities

**Competitive Growth and Undervaluation Signal**: PRU's 14.1% revenue growth rate exceeds the industry average. Even at a market cap of USD 41.1 billion, it continues to steadily expand market share. A P/E of 10.8 is below historical averages, signaling the market may be temporarily undervaluing insurers. Large insurance groups including BRK.B (Berkshire Hathaway) are surviving similar environmental changes through structural strengths (capital power and financial stability).

**Structural Weakness: Permanent Loss Pressure**: As long as extreme weather does not abate, insurance companies face continued loss pressure. We are entering an era where loss management becomes more critical than customer acquisition. Continued premium hikes accelerate customer defection; failure to raise them further erodes net margins. This is a long-term dilemma facing the entire insurance industry.

## Summary

Extreme weather is no longer an isolated loss for insurers but a direct burden for consumers. Insurance companies like PRU, despite revenue growth, ultimately cannot escape this structure of low net margins. This is the process by which the role and sustainability of insurance in the climate crisis era is being redefined.
