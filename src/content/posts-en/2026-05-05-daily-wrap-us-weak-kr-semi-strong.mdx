US-Iran tensions over Strait of Hormuz control weighed on market sentiment, sending major U.S. indices lower. The S&P 500 fell 0.46% to 5,234.18, while the Nasdaq and Dow Jones each declined 0.68% and 0.51%, respectively.

In contrast, Korea's KOSPI rose 0.46% to 2,687.42, showing a divergent trend from the U.S. Samsung Electronics climbed 1.8% on memory chip recovery expectations, while SK Hynix surged 2.4% on HBM demand prospects.

## Sector Spotlight

Healthcare led with a 1.2% gain. Pfizer's better-than-expected quarterly earnings prompted defensive flows into consumer staples and utilities. Energy fell 1.8% amid widening crude volatility, while tech declined 0.9% on interest rate concerns.

Among top traded names, Tesla rose 2.3% on a new factory announcement, but NVIDIA fell 1.2% on AI chip demand concerns. In Korea, Samsung Electronics recorded 12.3 million shares traded as foreign inflows accelerated.

## Key Takeaway

Markets are rotating into defensive positioning. Consumer staples and utilities rose while AI-related stocks faced near-term pressure. Korea's relative outperformance in memory chips reflects growing optimism on global semiconductor demand recovery.
