## Battery Material Supply Geopolitics Erodes EV Manufacturer Margins

As rapidly expanding electric vehicle production surges demand for battery materials like lithium and cobalt, geopolitical concentration in mining and strengthened environmental regulation support raw material prices, progressively eroding EV manufacturers' cost competitiveness. Intensifying competition for spot supply and long-term contracts to break supply bottlenecks reveals signals that improving manufacturing margins has become difficult.

## Growth Rates and Profitability of Three Companies Reveal Structural Interest Rate Differences

**TSLA** at USD 362.44 (as of 2026-10-15) showed revenue growth of 25.5% year-over-year but net profit margin of only 3.7%. This fast-growing company typically trades margin for growth, interpreted as a signal of EV manufacturing cost pressure. Lithium miner **ALB** at close USD 98.18 in the same period showed revenue growth of 31.1%, exceeding TSLA. Yet net profit margin was 3.8%, nearly the same as the manufacturer. The price-setting power normally enjoyed by mining companies as raw material suppliers appears weakened. In contrast, multi-commodity miner **RIO** at close USD 90.21 on 2026-10-15 with revenue growth of 15.5% dominates both with net profit margin of 19.6%. Companies with diversified portfolios and mining scale retain profitability.

## Why Battery Material Supply Now Increases Pressure: Four Factors

**1. Geopolitical concentration of lithium and cobalt mining**: Four countries—Chile, Argentina, China, and Australia—dominate lithium supply, while the Democratic Republic of Congo supplies a significant portion of global cobalt. Dependent on single regions or countries, variables like political instability, mining tax increases, and export restrictions rapidly shift raw material prices and availability.

**2. Strengthening environmental and human rights regulation**: As advanced countries and automakers emphasize "ethical mining" standards, entry barriers for existing mining regions have risen. Regulatory compliance costs such as water treatment, carbon emissions, and local community compensation drive up mining costs. Smaller mining companies cannot afford these costs and exit.

**3. Slow progress in battery technology diversification**: NCM (nickel-cobalt-manganese) ratio optimization to reduce cobalt use and alternative technologies like LFP (iron phosphate) batteries slowly commercialize, but high-performance EV manufacturers like TSLA remain heavily dependent on lithium-based batteries. During technology transition periods, it becomes difficult to fully reflect material price increases in manufacturing costs.

**4. Acceleration of global EV sales growth**: According to forecasts from the IEA and others, EV production is expected to double or more by 2030. While this supply outlook is firm, new mining project approval speeds fail to keep pace, entrenching structural supply gaps.

## Nevertheless / However: Dual Characteristics of Strengths and Weaknesses

**Manufacturers' weakening cost competitiveness**: TSLA's 3.7% net profit margin shows large divergence from 25.5% revenue growth rate. As it becomes harder to fully pass cost pressures through price increases, signals emerge that margin improvement room decreases. The prominence of moves to lock in raw material prices through long-term contracts is evidence of high spot volatility.

**Mining companies' margin bifurcation**: RIO's high net profit margin suggests scale and strong asset base allow tolerance of regulation and supply shifts. In contrast, specialized lithium miner ALB despite revenue growth shows declining margins, constraining regulatory cost absorption capacity and new investment capability. This hints at increasing consolidation pressure in mining industry going forward.

## Summary

Battery material geopolitical concentration and mining regulation strengthening erode manufacturers' cost competitiveness in the short term. TSLA's low net profit margin and ALB's stagnant margins reflect current conditions of this mechanism. Without support from technology diversification and mining investment expansion, the entire EV industry faces likely selection pressure to either accept high raw material costs or pass price increases to consumers.
