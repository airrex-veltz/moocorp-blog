## Collapsing Launch Costs Dismantle Barriers to Satellite Business

As reusable rockets have slashed launch costs tenfold, capital flowing into satellite communications and space internet has shifted. Traditional satellite operators battle margin pressure, while major tech firms backed by new capital and technology seize emerging opportunities.

## Satellite Communications: Crisis and Opportunity

**VSAT** (closing price USD 72.00, as of October 2, 2026) is a traditional geostationary satellite communications operator. Its financial metrics reveal structural slowdown. Revenue growth over the past year stands at -1.2%, and net margin sits at -0.6%. Within a fixed-asset business model, competitive pressure from collapsing launch costs has weighed on results. Market capitalization of USD 990 million remains depressed compared to years prior.

Meanwhile, **GOOG** (closing price USD 340.35, October 2, 2026) maintains strong momentum with revenue growth of 24.2%. Alphabet is positioned either to invest in its own low-earth orbit satellite internet infrastructure or to develop new satellite-based services made possible by lower launch costs. Its high net margin of 54.8% rests on data monetization capabilities already in place.

## Why Change Accelerates Now: Four Drivers and Pressures

**1. Plummeting Launch Costs from Reusable Rockets**: After SpaceX developed a reusable first stage for Falcon 9, low-earth orbit launch costs have fallen from tens of billions of dollars to hundreds of millions or less. This has made even small and microsatellites economically viable.

**2. Proliferation of Startup Launch Providers**: Encouraged by reusable rocket success, startups are developing and commercializing small, low-cost rockets. Satellites weighing tens of tonnes or less can now be launched affordably as orbital rides rather than dedicated missions.

**3. Explosive Demand for Low-Earth Orbit Megaconstellations**: Success in commercial satellite internet has inspired other projects. Simultaneously, demand has expanded for remote sensing, environmental monitoring, and earth observation data collection for AI training—all requiring low-earth orbit satellites.

**4. Major Tech Companies Enter Infrastructure**: Firms like Google, Amazon, and Meta are pursuing direct network infrastructure construction. As communications satellites become part of the internet backbone, capital from outside traditional telecom is reaching space.

## Structural Strengths and Weaknesses

**VSAT's Weakness**: Geostationary satellites carry high development, launch, and orbital maintenance costs. Even as launch costs fall, the absolute lifespan of a single satellite (15–20 years) creates steep initial capital recovery burden. In a market intensifying on low-cost competition, this fixed cost becomes a liability.

**GOOG's Opportunity**: Alphabet, already holding cloud, AI, and advertising databases, can layer satellite-based services as value-add. With launch cost barriers now lowered, synergy with existing business proves advantageous.

## Summary

Structural decline in launch costs has reset the space industry's game board. For traditional satellite firms, it is an era of margin erosion; for large technology platforms, it is a pathway to new data infrastructure.
