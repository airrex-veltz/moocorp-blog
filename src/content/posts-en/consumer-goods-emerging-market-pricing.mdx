## Emerging Market Price Hikes Hit Wall of Demand Elasticity

Consumer goods companies implementing successive large price increases in emerging markets are beginning to see actual weak sales. As strategies to counter currency depreciation and inflation collide with the demand elasticity ceiling in regions of weakened purchasing power, achieving the initial goal of improved profitability is becoming difficult.

## Inflection Point in Emerging Market Consumer Goods Pricing Strategies

**PG**'s closing price on 2026-09-23 was $147.39, with a one-month change of only +1.87%, indicating stagnant growth. More serious is that revenue growth (YoY) remained at 1.5%. **CL** was at $85.93 on the same basis, recording -5.65% over one month and -5.48% over three months, with clear stock price declines. However, revenue growth remains positive at 4.9%. **UL** was at $62.16 (one month -3.09%), with revenue growth at the lowest 0.5%.

All three companies have positioned emerging markets as key growth drivers, but actual revenue growth has been more lukewarm than expected. Particularly, the low growth of PG and UL suggests that the price increase strategy failed to offset declines in volume.

## Why Is Growth Slowing Now: Four Factors

**1. Discrepancy between emerging market currency depreciation and nominal price increases**: Currency depreciation in emerging markets should inflate dollar-based sales figures, but simultaneously consumers locally face sharply rising absolute prices. Companies face a vicious cycle where they must raise local prices to maintain dollar profitability. If the magnitude of price increases does not match rises in purchasing power, it directly results in volume loss.

**2. Price elasticity of low-income demand**: Lower-income consumers in emerging markets are highly price-sensitive. Consumer goods are necessities, not discretionary items, but being "necessary" does not mean prices can be raised indefinitely. Instead, consumers can easily switch to local competitors' products, and some reduce purchase frequency or shift to cheaper alternatives. CL's 4.9% revenue growth suggests price increases were maintained through revenue gains, while PG's 1.5% low growth suggests volume loss exceeded price gains.

**3. Collapse in premium portfolio demand**: As the emerging market middle class shrinks or purchasing power weakens, demand for high-priced product categories collapses. High-margin segments like skincare and health products are hit first. All three companies are heavily dependent on these premium categories, and the triple pressure of currency depreciation + inflation + price increases is suppressing purchases.

**4. Intensifying competition and divergence in pricing strategies**: Global giants like PG and UL stick to existing price increase policies, while CL applies price adjustments differently by country, or local competitors may pursue more aggressive discounts. Particularly in high-growth markets like India, Southeast Asia, and Brazil, the accelerating low-price entry of Chinese manufacturers narrows the price increase room for global giants.

## Are There Still Recovery Opportunities: Structural Strengths and Weaknesses

**Strengths**: All three companies dominate regional competitors in brand assets, distribution networks, and R&amp;D capabilities. PG's net profit margin of 18.4%, UL's 18.3%, and CL's 9.7% remain high. Volume loss can be partially recovered through localized product development and multi-tier pricing strategies.

**Weaknesses**: High dividend policies and shareholder expectations pressure short-term profitability, making long-term price cuts for market share difficult. With PERs at PG 22.1, UL 21.3, and CL 33.7, performance shortfalls are immediately reflected in stock prices. If emerging market growth falls short of expectations, investor attrition may accelerate.

## Summary

Emerging market price increases maintained short-term margins but eroded revenue growth through volume loss. Going forward, the pace of local purchasing power recovery and companies' ability to adjust prices will determine competitiveness. Unless the exchange rate normalizes, emerging market sales growth is likely to remain sluggish.
