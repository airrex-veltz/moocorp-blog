The global biotech sector is being reshaped by two distinct growth drivers. The US market is creating a new market worth over USD 100 billion annually by 2030 through the GLP-1 obesity treatment revolution led by Eli Lilly and Novo Nordisk. Meanwhile, Korean biotech firms are expanding their footprint in the biosimilar and CDMO markets by leveraging patent cliffs and increasing outsourced manufacturing demand.

## Sector Overview

The biotech healthcare sector divides into two major trends. First is the explosive growth of GLP-1 class drugs expanding indications from obesity and diabetes to cardiovascular disease and sleep apnea. Second is the opportunity for biosimilars following patent expirations of major blockbuster drugs, alongside rising outsourced manufacturing by global pharma. The former is driven by US Big Pharma, while the latter by Korean CDMO and biosimilar firms.

## US Trend: GLP-1 Obesity Treatment Revolution

The GLP-1 market led by Eli Lilly's Mounjaro and Zepbound, and Novo Nordisk's Ozempic and Wegovy, is expanding indications beyond simple obesity treatment to cardiovascular disease and sleep apnea, with the total addressable market projected to exceed USD 100 billion annually by 2030. As supply constraints ease and insurance coverage expands, market accessibility is improving as well.

<Callout type="catalyst" title="Upcoming catalysts">
Eli Lilly's Mounjaro/Zepbound FDA approvals for cardiovascular disease and sleep apnea indications are imminent, while Novo Nordisk projects Wegovy supply normalization in Q2 2026.
</Callout>

Eli Lilly is currently trading at $989.87, maintaining premium valuation reflecting rapid sales growth of Mounjaro and Zepbound. Novo Nordisk is at $47, holding the number-one market share in Ozempic's diabetes market, though intensifying competition from Lilly, Amgen, and new entrants is a headwind.

AbbVie faced revenue decline after Humira's US patent expiration in 2023, but is repositioning its portfolio with next-generation immunology products Skyrizi and Rinvoq, showing potential to achieve over USD 10 billion in annual revenue. The current stock price is $207.86.

## Korea Opportunity: CDMO and Biosimilars

The Korean biotech sector is growing by leveraging rising global outsourced manufacturing demand and entering the biosimilar market through patent cliff opportunities. Samsung Biologics has reached 3rd place globally in CDMO with 784,000 liters of production capacity as of 2025, continuing to expand its order backlog through its fourth manufacturing facility. The current stock price is KRW 1,410,000.

Celltrion is accelerating market entry into the US and Europe with its Humira biosimilar Remsima and Inflectra, with Stelara and Eylea biosimilar launches planned. The stock is trading around KRW 190,500, with patent cliff benefits and pipeline expansion reflected in the valuation.

## Sector Risks

The GLP-1 market faces pricing pressure from new entrants like Amgen and Roche. Safety concerns around cardiovascular side effects and suicidal ideation in obesity treatments could emerge as regulatory risks.

<Callout type="risk">
US Medicare drug price negotiations are a structural profitability headwind for the biotech sector broadly. Capacity expansion competition among Korean CDMO players increases downside pricing risk.
</Callout>

The Korean CDMO market faces potential unit price decline through capacity expansion competition among players, and global economic slowdown could constrain healthcare spending, limiting overall sector growth.

## Implications

The biotech sector is growing on two axes: US GLP-1 innovation and Korean CDMO and biosimilar competitiveness. Indication expansion and supply normalization from Eli Lilly and Novo Nordisk, order expansion from Samsung Biologics, and biosimilar launches from Celltrion are near-term catalysts. However, intensifying competition, pricing pressure, and regulatory risks must be monitored closely.
