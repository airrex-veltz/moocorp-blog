## Battery Thermal Management Materials Shape the EV Competitive Landscape

EV batteries generate more heat as they are designed with higher energy density. Failure in temperature control leads to reduced driving range, slower charging and discharging, and fire risk. As a result, the thermal conductivity efficiency between battery cells and heat dissipation paths directly determines product competitiveness. And the supply of Thermal Interface Materials (TIM) responsible for this efficiency has now become a bottleneck.

## The Role and Supply Structure of Thermal Interface Materials

Inside the battery pack, cells contact metal plates like aluminum or copper and heat sinks. However, due to microscopic surface roughness, direct contact alone causes significant heat loss. This is where intermediate materials like thermal paste, thermal pads, and liquid cooling loops are needed. High-performance TIM must maintain high thermal conductivity while preserving electrical insulation, and must withstand millions of thermal expansion and contraction cycles.

Very few companies specialize in producing this material. Globally, only a handful of companies possess both automotive-grade reliability and mass production capability. Consequently, EV battery pack manufacturers depend on a small number of material suppliers.

## Why is the EV Material Bottleneck Intensifying Now: 4 Factors

**1. Intensifying Battery Energy Density Race**: As automakers push battery pack energy density to extend driving range and reduce costs, heat generation increases. High-density designs have become impossible without high-efficiency thermal management materials.

**2. Surging Demand from Rapidly Scaling EV Production**: As global EV production grows rapidly each year, TIM demand surges proportionally. Existing material suppliers cannot expand production capacity fast enough to keep pace with demand growth.

**3. High Supplier Concentration**: With few TIM companies but many customers (battery manufacturers, automakers), suppliers gain stronger bargaining power. Technology premiums grow, and customers compete for delivery priority.

**4. Price Increases and Supply Volatility**: As supply shortages persist, high-performance TIM prices rise. Battery manufacturers and automakers face cost pressure yet cannot sacrifice material quality.

## Yet / However: Structural Strengths and Weaknesses

**Material Makers' Expanding Technology Premium**: In a supply-constrained market, companies producing high-performance materials can raise margins and dictate contract terms with customers. The value chain moves from simple "material sales" to "thermal management solution consulting."

**Automakers' Deteriorating Cost Structure**: EV batteries represent a significant portion of vehicle cost. As battery pack assembly costs rise, automakers' cost competitiveness declines. Especially startups and budget-segment makers are more vulnerable to material price increases.

## Summary

In the EV industry, battery thermal management materials are no longer mere components but competitive advantage factors. The longer the supply bottleneck persists, the stronger material makers' bargaining power becomes, and automakers dependent on high-performance TIM must endure cost pressure.
