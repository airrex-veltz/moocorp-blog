## Pipeline Infrastructure Becomes the Cornerstone of Energy Security

The US and Europe are rapidly expanding investment in new pipeline construction and maintenance to ensure stable energy supply. Geopolitical tensions and industrial restructuring have elevated energy infrastructure to the level of each country's national defense strategy.

## The Golden Age for Canadian Pipeline Companies

**TC Energy Corporation (TRP)** and **Enbridge Inc. (ENB)** are major pipeline operators in North America. As of September 14, 2026, TRP trades at $61.18 with a market cap of $63.6 billion, while ENB trades at $48.16 with a market cap of $105.2 billion, making them core assets in the energy infrastructure industry.

TRP's net profit margin is 22.9%, exceptionally high among crude and natural gas peers. ENB's sales growth rate is 97.1% year-over-year, reflecting recent asset expansion through acquisitions. Both companies have P/E ratios (TRP 24.1x, ENB 25.6x) higher than their historical averages, signaling that the market is front-running expectations of improved cash flows ahead.

## Why Pipeline Investment Is Surging Now: 4 Factors

**1. Russia–Ukraine Energy War**: Following the 2022 invasion of Ukraine, Europe had to significantly reduce its dependence on Russian gas and oil. To fill this gap, the US is simultaneously expanding LNG export terminals, increasing Middle Eastern supply lines, and strengthening North American domestic transport infrastructure. For TRP and ENB, which export Canadian crude and natural gas, this means expanding long-term operating revenues.

**2. Rising Energy Demand from Industrial Recovery**: With the 2024–2026 global manufacturing recovery, raw material demand from refineries, chemicals, and steel companies has resumed. In developed countries, handshaking and AI data center electricity demand is raising reliance on natural gas power plants, while developing countries are increasing energy imports to build industrial capacity.

**3. Clear Government Policy Support**: The Biden administration equates energy security with national security and has shortened environmental assessment periods for pipeline construction while expanding financial support. The Canadian government also views energy export revenue as a fiscal foundation and is moving to accelerate regulatory approvals. This policy stance significantly reduces business planning uncertainty.

**4. Aging Infrastructure and Replacement Demand**: North American pipelines average over 50 years old. Rising periodic maintenance and full replacement demand due to safety concerns guarantee companies like TRP and ENB stable workloads for decades to come.

## Key Strengths and Weaknesses to Watch

**Strength — Stability of Regulated Utilities**: Pipeline operations typically enjoy monopoly positions or regulated tariff structures. This means business revenues are not excessively swayed by crude and gas price volatility. In particular, TRP's 22.9% net profit margin proves this stability.

**Risk — ESG Capital Flight and Environmental Regulation**: Pipeline companies are targets for institutional investors with net-zero targets. Some US and Canadian states are limiting or reconsidering approval of new fossil fuel infrastructure. Three-month stock declines (TRP −11.03%, ENB −13.59%) show that policy risk is still priced in.

**Capital Intensity**: New pipeline construction and replacement require multibillion-dollar investments. In a rising rate environment, borrowing costs squeeze investment returns.

## Conclusion

As energy security has escalated from an economic and diplomatic issue to a national survival strategy, pipeline operating companies can pursue long-term contract-based business expansion independent of short-term energy price swings. Canadian companies like TRP and ENB are seen as the biggest beneficiaries of this structural shift. However, environmental regulations and ESG investment trends could suppress stock prices for the foreseeable future, making medium- to long-term perspective essential.
