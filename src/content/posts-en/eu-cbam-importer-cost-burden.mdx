## EU's Carbon Barrier Erodes Chemical Importers' Profits

As the European Union's Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) takes full effect, chemical, metal, and cement importers face surging costs. In a structure where costs cannot be passed through, margin pressure will intensify for companies already facing deteriorating profitability.

## CBAM's Operating Mechanism and Importer Status

The EU has operated CBAM as a transitional regime since 2026, imposing carbon emission costs on imported chemical, steel, and aluminum products. Importers must purchase carbon credits in the EU's emissions trading system (ETS) to cover these costs. Meanwhile, U.S. major chemical company **LYB** (LyondellBasell) trades at $60.01 on October 13, 2026, down 4.37% over the past month. More concerning is that its profit margin is -1.1%—already in loss territory. Yet revenue grew 19.8% year-over-year, suggesting that cost inflation inflated revenue rather than volume increases.

## Why CBAM Cost Pass-Through Is Difficult: Four Structural Problems

**1. Inability to Pass Through Cost Increases**: Chemical raw materials trade under long-term supply contracts. Prices set at contract signing are fixed, making it difficult to immediately pass CBAM cost increases to customers. Even when importers try to raise prices in new contracts, competitive pressure from local producers limits the increase.

**2. Already-Deteriorated Profitability**: With LYB's profit margin at -1.1%, additional costs enlarge losses. Major importer BASF (Germany) already has thin margins due to high energy costs and regulatory burdens at its European headquarters. New regulatory costs could push company-wide profitability below breakeven.

**3. Absence of Alternative Suppliers**: The chemical industry relies on economies of scale and regionally concentrated supply chains. Even non-EU low-cost suppliers must bear costs if their products are CBAM-targeted. Due to technical and logistics constraints, switching suppliers is difficult, leaving importers no choice but to absorb cost increases or reduce sales volumes.

**4. Downstream Industry Price Resistance**: Industries using chemicals as raw materials—automotive, pharmaceuticals, packaging—resist price increases. When end-consumer demand stagnates, it becomes hard to raise raw material prices, and importer margins face pressure from both sides.

## Why Endurance Continues: Structural Paradox of Weakness and Strength

**No Choice**: Even specialty chemical companies like ARMMF face the same environment. Since the entire industry is affected by CBAM, competing firms are equally damaged. Short-term, everyone must bear losses.

**Weak Advantage of Large Companies**: Large players like LYB can avoid some costs through production facilities and supply chains in multiple regions. However, the 19.8% high revenue growth doesn't translate to real profit increase, so this structural advantage has clear limits against CBAM cost surges.

## Summary

CBAM is both regulation and a cost shock. LYB, already with negative profit margins, and European producer BASF now face additional margin deterioration in a structure that prevents cost pass-through. With neither customer price increases nor supply chain switching as realistic solutions, importer profitability deterioration appears structural with little hope for near-term recovery.
