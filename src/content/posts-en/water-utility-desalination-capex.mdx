## Water Scarcity Crisis Accelerates Desalination Investment by Water Utilities

As global drought intensifies, securing fresh water has become the top priority of national infrastructure policy. This is the backdrop for **American Water Works** (AWK), the largest water utility in the United States, to invest significant capital in seawater desalination facilities. This moment represents a reversal: the physical constraints of water supply are becoming revenue opportunities.

## American Water Works and the Necessity of Desalination Investment

AWK is a publicly traded company operating water and wastewater services in the United States. As of October 1, 2026, its stock price stood at $127.86 with a market capitalization of $25.8 billion. Revenue growth is 6.2% year-over-year, and net profit margin is 21.3%, maintaining the stable profitability characteristic of public utilities.

Water supply depends heavily on precipitation and groundwater reserves. As prolonged drought impacts North America, an increasing number of regions cannot meet demand with existing water sources alone. Consequently, desalination—the conversion of seawater or brackish water into fresh water—is no longer optional but essential infrastructure. AWK is already investing in the construction and operation of desalination facilities across multiple states, and with government funding support backing these efforts, expansion in this sector is accelerating.

## Why AWK Is Acting Now: Four Factors

**1. Deepening Water Supply Crisis from Persistent Drought**: Declining precipitation in major demand regions—California, Texas, Arizona—is structural. Based on climate change projections, this trend is expected to continue, making desalination facilities a long-term essential investment.

**2. Improved Economics of Desalination Technology**: Modern desalination technologies, such as reverse osmosis, have significantly improved energy efficiency compared to the past. As retrofit costs decline, city-scale desalination projects are becoming viable.

**3. Expansion of Government Infrastructure Investment Funding**: U.S. federal infrastructure investment policies, including the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act, directly fund water infrastructure modernization. This reduces capital burdens for operators like AWK and improves return on investment.

**4. Regulatory Support and Justified Rate Increases**: As water scarcity becomes a public health issue, state regulatory authorities are becoming more tolerant of water rate increases. AWK's foundation for revenue growth is strengthening.

## AWK's Challenges: Capital Intensity and Profitability Uncertainty

**Structural Strengths**: AWK's decades of operational experience and deep regulatory relationships enable it to advance permits and operations for new water sources (desalination) faster than newcomers. Furthermore, water rates as public goods are collected regardless of economic cycles, providing consistent cash flow. As of October 1, 2026, a P/E of 22.5x is not above utility sector averages, and a 21.3% net margin demonstrates the ability to recover capital investments.

**Structural Weaknesses**: Each desalination facility requires investment in the hundreds of billions of won, and operating costs for energy and maintenance persist during operation. Regulatory approval processes are lengthy and uncertain for each project. Additionally, if government funding support decreases or rate regulations tighten, profitability could plummet.

## Summary

Water scarcity is creating long-term revenue opportunities for public infrastructure companies like AWK. However, desalination is capital-intensive and time-consuming, so it should be evaluated over a five-year or longer horizon rather than being swayed by short-term stock price fluctuations (1-month: -6.94%, 3-month: -5.95%).
