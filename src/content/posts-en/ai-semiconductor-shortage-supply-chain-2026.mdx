## AI Chip Supply Bottleneck: 18-Month Crisis from TSMC Advanced Process Shortage

As the generative AI boom drives a surge in data center demand, advanced process semiconductors (5nm and below) are expected to remain in a supply deficit through 2027. With **TSMC** accounting for over 80% of the world's cutting-edge chip manufacturing, capacity constraints have become the industry's growth ceiling.

## TSMC's Advanced Process Capacity Crunch

TSMC, a massive foundry currently processing over 2 million wafers annually, faces persistent 4-6 month delivery delays due to demand for AI accelerators and high-end CPUs. A vicious cycle is unfolding as previous-generation processes—memory, mobile, and automotive—are being squeezed out. TSMC management projects that supply balance will collapse through mid-2027 and has increased annual capital expenditures from the KRW 1 trillion range to the KRW 1.5 trillion range. However, new production lines still require 18-24 months to come online.

## Why TSMC—and the Semiconductor Sector—Are Facing Pressure Now: Four Factors

**1. Simultaneous New Product Roadmaps from NVIDIA and AMD**: As the generative AI market expands, **NVIDIA** is rolling out new-generation H200 and H300 chips in volume this year, while **AMD** is increasing supplies of MI300-series AI accelerators. Both companies are forcing additional orders worth hundreds of millions of dollars annually from TSMC, forcing competing demand layers—Apple, Qualcomm, Broadcom, and others—to accept reduced allocation.

**2. Intensifying Competition from Samsung and Intel Foundries**: Samsung began volume production of its 3nm process this year, and Intel is pursuing aggressive marketing through Foundry Services. Even if they capture some customers, their improvement pace falls short of TSMC's level, leaving supply gaps unfilled. Paradoxically, customers' growing push for diversification has deepened TSMC's dependency.

**3. Acceleration of Enterprise Data Center Deployments**: Cloud platforms (AWS, Google Cloud, Azure) and major enterprises are developing their own AI chips, shifting demand weight from smartphones and PCs to large-scale server chips. Data center chips carry higher per-unit prices and tolerate less inventory loss, making them **priority allocation** targets for TSMC.

**4. Heightened Geopolitical Regulation**: U.S. semiconductor export restrictions to China and national industry protection measures (CHIPS Act) constrain TSMC's global capacity allocation. Industry insiders worry that if geopolitical risk to TSMC Taiwan's operations materializes, global data center investments could face near-term paralysis.

## Why TSMC Nonetheless: Structural Dilemma Between Technical Monopoly and Supply Bottleneck

**Sustained Technology Lead**: TSMC's process technology remains 2-3 years ahead. Most assessments hold that **Intel**'s in-house manufacturing (IDM) and Samsung's precision and yields both fall short. Therefore, companies needing high-performance AI chips will have no choice but to depend on TSMC for years to come.

**Limits to Capacity Expansion**: Building new fabs costs $4 billion and takes 2-3 years for workforce and equipment deployment. TSMC's extreme choices are (1) forcing existing customers to accept delivery delays, (2) raising unit prices, or (3) selectively picking strategic customers. All three risk eroding market confidence.

## Summary

TSMC's advanced process bottleneck is likely to constrain industry growth through mid-2027. AI demand shows no sign of slowing, competitors need 1-2 more years to catch up in technology, and capacity expansion takes time—leaving no escape from this triangle. Investors must monitor both the possibility that near-term supply advantages will boost profitability and the corresponding valuation adjustment phase this may trigger.
