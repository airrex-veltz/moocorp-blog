## AI Inference Explosion Shakes Data Center Power Grids

Surging AI model training and inference demand is driving data center power consumption sharply upward. This signals more than simple hardware supply competition—it's remaking power infrastructure investment and semiconductor manufacturing cost structure.

## Data Center Chip Fundamentals in Growth Figures

**NVIDIA** (**NVDA**) closed at USD 229.28 (2026-10-09), with 105.9% YoY revenue growth. **TSMC** (**TSM**) at USD 453.31 (same date) logged 36.0% growth. **Broadcom** (**AVGO**) at USD 361.54 posts 85.5% growth. Domestically, **Samsung Electronics** (005930.KS) closed at KRW 263,000 (2026-10-08), achieving KRW 333.6 trillion consolidated revenue and KRW 45.2 trillion net profit in FY2025, with 130.0% revenue growth. These figures reflect surging AI chip and memory demand.

## Why Power Demand Explodes Now: Four Factors

**1. LLM inference's linear scaling limits**: Large language models dominate, requiring not just training but massive production inference. A single query needs minutes of high-power GPU cluster time. Billions of daily inference requests demand power infrastructure beyond existing grids.

**2. Data center density at limits**: Continued chip miniaturization packs more into the same space, yet cooling and power delivery bottleneck. A single high-density GPU rack consumes power approaching a small region's daily demand.

**3. Power grid expansion's capital intensity**: Running high-voltage lines to data center sites and building new substations cost trillions of won. Power companies, cloud operators, and chip makers share this cost; who absorbs it determines near-term profitability.

**4. Semiconductor process power intensity rising**: Sub-7nm nodes require lithography, EUV, extreme-temperature etching—all extremely power-intensive. Foundries like **TSMC** struggle to pass power cost increases to chip prices; suppliers like **Broadcom** must account for grid constraints in design.

## Strength and Weakness Reconstructed

**GPU designer margin advantage**: **NVIDIA**'s 63.7% net margin reflects custom design plus outsourced manufacturing. Power costs concentrate on the foundry; designers maintain margins through efficiency innovation.

**Foundry cost pressure deepens**: **TSMC**'s 49.9% net margin may compress as power costs rise and future node investment accelerates. Cash flow pressure emerges in real time.

**Memory demand volatility**: **Samsung** is sensitive to shifts in AI training versus inference memory needs. FY2025 KRW 333.6 trillion revenue and KRW 43.6 trillion operating profit reflect memory cycle recovery, but data center power constraints could reshape supply fundamentals.

## Summary

Data center power demand explosion supports short-term semiconductor growth but raises medium-term questions: which firm solves power infrastructure first and who survives cost increases. This creates a new competitive axis beyond chip performance: energy efficiency and supply chain resilience.
