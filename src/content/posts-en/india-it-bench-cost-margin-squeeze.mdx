The offshore IT services industry has enjoyed decades of continuous growth built on low labor costs and scalability. Yet as industry-wide growth slope has moderated in recent years, a different cost-structure problem has emerged.

Infosys (INFY)'s recent performance illustrates this shift clearly. As of 2026 October 23, INFY's annual revenue growth (YoY) stood at just 1.4%. Compare this to double-digit growth years of the past—a sharp decline. Meanwhile, net margin holds at 16.2%, yet the cost pressures required to defend this margin amid low growth are intensifying.

## Structural Drivers of Rising Bench Costs

IT services companies operate on a model of accepting customer projects and deploying engineers and developers. In high-growth years past, steady new project wins kept labor utilization high. Now as industry growth has slowed, project acquisition speed lags behind project completion pace.

The result: rising idle labor—"benched" staff. While bench workers must still receive salary and benefits, these costs cannot be billed to customers. This is not mere loss but a direct profit margin hit.

Additionally, talent acquisition competition remains fierce despite weak demand. Companies must accommodate wage increase demands to retain people, and even as new hiring shrinks, fixed labor costs for already-employed staff remain.

## The Nature of Margin Pressure

Low growth combined with rising bench costs fundamentally weaken the industry's margin structure. The high-growth model that once absorbed costs through economies of scale no longer functions.

Operating margin measured on service revenue declines as bench costs rise. Against stagnant or falling billable headcount, internal fixed costs accumulate unchanged—margin improvement is difficult. Hourly billing rate increases are also capped: customers emphasize cost efficiency and resist increases.

## Market Re-rating

INFY posted 17.62% stock price gain on a one-month basis in October, and is up 5.56% over three months. Stock traded in the 10.12-13.30 dollar range over the period. This volatility suggests investor views diverge even amid industry uncertainty.

A 15.2 PER and 16.2% net margin represent historically low levels. The signal: margin pressure is not being compensated by revenue growth.

## Structural Adjustment Begins

This situation hints that the India IT services industry has moved beyond simple cyclical slowdown into an era of business model restructuring. Bench cost management, service sophistication, automation investment for efficiency gains—these have become survival imperatives. If low growth becomes the new normal, margin pressure will persist without operational restructuring.
