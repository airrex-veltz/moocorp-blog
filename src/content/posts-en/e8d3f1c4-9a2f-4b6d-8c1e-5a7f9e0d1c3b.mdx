## Telecom Infrastructure Funds Accelerating Cell Tower Acquisition, Operational Model Paradigm Shift

Global large-scale telecom infrastructure investment funds are accelerating their acquisition of cell tower assets from telecom operators. This is not merely asset divestiture but a signal that the telecom industry's revenue-sharing structure is being fundamentally reorganized.

## Cell Tower Assets and the Emergence of Infrastructure Funds

Telecom cell towers were historically pure operational assets. Telecom operators owned, maintained, and extracted revenue from them directly. However, the situation changed from the mid-2010s onward. With the emergence of independent infrastructure companies like American Tower, Crown Castle, and Equinix, cell towers transformed into 'tradable financial assets.' More recently, a 'fund-led model' in which large infrastructure funds directly acquire and operate cell towers is spreading.

## Why Cell Tower Assets Are Moving Now: Four Drivers

**1. Telecom Operators' Capital Lightweighting Pressure**: Telecom operators have invested massive capital in building 5G infrastructure. As marginal return on invested capital (ROIC) declined, they are seeking to improve cash flow by lightweighting fixed assets. Cell tower sales represent immediate cash inflows, allowing them to reduce debt or increase dividends.

**2. Infrastructure Funds' Yield Pursuit**: When low-rate environments persist or economic volatility increases, infrastructure funds favor assets with guaranteed long-term fixed returns. Monthly rents from cell towers offer predictable cash flow as long as operators maintain creditworthiness. A stable yield of 5–7% is attractive.

**3. Scaling of 5G/6G Infrastructure Investment**: Next-generation networks require more cell towers and more sophisticated infrastructure. Single operators struggle to bear this investment entirely. A structure in which external funds supply capital and operators pay rent is cost-efficient.

**4. ESG and Energy Management Pressure**: Cell towers are power-consuming facilities. Operators must meet net-zero commitments and require major energy-efficiency investments. A model where external funds own towers and manage energy optimization eases operators' ESG burden.

## Yet Telecom Operators Face Risks: Structural Weaknesses

This model does not benefit operators alone. Cell tower rents become fixed costs. Even in downturns, operators must pay rents, and cost restructuring becomes difficult when telecom revenue declines. There is also risk of worsening lease renewal terms. Once assets are transferred, negotiating power diminishes.

Independent funds face their own challenges. Cell tower values are sensitive to technological change. Shifts in wireless frequency bands, transitions from 5G to 6G, and rapid energy-technology evolution can abruptly impair asset values.

## Summary

The scaling of telecom infrastructure funds and acceleration of cell tower acquisition means the telecom industry is shifting from an 'asset-ownership model' to a 'rent-payment model.' While this helps operators' near-term cash flow, over the long term it may increase cost rigidity and weaken competitiveness. Industry profitability will be determined at that inflection point.
