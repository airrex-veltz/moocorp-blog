The battery industry downturn is coming to an end. Following the slowdown in electric vehicle demand and inventory adjustment phase that lasted from 2023 to 2024, Samsung SDI faced performance challenges. However, signs of turnaround have been detected since the second half of 2025. Two catalysts — securing all-solid-state battery technology and benefiting from US IRA tax credits — are expected to provide momentum for a comeback.

<KeyPoints>
- Global top 5 EV battery manufacturer, supplying premium brands like BMW and Audi
- Clear all-solid-state battery roadmap: pilot production in 2027, mass production target in 2030
- Expected margin improvement through North American factory expansion driven by US IRA benefits
- Risks include price competition from Chinese manufacturers and EV demand recovery speed
</KeyPoints>

## Company Overview

Samsung SDI is a large-cap company with a market capitalization of approximately KRW 30 trillion, and a global battery manufacturer specializing in electric vehicle (EV) batteries, energy storage systems (ESS), and battery materials. It ranks among the world's top 5 battery makers alongside LG Energy Solution, CATL, and BYD, with particular strength in partnerships with premium electric vehicle brands. In recent 30 days, its stock price has shown volatility reflecting expectations of industry recovery and news of expanded North American factory investments.

## Fundamentals

Samsung SDI's 2024 revenue was approximately KRW 19 trillion, down 8% year-over-year. The primary causes were declining battery prices and slowed electric vehicle demand. Operating margin also fell to 2-3%, significantly lower than the historical 8-10% range. However, this is interpreted as the trough in the industry cycle, and actual recovery is expected from the second half of 2025 through inventory adjustment completion and improved premium product mix.

Key customers include premium brands such as BMW, Audi, Rivian, and GM. Notably, the full-scale mass production of cylindrical 46-series batteries has led to expectations for Tesla Cybertruck supply. In the North American market, the company plans to invest approximately KRW 5 trillion between 2025 and 2027 through Indiana factory expansion, aiming for margin improvement through IRA tax credits.

## Catalysts and Risks

<Callout type="catalyst" title="All-solid-state battery roadmap">
Samsung SDI is developing all-solid-state batteries targeting pilot production in 2027 and mass production in 2030. The company aims for energy density exceeding 900Wh/L, targeting twice the performance of existing lithium-ion batteries, while also expecting revolutionary improvements in safety and charging speed. The company is expected to form a duopoly with Toyota in the all-solid-state battery field globally.
</Callout>

<Callout type="catalyst" title="US IRA benefits">
Tax credits under the US Inflation Reduction Act (IRA) are expected to significantly improve profitability for Samsung SDI's North American factory investments. By expanding production capacity through Indiana factory expansion while simultaneously receiving tax credit benefits, the company can expect substantial margin improvement effects.
</Callout>

<Callout type="risk">
Price pressure from Chinese battery makers CATL and BYD represents a competitive challenge. Additionally, the possibility of sustained EV sales slowdown due to reduced electric vehicle subsidies in Europe and the US warrants careful monitoring. Near-term cash flow pressure from all-solid-state battery and North American factory investments, combined with high valuation (P/E 30+), also pose risks.
</Callout>

## Implications

Samsung SDI is preparing for a comeback following the battery industry downturn. All-solid-state battery technology leadership and US IRA benefits are core drivers of the medium to long-term growth story. In the short term, the timing of earnings turnaround in the second half of 2025 will be the key investment timing factor. Close monitoring of EV demand recovery speed and competitive dynamics with Chinese manufacturers, while observing whether the premium market strategy based on technological advantage succeeds, will be important.
