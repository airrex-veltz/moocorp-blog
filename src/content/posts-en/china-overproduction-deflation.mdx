Commodity mining stocks are plummeting in succession. **Rio Tinto** (RIO) closed at $72.73 as of November 20, 2026, down 30.60% over three months, and **Vale** (VALE) fell to $9.49 on the same day, down 36.90%. This sharp decline is not merely individual stock underperformance. China, the world's largest mineral consumer, is experiencing economic slowdown and industrial overcapacity, which are pressuring global commodity prices broadly, with mining company stock collapse cascading in response.

China's economic weakness signals are clear. As utilization rates of the steel, coal, and mineral production facilities that China holds in excess decline, raw material import demand has plummeted. Because China holds an overwhelming share of global mineral trade, contraction in China's demand immediately translates into global price signals. In Rio Tinto's case, net profit margin stands at 19.6% and revenue growth at 15.5% as of November 20, 2026, still showing positive momentum, but the three-month stock price decline of 30.60% reflects how severe the current price pressures are.

A more extreme case is Vale. With a 36.90% decline over the same period, Vale's net profit margin is 1.0%, nearly at break-even level, and revenue growth is minus 3.9%, with sales scale itself contracting. The P/E ratio is also extremely high at 59.3, suggesting the market has little expectation for future earnings. The difference in decline rates and profitability between Rio Tinto and Vale represents a process where China's industrial overcapacity crisis presses mineral prices in the short term while starkly revealing differences in operational fitness between mining companies.

The structural implication of this phenomenon is symmetric deflation. China's industrial overcapacity and resulting intensification of export competition are pressing global price levels downward, and beneath the appearance of inflation moderation in developed countries lies fundamental demand shortage. If commodity price weakness persists, profit improvement for mineral mining companies will become difficult.
