## Battery Pack $80/kWh Era: Structural Crisis in the EV Industry

Global battery production capacity is surging from 200 MWh in 2024 to over 400 MWh by 2027, locking in a vicious cycle of oversupply. The era of battery pack pricing at $120/kWh that guaranteed profitability is ending, and 2027 is expected to usher in extreme price competition with prices falling below $80/kWh. This is not merely a price decline but a signal that the economic model of the battery industry itself is collapsing.

## Snapshot of Overcapacity

As of 2024, global battery production capacity stands at approximately 200 MWh, representing a 33% surplus relative to demand of 150 MWh at the time. Major manufacturers including LG Energy Solution, CATL, and BYD are racing to build new factories, while Chinese players are making large-scale inroads into the ASEAN region. Cumulative capacity is estimated to reach 450 MWh by 2027, while global demand at that point is likely to remain at around 300 MWh. This will result in utilization rates plummeting to approximately 66%.

## Why Battery Prices Are Falling Now: Four Factors

**1. Aggressive Pricing Strategy by Chinese Battery Makers**: CATL and BYD, backed by overwhelming production scale, hold absolute competitive advantage in cost competition. Chinese battery packs are already selling at $90/kWh from 2026 onward, with prices projected to fall to $75/kWh by 2027. Global competitors have no choice but to match these prices or lose market share.

**2. Signal of EV Demand Plateau**: EV sales growth rates are moderating in advanced markets. U.S. EV sales in the first half of 2026 slowed to 3% year-over-year growth, with Europe showing a similar pattern. Battery capacity continues to expand based on past high-growth expectations, while actual demand falls short.

**3. Standardization Collapse from Battery Chemistry Diversification**: With multiple chemistry variants—LFP, NCA, NCM, LMFP—coexisting, manufacturers cannot concentrate production capacity on a single line. This dispersal of utilization rates across lines makes it difficult to recover fixed costs, intensifying price competition.

**4. Rise of Battery Recycling Market**: Improvements in end-of-life battery recycling technology have started to lower raw material costs. Lithium, nickel, and cobalt extracted from scrap are now trading at 80% of virgin ore prices, lowering the cost base for new battery manufacturing. This pushes down the price ceiling for the entire industry.

## Why the Battery Industry Persists Anyway: Structural Strengths and Weaknesses

**Irreversibility of Production Concentration**: Capital investment in battery production facilities is already in the hundreds of billions of dollars, and each country's battery self-sufficiency policies—including the Inflation Reduction Act and EU battery regulations—mandate regional production. Therefore, even at lower prices, production will not come to a complete halt.

**Long-Term Electrification Trend**: Although growth rates will slow in 2026–2027, EV sales are expected to reach 50% or more of total new vehicle sales by the mid-2030s. Absolute demand will be larger than today.

**Yet Margin Compression Is Permanent**: At the $80/kWh level, most manufacturers will likely record quarterly losses. Notably, Chinese EV makers like NIO and XPeng face direct pressure to cut battery costs, so their vehicle selling prices also face downward pressure. Li, a Chinese battery maker, will also struggle to protect margins amid price competition.

## Summary

2027, when battery pack prices fall below $80/kWh, marks a profitability inflection point for the EV industry. Entering an era of overcapacity, the market will consolidate around large-scale manufacturers strong in cost competition—CATL and BYD—while small and mid-sized players and emerging EV makers face severe profitability pressures.
