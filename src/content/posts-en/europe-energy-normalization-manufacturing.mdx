## Europe's Energy Normalization Revives Local Production Investment for Chemicals, Steel

Europe's industrial energy price normalization is reviving the economics of local production for chemicals and steel firms. As the severe energy supply crisis from 2021–2023 and price spikes have subsided, operational costs for energy-intensive industries across Europe are gradually stabilizing. This has become an inflection point where multinational corporations that shifted production bases to North America and Asia are now reconsidering local European investment.

## Post-Crisis Normalization of Europe's Industrial Energy Environment

From 2021–2023, Russia's invasion of Ukraine and supply chain disruption sent European natural gas and electricity prices to historic levels. Industrial electricity at the time jumped 3–5x year-over-year; energy-heavy sectors like chemicals and steel lost production economics. Multinational chemical and steel firms relocated or concentrated new investment in energy-cheaper regions like North America and India to cut costs.

Yet from 2024, Europe's energy market began stabilizing. Supply chains normalized, renewable expansion diversified power supply structure, and price volatility fell sharply. Industrial electricity has declined steeply over the past two years, and cost stabilization is expected to persist.

## Four Reinvestment Drivers Behind Europe's Energy Normalization

**1. Relative cost competitiveness restored**: With European energy costs normalized, energy-intensive industry productivity is improving. As the operating cost gap with North America narrows, Europe's structural advantages—short-haul transport, existing infrastructure, skilled labor—are getting fresh attention.

**2. Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) compliance imperative**: With EU carbon border adjustment looming, European production increasingly functions as a way to lower regulatory risk. On-site facilities directly control carbon emissions, easing future carbon tax burden.

**3. Supply chain diversification strategy**: Facing geopolitical tensions and expanding supply risks, multinationals are emphasizing nearby production within allied nations. Europe is being re-evaluated as a trusted production base within the democratic alliance.

**4. Government subsidies and incentives**: EU and member states provide various subsidies and tax benefits to support local energy-intensive investment. This lowers initial entry barriers.

## Three Chemicals-Steel Firms Signal Profit Recovery

**NUE** (Nucor) posts 23.0% YoY revenue growth, signaling steel price recovery and rising production utilization. Closing at 258.11 dollars on 2026-10-23, it reflects this year's profitability gains.

**LIN** (Linde) maintains stable performance with 20.4% net margin and 9.3% revenue growth. Closing at 508.83 dollars on 2026-10-23, it signals gradual industrial gas demand recovery.

**APD** (Air Products and Chemicals) is in an adjustment phase but trades at 307.15 dollars on 2026-10-23. Europe's energy normalization is expected to underpin long-term profitability recovery.

## Mid-term Structural Strengths and Challenges

European reinvestment carries structural advantages: existing customer bases, infrastructure, and regulatory leadership. Yet energy costs remain higher than North America, and long payback periods will constrain investment pace. If industrial demand weakness persists, European reinvestment discussions may fade.

## Summary

Europe's industrial electricity normalization post-energy crisis is reviving local production economics for chemicals and steel. Energy cost stability, carbon regulation response, supply chain diversification, and government support combine to drive reinvestment. How concretely this trend translates into investment over the next 3–5 years will determine the pace of global chemicals-steel industry restructuring.
