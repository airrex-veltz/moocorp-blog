## R&D Cost Explosion in AI Chip Design Signals Fabless Industry Restructuring

Advanced semiconductor development costs are rising sharply, and the semiconductor industry structure is undergoing rapid transformation. The fabless business model—which separates design from manufacturing—is losing its inherent advantages, and market consolidation around large well-capitalized and technically capable companies is intensifying.

As of October 27, 2026, the performance of major fabless firms clearly illustrates this shift. **AMD** trades at USD 560.26 with YoY revenue growth of +50.1%, but its P/E ratio stands at 145.6, showing that investors have already priced in strong growth. In contrast, **QCOM** trades at USD 184.17 with YoY revenue growth of -4.0%, struggling to gain momentum. **AVGO** achieved USD 342.33 with YoY revenue growth of +85.5%, yet its 3-month return of -7.39% suggests market expectations are growing stricter relative to reported results.

## Advanced Chip R&D Becomes a Barrier to Entry Wall

R&D costs for 3-nanometer and advanced semiconductor design have risen to levels incomparable to just a few years ago. Development of AI accelerators, advanced mobile processors, and datacenter chips demands investments in the tens of billions of dollars for design teams, verification, simulation, and collaboration with foundries like TSMC and Samsung. To recover these costs, companies must secure sufficient production volumes and ensure healthy margins.

Consequently, smaller fabless companies face enormous capital barriers from the outset. Design costs alone require several years' worth of a company's operating capital to be invested upfront, leaving no recovery path if they fail. Even established large fabless firms are shifting their strategies under cost pressures.

## Why Are Fabless Fortunes Diverging Now: Four Factors

**1. AI Demand Polarization**: In the AI accelerator chip market, only mega-scale players like NVIDIA and high-performance suppliers like AMD generate profits. Smaller fabless companies targeting mainstream segments are losing out in competition for advanced process capacity allocations from TSMC. AMD's +50.1% revenue growth signals the winner-take-most dynamic in advanced chip demand.

**2. QCOM's Weakening Licensing Business**: Historically, QCOM generated revenue from mobile processor design and wireless chipset licensing. However, YoY revenue growth of -4.0% reflects simultaneous headwinds from smartphone slowdown and China's chip self-sufficiency initiatives. Competition is intensifying in lower-barrier-to-entry segments.

**3. Stark Disparities in Net Margins**: AVGO's net margin of 42.9% stands among the highest in fabless, while QCOM's is 21.0% and AMD's just 15.6%. This suggests AVGO maintains high barriers to entry in its core segments (wired telecom chips and networking). By contrast, AMD's margin is compressed by massive R&D spending on CPU and GPU development.

**4. Intensifying Capital Turnover Requirements**: AMD and AVGO's strong revenue growth demonstrates success in capturing AI-driven opportunities, but also required massive upfront investment from the development stage. Smaller fabless firms lack the capital to sustain such rapid investment-to-growth cycles.

## Why the Fabless Model Still Survives: Structural Advantages

**High margins in niche segments**: Like AVGO, fabless firms can maintain high margins in competition-limited niche areas such as wired telecom infrastructure or specialized industrial chips. These segments combine low design costs with high barriers to entry.

**Design flexibility and rapid iteration**: Unlike ASIC design, speed to market matters in general-purpose chips. Fabless firms can focus on design without the burden of manufacturing facilities. This agility remains effective in specific segments.

**Deepening foundry partnerships**: As foundries like TSMC grow stronger, long-term partnerships with fabless firms are actually being reinforced. Mega-scale demand drives foundry investment in yield and process improvements, which ultimately raises cost competitiveness for all fabless players.

## Summary

R&D cost explosion in advanced chips during the AI era is deepening polarization in the fabless industry. QCOM's revenue stagnation, AMD's strong growth, and AVGO's elevated margin structure each reflect distinct competitive environments. While rising barriers to entry for smaller fabless firms are an industry reality, opportunities remain for companies with strong design capabilities and command of niche markets.
