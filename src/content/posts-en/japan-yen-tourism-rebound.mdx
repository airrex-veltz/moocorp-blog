## Yen Weakness and Japan's Tourism Demand Turnaround

As the yen weakens, foreign tourists visiting Japan are surging. Airlines and hotels are recovering profitability through increased passenger and guest volumes and higher fares.

## ANA Holdings' Performance Shift

**9202.T** ANA Holdings best reflects this shift. As of September 11, 2026 close, it stood at 2,935 JPY with year-over-year revenue growth of 22.6%. A net margin of 6.2% and PER of 9.2 suggest the stock is undervalued relative to the sector. This reflects both increased international passenger volumes and domestic currency gains.

## Four Drivers of Yen Weakness-Driven Demand Reversal

**1. Sharp Improvement in Exchange-Rate Value**: Yen weakness has caused Japan's relative prices to plummet from a foreign visitor's perspective. For Asia's middle class, Japan is no longer a premium destination.

**2. Surge in Asian Inbound Tourism**: Visitor demand from Korea, China, and Southeast Asia has surged. Budget airlines continue to expand routes.

**3. Fare and Accommodation Rate Increases**: Airlines and hotels have raised prices as demand surges. Load factors are improving while per-unit revenue is rising simultaneously.

**4. Direct Realization of Currency Gains**: International airfares are dollar-based, so yen weakness directly translates into higher yen-denominated revenue for Japanese carriers.

## Sustainability Review: Strengths and Constraints

**Persistent Inbound Demand**: If yen weakness persists, visitor growth is expected to continue. The currency benefit is structurally evident.

**Currency Reversal Risk**: If the yen strengthens, conditions could reverse sharply. Profitability is highly exposed to exchange-rate fluctuations.

**Oil Price Cost Pressure**: Rising crude prices increase fuel costs and compress margins. Passing all increases through fare hikes is difficult.

## Summary

Yen weakness and inbound tourism are structural tailwinds for Japan's airline industry. ANA's 22.6% revenue growth illustrates where the trend stands now. Going forward, exchange rates, oil prices, and capacity will determine the ceiling for growth.
