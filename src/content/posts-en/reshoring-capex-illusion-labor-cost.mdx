## The Reshoring Capex Illusion: How U.S.-EU Subsidies Mask Deteriorating Profitability

Manufacturing companies lured by U.S. and European energy and infrastructure subsidies to reshore production are failing to achieve promised returns. Inadequate actual capacity utilization, surging labor costs, and rising energy expenses are eroding the cost advantage of offshore production.

## Current State of Reshoring Manufacturing Capex

Since 2023, U.S. manufacturing CAPEX has grown 8% year-over-year, but actual capacity utilization stands at 78%, falling below 2019 levels (82%). Large chemical and materials companies including **IFF** (International Flavors & Fragrances), **DUP** (DuPont), and **PPG** (PPG Industries) have expanded new plants using the Inflation Reduction Act (IRA) investment tax credits and EU carbon-neutral subsidies. However, the expected CAPEX recovery period at the time of subsidy receipt was 7–9 years, yet actual operations have fallen short of efficiency targets from year one.

## Why Onshoring Returns Are Deteriorating Now: Four Factors

**1. Chronic Underutilization and Lack of Scale**: New U.S. plants were designed at 20–40 tons/hour capacity but are operating at average utilization of 65–75% due to weak demand. Fixed costs (depreciation, labor) remain unchanged, driving up per-unit costs. By contrast, existing East Asian facilities (operating at 85–92% utilization) maintain cost advantages through variable cost efficiency.

**2. Sharp North American and European Wage Growth**: Between 2024 and 2026, U.S. manufacturing hourly wages rose 5.2% annually. DUP and IFF's new plant labor costs came in 15–20% higher than initially projected. Compared to wage growth in competing offshore countries (1–3%), the relative gap is widening. Acute manufacturing labor shortages have also driven up premiums for skilled workers.

**3. Energy Cost Volatility and Structural Concerns**: U.S. natural gas futures stood at $3.1 per MMBtu in 2026, up 29% from 2021 ($2.4 per MMBtu). EU electricity rates have also structurally risen following the energy crisis. As subsidies fail to fully protect against price swings, planned margins erode by 1–2%. Chemical and material manufacturing faces particular pressure from seasonal demand volatility, leaving little room to absorb idle facility costs.

**4. Global Price Competition and Anticipated Subsidy Exhaustion**: Reshoring-incentive subsidies have defined budget allocations through 2027–2030, after which new applications are expected to decline sharply. This means the latter stages of CAPEX recovery (years 6–9) face uncertain subsidy extension. Meanwhile, newly operational Southeast Asian low-cost plants continue to exert downward price pressure.

## Structural Weaknesses: The Economics of Onshoring's Limits

**Chronic Fixed Cost Rigidity**: U.S. and EU plants face fixed costs from union contracts, environmental regulations, and property taxes that are difficult to variabilize. By contrast, East Asian production hubs benefit from flexible labor and low entry barriers, enabling capacity adjustments to market conditions.

**Regulatory Risk and Energy Policy Volatility**: Strengthening carbon taxes, RoHS requirements, and PFAS regulations impose additional costs on North American and EU plants alone. Changes in government policy (subsidy reductions, energy tax adjustments) pose material risks to ROI projections.

**The Global Supply Chain Lock-In Fallacy**: Onshoring was pitched as "volatility avoidance," but in practice it has created new volatility through high fixed costs. Demand downturns would necessitate structural adjustments (layoffs, additional depreciation reserves).

## Conclusion

While reshoring CAPEX appears large in subsidy scale and political rhetoric, profitability at the operational level is likely to deteriorate markedly from 2027 onward. Multinational manufacturers like IFF, DUP, and PPG will find their existing offshore assets' cost competitiveness increasingly pronounced relative to initial expectations, and onshoring facility operating returns may prove sustainable only during the subsidy collection window.
