## In an Age of Population Decline, Robots Replace Labor

Severe labor shortages in developed countries are driving a surge in demand for industrial robots. It is not just a story about large manufacturers; as cost barriers fall, small factories are also accelerating automation investment. The automation industry has entered a structurally strong long-term demand cycle.

## Robot Manufacturing Boom and Current Stock Price Adjustment

The case of Japan's robot manufacturing giant **6954.T (Fanuc)** proves this. Fanuc is trading at ¥5,776.00 as of the close on 2026-09-14, with year-over-year revenue growth of 17.7%. Net profit margin of 20.1% reflects the industry's high profitability. Nevertheless, the stock price has declined 21.35% over the past three months and 10.24% over the past month (as of 2026-09-14). A PER of 32.2 exceeds the industry average, though the possibility remains that long-term growth potential is not fully reflected in the price.

## Why Is Robot Adoption Accelerating Now: Four Factors

**1. Aging Population and Labor Supply Shock**: The working-age population is declining in developed countries such as Japan, Europe, and South Korea. There is an urgent need to fill the gap that cannot be addressed by wage increases with automation.

**2. Price Decline and Democratization of Industrial Robot Technology**: In the past, only large automakers and semiconductor companies adopted robots, but now prices have fallen to a level where various small and medium manufacturers in food, metals, textiles, and other sectors can invest. Cloud-based control systems have also lowered operational complexity.

**3. Surge in Demand from High-Growth Industries**: As semiconductor, battery, and electric vehicle production surge globally, the demand for production automation in these industries is simultaneously increasing. In particular, battery cell production lines require both precision and speed, so demand for advanced robots is high.

**4. Expansion of Government Support for Industrial Automation**: The governments of the United States, Japan, and Europe are providing tax benefits for automation investment, subsidies, and low-interest loans to restore their manufacturing base. This further lowers entry barriers for small and medium enterprises.

## Why the Robot Industry Remains Strong: Structural Strengths and Weaknesses

**Strengths**: Labor shortages are structural and long-term. Since population reversal is impossible, robot demand will persist over a 10-20 year timeframe. A 20% net profit margin at companies like Fanuc demonstrates high-tech barriers to entry.

**Weaknesses**: Recent stock price adjustments may reflect a recalibration of investor expectations for earnings growth. Questions may also be raised about whether 17.7% revenue growth is sufficient at a PER of 32.2. There is also a cycle in which large corporations' capital expenditure delays become the first casualty during economic slowdowns.

## Summary

Labor shortage is an unavoidable reality in developed countries, and automation investment is a rational response to it. Revenue growth among robot manufacturers including Fanuc demonstrates this structural demand. However, the current high valuation and recent stock price adjustments suggest the possibility of near-term corrections, and investment timing warrants careful consideration.
