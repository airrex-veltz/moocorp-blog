## The Corrugated Cardboard Oligopoly: Why Three Companies' 60% Control Pressures Last-Mile Logistics

Structural market power in the cardboard box industry is intensifying sharply. Three companies now control 60% of North American corrugated cardboard production capacity, a concentration set to deepen as new plants come online by 2027. Procurement teams' bargaining power has already weakened, and price increases are now inevitable within the next two years.

## North American Corrugated Cardboard Market Concentration Intensifies

Corrugated cardboard is the lifeline of e-commerce shipping. Every last-mile logistics network—from Amazon to local couriers—depends on it. Yet this market is a textbook case of capital-intensive manufacturing. Building a single plant costs $500 million to $1 billion, and economies of scale are absolute. The result is an oligopoly by design.

Today, three companies control roughly 60% of North American corrugated capacity. They are simultaneously modernizing existing plants and installing new production lines. By the end of 2027, as 1 million tons of new capacity comes online across North America, the competitive structure will become even more entrenched—small independent manufacturers cannot keep pace with this investment cycle.

## Why Prices Are Rising Now: Four Pressure Factors

**1. Raw Material Cost Lock-In and Energy Inflation**: Pulp and recycled fiber costs remain elevated despite global economic slowdown. As North American natural gas prices have rebounded due to summer cooling demand, factory operating costs will continue to climb. Procurement teams have already accepted minimum price increases of 3–5% per container.

**2. Structural Demand in Shipping and Logistics Networks**: Since the pandemic, sellers' preference for direct-to-consumer shipping has more than doubled demand for small corrugated boxes. With supply critically short, companies are being forced to use more expensive premium-grade products.

**3. Supply Disruptions Persist Until 2027 New Capacity Comes Online**: The North American corrugated industry currently maintains high utilization rates of 85–90%, meaning there is no buffer for unexpected shocks. Any demand fluctuation or plant maintenance instantly creates shortages. Procurement teams must pre-position inventory, which ultimately strengthens manufacturers' pricing power.

**4. International Trade Uncertainty and Localization Strategy**: As Chinese corrugated imports face restrictions, North American manufacturers are adopting increasingly hard-line positions justified by "local supply chain first" narratives. Competitors from Europe and Asia have become reluctant to enter the North American market.

## Still, Logistics Firms and 3PLs Face Structural Weaknesses

**Lost Bargaining Power**: For procurement teams, only "take-it-or-leave-it" negotiations remain. They can no longer solicit competitive bids from multiple suppliers. If one or two companies stop supplying, alternative sources require lead times of three weeks or longer.

**The Vicious Cycle of Financial Pressure**: Rising logistics costs hit small and medium-sized sellers hardest. Third-party logistics providers bound by fixed-margin contracts cannot pass corrugated price increases to customers and must absorb them.

## Takeaway

The oligopoly in the corrugated market is not merely a pricing issue—it signals a restructuring of last-mile logistics' entire cost architecture. Until new capacity comes online in 2027, procurement teams' bargaining power will continue to weaken, and structural vulnerabilities will intensify, particularly for large-scale logistics providers.
