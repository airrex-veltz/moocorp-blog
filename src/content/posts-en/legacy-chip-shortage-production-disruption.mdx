## Hidden Bottleneck Behind AI Boom: Legacy Chip Shortage Divides Auto and Industrial Equipment Supply

The semiconductor industry in the age of artificial intelligence faces a paradox. Demand for advanced chips for large-scale AI models has reached an all-time high, yet legacy process chips essential for automobiles and industrial equipment are becoming critically scarce. This gap is creating a fissure in the entire industrial ecosystem.

## Exploding Demand, Empty Spots Elsewhere

**TSMC**'s growth illustrates this well. As of October 2, 2026, the closing price was $472.78, up 14.09% over one month. Even more striking is revenue growth, which rose 36.0% year-over-year. Most of this sharp increase stems from orders for generative AI chips based on advanced nodes (5nm, 7nm).

But on the other side, companies like **INTC** and **F** show different signals. Intel rebounded 32.52% over one month, yet its net margin is negative (−19.8%). Revenue also grew 25.4% year-over-year, but structural losses remain. Ford is even more severe, falling 14.43% over one month while revenue declined 3.8% year-over-year. This difference reveals not a simple business cycle but a distortion in the semiconductor supply chain.

## Why Chips Fall Short Now for Auto and Industrial: Four Factors

**1. AI chip profits overshadow legacy nodes**: Global foundries like TSMC have deployed most of their 36% revenue growth to advanced nodes. The unit price of 5nm and 7nm chips is dozens of times higher than 28nm and older nodes. Return on investment does not work for legacy nodes. As a result, production capacity for 28nm and older nodes—essential for automobiles and IoT devices—has relatively contracted.

**2. Automotive semiconductor demand has surged**: In the transition to electric vehicles, the types and quantities of semiconductors per vehicle have grown sharply. Battery management systems, autonomous driving sensor processing, and infotainment systems all require semiconductors. Most of these are still produced at legacy nodes like 28nm, 40nm, and 55nm as of 2026. Process migration takes 3–5 years.

**3. Legacy fabs cannot realistically be refurbished**: 28nm and older nodes are now classified as "lagging nodes," operating only in facilities built in the 1990s to early 2010s. Expansion and modernization of these fabs are not pursued seriously by any foundry because return on investment is low. Fierce cost competition also means margins are one-tenth those of advanced nodes.

**4. Planning for process transition was inadequate**: During the semiconductor boom of 2020, demand was abundant across all nodes. Few industry players anticipated the rapid restructuring around AI chips that followed. Today's shortage is the consequence of decisions made 3–4 years ago. Even if new fabs are built and processes developed now, completion takes a minimum of 24 months.

## A Structure That Cannot Stop: Strengths and Weaknesses

**TSMC's Dilemma**: The 14.09% rise over one month reflects the AI chip boom. However, even as automotive and industrial customers continuously request 27nm and 40nm nodes, unfilled orders (backlog) only grow. Customers denied once do not return on recontract costs.

**Intel's Turnaround Potential**: The 32.52% gain over one month despite negative net margin signals market recognition of structural reform. Intel is transitioning to a vertically integrated model where it can supply both AI chips and legacy chips as it strengthens fab internalization (IDM). However, this process may take five years or more.

**Ford's Supply Sensitivity**: The 14.43% decline over one month and 3.8% revenue drop are immediate impacts of chip supply disruption. The automotive industry's production swings sharply even with 1–2% changes in chip supply rates. This is because there are no substitutes for legacy chips.

## Takeaway

The surge in AI chip demand simultaneously creates light and shadow for the semiconductor industry. While advanced nodes grow 36% annually, automotive and industrial legacy chips emerge only from decades-old facilities. This imbalance will persist as long as electric vehicle transition and industrial IoT expansion continue. Companies exposed to this dual supply structure have no choice but to experience stark contrasts.
