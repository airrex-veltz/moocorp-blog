# Commercial Real Estate Interest Rate Shock: Buildings Trapped by Refinancing Wall and REIT Value Decline

The sharp interest rate increases from 2022 through 2023 delivered a structural shock to the commercial real estate (CRE) market. Buildings that secured funding at low rates are now facing maturity and forced refinancing in today's high-rate environment, while sale options remain constrained. The September 2026 stock price movements of major commercial real estate REITs demonstrate that this pressure continues to operate.

## Broad Stock Price Decline, Particularly Among High-Vacancy New York Office Tenants

As of September 28, near-term declines among tracked major REITs stand out. **SL Green Realty Corp. (SLG)** posted a one-month drop of -14.70%, **Vornado Realty Trust (VNO)** fell -11.93%, and **Boston Properties (BXP)** declined -10.75%. **Kimco Realty Corporation (KIM)** showed relative resilience at -4.40%, though on a three-month basis it is down -11.88%.

These declines should be read not as simple market adjustment but as signals of asset revaluation. SLG in particular operates a portfolio concentrated in New York Manhattan office buildings, where post-pandemic remote work expansion combined with high-rate-driven occupancy rises are working in tandem. VNO shows heavy dependence on New York and Washington DC assets, carrying similar structural vulnerabilities.

## Deteriorating Profitability and Valuation Compression

Profitability metrics revealed by market data are more concerning. SLG's net profit margin stands at **-16.7%**, in loss territory, while VNO's net margin is merely 3.6%. BXP maintains 9.3% and KIM 27.8% net margins, relatively sound, yet suggesting that across the sector profitability is under pressure when benchmarked against industry peers.

Valuation has compressed dramatically in reflection. VNO's P/E ratio has reached **1136.7**, a level that is nearly meaningless—indicating that earnings per share is near zero. This signals that VNO is experiencing severe asset value impairment relative to net income. BXP (P/E 33.6) and KIM (P/E 26.2) maintain relatively normal multiples, but both trade at valuations well below historical levels.

## Revenues Growing But Profits Deteriorating: A Signal of Structural Trouble

Paradoxically, revenue growth rates are mixed. SLG recorded high revenue growth of 27.3% despite negative net margins, indicating that tenant recruitment difficulty—manifest in rent concessions, vacancy-period losses, and heavy interest burdens on high debt loads—is consuming all revenue gains. VNO (5.0%), BXP (3.7%), and KIM (4.9%) show stable revenue growth, but this likely reflects existing lease renewals or minor price increases rather than genuine tenant demand recovery.

## Refinancing Wall and Valuation Adjustment

The core challenge facing commercial real estate REITs is **concentrated refinancing maturity**. Debt incurred at low rates before the 2022-2023 rate increases is maturing in large tranches between 2025 and 2027. The reality is setting in: debt raised in the low-rate era must be re-financed in today's high-rate environment, while net operating income (NOI) on buildings stagnates or declines, causing debt service burdens to surge.

Sale as a solution is also blocked. In the high-rate environment, CRE buyers' required investment returns have risen, forcing buildings into valuation impairment. Between 2024 and 2025, many owners postponed or abandoned sales, raising suspicion that assets' true values are far below book values.

## Sector Differentiation: Asset Type Is Destiny

Within this crisis, Kimco Realty is tracing a somewhat different trajectory. KIM operates a portfolio concentrated in retail real estate such as shopping centers, where economic activity ensures basic tenant demand to persist, and achieves a higher net margin of 27.8%. Boston Properties, though an office landlord, is diversified across major global cities including Boston, San Francisco, and London, facing less acute pressure across the board than New York-concentrated REITs.

By contrast, VNO and SLG face direct exposure to New York's structural office market contraction. With New York office vacancy rates persisting near historic highs, refinancing pressure is no longer a mere financial issue but a fundamental re-evaluation of asset earnings power.

## Preemptive Valuation Adjustment Needed, Investor Caution Phase

The broad decline of September 2026 suggests the market already judges these REITs' disclosed NAV (net asset value) as overstated. VNO's extreme P/E in particular reflects value destruction so severe that net income itself has lost meaning. Over the next three to four quarters, as refinancing outcomes and tenant retention reports emerge, the market will likely face further declines or dividend-cut anxieties.

Investment judgment on commercial real estate REITs has now shifted from the historical dividend-yield framework to a comprehensive assessment of **asset revaluation, refinancing feasibility, and portfolio geographic diversification**. Though the rate shock passed two years ago, the refinancing wall is projected to persist for another two to three years, and within this period REITs carrying weak portfolios are likely to see accelerating share price adjustment.
