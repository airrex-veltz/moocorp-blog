## Green Manufacturing Under Pressure: IRA's Profitability Challenge and the Limits of Government Incentives

Three years after the Inflation Reduction Act (IRA) of 2023 unleashed massive funding into US green manufacturing, renewable energy and battery companies that launched with government support are recording unexpectedly low profitability, forcing a reassessment of policy effectiveness. The soundness of both capital allocation and technology economics is now in question.

## The IRA and Green Manufacturing Reality

The IRA provided annual tax credits worth $39 billion for climate investments. It wasn't just subsidies—tax credits fundamentally redirected capital flows. The problem is that money flowing in doesn't automatically produce returns.

**TSLA** is a major corporation valued at $1.6 trillion with a closing price of $391.62 as of October 26, 2026, but its net profit margin is only 3.1%. More concerning is the extraordinarily high valuation with a P/E of 445.6. The market expects Tesla to deliver enormous profits in the future, yet current revenue growth is -0.2%, stalled in place. This signals the end of the early high-growth story as the EV market matures.

**NEE** (NextEra Energy) is a key player in renewable energy generation. With a closing price of $82.04 on October 26, 2026, it boasts healthy profitability with a net margin of 40.4%. Yet revenue growth came in at just 1.6%. It has established itself as a stable cash-flow business, but the growth engine has stalled.

The most dramatic signal comes from **PLUG** (Plug Power). A hydrogen energy startup, PLUG closed at $1.37 on October 26, 2026, with a one-month decline of -30.81%. Its net margin stands at -220.6%, recording severe losses. Despite government support, it cannot absorb costs at the commercialization stage.

## Why Profitability Is Deteriorating Now: Four Pressure Factors

**1. Gradual Reduction in Government Incentives:** IRA tax credits decline on a predetermined schedule. Initially, companies receive maximum credits, but as years pass and deployment volumes grow, per-unit support diminishes. For companies dependent solely on government funding, that moment of declining reliance is now—2026.

**2. Excess Competition and Margin Pressure:** As growth signals in green industries became clear, global giants entered the market. Tesla, Ford, and GM battled in EV competition, while China's BYD dominated with battery cost advantages. When government subsidies exist, competition is muted; when incentives disappear, cost competitiveness is laid bare.

**3. Capital Intensity and Insufficient Scale:** Battery factories and hydrogen fuel cell facilities require initial investments in the billions. Both Tesla's high P/E and Plug Power's severe losses stem from the same root cause: companies cannot absorb fixed costs without achieving sufficient scale.

**4. Uncertain Technology Economics:** Green technologies like hydrogen, advanced batteries, and direct air capture (DAC) are not yet fully commercialized. Plug Power's plunge signals growing market skepticism about hydrogen energy economics itself.

## Why This Momentum Remains Unbroken: Structural Strength

**Policy Continuity and Long-Term Demand:** The IRA is a 20-year US energy policy. Short-term profitability declines won't reverse it. Renewable energy capacity expansion targets remain on track, and EV sales mandates are strengthening.

**Validation of the NextEra Model:** Companies like NextEra have proven at 40% net margins that renewable energy business itself generates healthy cash flow even without government incentives. Growth is simply limited.

## Summary

The IRA's green investment has accelerated technology progress and economies of scale. Yet three years of data show that government money cannot save every company. Companies like Tesla content with high valuations and startups like Plug Power with unproven technology economics are vulnerable to incentive reduction. Cash-flow businesses like NextEra will survive the long game. Capital allocation efficiency is now determined not by government support but by each company's competitive strength.
