## Rising Treasury Yields: The Tax of Aging

Government debt in advanced economies is a structural product of fiscal deficits. As pension and healthcare costs rise with aging populations while tax revenues stagnate, deficits become inevitable. This growing demand for government bonds and the resulting yield increases are becoming an irresistible trend. Treasury bonds are government borrowing instruments, and their yields reflect both the market's credit assessment and the magnitude of fiscal deficits.

## U.S. Treasury ETFs Today: Signs of Medium-Term Weakness

**iShares 20+ Year Treasury Bond ETF (TLT)** trades at $81.69 as of November 23, 2026. Its one-month change is +0.85%, but the three-month change is -0.95%, signaling medium-term weakness. The six-month range has been between $76.11 and $86.07.

**Vanguard Total Bond Market Index Fund ETF Shares (BND)** trades at $71.36 as of November 23, 2026, with a one-month change of +0.59% and a three-month change of -0.75%, also showing medium-term weakness. The six-month range has been between $69.40 and $72.45. This weakness reflects the inverse relationship between rising interest rates and existing bond values.

## Why Are Treasury Yields Rising Now? Four Factors

**1. Structural fiscal deficits:** Healthcare and pension spending in aging societies is non-discretionary. Advanced economies have already embraced welfare-state frameworks, and the beneficiary pool is growing rapidly. Tax revenue growth is tied to economic expansion, but spending is not. The vicious cycle of filling this gap with government bonds has become entrenched.

**2. Central banks' quantitative easing tapering:** As liquidity injected after the pandemic is withdrawn, a major pillar supporting Treasury demand has disappeared. Now private investors demand higher yields. This is why central banks have no choice but to raise policy rates.

**3. The structural roots of inflation:** Supply-chain disruptions and labor shortages are not temporary. Particularly in aging advanced economies, declining labor supply creates upward wage pressure. Persistent price increases that cannot be controlled by rate hikes alone erode the real returns of bonds.

**4. Bond market credit reassessment:** As the government debt ratios of the United States and Europe approach historic highs, concerns about credit rating adjustments are growing. While default risk remains low, the signal is clear: the safe-asset status of advanced-economy government bonds is not as solid as it once was. Investors demand higher yields to compensate for this risk.

## Structural Weaknesses: The Bondholder's Dilemma

**In a rising-rate environment, bonds are no longer a perfect hedge.** Over the past decade, bonds have offset equity volatility. But at the outset of a rate cycle reversal, existing bondholders face losses. The negative three-month returns on both TLT and BND are evidence of this.

**There is no reversibility in the government deficit structure.** In advanced-economy political systems, it is practically impossible to slash social spending drastically. That leaves only three options: raise taxes, achieve rapid economic growth, or default on government debt. All three are painful or impossible.

## In Summary

The rise in government bond yields in advanced economies is an inevitable result of the structural problem of aging populations. This is not a cyclical fluctuation but rather reflects the long-term trend of demographic change. Bondholders must accept this reality and reconsider their portfolio allocation at this juncture.
