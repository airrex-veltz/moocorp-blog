## How US Interest Rate Hikes Drive Emerging Markets Into a Dual Crisis

In the global economy, the Federal Reserve's interest rate decisions deliver direct and severe blows to emerging markets. In particular, during an interest rate hike cycle, emerging markets face a simultaneous two-pronged crisis: forex reserve depletion and sovereign debt default risk. This is an inevitable consequence arising from structural imbalances in financial markets.

## The Structural Origins of the Dual Pressure Facing Emerging Markets

When the US raises interest rates, emerging market economies face immediate capital outflow pressure. As US bond yields rise, international capital begins to prefer US Treasury bonds or high-yield US corporate bonds over emerging market assets with higher risk. This is not a simple choice but rather the operation of global capital's "safe asset preference" phenomenon.

Simultaneously, US interest rate hikes increase the cost of foreign debt repayment for emerging markets. Most emerging markets have issued foreign-currency-denominated debt, particularly dollar-denominated sovereign debt. As rising rates increase the cost of dollar borrowing, the value of already-issued debt becomes relatively heavier. When issuing new debt, they must offer higher coupons, so the burden on national finances surges.

## Four Mechanisms That Intensify the Emerging Market Crisis

**1. The Vicious Cycle of Capital Outflows**: As international investors withdraw from emerging market assets, demand for local currencies falls. The weakening of local currencies makes the repayment burden of foreign debt borrowed by emerging markets even heavier. When sovereign wealth funds or central banks attempt to defend their currencies by using foreign exchange reserves, the depletion of these reserves itself acts as a 'default risk signal,' inducing further outflows.

**2. Surge in Foreign Debt Repayment Costs**: Most emerging market foreign debt is exposed to floating-rate conditions or refinancing terms based on maturity dates. When US rates rise, they must cope with much higher rates when renewing borrowing. When a country's foreign exchange earnings stagnate or decline while foreign debt repayment increases, forex reserves deplete rapidly.

**3. Global Demand Weakening and Export Shock**: US interest rate hikes weaken global demand. When the US and European economies, major export markets for emerging markets, contract, emerging markets' export revenues decline. As foreign currency inflows shrink while foreign debt repayment remains fixed, the relative value of forex reserves deteriorates rapidly.

**4. Self-Reinforcing Currency Depreciation Spiral**: Local currency depreciation increases the burden of foreign debt and induces additional capital outflows. The more emerging market central banks attempt to defend their currencies by using forex reserves, the faster the depletion accelerates. The news of forex reserve depletion itself amplifies default concerns, triggering another wave of capital outflows, forming a vicious cycle.

## Structural Weaknesses and Policy Constraints Facing Emerging Markets

**Foreign Exchange Dependency and Fundamental Limitations**: Most emerging markets must import energy, raw materials, and food, making foreign currency acquisition essential. However, sources of foreign currency revenues depend heavily on global economic conditions and commodity prices. During US interest rate hike cycles, weak global demand and falling commodity prices occur almost simultaneously, delivering a double blow to emerging markets' foreign currency inflows.

**The Dilemma of Policy Options**: Emerging market central banks must raise interest rates to protect foreign exchange reserves, but this suppresses domestic economic growth. If they try to lower rates, currency depreciation accelerates and foreign debt burdens increase. Within this dilemma, emerging markets lack an optimal policy choice. To receive support from international financial institutions, they must accept austerity policies, further compounding domestic political burdens.

## Conclusion

US interest rate hikes do not inflict a mere economic shock on emerging markets but simultaneously trigger a dual crisis of forex reserve depletion and sovereign debt default risk. Capital outflows, surging foreign debt repayment costs, weak exports, and currency depreciation interact to form a vicious cycle. For emerging markets to overcome this crisis, diversifying pathways to foreign currency acquisition and improving the medium-term structure of foreign debt are necessary, but implementing such reforms during a crisis is practically difficult.
