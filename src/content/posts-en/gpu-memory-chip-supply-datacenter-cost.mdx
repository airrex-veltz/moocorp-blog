## GPU Memory Chip Price Surge Erodes Datacenter Investment ROI

Generative AI and cloud infrastructure booms are driving datacenter investment explosions. Yet as HBM and GDDR memory chip supplies—critical GPU components—fail to keep pace, prices have surged. This bottleneck is not merely a chip problem. It is reshaping the structure of entire datacenter construction and operating costs.

## GPU Memory Supply Bottleneck Pressures Entire Semiconductor Value Chain

**NVIDIA** (**NVDA**) trades at USD 221.30 close (as of 2026-10-14). One-month gain of 4.30% is modest. Yet the more noteworthy metric is revenue growth. YoY shows 105.9% growth with net profit margin reaching 63.7%. GPU chip demand remains explosive.

**AMD** (**AMD**) occupies a different position. Close USD 570.82 on 2026-10-14 with one-month gain of 13.21%, exceeding NVIDIA. Revenue growth rate is 50.1%. Yet PER of 150.5 shows extremely high multiples. Net profit margin of 15.6% is one-quarter of NVIDIA's level.

**Micron** (**MU**) is more extreme. Close USD 914.13, one-month change -1.44% shows weakness. Yet revenue growth rate of 379.3% overwhelmingly exceeds the three. Net profit margin of 63.8% nearly matches NVIDIA, while PER 12.8 is cheapest among the three. As memory chip supply shortages force prices up, memory manufacturers like Micron enjoy extreme growth.

## Why Memory Chip Shortages Become Main Issue Now: Four Pressure Factors

**1. HBM and GDDR supply shortages raising GPU costs**: GDDR6/GDDR7 and high-bandwidth HBM chips can be mass-produced only at a few Taiwan and Japan factories. Amid AI chip demand explosion, memory chip supply remains chronically short. This directly raises GPU board costs.

**2. Datacenter capital expenditure plans delayed and reviewed**: Hyperscale cloud operators are postponing construction plans or scaling them down as GPU prices exceed expectations. Public statements mention memory chip prices rising 30-50% from initial budget estimates. Overall project ROI deteriorates.

**3. GPU board cost expanding share in datacenter CapEx**: Early datacenter construction saw land, cooling, power infrastructure as main costs. Now GPU board prices approach 40-50% of total. Memory chip cost increases increasingly determine entire project economics.

**4. Long-term contract renegotiation and multi-tier supply chain complexity costs**: Many cloud operators weakened negotiation power trying to fix memory chips through long-term futures. Complex supply chains enlarged intermediate distribution margins. "Hidden costs" reflected in final GPU prices expand.

## Yet the Three Stocks' Structural Positions Differ Sharply

**NVIDIA's margin power and GDDR/HBM dominance flow**: NVIDIA's 63.7% net profit margin starkly reveals pricing power in supply shortage conditions. Short-term, cost increases pass sufficiently to customers. Yet PER 28.4 already embeds high expectations. Unclear what happens if datacenter investment stalls.

**AMD's valuation risk**: 50.1% revenue growth is excellent, but 15.6% net profit margin suggests cost structure vulnerability. PER 150.5 is speculative. If memory costs rise, defending margins appears difficult.

**Micron's structural memory oversupply benefit**: The severer memory shortages, the more suppliers like Micron gain. At PER 12.8 undervaluation, recording 379.3% growth. 63.8% net profit margin stays strong despite cost pressure. Yet valid only if memory shortage persists.

## Summary

GPU memory chip shortage is not merely raw material price issue. It deteriorates entire datacenter investment ROI structure. NVIDIA remains strong, but if datacenter customer investment enthusiasm wanes, growth structure could falter. AMD vulnerable to margin defense. Micron maximum beneficiary if shortage worsens but reverses 180 degrees once supply normalizes. This tension likely continues until memory supply loosens.
