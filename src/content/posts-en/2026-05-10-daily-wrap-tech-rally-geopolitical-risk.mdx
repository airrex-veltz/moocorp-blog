The U.S. stock market showed strong gains led by technology stocks. Nasdaq surged 2.34%, far outpacing the S&P 500's gain of 0.83%. AMD led the semiconductor sector with an 11.44% jump, while Tesla rose 4.02%, continuing electric vehicle strength. Microsoft, however, fell 1.34%, showing some profit-taking in large software stocks.

## Today's Market

The S&P 500 closed at 737.62, rising 0.83%, while Nasdaq gained 2.34% to 711.23. Among individual stocks, AMD led with an 11.44% surge to $455.19, likely reflecting expanded AI server chip demand and earnings expectations. Tesla rose 4.02% to $428.35, while Apple and Nvidia gained 2.05% and 1.75%, respectively. Microsoft was the exception, declining 1.34% to $415.12.

## Sector Trends

The semiconductor sector showed the most pronounced gains, led by AMD's strength. Autonomous driving and electric vehicle sectors continued their momentum alongside Tesla's 4% rise. Most large-cap tech stocks rose modestly, but profit-taking emerged in some large software names, causing a pullback. Microsoft's decline and AMD's surge suggest a possible rotation of capital from large-cap to mid-cap stocks.

Meanwhile, the strength in U.S. semiconductors could send positive signals to Korean semiconductor firms like Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix. However, Korean market data collection faced constraints today, making confirmation difficult.

## Geopolitical Risk

Geopolitical tensions in the Middle East are escalating. Reports of drone attacks on Kuwait, ship attacks on Qatar, and Iranian military movements followed one another, while the WSJ covered allegations of Israeli covert base operations in Iraq. Britain dispatched naval vessels to the Middle East in preparation for Strait of Hormuz operations. Such tensions could raise inflation pressure if they lead to oil price increases and increase energy sector volatility. Interest in defense stocks is also expected to rise.

## Tomorrow's Watch Points

Watch whether tech strength will persist or profit-taking pressure emerges. It remains to be seen if AMD's 11% surge is a one-time event or a signal of broader semiconductor momentum reversal. The impact of Middle East geopolitics on oil prices and the possibility of Fed policy shift are also watch points. The rotation flow between large-cap and mid-cap stocks should also continue to be tracked.
