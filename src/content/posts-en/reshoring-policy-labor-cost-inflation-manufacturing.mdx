## Manufacturing Returns to Advanced Economies, Costs Rise

The reshoring frenzy unleashed by the CHIPS Act, IRA (Inflation Reduction Act), and EU Green Deal is now beginning to show in the numbers. Expansion of production within advanced economies promised job recovery, but soaring labor costs are driving up manufacturing costs sharply, signaling a reignition of inflation.

## Government Support Fuels Rapid Growth—But Costs Rise Too

**GE** recorded 21.1% annual revenue growth as of 2026 (closing price USD 327.09 on 2026-09-25). Aerospace and energy segments are serving as key beneficiaries of reshoring policy. **RTX** continues 14.5% growth (USD 189.40), while **LMT** (USD 519.56), the largest defense contractor, shows 10.5% growth as it responds to increased U.S. Department of Defense orders.

Demand for base metals like copper and aluminum is particularly strong. **SCCO** leads with 40.6% revenue growth (USD 203.92). Demand for copper has surged due to semiconductor, battery, and power grid expansion needs.

However, beneath the growth lies a concern. With wages in the U.S. and Europe more than twice Asia's levels, expanding local production means rising manufacturing costs.

## Why These Pressures Are Building Now: Four Factors

**1. Expanding and deepening policy support**: The CHIPS Act provides up to USD 39 billion in semiconductor investment support, and the IRA offers three years of tax benefits for clean manufacturing. The EU is directly funding factory construction to raise battery and chip self-sufficiency. As these policies overlap, localization competition intensifies.

**2. Geopolitical risk crystallizes**: Mounting geopolitical uncertainty around Taiwan and China has led companies to view supply chain diversification as a necessity, not a choice. Reshoring is now a survival strategy, not an option.

**3. Advanced economy wage pressures**: The U.S. and Europe maintain historically low unemployment rates. With tight labor supply and demand, wage increase demands in manufacturing are intensifying.

**4. Commodity price strength**: Reshoring has triggered surging demand for copper and aluminum, pushing prices higher. This is why mining companies like SCCO record 40.6% growth. Rising costs ripple across all industries.

## Winners Exist, But Consumers Pay the Price

**Government contract protection**: Defense and heavy industry have fixed government contract volumes. There is greater room to pass cost increases to government. This explains why LMT and RTX maintain high growth despite rising costs.

**But consumer goods differ**: Consumer electronics and automobiles have limited pricing power. Manufacturing inflation is likely to flow through to consumer prices. This could pressure central banks to raise rates, slowing economic growth.

## Takeaway

Reshoring is actually happening, and growth signals are clear. But the cost of that growth is already rising. Industries like defense, where government absorbs costs, see benefits; however, it will likely end up as consumer price inflation.
