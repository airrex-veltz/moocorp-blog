## Hypersonic Passenger Aircraft Era Opens New Revenue Channels for Aviation Parts Suppliers

As the commercialization race for hypersonic aircraft accelerates, existing aviation parts suppliers and engine manufacturers are gaining new contract opportunities. Most components—engines, fuselages, and materials—must be newly developed to enable sustained flight at Mach 5 and above.

## Growth Trajectories of Aviation Parts Giants BA and RTX

**BA (Boeing Company)** and **RTX (RTX Corporation)** are the twin pillars of aviation parts and defense industries. As of 2026, RTX's revenue growth rate of 14.5% significantly outpaces BA's 8.0%. In net profit margin, RTX's 8.3% far exceeds BA's 2.6%. Market capitalization also shows RTX dominating at USD 265.3 billion versus BA's USD 159.6 billion. With demand recovery in existing aviation infrastructure behind them, both companies need new growth drivers.

## Why Hypersonic Aviation Is Critical to Growth Now: Four Factors

**1. Radical Advances in Engine Technology for Hypersonic Flight**: To sustain flight speeds above Mach 5 reliably requires engine designs entirely different from conventional turbofan engines. Development of hypersonic-specific engines demands R&amp;D investment in the trillions of won, guaranteeing sustained contracts for engine manufacturers like RTX.

**2. Airlines' Growing Backlog of New Aircraft Orders**: While existing large aircraft adoption cycles have lengthened, hypersonic passenger aircraft are positioned as "next generation." As major airlines such as American and United express pre-order interest, new supply chain demand emerges.

**3. Need for Aircraft Material Innovation**: Because fuselage exterior temperatures during hypersonic flight reach hundreds of degrees Celsius, existing aluminum and composite materials alone are insufficient. Demand for high-value components—ceramic-based thermal barrier materials and ultra-high-strength titanium alloys—is surging.

**4. Expansion of Simulation and Testing Infrastructure**: Wind tunnel testing, computer simulation, and approval processes to demonstrate hypersonic flight safety span multiple years. During this period, parts manufacturers generate initial revenue through prototype supply and technical validation services.

## Structural Opportunities and Constraints for Existing Aviation Parts Suppliers

**Supply Chain Entry Barriers Strengthened**: BA and RTX possess aviation customer networks, delivery systems, and quality certification frameworks accumulated over decades. Developers of hypersonic aircraft have no choice but to prefer collaboration with existing parts suppliers whose reliability is proven. Meanwhile, trading at PER 72.4 (BA) and 34.6 (RTX), the market recognizes strong growth potential in these companies.

**Time and Cost of Material Development**: Hypersonic aircraft-specific parts—such as ultra-low-temperature oxygen tanks and ultra-high-temperature engine casings—demand material innovation beyond simple machining. This means development cycles of 3–5 years and high failure rates. Only suppliers with significant scale can bear these costs.

## Summary

Hypersonic passenger aircraft commercialization is an opportunity for major aviation parts suppliers like BA and RTX to diversify revenue. In particular, RTX's strong profitability—8.3% net profit margin and 14.5% revenue growth as of September 16, 2026—demonstrates capacity for sustained R&amp;D investment. However, the high difficulty and extended timeline of hypersonic parts development could be a burden on near-term earnings.
