## The World's Semiconductor Industry Seeks to Break Taiwan Dependence

The semiconductor supply chain has emerged as a frontline of geopolitics. With simultaneous investment under the U.S. CHIPS Act, Japan's strategic strengthening of foundry capacity, and South Korea's advanced technology race, **TSMC's** overwhelming dominance is no longer a safe harbor. Diversification pressure is fundamentally reshaping the long-term structure of the global semiconductor industry.

## TSMC's Standing and Reality

**TSMC** remains the absolute powerhouse. As of September 18, 2026, closing price of $434.67 and market capitalization of $2.25 trillion dominate the industry. A net profit margin of 49.9% and revenue growth rate of 36.0% are indicators of efficiency. Yet these figures offer no guarantee of future profits. Customers have begun chanting "our own chips, our own lines."

## Why the Supply Chain Is Being Reorganized Now: Four Factors

**1. The U.S. CHIPS Act Comes to Reality — Intel's Last Chance**

The United States treats domestic semiconductor manufacturing as a security issue. **INTC** stands at $108.60 as of September 18, 2026, with a market capitalization of $574.1 billion. With a net profit margin of -19.8% in deficit, annual revenue growth of 25.4% and government support plant seeds of hope. The U.S. government will not stop investing while Intel recovers its ability to produce advanced processes on its own. TSMC operates a factory in Arizona, but advanced nodes remain concentrated in Taiwan.

**2. Geopolitical Tension Becomes Structural — Taiwan Risk Materializes**

As U.S.-China conflict transforms from contingent shock to structural constant, dependence on Taiwan alone has been branded as business risk. Placing the semiconductor industry's critical pivot at a geopolitical hotspot is unacceptable from shareholders' perspectives. The moment when TSMC's strength paradoxically becomes its vulnerability arrives.

**3. Japan's Fiscal Offensive — Sony and Others Strengthen Foundry Capacity**

Japan is moving into action as well. With government support, Sony and other manufacturers are gradually strengthening their foundry capabilities. While not cutting-edge, specialized processes and stable supply will be their weapons to secure some customers.

**4. South Korea's Samsung Electronics' Technology Reversal — The EUV War**

**SSNLF** stands at $65.21 as of September 18, 2026, with a market capitalization of $428.2 billion. Annual revenue growth of 130.0% and net profit margin of 30.8% demonstrate recovery. Samsung's progress in extreme ultraviolet (EUV) technology represents the most direct challenge to TSMC. The will to seize industry leadership meets demand for supply chain diversification, creating rising momentum.

## Why It's Still TSMC: Structural Advantages and Practical Weaknesses

**The technology gap will not narrow easily.** TSMC's design-to-manufacturing integration and customer relationships cannot be replicated with one to two years of investment. A net profit margin of 49.9% demonstrates how strong economies of scale are.

**But geopolitics trumps everything.** Diversification is not a matter of efficiency but a survival strategy. TSMC must establish production facilities in the U.S., Japan, and South Korea alike. The resulting cost increases and margin pressures are unavoidable.

## Summary

The reorganization of the semiconductor supply chain is not a TSMC stock issue but an industry structural shift. Technology leadership will persist, but the cost of geopolitical diversification will erode margins for years to come. Meanwhile, capital directly injected by U.S., Japanese, and South Korean governments will enhance the stability of global chip supply, but will gradually dilute TSMC's dominance.
