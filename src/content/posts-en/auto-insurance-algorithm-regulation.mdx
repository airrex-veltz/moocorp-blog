## Auto insurance rate algorithms are facing regulatory scrutiny, shaking up industry structure.

Data-driven rate-setting has been a core competitive weapon for property and casualty insurers for decades. By inputting driving records, credit scores, location, age, and vehicle model, insurers predicted individual risk and priced accordingly. But recently, regulators in the US and Europe have begun publicly scrutinizing the transparency and fairness of these algorithms. Claims that algorithms may work discriminatorily against certain racial groups or low-income populations have prompted insurers to redesign how they calculate rates.

## Insurance big three send mixed signals.

**Progressive Corporation (PGR)** specializes in auto insurance. As of September 18, 2026, the closing price was $213.48, up 4.25% over the past three months. Net margin of 12.8% and revenue growth (YoY) of 7.3% outpace industry averages. By contrast, **The Travelers Companies (TRV)** is a diversified insurer with the same day's closing price at $374.62, up 22.12% over three months, but revenue growth was only 0.3%. A net margin of 17% exceeds PGR but growth fundamentals are weaker. **Berkshire Hathaway (BRK.B)** is a large conglomerate holding its own insurance subsidiaries (such as GEICO), making it harder to gauge direct regulatory impact on it.

## Four pressure factors surrounding rate-setting

**1. Algorithm disclosure mandate**: States like New York and Vermont are requiring insurers to disclose the rate-determination models they use or submit to regulatory review. This exposes decades of accumulated data modeling, raising concerns about trade secret infringement.

**2. Constraints on permitted variables**: Variables beyond driving records and credit scores—such as location, occupation, gender, and age—are under renewed scrutiny for fairness. The argument is that these variables can operate discriminatorily. Removing variables reduces accuracy and overall profitability.

**3. Rising discrimination lawsuits**: Class-action suits are being filed claiming that minorities and low-income groups are charged disproportionately high rates. Settlement and legal costs accumulate, and reputational damage is significant.

**4. Low-cost competitor entry**: If algorithm regulation hardens, incumbent insurers' data advantage erodes. Usage-based insurance platforms and startups lacking subscriber data would be able to offer competitive pricing.

## Why insurers won't collapse entirely: scale and data assets

**Data-driven resilience**: PGR's 7.3% growth and 12.8% net margin remain healthy. Even if regulation constrains some variables, massive customer bases and long-loss history remain strengths of large incumbents—difficult for small competitors to match.

**Economies of scale and pricing flexibility**: Insurance is a network business. TRV's 17% net margin demonstrates economies of scale. Adjusting rate structure doesn't erode cost efficiency much.

**The weakness is clear**: If algorithm transparency is mandated and permitted variables shrink, risk differentiation weakens. When individual risk categorization becomes murky, premiums for good customers may rise, or insurers must absorb lower-quality risk. PGR's one-month decline (-1.74%) may signal these concerns.

## Summary

Regulatory debate will heighten near-term stock volatility, but insurers will adapt by reconstructing algorithms with manual review, statistical methods, and fairness audits. The question is how much profitability drops and how long the adjustment takes. A company like PGR, concentrated in auto insurance, may be more sensitive to regulatory shock.
