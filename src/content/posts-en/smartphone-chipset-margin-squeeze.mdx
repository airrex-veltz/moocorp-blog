## Smartphone Chipset Oversupply Creates a Double Crisis for Designers and Foundries

Prolonged weak smartphone shipments have left application processor (AP) supply far exceeding demand. As chipset prices fall, both designers and contract manufacturers compress margins. Historically, semiconductor cycles run on a 3-year rhythm, yet this margin pressure persists beyond one year unrelieved.

## Current Status: Signals from Major Designers

**Qualcomm** (**QCOM**) holds about 50% of the global smartphone chipset market share, but this year (2026) revenue fell 4.0% year-over-year. This reverses industry growth trends. Over the same period, net profit margin is 21.0%, showing a decline from the past. A sign that pricing power has weakened.

**AMD** (**AMD**) shows the steepest one-month decline at 17.87% (as of October 23, 2026). Revenue growth of 50.1% is solid, but a P/E of 131.8 suggests the market was already pricing in high future earnings. A net profit margin of 15.6% is on the lower end for designers of this scale.

Foundry leader **TSMC** (**TSM**) dominates with a market cap of KRW 2.17 trillion, yet it lags with a one-month decline of -7.23% (as of October 23, 2026). A net profit margin of 51.1% remains solid, and revenue growth of 50.9% is robust, but the stock's weakness suggests investors are front-running future margin contraction.

## Why the Double Crisis Now: Four Pressure Factors

**1. Prolonged smartphone demand weakness**: Global smartphone shipments have recovered sluggishly since 2023. Extended replacement cycles and weak economic conditions overlap. This means AP demand stagnation.

**2. Chipset price declines**: Continued oversupply has depressed average selling prices (ASP) for chipsets. Designers responded to oversupply with price cuts to avoid losing market share. Without cutting margins, they lose customers.

**3. Designer cost inflexibility**: Chip design is capital-intensive with high fixed costs. Companies must invest in marketing, platform development, and validation, but when revenue falls, profitability deteriorates more than proportionally. QCOM's margin erosion proves the point.

**4. Foundry profit pressure**: TSMC too relies on smartphone chipset business. When customer designers force price negotiations, foundry fees fall too. But wages and equipment maintenance are fixed while revenues shrink.

## Still, Recovery Is Possible: Structural Strengths and Weaknesses

**TSMC's leadership position** is the only short-term shock absorber. There is no competition, and customers depend on it, leaving them disadvantaged in negotiations. A 51% net margin is monopoly premium. Long term, it can diversify into AI chipsets and high-bandwidth memory demand.

**QCOM and AMD's weakness** is high smartphone exposure. QCOM maintains long-term contracts with Google and Samsung but is not free from price pressure. AMD is betting on PC chipset recovery, but its share of AI inference chipsets lags far behind **NVIDIA** (**NVDA**) (NVDA's 63.7% net margin is proof of AI monopoly earnings).

## Summary

Smartphone chipset price declines flow into designer revenue weakness and foundry margin deterioration. Without demand recovery, this pressure is likely to persist through 2026. Margin improvement for TSMC, QCOM, and AMD hinges on a recovery in smartphone shipment volumes.
