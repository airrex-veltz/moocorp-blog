## Company Overview

Samsung Electronics is a comprehensive electronics company with a market capitalization of approximately KRW 400 trillion, ranking first in Korea and second globally in the semiconductor market, surpassing Intel. Major business segments include memory semiconductors (DRAM/NAND), mobile (Galaxy), display, and appliances, maintaining a leading 43% share of the global DRAM market.

As of May 2026, the stock trades in the KRW 60,000-65,000 band and has entered a rebound phase amid expectations for AI semiconductor demand. Foreign investors have shifted to net buying following HBM3E mass production announcements, with trading volumes increasing 20-30% above average.

## Fundamentals

Based on fiscal year 2025, Samsung Electronics recorded revenue of approximately KRW 300 trillion (up 8-10% year-over-year) and operating profit of KRW 40-45 trillion, demonstrating recovery in the memory market. Operating profit margin improved to 13-15%, with net income reaching approximately KRW 30 trillion.

Growth drivers can be summarized in three areas. First, demand for DDR5/LPDDR5 and server/data center inventory depletion are driving recovery in memory semiconductors. Second, HBM3E mass production has secured supply contracts for NVIDIA H200/B200, with margins 3-4x higher than standard DRAM. Third, Samsung Foundry's 2nm process is scheduled for mass production in H2 2026 with an established customer base including Google and Qualcomm.

## Implications

Samsung Electronics is being re-evaluated as an AI semiconductor beneficiary due to HBM3E mass production and memory market recovery. Key watch points at the Q1 earnings announcement in mid-May are the return to profitability in the memory division and Q2 guidance. A duopoly with SK Hynix is forming in the HBM market, with the 50% market share target through 2027 serving as a gauge of medium-to-long-term growth potential.

While successful 2nm foundry process mass production and Texas plant operations open the possibility of catching up to TSMC, competitive risks and geopolitical factors remain headwinds. The 12-15x PER represents undervaluation vs. historical averages, but a cautious approach is warranted given Korea discount and valuation risks.
