## Introduction: Inference Cost Pressure Exposes Chip Design Competition and Foundry Bottleneck

As generative AI usage surges, the proportion of cost taken up by inference rather than training is rapidly growing. As competition intensifies among cloud providers and large AI companies to design and manufacture their own inference-optimized chips, capacity pressure on semiconductor foundries for advanced processes has become visible. This is not merely a supply shortage, but a signal of restructuring in the semiconductor industry.

## As Inference Becomes the Business Center, Chip Design Importance Rises Sharply

Large language models for generative AI incur training costs only once, but inference occurs with each user query. As usage increases, inference becomes the factor that determines operating costs. This is why each cloud and AI company is developing inference chips tailored to their own workloads. An inference-dedicated processor, rather than general-purpose GPUs or CPUs, can improve cost-effectiveness in both energy efficiency and processing speed. It has become both a source of differentiation and a necessary weapon in cost competition.

## Why Is Foundry Competition Heating Up Now: Four Structural Pressures

**1. Multilayering of Inference Chip Design Competition**

As NVIDIA's GPU monopoly weakens, major platform companies like Google, Meta, Amazon, Apple, and Tesla are either directly designing inference-optimized chips or developing them through fabless partners. As each company wants chips tailored to its own models and usage patterns, foundry demand is becoming diversified beyond simple growth. Multiple companies are now competing for tape-out using the same advanced processes.

**2. Foundry Advanced Process Capacity Limits**

The production capacity of cutting-edge processes such as TSMC's sub-3nm, Samsung's GAE, and Intel's Intel 4 increases only linearly. The surge in inference chip demand becomes competitive with existing training chip demand (e.g., H100, L40). As foundries allocate advanced process slots and price pressure and delivery competition intensify, newcomers with limited design experience or weak negotiating power face elimination.

**3. Inference Chips' Low-Margin Structure**

Inference chips tend to have lower profitability compared to training accelerators (GPUs, TPUs). Since optimization for energy and throughput is the goal, it is difficult to maintain high-price premiums, and competition accelerates price declines. When semiconductor manufacturing costs (packaging, testing, foundry fees) become relatively heavy, the period to recover initial R&D investment lengthens. This is a form of competition only possible for large companies with sufficient cash reserves.

**4. Mismatch Between New Process Adoption Cycle and Market Change**

Moving to a new process node takes 1-2 years, during which AI model architecture or inference methods (e.g., quantization, pruning, mixed precision) can change rapidly. There is a risk that today's optimal design becomes suboptimal next year. Given the unchangeable nature of semiconductors after tape-out, this uncertainty significantly raises the cost of design choices.

## Structural Advantages and Vulnerabilities

**Entities with advantages**: Companies with capabilities in both design and foundry (e.g., Apple's in-house chips plus foundry partnerships, Google's TPU/cloud integration) can iterate rapidly through short feedback loops. With sufficient capital, they can develop multiple generations of chips simultaneously to diversify risk.

**Vulnerabilities**: If substantial investment is placed in one generation of inference chips and that chip is rejected in the market, recovery is impossible. Foundry schedule slips also hit these players before competitors. If adoption of a new process results in yield issues with low initial utilization, fabless players with high fixed costs face sharp losses.

## Summary

Inference chip competition will intensify foundry utilization pressure and price competition in the short term. Over the medium term, there is a high likelihood of deepening industry polarization based on design capability and foundry access. The very process of diversification in the semiconductor supply chain from training chips to inference chips will determine future AI cost structures.
