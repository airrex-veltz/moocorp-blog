# Waste Haulers' Pricing Power and Recycling Revenue Volatility

Waste management companies generate revenue through a dual business model. One stream is fees collected from households and industrial customers for collection services. The other is revenue from selling recyclable commodities—aluminum, paper, plastic—recovered from collected waste. The difference in profit margins across these two revenue streams reveals each company's pricing power in the market.

WM (Waste Management) reports a net profit margin of 11.8%, while RSG (Republic Services) posts 12.9% (as of November 5, 2026). RSG is higher. This reflects not just operational efficiency but a difference in **fee-raising ability**. Since residential waste collection is an essential service with inelastic demand, companies have room to push through rate increases faster than inflation. WM's revenue growth rate (YoY) of 6.3% versus RSG's 8.2% demonstrates the concrete results of this pricing power differential.

But looking at P/E ratios, WM trades at 30.9 while RSG trades at 33.9—RSG's valuation is higher despite stronger profitability and growth. This signals that investors may be **concerned about recycling revenue volatility**.

Recycling commodity prices move with global markets. Scrap metal, aluminum, and waste paper prices are heavily influenced by crude oil prices and Chinese import demand. During economic downturns, when these prices plummet, a waste hauler's recycling division takes a major hit. This means recycling revenue carries substantial market exposure—no minor risk.

The shift to a circular economy is justified under the banner of "sustainability," but **actual margin improvement has been limited**. Recycling facility investment requires large upfront capital while commodity prices remain outside company control. Several waste haulers recorded negative margins in their recycling segments after depreciation in 2023–2024, illustrating this point. Current net margins at these firms derive chiefly from **rate increases in the core collection and transport business**.

For investors, then, **fee-raising capability** deserves more attention than an ESG-friendly business portfolio. WM's $93.6B market cap and RSG's $74.6B both reflect oligopolistic positions in waste management in developed markets, yet it is easy to overlook that within each firm, recycling's contribution to overall profitability may differ from expectations.
