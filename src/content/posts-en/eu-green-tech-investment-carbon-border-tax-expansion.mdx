## EU Carbon Tax: Starting a 'Super Cycle' for European Renewable Energy Firms

As the European Union (EU) prepares for full implementation of the carbon border adjustment mechanism (CBAM) and continuously strengthens ESG regulations, the business environment for renewable energy and electrification infrastructure companies is undergoing fundamental restructuring. Companies that have flexibly navigated regulatory challenges are now transforming into beneficiaries of a forced investment wave.

## CBAM: The Light and Shadow of Regulatory Costs

The EU began trial operations of the Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) in 2023 and will formally impose carbon border taxes starting in 2026. The mechanism charges a tariff equivalent to the difference between the EU's internal carbon price for imported steel, cement, fertilizer, electricity, and other products. This measure increases the price competitiveness of low-carbon imports while simultaneously pressuring EU manufacturers to accelerate their transition to low-carbon processes.

Particularly in Europe, where energy costs are high, carbon reduction is no longer optional but a condition for survival. According to the 2025 EU energy demand forecast, renewable energy's share increased 2.5 percentage points year-over-year, and investment demand for electrification infrastructure—transmission and distribution networks, charging stations, and smart grids—has more than doubled.

## Why These Companies Are Rising Now: Four Driving Factors

**1. Forced Carbon Reduction Becomes a Capital Investment Explosion**: CBAM tariff penalties force corporate action. EU steel, cement, and chemical manufacturers are pouring tens of billions of dollars into low-carbon process transitions. In this transformation, **SIE** (Siemens), a supplier of electrification technology and renewable energy systems, becomes the largest beneficiary of smart grid, wind turbine, and high-efficiency motor sales.

**2. ESG Disclosure Requirements Make the Entire Chain 'Visible'**: The EU's Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD) has been mandatory since 2024, with large enterprises leading the way. To disclose Scope 1, 2, and 3 emissions, companies require carbon tracking systems across their entire supply chains. This creates opportunities for companies like **ASML**, a precision measurement and automation technology provider. Demand for energy monitoring solutions in semiconductor and electrical equipment manufacturing processes is surging.

**3. Expanding EU Financial Support Policy**: The EU Green Deal and Recovery Fund will invest around EUR 2 trillion in green transitions from 2025 to 2030. Germany, France, and the Benelux countries prioritize battery, hydrogen electrolysis, and transmission and distribution network modernization. **EOAN** (Eon) is expanding its renewable energy portfolio with these funds, with network operating costs precisely covered by regulators.

**4. Affirming Energy Independence Intentions After Russian Sanctions**: Following the 2022 gas energy crisis, the EU revised its target to increase renewable energy's share to 45% or higher by 2030, up from the previous 42.5%. Cumulative installed capacity of wind and solar is projected to more than double between 2024 and 2030. This means Siemens Gamesa's wind technology and Eon's grid integration capabilities are essential assets.

## Structural Weaknesses to Still Watch

**Siemens' Advantages and Risks**: A broad beneficiary of industrial electrification, but industrial orders can shrink sharply during economic downturns. Price competition from Chinese competitors is also a threat factor.

**ASML's Indirect Benefits**: As a semiconductor equipment company with no direct renewable energy operations, it can benefit as the EU's chip self-sufficiency strategy (Chips Act) and green process automation investments align. High macroeconomic dependence remains a concern.

**Eon's Regulatory Uncertainty**: Germany's energy policy can shift politically. If the next government reduces financial support, investment plans face the risk of cascading revisions.

## Summary

EU carbon taxes and strengthened ESG regulations are not mere restrictions but signals of massive capital reallocation. Companies with competitiveness across three axes—renewable energy, electrification, and smart infrastructure—are likely to lead the next 3–5 year super cycle.
