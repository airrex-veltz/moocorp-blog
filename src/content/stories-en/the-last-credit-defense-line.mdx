At 2:43 a.m., the review room on the 17th floor of Seryupay's headquarters held only the smell of coffee and the hum of fluorescent lights.

Su-jin Choi stared at three monitors at once. On the left screen, the number 847 sat boxed in red. Tonight, she had to decide on limit adjustments for that many merchants. On the right, a corporate bond spread chart flickered. Forty-eight hours ago, it was at 90bp. Now it was 273bp. The highest in three years. The market had seen something first.

"Team lead, the batch settlement window opens at six," Do-hyun Kim said without turning his chair. At twenty-nine, he looked far older tonight.

"Got it."

Su-jin answered curtly. Three hours and seventeen minutes. Seryupay processed KRW 1.2 trillion in daily payments, and 847 of its merchants were now classified at risk grade A or above. If she left limits as they were, some would complete payments tonight but not receive settlement tomorrow morning. If she cut limits now, many of those 847 would face cash flow cuts at dawn.

Risk Officer Yeon-seo Park pushed through the conference room door, thick printouts of spreadsheets gripped in both hands.

"Classification is done. Cutting just the top three grades cuts exposure by half. But—"

He paused and spread the sheets across the table.

"Thirty-two delivery platforms are in the top three. Fifteen of them have rider payouts scheduled for tomorrow morning."

Do-hyun Kim leaned back in his chair. His eyes lifted to the ceiling, then came back down.

"The riders aren't at fault."

It wasn't clear who he was asking.

Su-jin picked up the sheet. Numbers were dense. Each row was someone's lifeline. She stopped at the twenty-second row. A memo in parentheses after the merchant code caught her eye. 'Proprietor personal guarantee, 60s.' The guarantor field. She remembered the day the review team first set that merchant's limit and the guarantor signed. Spring, three years ago.

"Yeon-seo, did you reach out to the three financial institutions again about liquidity support?"

"Two of them closed their windows this morning, and the third won't return calls."

Su-jin set the sheets down.

Outside the window, Seoul's night skyline spread out indifferently. Buildings with lights still on. Somewhere in those buildings, someone else was staring at a spreadsheet at this very hour. Facing similar numbers. In similar silence.

"Give me two scenarios only. A: cut the top two grades immediately. B: reduce all limits by 30% across the board. Show me affected merchant count and same-day liquidity gap for each."

Do-hyun started typing.

Su-jin picked up her phone and texted the CFO at headquarters. Two lines summarizing the situation, two scenarios, a decision request. The read receipt appeared and faded. That meant he'd seen it. No reply came.

Three minutes passed.

Do-hyun brought the results up on screen. Scenario A: 214 merchants cut off, liquidity gap KRW 38.7 billion, estimated rider payout impact 11,000 people. Scenario B: all 847 merchants affected, gap KRW 9.1 billion, estimated rider payout impact 3,000 people.

The numbers were clear. The direction was not.

"Scenario B and no one gets cut, but Seryupay is at risk. Scenario A and 214 go dark at dawn and we survive," Yeon-seo said, laying a hand on the edge of the table. A statement, not a question.

Su-jin said nothing. Her finger sat on the mouse. The cursor blinked just above the execute button.

It was 3:05 a.m. Two hours and fifty-five minutes until the settlement window opened.

The wall clock's second hand ticked forward one notch.

Su-jin took a breath and looked at the screen.
