The day Seo Yun-a first held that box, the world looked different.

It was a small rectangular package with a green tint, resting in the palm of her hand. The plant-based resin product Nokwon Packaging had developed over three years. She pressed the corners with her fingers—it was flexible. The texture was nearly identical to conventional plastic, yet extracted from corn starch. What she had dreamed of was here, now.

"Let's go with this, Director."

The researchers laughed. Seo Yun-a laughed too. She would only remember later how light that laughter felt.

Nokwon Packaging's green box hit the market that spring. Analysis showed it reduced carbon emissions by 28 percent compared to conventional packaging. Major retailers lined up to sign contracts. At an environment ministry event where Seo Yun-a was invited as a speaker, she said carefully from the podium, "I believed packaging materials should return to the earth. That's why we made this." Applause followed. She held onto that sound for a long time—not for any wrong reason, but because it felt so pure.

---

The crack began with a single line of data.

Park Jun-hyuk, the junior researcher, handed over a draft paper. There was no date on the cover. Seo Yun-a sat and read, then stopped midway, then read again.

Nokwon's primary material was PLA-based. According to the paper, biodegradable plastics in this family only fully decomposed in industrial composting facilities maintained above 55 degrees Celsius. When they ended up in conventional landfills, rivers, or coastal waters, their decomposition rate was nearly identical to conventional plastic. Worse, they broke into smaller particles that seeped into soil and ocean. The paper cited multiple domestic and international cases confirming this. Domestic composting facility capacity covered only 8 percent of the total amount of biodegradable packaging circulating in the market.

After a moment, Seo Yun-a asked.

"Are these figures accurate?"

"Yes, Director. Our product is included in them."

That night, Seo Yun-a walked near the hillside entrance close to the factory. Below the sign forbidding illegal dumping, dozens of green packaging materials lay stacked. Some wet from rain, some too heavy for wind to move—between them, a single insect crawled slowly. Seo Yun-a stood there for a while. It wasn't cold, yet her shoulders felt narrow, tight.

---

Over the following weeks, Seo Yun-a's daily routine changed.

She no longer opened meetings by first saying "innovation." She returned marketing team drafts more frequently. She postponed two follow-up interview requests from the award-giving organization. The board began watching her with strange eyes.

One investor called.

"Director, why are you like this lately? Things are going well."

"Yes."

Her answer was brief. It wasn't that words failed her—rather, the words she needed to say had changed.

There was something Seo Yun-a didn't tell him, didn't tell the board. A single line from her early startup notebook. Something her thesis advisor had repeated habitually. "There is always a distance between what you believe is good and what is actually good." Back then, she saw it as scholarly caution. Now it read differently. She was beginning to understand how wide that distance could be.

---

One morning, Seo Yun-a remained alone at one edge of the factory, studying new material test results.

This material decomposed without industrial composting facilities. The cost was triple the current one, and durability issues remained. Internal estimates put commercialization at minimum two years away. Not a number investors would welcome. The distributors waiting on supply contracts had no idea.

Seo Yun-a looked out the window. Green shipping containers sat arranged in the factory yard. Finished products waiting for dispatch. They still looked beautiful to her. That fact troubled her.

The color green still felt like hope to her. Now, that feeling carried one layer more weight.

---

Whether she decided anything, nobody knew. But that afternoon, a brief text went to Park Jun-hyuk.

"Prepare the paper for journal submission."

It took a long time for his reply to arrive. Jun-hyuk was thinking too. When the reply finally came, it was just two words.

"Understood."

Seo Yun-a locked the screen. Beyond the window, the green boxes caught the light. A breeze passed through.
