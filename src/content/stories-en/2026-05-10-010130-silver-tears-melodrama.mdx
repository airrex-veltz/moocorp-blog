On the last day of December 1982, the cold wind sweeping through the Onsan industrial zone was bone-chilling. Kim Tae-su pulled up the collar of his jacket over his worn work uniform and entered the gates of Korea Zinc's smelter. At forty-eight years old, he had already spent fourteen years in this factory.

"Night shift again?"

His coworker Park Ki-chul asked with a slight smile from the changing room. Tae-su nodded. The furnace could never stop. The molten metal, hotter than a thousand degrees Celsius, waited for someone's hands around the clock, and that someone was always men like Tae-su.

"How are the kids doing?"

"The oldest just got into university. Engineering program."

A smile spread across Park Ki-chul's face. Tae-su smiled too, but behind that smile lay an unspoken heaviness. To pay for university tuition, he needed the night shift premium. Eight hours from eleven at night to seven in the morning, standing before the furnace—that time was buying his son's future.

Stepping into the smelter, the familiar heat hit his face. The blast of hot wind from the massive furnace, the sound of zinc ore melting and flowing, the metallic smell mixed with sweat. The air he had breathed every day for fourteen years was now etched deep in his lungs.

"Check the temperature!"

The shift leader's voice rang out. Tae-su stirred the molten metal with a long rod and checked the thermometer. 1,250 degrees. Perfect. If this temperature wavered, the entire smelting process would be thrown off, and hundreds of tons of raw material could become defective. Fourteen years of instinct allowed Tae-su's hands to read even the slightest fluctuations.

Past midnight, as two in the morning approached, the factory grew quieter. Only the roar of the furnace filled the darkness. During a brief break, Tae-su stood by the window of the workshop. In the darkness of the distant Onsan Bay, lights flickered here and there. Beyond that lay the small house in Busan where his wife and children would be asleep.

"Father."

A young voice came from behind. He turned to see Kim Min-jun, a new worker, standing there. Twenty-three years old—the same age Tae-su had been when he first entered this factory.

"Is it tough?"

"It's fine, sir. But... are you going to do this your whole life?"

Tae-su stared silently at the furnace for a long moment. The red glow of flowing molten metal lit up his weathered face.

"At first, I just came here to make money. But then, at some point, I realized something. The zinc we make here goes all over the country. Into shipyard steel plates, automobile parts, building exteriors. The molten metal I melted with these hands becomes someone's house, someone's car, someone's ship."

Min-jun nodded quietly.

"My son is going to engineering school. He'll probably become an engineer, maybe even develop better smelting techniques. The process my son will improve someday—I'm protecting it right now. That... that turned out to be my role."

At five in the morning, shift change approached. Tae-su checked the furnace temperature one last time and carefully recorded it on the log sheet. 1,248 degrees. Perfect. The next shift would have no problems continuing the work.

On his way back to the changing room, a banner hanging on the factory wall caught his eye. "Korea Zinc creates the world's finest quality zinc." Tae-su paused for a moment before it. World's finest. Behind those two words stood thousands of men like Tae-su. The nights spent guarding a scorching furnace, the time separated from family, the metallic smell embedded in skin, the lower back pain.

The early morning air was cold. As Tae-su left the factory, he pulled out a crumpled cigarette from his pocket. He reached for his lighter but stopped. The eastern sky was beginning to brighten faintly. Another day was starting. Somewhere, someone using a product made with the zinc he produced was greeting that dawn.

Putting the cigarette back in his pocket, Tae-su smiled quietly. The image of silvery tears of zinc molten metal flowing down still vividly filled his mind. It was his youth, his sacrifice, and at the same time, his pride.

On the bus ride home, Tae-su fell asleep. In his dream, he sat in a university graduation hall. His son stood on the platform in an academic gown. Beneath their feet lay the technology and dedication built up by countless nameless people like Tae-su, forming a solid foundation.
