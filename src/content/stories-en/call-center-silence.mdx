Odalsim first put on a headset in 2009.

The job posting said "global customer service specialist company," and back then he didn't think deeply about the weight of those words. Hanuol Connect Seoul, 7th floor, a seat without windows, 2 p.m. when 230 headsets rang simultaneously. That was the busiest time.

There was a sensation of a broken line reconnecting. How sharp the first voice of a complaint call was, how that sharpness wavered around the third sentence—Odalsim had learned it over fifteen years. Contract cancellations, refund requests, shipping delays. And sometimes—just a call because someone wanted to talk. That last kind was the hardest. Because nothing could be resolved.

In early 2024, Hanuol Connect headquarters announced plans for full-scale implementation of an AI consultation system. Employees received the news via email on Friday afternoon that week. Odalsim read the email three times. The first time he didn't understand the content, the second time he did, and the third time he turned off his screen.

Around the same time, similar announcements came out one after another from various places around the world. A Nordic fintech company announced that one AI consultant was handling the number of calls that 700 employees had been processing. The way numbers jumped out from the news was strange. As if announcing something remarkable. Not the story of 700 people, but the story of one AI.

Hanuol Connect Seoul was also within the flow of those numbers.

In January, three sets of layoff notices went out. In April, two floors merged. In the first month of implementation, the press release said the first-contact resolution rate had risen from 73 percent to 91 percent. Odalsim read that number on his screen and couldn't think of anything for a long time.

Where did the people who were handling 73 percent go?

The question came up and disappeared. It was the kind of question that couldn't be answered.

The remaining sixteen employees take second-level consultations. Calls where AI withholds judgment, calls with heightened emotions, calls requiring exception handling. Odalsim receives at most twenty calls a day. There was a time when he received ten times as many.

At 2 p.m., the office is quiet. The sound of fluorescent lights can be heard. His neighbor Choi Jin-ah types while looking at her screen. Three empty seats beyond her, and more emptiness beyond that. The seats that once numbered 230 are mostly folded. Chairs are pushed under desks. Dust accumulates on monitors.

Odalsim wears his headset and looks out the window. People drink coffee at the café on the floor below. The sky is overcast.

A call comes in.

He presses the button on his headset.

"Thank you, Hanuol Connect."

His voice is still steady. Fifteen years made it that way.

A moment of silence flows from the other end of the line. Then a voice comes through. Quiet, a little anxious, a human voice.

Odalsim listens intently.
