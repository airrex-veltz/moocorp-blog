The front desk at 11 p.m. is quiet. Or at least it appears to be.

Seo Hyun-woo scrolled through the reservation system and spotted a familiar name. Kim Tae-shik. Room 202. Every Friday between 11 p.m. and midnight, he checked in. Since spring last year, he had never missed once. Not on anniversaries. Not on the eve of holidays.

The door opened.

"Hello, Mr. Seo."

Kim Tae-shik arrived without luggage. He always did. Just a small bag, his dress shirt unbuttoned, his tie half-stuffed in his pocket. A ballpoint pen with a company logo sat in his breast pocket.

"Have a restful weekend."

As Seo Hyun-woo handed him the keycard, Kim Tae-shik nodded and walked toward the elevator. Watching the back of his head, Seo Hyun-woo thought: This man must have a home. Yet every Friday night, he comes to Room 202 here.

Park Seon-joo, the manager, emerged from the back office with coffee. The senior night manager, she was still several centimeters shorter than Seo Hyun-woo even in her low heels. Yet no one in this building was quieter about noticing things than she was. Just by watching how a guest moved through the lobby, Park Seon-joo could tell whether they were hungry, exhausted, or about to cry.

"The regular for 202 is here." She glanced at the monitor.

"Yes. He's hardly missed a week since last year."

"You know what's in that room?"

Seo Hyun-woo didn't answer.

"Nothing. That's why he goes."

With that, Park Seon-joo returned to the back office. Seo Hyun-woo looked out at the empty lobby. Soft LED accent lighting diffused across the marble floor. Somewhere, the air conditioning system hummed low.

It was past midnight when Yu-na entered. Her reservation was already online. A minimal double room. The smallest in the hotel. She had only a shoulder bag, and that was all her luggage. Her hands trembled slightly as she filled out the check-in card.

Seo Hyun-woo didn't ask anything. Reservation confirmation, card scan, keycard issued. Slowly, but without unnecessary words.

"If you need anything, extension 0 is available. I'm here all night."

It was only then that Yu-na really looked at Seo Hyun-woo's face. Something seemed about to emerge, but she just nodded and turned away.

---

Three a.m. The lobby was empty. Seo Hyun-woo was reaching for the bottled water he kept below the desk when the elevator doors opened.

It was Yu-na. A cardigan over her pajamas, slippers on her feet. She went to a corner sofa and sat down, knees together, hands folded on them. Her eyes seemed to be looking at something and nothing at once.

Seo Hyun-woo pretended not to notice. It seemed like the right thing to do.

About fifteen minutes later, he got up, went briefly into the staff kitchen, and came back out. He walked to the sofa table with a warm cup of herbal tea.

"Sometimes guests who can't sleep come down at night," he said, setting down the cup. "If you don't want it, you can just leave it."

Yu-na looked at the cup for a moment, then wrapped both hands around it. As if testing whether it was hot, or wanting to hold onto its warmth a little longer.

"Is there something special about this place?"

It wasn't a question about the location or facilities. It was a question aimed at something vague.

Seo Hyun-woo thought for a moment.

"Not really. It's just that there's night here."

Yu-na laughed softly. She seemed surprised by her own laughter, as if the act itself was unexpected.

It was around that time when statistics were released showing that the average weekend occupancy rate at four-star hotels in Korea had exceeded 78 percent for the first time that year. People inside and outside the industry spoke of "full recovery." But when Seo Hyun-woo encountered that number, he felt something strange. 78 percent. The remaining 22 percent sits empty. And even the rooms that are filled aren't all filled for the same reasons.

Some people come to rest. Some come to get away from something. And some come simply because they need one night somewhere. The sound of someone's suitcase wheels, the sound of a television from the end of the hallway, the glow of a vending machine. Working the night shift had taught Seo Hyun-woo, gradually, what all these things together create.

---

Yu-na woke up a little before 5 a.m. and headed back to her room. She looked at Seo Hyun-woo one last time. She didn't say anything.

Checkout was at 11 a.m.

After handing over to the day shift, as Seo Hyun-woo was walking toward the staff exit, he saw Yu-na standing in front of the desk. She had a suitcase now. She hadn't before. It seemed she had put her bag in storage.

She recognized Seo Hyun-woo and paused.

"Rest well."

It could have been a declaration or an expression of gratitude. Seo Hyun-woo nodded.

As he watched Yu-na pass through the revolving door and out into the street, Seo Hyun-woo thought: I think I finally understand what this hotel sells. Not beds. Not breakfast or a swimming pool. One night that's hard to find anywhere else. And someone who asks nothing.

Morning sunlight streamed through the revolving glass door and spilled across the lobby floor.

Kim Tae-shik in Room 202 hadn't checked out yet. Probably he was sleeping a bit longer. He would come this week. Probably next week too.
