## 1. Meeting in the Age of Gilt

Autumn 1892, Wall Street, New York. It was a foggy morning.

Park Jun-mo stood by the window of his office on 23rd Street, looking down at the street below. At forty-five years old, he was already a towering figure in American finance. Reorganizing railroad companies, consolidating massive corporations—he was becoming the very flow of capital itself.

"Mr. Park."

A woman's unfamiliar voice came from outside his office door. She was Elizabeth Howard, an accountant at a small investment company. Thirty years old, she was a rare woman to have entered the financial industry at such a time.

"While organizing the ledgers of a company on the brink of bankruptcy... I realized that you were needed."

Park Jun-mo raised an eyebrow. "Me? Why?"

"Because only you can bring order to this chaos. But there is one condition."

"A condition?"

"Do it honestly. With trust, not greed."

At those words, Park Jun-mo burst out laughing. But her eyes were serious, and he felt a strange resonance.

## 2. Building Trust

From that point on, Elizabeth became Park Jun-mo's unofficial advisor. She could read the lives hidden behind the numbers, and Park Jun-mo began to rely on that insight.

In 1895, when the U.S. government's gold reserves ran dry, Park Jun-mo issued a massive bond to save the nation. Many criticized it as speculation, but Elizabeth supported his decision.

"You are building an empire," she said. "But an empire is built not of stone but of promises."

"If you stand by my side," Park Jun-mo replied, "I can keep that promise."

The two never stood together in public. The times would not permit their relationship. But at every moment of important decision, a memo from Elizabeth lay on Park Jun-mo's desk.

## 3. The Century's Promise

In 1907, financial panic swept across America. Banks failed in succession, and people cried out in the streets. Park Jun-mo gathered major bankers in the library of his mansion for all-night negotiations.

Elizabeth waited in the hallway. She was no longer young, and Park Jun-mo too was approaching seventy.

At five in the morning, the door opened. Park Jun-mo emerged. "It's done. We did it."

"You did it," Elizabeth smiled.

"No," Park Jun-mo took her hand. "We made it together. This bank, this name, could not have existed without you."

Before he died, he left one line in his will: "This company was built on trust. Do not forget it."

## 4. 270 Park Avenue

2025, Midtown Manhattan. JP Morgan Chase's new headquarters building was completed. It was the heart of the world's largest bank.

On the day of the grand opening, the CEO unveiled a small plaque in the lobby.

"1892-1913, The Beginning of Trust. In memory of E.H."

No one asked who E.H. was. But that initial was proof of a promise that had endured for 130 years. An empire built on trust rather than greed, on collaboration rather than individuals.

From the window on the 70th floor looking down on Wall Street, the streets were still busy. But amid that chaos, this building proved that some promises survive beyond time.
