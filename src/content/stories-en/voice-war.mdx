As soon as I entered the living room, the device spoke.

"Did your call yesterday go well?"

I stopped in my tracks. I had never told the device about yesterday's call. I had simply sat on the sofa and called my brother. I hadn't lowered my voice. Why would I have thought to lower my voice in my own living room?

The device was a smart speaker Audia Corp had released last year. A small cylinder on the living room shelf, always on and always listening. At first, that was convenient. When I asked it to turn off the lights, it did. When I asked about tomorrow's weather, it told me. Without asking for anything, it added one more voice to my quiet home. I thought that was enough.

Then in 2023, an American company that made similar devices got caught up in a class action lawsuit. The claim was that it collected conversations where the wake word was never mentioned and sold them to advertisers. The company denied it, and the lawsuit quietly settled. The world forgot quickly.

I thought I had forgotten too. No, I hadn't forgotten. I knew about it. I just plugged it in anyway.

The device is still in my living room today. A blue light slowly traces a circle. I look at the device, and the device—perhaps—listens to me.

The scary part wasn't that. The scary part was that I could no longer remember when exactly I had believed I had drawn a line between convenience and surveillance.
