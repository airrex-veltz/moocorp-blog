March 2010, a small office in Seoul. Kim Beom-su gazed out the window, lost in thought. It had been just a year since leaving Hangame and Hangilsearch. A new beginning always brought fear, but he had a promise he had to keep.

"I will connect people."

Ten years ago, the day his father passed away. He was on a distant business trip and couldn't say his final farewell. He had a phone, but call charges were a burden, so he couldn't reach out often. That regret had driven him to this point.

His first product was a messenger named Daon Talktalker. A world where anyone could talk to anyone, anytime, for free. That was the world Kim Beom-su had dreamed of.

"Who would use this?" Investors shook their heads. Nate On and MSN Messenger already dominated the market. There seemed to be no room for a small startup to squeeze in.

But Kim Beom-su did not give up. He coded through the night and worked with his team to refine the UX. What they created was not just a simple messenger. It was a space where warm emotions could be held within yellow bubbles.

March 18, 2010, the launch of Daon Talktalker. In the first three days, it had just 10,000 users. But a month later, 100,000. Three months later, 1 million. People asked about each other's well-being on Daon Talktalker, confessed their love, and announced their breakups. Yellow bubbles became part of everyday Korean life.

2014, a merger with Daum Communication. The meeting of the two companies was not easy. Their cultures were different, and so were their visions. But Kim Beom-su believed. He believed that two different people could meet and create a greater future together.

On the day the merger was announced, he spoke to his employees.

"We are now one family. When Daum's search technology meets Daon Talk's philosophy of connection, we can make more people happy."

That promise was kept. Easy transfers through Daon Talk Pay, convenient travel with Daon Talk Taxi, and lowered barriers to finance with Daon Talk Bank. At the heart of every service was the philosophy of 'connection.'

Now in 2026. Daon Talktalker has surpassed 50 million users. Most Koreans open the yellow app every day and share conversations with loved ones.

Kim Beom-su still works late into the night in his office. But now he is not alone. Thousands of colleagues dream together with him. And somewhere across the country, someone opens Daon Talktalker and sends a message to someone they love.

"I miss you."
"I love you."
"Sleep well."

Today too, yellow bubbles hold promises. Small promises to connect people with people, to create warm technology.
