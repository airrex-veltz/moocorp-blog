Gang Tae-in closed his laptop at two in the morning.

In the research building near the Incheon Southeast Industrial Complex, the office fluorescent lights were already off. Only the emergency light glowed red at the end of the hallway. He sat in the darkness for a long time. Ruzin AI's press kit wouldn't leave his head.

Numbers. Just a few numbers. But they had torn down three years.

---

Daon Tech started the 'Arc-7' project in spring 2023. The plan was to design an AI inference processor in-house. At that time, there were only a handful of companies in Korea capable of designing chips with such complexity. Gang Tae-in was the project's chief architect.

The approach they chose was original. It was a 'proximity cache fabric' structure that minimized data movement distance by placing computational blocks and memory as close as possible. It was the first attempt to implement an idea the team had long debated into actual silicon.

In 2024, startups exploded onto the global AI chip market. That year, industry research organizations tallied over 240 AI semiconductor design firms operating worldwide. When Gang Tae-in saw that number, he wasn't particularly concerned. The barrier to entry wasn't design capability—it was validation data and customer trust. Those things only accumulated with time.

He first heard the name Ruzin AI in the summer of last year. A fabless company with headquarters in Beijing, founded four years ago. They had submitted a paper to a semiconductor design conference. Gang Tae-in paused when he saw the paper's title: 'Low-Latency Proximity Memory Fabric-Based Inference Accelerator.' Every word felt familiar.

It took two hours to read the paper. Their approach was different. The structure was different. But the direction was the same. Or rather, it was more than the same. Ruzin AI had already arrived where Daon Tech was headed.

---

After that day, Gang Tae-in began quietly tracking Ruzin AI's movements.

Four months later, Ruzin AI released the press kit for the 'RJ-A1' chip. It included public benchmark results. Gang Tae-in went through the numbers one by one. Inference throughput, power efficiency, memory bandwidth utilization.

Arc-7 had internal target specifications. Numbers not yet disclosed to the outside. When he placed those numbers side by side with RJ-A1's figures, at first he thought he had misread the table.

Looking again, it was the same. RJ-A1 was ahead. Eleven percent in throughput, eight percent in power efficiency. Not a small difference.

He wasn't the only one who saw it. Team member Lim So-yeon sent a messenger message the next morning. "Did you see the Ruzin AI announcement?" That was all. No emoji, no question mark. That made it heavier.

---

The report couldn't be avoided.

The content was simple. A competitor reached the same destination faster than us. With the current Arc-7 design, it's difficult to claim market advantage at launch.

The director said nothing for a while. Then he asked, "Is there a possibility of a leak?"

Gang Tae-in shook his head. "The structure is different. Someone didn't copy ours. They just solved the same problem at the same time we did."

That statement only created a deeper silence.

If it had been technology theft, there would have been a response. They could have called in the legal team. But simultaneous invention, independent arrival—there was no one to blame. The same physics, the same process constraints, similar market pressures. Engineers around the world were being pushed in the same direction, and someone had just arrived first.

---

The project wasn't halted. A strategic review meeting was scheduled. Internally, an early transition to Arc-8 or a specialized market strategy for Arc-7 was discussed. Gang Tae-in prepared materials reviewing both options.

On the subway ride home, he opened Ruzin AI's engineering blog again. A recent post had a team introduction. The founder had graduated from a U.S. university. The CTO had European foundry experience. There was even someone with a Korean name on the team.

Gang Tae-in turned off his screen.

He always heard the saying that the world had become smaller. It was usually used in a positive context. Collaboration became easier, information spread faster, talent moved. All of it was true. And all of it explained exactly where he was standing now.

It wasn't that competition had accelerated. It was that destinations had multiplied simultaneously.

---

Back home at his desk, he opened the Arc-7 design file. He didn't close it. From a distance, it looked like three years had collapsed. But up close, it was different. Nothing was finished yet. The map had only changed.

Under the fluorescent light, circuit blocks filled the screen. He picked up his mouse.
