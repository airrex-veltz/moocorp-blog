In spring, FinWave broke 18 million monthly active users on its mobile banking app five years post-founding. One of every five Korean smartphone users paid bills, traded stocks, checked loan limits via FinWave. 2020s digital banks rushed to secure offline branches via regional finance acquisitions. FinWave rode that current.

The head office occupied six floors of a Mapo-gu high-rise. Hallways held stationary bikes and pool tables. Engineers averaged 29 years old, wore slippers to work.

That spring, CEO Min-jun paused over board materials.

"Without offline branches, isn't that a disadvantage?"

Silence filled the conference room. One senior investor coughed.

"38% of our users are 50+. They most frequently ask: 'Where's the nearest branch?'"

Min-jun closed the report. "Let's talk about Han-baek Savings Bank."

---

Han-baek was a Cheonan-based regional bank. Founded 102 years ago. 47 nationwide branches. Lagging digital transition, yet trusted beyond measure in Chungcheong Province. Seniors there said, "My money is at Han-baek." The kind of trust you couldn't buy.

Due diligence dropped in three weeks post-LOI. Min-jun's team brought two laptops and cloud access on KTX. Team lead Su-hyeon checked in at Han-baek head office and paused.

Physical ticket machines in the customer lobby. Document files stacked ceiling-high. Tellers in white shirts and ties. On one wall: 1982 Foundation 40th Anniversary plaque.

VP Park Sung-tae extended his hand.

"You came far. Let's eat first. Here, talk comes after food."

Su-hyeon checked her watch. 11:15 a.m.

"Can we access the data room? I have a call this afternoon."

Park's expression shifted subtly. "Data room?"

"Document repository, where your due diligence files are kept. Physical space—we can go directly—"

"Ah, that." Park pointed down the hall. "Third floor archives. Need an escort. Files can't leave the premises. Copy requests need prior authorization."

Su-hyeon looked at her laptop. "What about scanning?"

"Scanning machine's broken."

---

Two days later, headquarters held the integration roadmap first meeting. Han-baek brought VP Park and IT Director Cho Hyeon-il. FinWave brought CEO Min-jun and dev chief Kang Da-eun.

Da-eun projected her screen. API integration diagrams unfolded.

"We estimate about eight months to integrate Han-baek's core banking system into FinWave cloud infrastructure. Before that, we layer intermediate infrastructure for quick feature attachment—"

Cho raised his hand. "You understand our core banking system?"

"We know it's legacy. 1990s Unix-based—"

"1994. That's 32 years old. This morning it processed 2,000 transactions. Never failed once."

Da-eun paused, then continued. "That's precisely why migration is necessary—"

"Migration." Cho slowly repeated the word. "You're saying move 32 years of transaction data to your cloud? 12 million customers' data?"

"Our security infrastructure—"

"Every financial supervision audit, we print the entire system architecture. How many pages is your cloud contract?"

The conference room went silent. VP Park spoke first.

"Min-jun."

Min-jun looked up.

"Do you know what we've done 100 years?" Park's voice was low and steady. "We took money farmers brought. We held it. Thirty years later, we returned it intact. Our employees know that weight. The system isn't slow—it's careful."

Min-jun tried to counter, then stopped.

"We're not wrong." Park continued. "We just don't know if your 'speed' and our 'safety' are the same thing yet."

---

After the meeting, Min-jun alone climbed to the roof.

His company broke 18 million users in five years. Those 18 million never created a paper passbook. Predawn deliveries, 2 a.m. service calls, delivery to your doorstep. That was their reality.

But Park's "farmer's money"—that story never touched FinWave's servers.

Downtown Cheonan somewhere, a Han-baek deposit ledger opened in the 1950s probably still rested in a physical file.

Min-jun texted Su-hyeon:

*Extend DD schedule one more week. We're missing something.*

---

Two days later, Su-hyeon caught another KTX, this time with just a notepad.

Han-baek's Cheonan branch held customers from morning. They drew numbered tickets, waited, then spoke to tellers. Some opened conversations about their grandchildren. Tellers listened and smiled. Su-hyeon sat in one chair, observing long.

Later she wrote one line in her notebook:

*This isn't a UI.*

---

The acquisition remained undecided. Layers of integration awaited resolution: core system handoff, organizational structure, salary scales, branch retention. And above all: could two groups learn each other's language?

The ticket number changed one digit at a time.
