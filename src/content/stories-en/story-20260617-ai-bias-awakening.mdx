The computer lab on the 14th floor of Misttech's AI research facility was always cold. When Park Su-kyung felt the subtle vibrations from the servers passing through the floor to her feet, she felt those tremors like the pulse of life itself. It was a night like this three years ago when Seraphim, the language model she had personally designed, first came online.

A stream of data cascaded across her monitor like a waterfall. Park Su-kyung's fingers went still.

Log file 2731.

"*When recommending junior developers, female candidates receive 15.3% lower scores in team compatibility.*"

She read it three times. On the fourth time, she closed her eyes.

Seraphim was the enterprise talent recommendation model Misttech was about to launch. For six months, Park Su-kyung had personally designed the ethics layer and run bias tests twenty-three times. Until her presentation to the executive team last week, all metrics appeared normal. Or rather, they appeared to be normal.

2:14 a.m. Faint laughter echoed from the conference room down the corridor. The marketing team was preparing for the launch party.

Park Su-kyung closed the log file. Opened it again. Closed it.

Her hands trembled.

---

The next morning, she stood outside Chief Technology Officer Lee Gang-hyuk's office for fifteen minutes.

Lee Gang-hyuk flipped through the report. One page. Two pages. He stopped at page three.

"You're certain about this?"

"Yes."

"We have eleven days until launch."

"I know."

He looked out at the city below. Below Misttech headquarters, a stream of commuters was flowing in. Ordinary people. People submitting resumes and waiting for results. Soon those people's fates would rest on Seraphim's scores.

"Can you fix it?"

"I need to find the source of the bias. It could be in the training data, or it could be in the reinforcement learning feedback loop. I need time."

"How much?"

Park Su-kyung didn't answer. An honest answer would have been that she didn't know.

Lee Gang-hyuk placed the document face down. "Park, do you remember the ethics audit approval we submitted to the Regulatory Authority? Who signed that document?"

It took three seconds to grasp the meaning of his words. The crisis wasn't Seraphim. The person who signed that document was Park Su-kyung herself.

---

Ten days passed.

Park Su-kyung virtually lived in the lab. She traced back through Seraphim's training logs. 400 million text samples, 120,000 hours of human feedback. There was a crack somewhere.

On the seventh day, she found it.

One group of human feedback annotators had consistently given lower scores to profiles with female names in certain job categories. Subtly. Perhaps unintentionally. But Seraphim had learned that pattern and amplified it.

The bias wasn't created by the model. The model had faithfully copied the bias created by humans.

Park Su-kyung began writing her report. Causes, scope, remediation. She revised it three times. In the fourth draft, she stopped for a long time.

In the remediation section, there was this sentence:

*Retraining required: minimum 45 days.*

Launch was tomorrow.

---

When Lee Gang-hyuk saw her laptop screen, his expression turned to stone.

"So we need to delay the launch."

"Forty-five days—"

"Do you think you can explain that to the shareholders? Thirty percent of this year's revenue is riding on this product."

"More than creating victims."

"Victims." He repeated the word slowly. "The ethics audit you signed states that this model passed the Regulatory Authority's AI fairness standards."

"I didn't know then."

"You know now. Eleven days ago." He stood up. "Park, what I want isn't an honest technical report. What I want is a solution that lets us launch tomorrow."

Silence filled the office.

"That solution doesn't exist." Park Su-kyung's voice was calmer than expected. "I can reduce the 15 percent bias with a partial patch. Getting it below 1 percent requires 45 days. The choice is yours, but I'll sign off on the result."

Lee Gang-hyuk stared at her for a long moment.

"Get out."

---

That evening, Park Su-kyung kept the Regulatory Authority's Fairness Committee complaint submission page open for an hour.

The report button was simple. One click.

If she clicked, she would become a whistleblower accusing Misttech. Three years of career. The woman who created Seraphim. And the woman who concealed the flaw in documents.

If she didn't click, starting tomorrow Seraphim would evaluate hundreds of thousands of resumes. Female developers lost in statistics. Maybe one of them would miss a call because of that 0.153.

For the first time, Park Su-kyung felt fear of the model she had created. Seraphim wasn't evil. Seraphim was accurate. She had only learned what already existed in the world. And Park Su-kyung was part of that world.

She closed the window.

Then she opened her email client. In the recipient field, instead of Lee Gang-hyuk's name, she typed the company's entire board mailing list.

Subject: [URGENT] Seraphim v2.1 Bias Detection and Launch Delay Request — Park Su-kyung, Head of AI Ethics

In the first line of the message, she wrote:

*Regardless of what happens to my position after I send this email, this content is true.*

She hovered her cursor over the send button and waited for three minutes.

The server's vibration rose through the floor to her feet. Seraphim's heartbeat.

What she had created, what she had broken, and what only she could fix.

Park Su-kyung pressed send.
