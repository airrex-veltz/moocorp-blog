The dawn in the Atacama Desert has no sound.

Rodrigo Paredes adjusted his helmet strap and stood at the edge of the mining site. Born and raised in the highlands of northern Peru, the Chilean sandstorms were still unfamiliar even after five years. His nose stung. Jose Quispe stood beside him and coughed softly.

"Did you hear what the foreman said this morning?"

Rodrigo shook his head.

"Wages are going up. Fifteen percent."

Silence hung for a moment. In the desert, 15 percent was not just a number. It was his daughter's Santiago school uniform. Three months of his mother's blood pressure medication. Two months of the remittance he sent home to Cusco each month.

"Why?" Rodrigo asked.

Jose pulled a folded sheet of paper from his work pocket. A printout of a Spanish-language article. The headline had the word "semiconductor" in it. Rodrigo read slowly. Car factories around the world were shutting down. Electronic deliveries were delayed by months. All for one tiny chip. To make that chip, high-purity silica was needed, and there were only a handful of places on Earth where that silica could be mined. Atacama was one of them.

He handed back the article. Looking down at the sand beneath his feet.

It was grayish-white with a fine grain. He stood here every day, but he had never seriously traced where this sand went. Loaded onto a ship, leaving the port. Loaded onto a truck, heading to a refinery. Melted at high heat, becoming an ingot. Cut thin, becoming a wafer. Circuits etched into it, becoming a chip. That chip inserted, lighting up some screen somewhere. Someone's smartphone. Someone's car dashboard. A monitor in some hospital's ICU.

Only after the world stopped did he see the end of the chain.

Everyone knew that Site Manager Flores had received an unexpected visitor early in the month. Two Asian men stepped out of a pickup truck and entered the manager's office, emerging about two hours later. Word spread that they were procurement officers from Silica Co. They wanted to increase contract volumes. How that conversation traveled through what channels and touched the labor cost item, becoming 15 percent and landing in his account, Rodrigo had no exact way of knowing. It was the kind of logic that would appear in textbooks, but watching that circuit work in real-time on his own hands was the first time.

The work siren wailed.

He picked up his shovel. The sensation of digging sand was no different from yesterday. His back was sore, the wind was cold, dust forced its way into his eyes. Yet strangely, today what he held in his hand felt different. Whether it was the sand that had changed, or himself, he couldn't say.

Jose spoke up again from beside him.

"Isn't it strange that factories somewhere in the world have to stop for our wages to go up?"

Rodrigo paused for a moment, then continued digging.

"It's strange. But it went up."

Jose laughed softly. It was a conversation that would not continue. The two men moved their shovels in silence.

Far off, the Andes ridge was still shrouded in darkness. When the sun rises, silica dust catches the light and sparkles silver. Jose had said once that watching that light was the only reason he kept doing this job. Rodrigo had simply listened at the time, but today those words caught him in a different way.

The sun began to rise above the ridge.

The sand shone.
