At eleven p.m., Seo Ji-an was alone in the quality analysis room at Danwoohee Metals. In the room lit by a single fluorescent panel, the monitor glow made two squares in her glasses.

The problematic number was one line in the month-end ledger from Plant 3's gallium refining line: 94.2% recovery rate. For fourteen months, that line's recovery rate had never exceeded the 91.8 to 92.3 range. If the process had suddenly improved, it would be cause for celebration. But Seo Ji-an, only two years into the job, already knew this much. Numbers that improved hid more than numbers that worsened.

"Another late night?" a security guard peered through the doorway.

Seo Ji-an smiled and turned back to the screen.

She traced the denominator backward. Raw material intake volume, lot numbers, weighing slips. The numbering was straightforward. Six-digit date, two-digit supplier code, three-digit sequence. Yet a raw material lot numbered `241106-KR-017` that came in the second week of November appeared twice in the ledger—once in Plant 3, once in Plant 1's shipping record.

The same material had been consumed in two places once each. Or material consumed once had been counted twice.

If the latter, the denominator should have inflated. But the recovery rate had gone up, which meant the denominator had shrunk. Seo Ji-an's fingers stopped. Someone had reduced the intake volume. Where had the missing gallium gone?

She went downstairs to the storage room where the original weighing slips were kept. The storage room at one a.m. held only the smell of paper and the hum of a cooling unit. As she turned through the file of slips, her hand stopped. On slip 017, in the weight column, there was a handwritten correction. 412 kilograms crossed out and written over as 382 kilograms. A 30-kilogram difference. The initials in the signature field were `M.J.H.`—Manager Ma Jeong-hun.

"What are you doing down here at this hour," a voice came from behind.

Seo Ji-an returned the slip to the file and turned. Manager Ma stood at the storage room entrance. He wore a jacket and carried nothing in his hands.

"I'm auditing the month-end figures. Plant 3's recovery rate seemed unusual."

"Nothing unusual about it. Process improvement," Manager Ma said, his tone calm. "Ji-an, thirty kilograms isn't the issue. This year the customer certification renewal is on the line. If the recovery rate drops below 92 percent, our qualification to deliver is threatened. And when our qualification shakes, does only ours shake? The lines receiving our alloys shake too."

"Then where is the 30 kilograms?"

The silence lasted. The cooling unit cycled off and on.

"In the slag tank," Manager Ma said. "We didn't discard it. We're stockpiling it. Next quarter we'll reprocess it and adjust the ledger."

Seo Ji-an knew that wasn't a lie. That made it colder. If it had been a lie, it would have been one person's misconduct. But this was a way of borrowing time. This month's numbers repaid by next month. And once she could see that structure, everything else began to show.

She went back to the office and pulled up the month-end ledgers from the past two years all at once. There were eleven months where lot numbers overlapped. It began that year, two years ago, when gallium and germanium exports came under licensing restrictions and raw material prices fluctuated. As raw materials grew scarce, the ledger softened first. The material test certificates sent to customers quoted these lot numbers directly from the ledger. Below the certificates came the component history using that alloy, and below that the finished product history.

When one line is off, every line stacked on it is off by the same amount. Whether the misaligned lines numbered one or thousands, no one had counted yet.

At four a.m., Seo Ji-an opened a new email. She typed the audit office address in the recipient field, then deleted it. She typed the quality assurance director's address, then deleted it. Before the customer certification contact's address, the cursor blinked for a long time.

Outside the window, the first cargo truck passed through the main gate. White steam still rose from Plant 3's smokestack. Only two people in this building now knew what percentage the line actually ran at, when the ledger said 94.2 percent.

She left the recipient field blank and typed a single line in the subject.

`Regarding lot 241106-KR-017.`

Then she began writing the first line of the body.
