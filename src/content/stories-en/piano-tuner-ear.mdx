> *"Even after fifty years, the trembling of strings never lies."*
> *— Oh Han-gyeol, Piano Tuner · emotion: resolve*

Every time Oh Han-gyeol walked down the basement corridor of the concert hall, his footsteps sounded different. On days when the old carpet laid over the concrete was damp, his steps were slightly heavier. In the dry winters, they rang out lightly. From that difference, he gauged the day's stage humidity. It was a habit accumulated over forty-three years.

In his bag was one tuning lever, one tuning fork, and nothing else. He had stopped using the digital tuner twenty years ago. Sometimes the numbers matched, but the sound was wrong.

---

That morning, Lee Su-yeon, the concert hall's operations manager, came down to the basement herself. That alone was unusual. On her tablet screen was a promotional video for HanEum Tech's new product, paused. It described how a precision microphone would capture the frequency of each string, and a deep learning algorithm would calculate the optimal tension and guide the tuner with precise numbers.

"Twelve music conservatory practice rooms across the country completed installation this year. There are also reports that the accuracy is three times higher than the human ear."

Oh Han-gyeol closed his bag's zipper without looking up.

"So."

"Before next season's contract renewal... I wanted to review it at least."

Before she finished speaking, he was already climbing the basement stairs. His silence was the answer.

---

A grand piano stood alone on the stage. Oh Han-gyeol opened its lid, sat down, and slowly played a full octave, do through ti. The sound traveled through the air and settled deep inside his ear.

It was there.

One of the three strings on G-sharp, an octave above, was sagging very slightly. It was within the software's tolerance margin. But Oh Han-gyeol knew. This hall had sunlight streaming through the south-facing window at two in the afternoon, and at that moment, the wooden stage floor expanded silently. That half-cent discrepancy would become a full cent at the exact instant the day's first note would sound.

It was not a measurement. It was what had accumulated over forty-three years of living through every spring, summer, fall, and winter in this hall. No matter how accurate HanEum Tech's algorithm was, it had never been here before. It did not know how much the floor of this hall would expand this afternoon.

He picked up the lever and raised the pitch by half a cent. The three strings began to breathe together.

---

When the tuning was done, pianist Park Min-jae came up onto the stage. He tapped a few keys lightly and stopped.

"The sound is different today."

Oh Han-gyeol spoke quietly.

"Raised it by half a cent."

Park Min-jae nodded once and began his practice. Oh Han-gyeol descended the stairs without looking back. There was no need to look back.

---

Su-yeon was waiting in the corridor. Her tablet was off.

"Teacher, we haven't made a decision about HanEum Tech yet."

Oh Han-gyeol adjusted his bag on his shoulder.

"There's nothing wrong with that machine. I sometimes use a frequency calculator myself."

He paused and continued.

"But there's no way to ask something that has never been here what sound this hall wants to make this afternoon."

Su-yeon said nothing. Oh Han-gyeol said nothing more. The conversation ended like that, left open.

The afternoon sunlight at two o'clock slanted diagonally across the corridor windows. From somewhere inside the stage, the sound of Park Min-jae's practice drifted out. The G-sharp raised by half a cent held its place, at least for this afternoon.
