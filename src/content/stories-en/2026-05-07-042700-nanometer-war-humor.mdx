## 1. The Pride of 0.001 Millimeters

Autumn 2019, meeting room at NanoTech's Cheonan plant. Team leader Park Jin-soo sighed as he read an email from a customer.

"Precision tolerance within ±0.5 micrometers. Request for 10x improvement over existing specifications."

Deputy Kim nearly spat out his coffee.

"Team lead, is that physically possible? Human hair is 50 micrometers thick. You're asking us to divide that into 100 parts."

"It's possible. If it can't be done, we'll make it work."

Park's tone seemed joking, but his eyes were serious. NanoTech was a company that manufactured critical equipment for the semiconductor manufacturing process—machines that fixed and moved wafers. In the semiconductor industry where circuits are etched at the nanometer scale, a 0.001 millimeter error could turn a wafer worth hundreds of millions of won into waste.

## 2. The Engineers' War

Development ran into obstacles. The limits of mechanical components were clear. Temperature changes of just one degree caused metal to expand, introducing errors.

"Then we just fix the temperature."

New employee Lee's remark made the team burst out laughing.

"Sir, is this a refrigerator factory? We can't turn the entire plant into a climate-controlled chamber."

But Park leaned forward, lost in thought.

"...Maybe not constant temperature, but real-time correction?"

From that day on, the team began attaching 200 temperature sensors throughout the equipment and developing a system that predicted thermal expansion with AI to correct the motion. Colleagues joked about "over-engineering by tech nerds," but six months later, when the prototype arrived, everyone fell silent.

Tolerance ±0.3 micrometers. It exceeded the customer's requirements by 50%.

## 3. The Customer's Reaction

A demonstration held at Global Semiconductor's Hwaseong campus. The NanoTech team, tense-faced, set up the equipment. Global Semiconductor engineers watched expressionlessly.

"We'll begin."

The wafer was placed on the equipment. A robotic arm moved in nanometer increments, etching a test pattern. The measurement result appeared on screen.

Tolerance 0.28 micrometers.

The customer's team lead took off his glasses and looked at the screen again.

"...Is this real?"

"Yes. Thanks to our real-time thermal compensation system."

"200 sensors? Wouldn't maintenance costs be..."

"Each sensor costs 500 won. The total is KRW 100,000."

The room fell silent. Then one of Global Semiconductor's engineers quietly began to clap. That applause spread throughout the room.

## 4. Beyond the Nanometer

That evening, at the team dinner. Park raised his soju glass.

"Everyone worked hard. But you know..."

"What now, Team lead?"

"The customer brought up the next project. This time they're asking for ±0.1 micrometers tolerance."

The team members let out groans in unison. Kim drained his soju in one shot.

"So we'd need to attach 2,000 sensors?"

"No, let's try something different this time. I heard there's something called a quantum sensor..."

Lee's eyes widened.

"Team lead, isn't that Nobel Prize-winning technology?"

"That's what makes it fun. We could be the world's first."

As of 2026, NanoTech holds a unique position in Korea's semiconductor equipment market. When asked by a reporter about the secret to their technological prowess, Park answered with a smile.

"The secret? It's pretending not to hear the word 'impossible.' And not skimping on 500 won sensors. Nanometers aren't bought with money—they're earned through obsession."

On the back of his business card was written in small letters:
