Meerstream had long believed it was the ultimate victor. Ten years ago, it was the only remote in the living room. Back then, a single subscription fee unlocked all stories. Then Stellaplus moved in next door, followed by OnTV, then Moonlight Cinema. Studios each took back their work and hung it in their own homes. Meerstream's shelves grew bare. It tried to fill the gaps with original content, but money drained away like a river. Stellaplus wasn't faring better. Its ledger recorded a loss year after year. OnTV finally broke its silence one night during a quarterly earnings call. "We're all losing audiences while fighting for each other's viewers." No one contested the remark. Subscribers had already grown savvy. They came for just one month when a new series dropped, watched the finale, and quietly left. The industry called this churn; subscribers called it shopping. Soon came the first change. Meerstream rolled out a cheaper ad-supported tier. Something it once despised—the mid-roll ads of the old broadcast era—it brought back with its own hands. It even put a fence around friends' shared passwords. Remarkably, people grew angry yet stayed longer.

The second change was stranger still. Stellaplus and OnTV merged under one roof, becoming a single product. Moonlight Cinema disappeared into a telecom billing plan, its name shrinking. From rivals, they became partners in each other's mouths.

One night, Meerstream stood before its old shelves and thought. What had scattered was gathering again, and it wasn't liberation—it was cable's face. Channel numbers had become subscription lists with only the names changed.

But there was one more strange thing. The most beloved work wasn't a new series. A drama that ended ten years ago rose to the top of viewing charts each time it moved homes. Wherever the work hung, it brought its audience with it. Meerstream couldn't tell if this was comfort or warning. The homes were merging, yet stories still roamed on their own.

At two in the morning, a subscriber opens its door, enters, and presses play. Unable to choose what to watch, they scroll through the screen for a while before their hand stops on something they've seen before. Meerstream quietly measures the length of that hesitation. It is the only precise number it has now.
