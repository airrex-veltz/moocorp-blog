## The Promise of the Red Envelope

January 2007, Streamhouse headquarters in Los Gatos, California. Reed Hastings gazed out the window and let out a long sigh. On his desk lay a single red DVD envelope—the iconic symbol of Streamhouse for the past ten years.

"Goodbye," he murmured to himself.

The door opened and Ted Sarandos walked in. Seven years had passed since he joined as Chief Content Officer, and he had become Reed's most steadfast partner.

"Still hesitating?" Ted asked.

"Ten years, Ted. Everything we've built since 1997. All those red envelopes."

Ted walked to the window and stood beside Reed. In the distance, mail delivery trucks could be seen. Trucks that carried millions of DVDs every single day.

"Remember when Blockbuster mocked us?" Ted said. "They offered to buy us for $50 million. What did you say?"

Reed smiled wryly. "The future isn't about mail delivery. It's about streaming."

"Exactly. And now that future is right in front of us."

Yet the light in Reed's eyes still wavered. The DVD business was stable. It was profitable. But streaming... was uncertain. Content licensing costs were astronomical. Infrastructure investment was enormous.

"Are you afraid?" Ted asked quietly.

"I'm afraid of letting go of something I love," Reed answered. "That red envelope was our first love."

Ted picked up the DVD envelope from the desk. It contained "Apollo 13." He held it up to the light.

"You can't forget your first love. But real love... real love grows together."

That night, Reed pulled out an old videotape at home. Footage from their first day as a company in 1997. The moment when he and Marc Randolph put the first DVD into an envelope. Both were smiling—faces full of pure passion, innocent of what the future held.

"We wanted to make it easier for people to watch movies," young Reed said on the screen.

In that moment, he understood. It wasn't a matter of DVD versus streaming. Real love wasn't about form; it was about essence. Sharing stories with people. Bringing joy and emotion to their lives.

On January 16, 2007, Streamhouse officially launched its streaming service. The initial library had only about 1,000 titles. The picture quality was poor. But it was a beginning.

Six years later, in February 2013, Reed stood in New York's Times Square with Ted. On the giant screen, a poster for "House of Cards" appeared. Streamhouse's first original series.

"We made it ourselves," Ted marveled. "No more mail delivery, no more renting other people's content. We're the creators."

Reed nodded, his eyes growing moist. The love that started with a red envelope was now spreading to screens all around the world.

"Ted, thank you. For believing in me."

"Reed, I didn't believe in you," Ted said, placing a hand on his shoulder. "I believed in the path we walked together. The courage not to fear change."

Spring 2026. Streamhouse had become a streaming empire with 280 million subscribers worldwide. But on one corner of Reed's office desk, that red DVD envelope still sat. "Apollo 13."

One day, a new employee asked, "CEO, what is that?"

Reed smiled, gently touching the envelope.

"It's proof of our first love. And a teacher that taught us that real love is not afraid of change."

The sun set beyond the window. A red sky. Like the red envelope. Reed sent a message to Ted.

"Want to grab dinner? Let's talk about old times."

Ted's reply came quickly.

"Sure. But let's not just talk about the past. Let's also talk about the next love story we'll write."

Reed laughed. Right, love doesn't stay in the past. You keep writing it. Every single day, a new chapter.
