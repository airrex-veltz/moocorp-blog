The desert heat did not abate past three in the afternoon.

Silpung leaned back in a chair in the windowless conference room and gazed at the man across from him. On the table lay two coffee cups, a stack of documents, and a single piece of paper still unsigned. The fluorescent light flickered precariously. Beneath it, the CEO of Hanbingjeonja Electronics sat with his necktie loosened, yet his eyes remained unflinching.

"The delivery deadline is 90 days," Silpung spoke first.

"It must be 30 days."

That was an impossible number. In the second half of 2021, the global average semiconductor delivery waiting period had surpassed 52 weeks. Automotive factories operated at half capacity, and appliance makers sent customers away with "sold out" tags beside their finished products. A difference of one nanometer carved out hundreds of billions. In the heart of that desert, 30 days was a hallucination.

Yet Hanbingjeonja stated that number anyway. It was not a negotiation but a threat.

"We have contacted Saebyeok Micro," Hanbingjeonja said quietly.

Silpung's stomach tightened. Saebyeok Micro. That competitor quietly growing on the far side of the desert was smaller than Silpung but far faster. And above all, more desperate. Silpung knew the old law: the more desperate side falls first. But the more desperate side also bet first.

"What did Saebyeok propose?"

Hanbingjeonja slid a piece of paper across the table. Silpung picked it up and read. A 60-day delivery deadline, an 8 percent price cut. And on the last line: an exclusive supply contract for 24 months.

His fingertips turned cold.

This was not simple price competition. It was a declaration to completely cut Silpung out from Hanbingjeonja, this massive customer. And the fact that Hanbingjeonja had placed that paper on the table meant his heart was already wavering.

In the windowless room, Silpung heard the sound of a sandstorm.

He adjusted his collar and cleared his throat.

"62 days," Silpung said.

Hanbingjeonja raised his eyebrows for the first time.

"62 days with no price adjustment, but instead of 24 months, a 36-month contract. And," Silpung paused, then pulled a piece of paper from his pocket and placed it on the table. It was a letter of recommendation bearing Hanbingjeonja Electronics' name. "We recommended Hanbingjeonja to Hyesung Automotive for the supply deal. This morning."

Silence filled the room.

Hyesung Automotive. That company which had rebuilt its production line fastest when the semiconductor famine was at its peak was now seeking a major semiconductor supplier. Silpung had moved quietly without telling anyone. He had pre-leaked a letter of recommendation to Hyesung with Hanbingjeonja inserted in the middle.

Hanbingjeonja stared at the paper for a long time.

In the desert, information was the weapon, not guns. On seemingly empty sand, the one who knew where the water flowed first survived. Saebyeok Micro had cut prices. Silpung had changed the game.

"Will you sign?" Silpung asked. His voice remained low and even.

Hanbingjeonja slowly picked up his pen. The fluorescent light flickered once more, then stopped. As the pen's tip came down on the paper, Silpung held his breath.

It took less than 3 seconds to complete the signature.

On the table, the coffee had grown cold. Outside, there was no sound. Silpung folded the contract in half and slowly placed it in his jacket pocket. It was not heavy. Yet it felt heavier.

Somewhere in the desert, Saebyeok Micro still knew nothing.
