Lim Bok-soon wept the first time she heard that voice.

It was her own voice. More precisely, it was her voice recorded twelve years before she suffered the stroke. Her daughter Ji-su had converted an old videotape to digital, extracted voice samples from it, and sent them to VoiceOne, a voice restoration startup. What the company returned to eighty-two-year-old Lim Bok-soon was the voice of fifty-year-old Lim Bok-soon.

Sitting in a chair by the window of the physical therapy room, Lim Bok-soon tapped the tablet screen with her finger. She typed "I would like some coffee." Then it came from the speaker—clear, crisp, and unwavering. The voice from thirty years ago.

Every time Ji-su heard her mother speak, something swelled in her chest. At first, it was comfort. After that, she didn't know what it was.

Speech therapist Choi Sun-ho first noticed something unusual at the beginning of winter. It was six months into Lim Bok-soon's hospitalization. Since receiving the VoiceOne device, Lim Bok-soon had hardly opened her mouth. Even after the stroke, some of her vocalization ability remained, and with training she could produce sounds for short words—that was the work Sun-ho had been doing with Lim Bok-soon for half a year.

But Lim Bok-soon no longer made effort in the training. When Sun-ho said "Try pronouncing 'ah,'" Lim Bok-soon picked up the tablet.

Sun-ho wrote down the observation in her pending report. The same phenomenon appeared in three of her patients. There were already several studies showing that assistive devices suppress speech attempts in patients using AAC devices. But the case with a patient's own restored voice was different. The patients didn't feel discomfort. Rather, they became too comfortable. That voice was so perfect—unwavering, unfaltering, unhesitating—that the patients seemed to begin experiencing their own voice as something incomplete.

One day, Sun-ho carefully broached the subject with Lim Bok-soon. Lim Bok-soon stared at the tablet screen for a long moment, then typed.

"That's me."

Sun-ho looked at the sentence for a moment. "The voice from before was yours," he said. "Your voice now is also yours."

Lim Bok-soon turned her head and looked out the window. She didn't type a response.

One afternoon when Ji-su came to visit, her mother's tablet wasn't charging. Lim Bok-soon didn't know this and tapped the screen. No sound came out. While Ji-su searched for the charging cable, Lim Bok-soon sat quietly for a moment. Then—she opened her mouth.

The sound was small and rough. "Ji...su." Thin and uneven, a sound that could not become text.

Ji-su froze, cable in hand.

Lim Bok-soon didn't look at the tablet. She didn't wait for it to charge. She just gazed toward the window. Ji-su sat beside her mother and placed the tablet on her lap. Without plugging in the charging cable.

The two of them sat in silence for a while.
