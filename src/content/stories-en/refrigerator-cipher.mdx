Seo Jun pushed the envelope to the edge of his desk. He had been doing this for the past ten days.

Inside the envelope were a merger and acquisition proposal and two pages of legal review. The sender was Dae-Ho Foods—a behemoth with annual revenues of KRW 12 trillion that had established a frozen and refrigerated logistics network across Asia. The company had knocked on Cheong-Pa Refrigeration's door on the day the first snow fell.

Cheong-Pa Refrigeration was a company that had distributed fresh food from three logistics hubs in southern Gyeonggi Province. Forty-seven employees, twenty-three refrigerated trucks. The warehouse was old and the headquarters building had peeling exterior walls. Yet Dae-Ho Foods wanted this company. There was only one reason.

The refrigerator cipher.

The exact name was "temperature management protocol." A control system that linked twelve refrigerated warehouses, sixty-one frozen containers, and two hundred thirty mobile temperature loggers into one unified system. The problem was that it appeared in no system manual. The authentication codes for the warehouse temperature control devices, the manual emergency routines when the power went out, the procedure for suppressing alarms when humidity deviation exceeded the standard—all of this existed only inside Myeong-ja's head and inside a metal clipboard on the wall of warehouse 2.

Myeong-ja had endured at this company for twenty-two years. His hands understood the language that refrigerators spoke. By the sound of the machine alone, he sensed temperature deviations. When the control panel failed, he made manual adjustments by eye. The due diligence team sent by Dae-Ho Foods spent four days circling around before eventually settling beside Myeong-ja and beginning to take notes.

"We appreciate your cooperation with the system integration."

It was the words of the Strategic Planning Department head at Dae-Ho Foods. Cooperation. Seo Jun was uncomfortable with the word. Cooperation was always one-directional.

Dae-Ho Foods' proposal was straightforward. Within ninety days of completion of the acquisition, all refrigerated control devices at Cheong-Pa Refrigeration would be integrated under its ERP system. On-site staff would receive training to transition to the new dashboard. Veterans like Myeong-ja would be given an additional six-month contract as a "system transition consultant."

The rest of the story was not in the contract.

Seo Jun remembered something from two years ago. It was when a similar-sized competitor had been incorporated into another large food company. The integration process was flawless on paper. Standard protocols, single platform, real-time monitoring. But on the fifth day after incorporation, alarms went off in three frozen zones. The new system had mistaken non-standard settings at the site for normal values and overwritten them. Dozens of tons of frozen chicken had deviated from the standard temperature for four hours. The industry newsletter carried one sentence: **"A temporary error in the process of technology transition."**

The industry newsletter did not report the volume discarded.

"Myeong-ja, bring me that clipboard."

Seo Jun opened the conference room door. Three people from Dae-Ho Foods were waiting with their laptops open. Outside the window it was already dark, and in the warehouse a truck idled with its engine running.

Myeong-ja entered holding the clipboard—a sheet densely filled with handwritten code tables, temperature response sequences, layers of corrections accumulated over many years. The head of Dae-Ho Foods reached out his hand.

Seo Jun reached for it first.

"I'm not handing this over. I'm reading it to you."

The manager's eyebrows raised.

"The protocol only works if you memorize it. The moment you copy the paper and upload it to a server, the skill leaves the field. What that skill means is answered by the case I mentioned earlier."

Silence flowed. The truck engine sound briefly shook the conference room's glass and then faded.

The Dae-Ho Foods manager closed his laptop. And for the first time, he looked at Seo Jun properly.

"Tell me your terms."

Negotiation restarted from that moment. Not on acquisition conditions, but on integration methods.

The refrigerator was still running. The cipher was still in Myeong-ja's hands, and the clipboard from warehouse 2 was tucked under Seo Jun's arm. What Dae-Ho Foods wanted was not that piece of paper but the person who could read it. And that person had not yet signed anything.
