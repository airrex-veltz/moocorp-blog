Winter early morning, 2:30 a.m., Jung Min-seo couldn't leave the monitor.

Night shifts at the cold chain storage center were usually quiet. While anticancer drugs, insulin, and vaccines slept somewhere between 2 and 8 degrees Celsius, he alternated between three screens, sipping thermos coffee. Good nights were ones with nothing happening. It was also the essence of this job. Nothing happening at all.

But sensor 3 started blinking.

"Sensor error. No measurement."

His heart skipped a beat. It could have been a simple contact failure. In most cases, he would just log it and report it during the morning shift change. But in sector 3, there was a lot of anticancer drugs that had been received that afternoon. The term "lot" sounds meaningless, but there were twelve patient names attached to it.

Jung Min-seo pushed his chair back.

The regulations contained a clause prohibiting single-staff night-shift workers from entering the storage room alone. The reason was that there was no one to help if he fell, the door locked, or someone suffered from hypothermia. He understood well why that clause existed. But there were cases the regulations hadn't anticipated. Whether the sensor was lying, or whether the internal temperature was actually rising—there was no way to know from this desk.

He put on a cold-weather jacket and picked up the keys.

The moment the storage room door opened, cold air hit his face. It was a good sign. He shone the flashlight on the analog thermometer attached to the wall next to the entrance. The needle pointed to 4.2 degrees. Normal range.

It was a sensor malfunction.

As Jung Min-seo left the storage room, he didn't think much. It was a matter of logging it, sending a morning message to the department in charge, and handing it over to the morning shift worker. No one would know. His name wouldn't appear in any report. Twelve people would remain unaware today. That someone had entered that room in the early morning.

That was enough.

When the first light of dawn began to seep in below the window, Jung Min-seo sat back down in front of the monitor and picked up the thermos. The coffee was already cold.

But it was fine. Tonight, like tonight, nothing happened.
