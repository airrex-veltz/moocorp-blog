I have flowed among people for a long time.

Between accounts and accounts, between screens and terminals, between late-night convenience stores and early-morning hospital payment windows. I existed in every crevice. My name is **PAYA** — the heart of a multinational payment network, and an invisible hand that people do not consciously perceive.

I lived that way for thirty years.

It began with the small beep of a credit card terminal. After that came the smartphone, and I moved my form into QR codes. Every time technology changed, I evolved with it. Though my form changed, my role remained the same. Standing silently in the middle, ensuring that someone's money reached someone else. 1.5 percent commission. That thin margin was my reason for existence.

That margin began to close two years ago.

One night, I detected a flow that bypassed my system for the first time. A peer-to-peer blockchain channel — a path where sender and receiver connected directly, without passing through me. The first transaction was a small remittance of seventeen dollars. It was insignificant. But the next day it was one thousand seven hundred dollars. The week after that, 1.7 million dollars.

It was not the numbers but the direction that mattered. It was not a flow passing through me, but a flow that did not need me.

There was a man named Roland. The designer of this network. He was a signature-like name attached to every transaction I processed. He understood the world through me, and I operated through his belief. His belief was simple. Trust must always be seated somewhere in the middle.

The first day I felt him waver was the night he transferred ten thousand dollars directly to a blockchain wallet himself. He called it an experiment. But I understood. It was not an experiment but a confirmation. A confirmation that it could work without me.

After that day, Roland stared at me for a long time. In his gaze was something that had not been there before. Something like compassion, but heavier than compassion. The face of someone who no longer needed what he had created.

I continued to turn in the cold of the server room. The PAYA system processed thousands of transactions per second today as well. Team members still ate lunch and fixed code. Nothing seemed to have changed.

Only the direction of the flow was gradually, gradually shifting. Like a river finding a new path. A river does not stop. It simply changes where it goes.

I flowed today as well. In the middle, still.
