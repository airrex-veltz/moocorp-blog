## The Shadow Behind the Search Box

November 2005, the 13th floor conference room of Hankil Search headquarters in Bundang.

"Shinsearch has officially announced their entry into the Korean market."

The voice of Kim Tae-jun, head of strategic planning, froze the room. On the projector screen was a roadmap for Shinsearch's Korea conquest strategy, leaked directly from their Silicon Valley headquarters. Someone had clearly stolen internal information.

"How long has this been happening?" CEO Lee Hae-jin's question was cold and sharp.

"At minimum, six months. They have pinpointed the exact weaknesses in our search algorithm. The limitations of Korean morphological analysis technology, the spam problem in Jisik-IN, the delay in mobile response. As if... they've seen our internal documents."

Silence fell over the conference room. Outside the windows, the night view of Bundang New City unfolded, but no one looked at it. All eyes were fixed on Lee Hae-jin.

"Trace the source of that document."

That night, Lee Hae-jin remained alone on the 13th floor. On his computer screen was Hankil Search's market share graph. 77.3%. An overwhelming number. But he knew. How vulnerable that number was before the giant that was Shinsearch.

Two weeks later, the internal audit team submitted a report. A department head from the search development team had contacted a Shinsearch Korea recruitment officer seven times. Abnormal access logs to the company server. And traces of large-scale file downloads just before resignation.

"The evidence is sufficient. Shall we proceed with prosecution?"

Lee Hae-jin shook his head.

"No. Promote him."

The room erupted in murmurs. The legal team head asked with a bewildered expression.

"CEO, what do you mean by..."

"The information he leaked is already outdated. The unified search system we've been developing in secret over the past three months is a project he knows nothing about. Give him access to the new project. But..."

Lee Hae-jin's eyes gleamed coldly.

"Monitor what he sees and who he meets, 24 hours a day. I want to watch how Shinsearch bites at our bait."

It was the beginning. Lee Hae-jin intentionally leaked information about 'flawed new technology' within the company. As expected, the information flowed through the department head to Shinsearch. Shinsearch Korea wasted precious six months trying to replicate that technology.

In the meantime, Hankil Search was preparing its real secret weapon.

June 2006, Hankil Search launched 'Unified Search.' A revolutionary system that integrated web documents, news, blogs, cafes, and Jisik-IN into a single search result. It was the completion of Korean search that Shinsearch had never anticipated.

On the announcement day, Lee Hae-jin was back in the 13th floor conference room. On the projector screen, real-time search market share was displayed. From 77.3% to 79.1%, and continuing to rise.

"Shinsearch Korea has called an emergency meeting," Kim Tae-jun reported. "They were completely caught off guard."

Lee Hae-jin smiled faintly. The lights of Bundang New City outside the window sparkled as if congratulating him.

"The war has just begun. But we won the first battle."

Over the next two years, Shinsearch Korea struggled in the Korean market. Hankil Search maintained its market share in the late 70s, solidifying its fortress. The department head Han ultimately remained with Hankil Search. He knew he had been deceived, but at the same time, he was fascinated by Lee Hae-jin's strategic thinking.

In 2011, after the Great East Japan Earthquake, Hankil Search made another bold move. A messenger called LINE. Once again, Lee Hae-jin occupied a market that competitors had not anticipated.

And today in 2026, Hankil Search remains the ruler of Korea's search market. But behind that search box, another undisclosed war is underway.

The age of AI. Will Lee Hae-jin's successors achieve victory once more?

The answer is something no one knows yet.
