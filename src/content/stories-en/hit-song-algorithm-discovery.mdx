Seo Ji-yu drank cold coffee while keeping two monitors on. After 2 AM, the Sorigil Curation Team office was always like this. Her colleagues had left hours ago, and the Han River outside the window glowed faintly with night lights.

Today she reviewed a report passed from the AI team. Sorigil introduced a new discovery system last December—internally called "Ear"—that scanned streaming data weekly and flagged tracks with anomalies. Average completion rate, save-to-share ratio, listening time distribution, repeat-play intervals. Tracks scoring highest resembled hit song patterns.

One name jumped out from this week's report.

Park Do-ha.

Three released songs. 4,200 monthly listeners. The oldest upload was eight months ago. Yet "Ear" assigned him the top discovery score across all new artists that week.

Ji-yu pulled out headphones.

The first track had no title—just the filename `track_01`. One acoustic guitar and one voice. No backing, no arrangement, just the voice. Lyrics seemed set in a rooftop room somewhere in Seoul. No specific location appeared, yet somehow Ji-yu could see the scene.

When the second song played, her coffee was completely cold.

When the third song ended, the clock read past 3 AM.

Ji-yu found an email address listed at the bottom of the report. The AI system automatically scraped public profile data and attached contact information. It was a free mail service account. Judging by the name rather than domain, it was almost certainly the artist's own.

Ji-yu stopped at the screen for a moment, then opened a new email.

---

Park Do-ha worked two jobs at a café.

Days at a café, nights in a small studio—a studio that was really a gosiwon room with soundproofing—he moved between the two. He started uploading music because he wanted to share with a friend, but one day he noticed strangers listening on repeat.

He received Ji-yu's email right after leaving the café shift.

Sorigil Curation Team. Seeing that name, Do-ha initially thought it was spam. On second read, he realized it wasn't. On the third, he stopped at the sentence about "Ear" discovering his tracks.

Something about being found by AI first felt uncomfortable.

He replied four days later.

---

They first met at a café near the Sorigil office. Do-ha arrived five minutes late; Ji-yu had already ordered two Americanos.

"When you reached out to me—was that you, or the system choosing me?"

Do-ha asked the moment he sat. Not a greeting but a question.

Ji-yu thought for a moment.

"Both."

"What does that mean?"

"The system selected you, but I listened too. Until 3 AM."

Do-ha said nothing.

"As of 2023, over 100,000 new songs upload globally every day," Ji-yu continued. "One person can't listen to all of them. That's why the system filters first. But the final judgment is human. For me, it was your songs."

Do-ha looked at the coffee in front of him for a moment.

"Why?"

"Listening, something comes back. It feels like remembering something that hasn't happened yet."

Do-ha looked up.

---

They spent two hours at the café that day. Almost no contract talk. Do-ha spoke about where he wrote the songs, and Ji-yu spoke about what she thought while the music played. The conversation felt strangely natural.

As they parted, Ji-yu said, "Next time we'll meet officially. I'll bring contracts."

Do-ha nodded, then stopped.

"You said 3 AM earlier."

"Yes."

"Why were you working at that hour?"

Instead of answering, Ji-yu laughed.

"That's just how this job is."

Do-ha walked toward the bus stop thinking about those words. October night air was cold. He pulled out his earbuds and put them back in his pocket.

The fact that Ji-yu had been there until 3 AM listening to his music stayed in his head longer than the contract.

---

What happened next to the two of them, where Do-ha's music went—that story hasn't been written yet.

Tonight, Sorigil's system is scanning data again. Somewhere in a rooftop room, someone is uploading a file with no title. And Ji-yu is probably looking at the monitor right now, drinking cold coffee.
