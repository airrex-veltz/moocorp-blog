At two in the morning, Minjae started the refrigerated truck.

He wiped the frost coating the dashboard with his hand and scanned today's delivery list. Hwaseong to Incheon, Suwon, Gapyeong. Eighty-three boxes. He'd read reports last year about how the early-morning delivery market had grown more than tenfold in recent years. That weight fell entirely on the sleep of contract drivers. Minjae, assigned to Nokya Express's Hwaseong center, knew this truth in his bones. Three years on the job. Average sleep: five hours a day.

He wasn't completely unhappy about it. The empty highways, the darkness that asked no questions. That sense of moving alone through a world asleep felt like a kind of freedom to him.

The problem came just before departure.

As he was organizing the pallets, he noticed a box that wasn't on the manifest. Thirty by thirty centimeters. A white box sealed tightly with brown tape. Both the consignee and sender fields were blank. Only a single address label: a location in Gapyeong, Gyeonggi Province, with only a street number.

"Must have been loaded by mistake."

Minjae muttered to himself and placed it in a corner of the truck. The duty manager had already left, and it was two in the morning—too late to call.

Twenty minutes after entering the Yeongdong Expressway, something vibrated. From inside the box. A sharp, brief tremor. At first he thought it was just road unevenness. Then it sounded again. Longer this time.

He pulled onto the shoulder.

Flashlight in hand, he climbed into the back. Cold air hit his throat. He knelt before the box and grasped the corner of the tape with his fingernails. Just as he was about to peel it back, his phone rang.

An unsaved number.

"Don't touch that."

A man's voice. Low and flat. No excitement, no threat. That made it stranger. That calm was what terrified him.

"Who is this?"

"One kilometer before Gapyeong IC. There are two drums on the right side of the shoulder. Leave the box next to them and just go."

The line went dead.

For five minutes, Minjae didn't move. The truck was twenty degrees below zero. The box made no sound anymore. It was as if nothing had ever been there at all.

He turned off the flashlight. Turned it back on.

He looked at the box. The seal was intact. The address on the label caught his eye. Gapyeong, Bukmyeon. Deep in the mountains.

For reasons he couldn't quite articulate, he drove.

The drums were really there. At the end of a shoulder with no road signs—two rusted drums. Minjae set the box down. He looked around, but there was no one. The night was so quiet that the highway noise felt distant.

He didn't look back.

He delivered all eighty-two remaining boxes. He got home at 5:23 a.m. and collapsed into bed.

Four days later, the Nokya Express Hwaseong logistics center caught fire. Cause unknown. No casualties. A news article appeared online and vanished the next morning.

Minjae took a screenshot of the article.

He still drives that same road every night. Still leaves at two in the morning, still works as a Nokya Express contract driver. What has changed is this: when an unlisted box appears on the pallet, he does nothing now. He doesn't load it. He doesn't report it. He simply leaves the parking lot, pretending not to see.

He doesn't know what was in that box.

He doesn't want to know.
