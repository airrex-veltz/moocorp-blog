The bull opened his eyes at four in the morning.

When darkness still clung thick over New York, he looked down at his territory woven from concrete and fiber optics. The lights of Wall Street reflected and rippled across the Hudson River. It was the land he had reclaimed, step by step, since the panic of spring 2020. With each rate cut, his shoulders grew broader. With each earnings beat, his hooves dug deeper into the ground.

He had lived long. The fact both filled him with pride and, sometimes, with weight.

A single artery labeled **SPY** ran along his spine. The sound of five hundred hearts beating in unison. That was his pulse. In February, that pulse had easily exceeded six thousand. Numbers that seemed untouchable had fallen into his palm. The dream of artificial intelligence had lifted valuations, and valuations had inflated the dream in return. The bull had enjoyed that cycle. Perhaps too much.

But this morning, the wind was different.

Mixed with the wind from the northwest was the dust of tariffs. Washington said it would erect new barriers. Semiconductors, automobiles, agricultural products. Items listed in succession pushed down the numbers in the pre-dawn futures market. The bull flared his nostrils. He knew that smell well. The smell of uncertainty. The smell that rises from somewhere in the long calculation of converting future earnings to present value, when the denominator grows larger.

A sound came from far away. A slow, low bellow. Heavy footsteps.

It was not yet visible. But the bull knew. Something was coming.

---

He had faced similar omens many times before. In 2022, when the Federal Reserve began raising rates, his legs trembled for the first time. That October, at the bottom of the deepest ravine, even he thought it was over. But he rose again. He always rose again.

That had been his confidence. Perhaps also his blind spot.

The bull stood above the city lights and inspected his body. His muscles were still firm. His mane still shone. The memory of running all through 2024 remained somewhere in his body. The sensation of speed. The feel of wind sweeping his mane backward. For the running bull, there was no gravity, no doubt. Only direction and velocity.

But on this early morning, he did not feel that sensation. Instead, there was stillness. A deep, waiting stillness.

---

When the first Monday of March dawned, the losses had already begun.

Before the opening bell even rang, the futures index had collapsed 1.2%. In market capitalization terms, it was a figure of hundreds of billions of dollars vanishing into thin air. The bull felt it in his body. A sudden heaviness in his back. As though someone had climbed onto him, yet no one was there.

The tariff announcement was broader than expected. Numbers that left no room for negotiation lined up one after another. Analysts began revising earnings forecasts. Revision always began cautiously, then at some point accelerated like a race. When one firm cut by 5%, another cut by 7%. What had been called premiums began to be called by new names. Bubbles. Or overheating. Or simply, delusion.

At first, the bull did not take it seriously. He had weathered dozens of corrections. 5%, 7%, sometimes even 10%. Each time, he steadied his breath, planted his feet, and ran again. This time would be the same. Surely this time too.

On the fourth day, that sound drew nearer.

The bull raised his head. At the far end of the market, where bond yields climbed, he saw the silhouette. Massive and dark and slow. The bear did not move with haste. He had no need to. He existed like gravity. The way heavy things are pulled downward was not his will but his nature.

Their eyes met.

The bull felt an emotion he could not name. It was not anger. Not fear. Something like a reunion with something old. Yes, I know this face. We take turns living through the same seasons.

---

The small caps trembled first.

Then came the growth stocks. Those carrying high valuations shook all the more as their size increased. Names trading at price-to-earnings ratios of forty, fifty, sixty suddenly found themselves at half those levels. Whether those numbers were returning to where they should have been, or whether they had fallen too far, no one could say for certain.

The bull experienced this as the most disorienting of sensations. Am I now becoming too small? Or was I originally too large?

That question had no answer. Not yet.

Ten days later, the index had fallen 12% from its peak.

It was officially the period called a correction. The newspapers used the word carefully at first, then with increasing boldness. The channels summoned charts from the past. 2000. 2008. 2020. The bull turned away from those channels. The past was the past, and now was now. People said history had rhythm. But knowing that history had rhythm and knowing exactly how this particular verse would end were entirely different things.

The noise did not stop. That noise consumed judgment.

---

It was the fifteenth night.

The bull stood alone. His territory had shrunk by a tenth. It was a figure measuring trillions of dollars lost, but to the bull it was not an abstract number. It was weight. The actual weight of shoulders sagging.

The bear had drawn nearer.

The bull asked himself: Should I run now?

Charge forward. That was his instinct. When he ran, the wind swept his mane backward, and in that sensation of speed he knew he was alive. But now the ground beneath his feet trembled.

He knew the name of that trembling. Trust. When trust trembles, the ground trembles. Somewhere in the calculation that converts future earnings to present value, when the denominator grows, the result shrinks. That calculation was cold and mechanical, but the hand that turned it was always human. When humans were afraid, they seized the denominator more tightly. Sometimes far more tightly than reality.

The bull knew this, yet he could not stop.

---

The bear stopped.

It was an unexpected moment. Ten steps ahead, he paused, then sat. The sound of his massive body settling onto the ground was dull and resonant. And then he spoke. For the first time.

"I am not hurrying."

That was all. Neither a threat nor a declaration. Simply a statement of fact. I am not hurrying. This direction is to some degree already determined, and because I know it, I do not run.

The bull heard those words for a long time. They remained like an echo.

Something inside his body recalibrated. Anger rose and fell away. Fighting spirit wavered and subsided. What remained was something strangely quiet. It was not fear. Not resignation either. It was, perhaps, awareness.

There was a saying that the market is always right. The bull disliked that saying. The market was not right. The market was the sum of collective emotion, and emotion was frequently wrong. But the market eventually found its place. Layers and layers of wrong emotions accumulated, and at some point reversed. In that moment of reversal, he ran again.

Then what was this moment now?

---

The bull did not run.

It was his choice. He could always run. Charging forward was the bull's instinct, but instinct was not always wisdom. Here in this place, on an index that had fallen 12%, he chose simply to stand.

Standing itself was a position.

He knew. That no one could say with certainty whether this correction was the end or the beginning. Charts were always read after the fact. No one could call the bottom. The bottom only received its name after it had already passed.

And so the bull waited. Without knowing the ending, standing, waiting.

Not far away, dawn was approaching. It was not yet light. But the texture of darkness had changed slightly. Whether that was dawn, or the prelude to a deeper night, the bull could not say.

He only knew that he was still standing.

That was enough. For now.
