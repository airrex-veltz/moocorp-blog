Min-jun unfolded the contract again. Thirty-seven pages. Signature line on the last page still blank.

2 a.m. Conference room of Sebit Semiconductor, sole fluorescent lights on. City night reflected dimly in glass. Min-jun reached for the coffee cup. Already cold.

Seven years. Aurora Games years.

When Sebit opened, gaming chip markets were growing. Min-jun and co-founder Su-yeon handled Aurora's graphics processor design from a three-person office. They never missed deadlines. Bugs appeared, nights erased them. Sebit grew to fifteen people. Aurora Games became 61% of revenue. Their most-trusted partner.

Aurora's dev director Park Tae-jun called three months ago.

"Heard rumors about you."

Min-jun knew. "You mean Dream Systems."

"Heard there's exclusivity."

"Still reviewing."

Silence over the line. Tae-jun's tone stayed calm, but the calm cut sharp. "Our next chip integrates NPU. AI inference capability. What does that trigger in the Dream Systems contract?"

Min-jun didn't answer.

---

Dream Systems contacted three months prior. AI inference accelerator design request. Contract scale matched Sebit's annual revenue—largest single deal since founding. Global AI accelerator market exploded post-2023. One analysis claimed market size tripled in two years. Dream companies went frantic for design capacity.

Contract issue: Section 12.3.

*"Supplier shall not, during contract period and 18 months after, without Dream Systems' prior written consent, engage in development, design support, or technology transfer of competing AI compute technology."*

"Competing AI compute technology" was defined in Appendix A:

*"Any semiconductor design including inference compute, neural accelerators, NPU architecture, or equivalent functionality."*

Aurora's next chip embedded NPU.

Min-jun requested Appendix A modification two months ago. Explicitly except gaming chips. Dream's legal team, led by Son Jae-won, stalled a week, then answered: "Company risk policy forbids. Contract proceeds as stated or doesn't proceed at all."

Since that day, Min-jun spent every night in this conference room.

---

Su-yeon held no objection. Sebit's CTO and co-founder, Su-yeon didn't understand Min-jun's hesitation. This afternoon's meeting confirmed it. Su-yeon sketched numbers on whiteboard.

"Dream contract means eight more engineers. AI market growth is five times gaming. Aurora already posted internal design team job postings. They'll eventually drop us."

Min-jun watched the whiteboard. All numbers correct.

"Remember the advance?" Min-jun asked.

Su-yeon's hand paused.

Startup's first year. Sebit's second project faced funding depletion just before delivery. Aurora's name transferred an 80-million-won advance before contract signature. No negotiation. Tae-jun convinced his company. That money meant Sebit survived that month.

Su-yeon set down the marker. Said nothing.

"I know the math," Min-jun continued. "I just don't know."

---

Chief Engineer Choi Ho-jin passed the glass conference room, paused. Alone inside, Min-jun stared at papers. Ho-jin hesitated about knocking, then passed.

The CEO's face when you don't decide isn't the face for conversation.

---

Tae-jun texted 11:30 p.m.

*"Lunch tomorrow? Let's talk."*

Min-jun held the phone. Tae-jun initiating meant either discontinuing their next chip (warning before formal notice) or final probe whether Sebit would drop Dream Systems.

If Dream drops Aurora's next generation...

Min-jun could pitch Dream. But that meant Sebit doesn't build for its oldest customer.

Or counter: refuse Dream. Then no new equipment investment. No team expansion. Dream would likely follow through anyway—outsourcing to someone hungrier.

Or: Tae-jun might simply want to eat together. Old partners.

Min-jun wrote the reply. Erased three times. Fourth try sent.

*"Good."*

---

Dream contract deadline: four days.

Son's last word: "No further extension."

Min-jun closed the contract.

The conference room fluorescents flickered faintly. Outside, an early morning delivery truck passed. City still awake. Sebit's conference room, single lights on. Clock read 2:12 a.m.

Min-jun picked up the pen.

The signature line remained blank. Four days. Meeting Tae-jun, hearing Son again, reviewing Su-yeon's numbers one more time. Time remained.

He set the pen down. Not yet.

Outside the night lights flickered across glass.
