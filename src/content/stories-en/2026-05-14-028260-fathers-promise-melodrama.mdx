In a high-rise office in Seoul's Gangnam district. Summer 2015. Manager Jang held the merger agreement in his hand as he gazed down from the window. The merger of Sejin Commerce and Cheil Industries. He was a construction engineer who had worked for this company for 27 years.

"Senior, are you alright?" Junior Manager Kim asked hesitantly.

Manager Jang nodded, but his eyes grew red. On his first day in 1988, as a 24-year-old young man, he had vowed to build the world's greatest buildings. That dream had come true. In 2010, Burj Khalifa rose in the Dubai desert—828 meters. He was a member of the structural design team.

"When we built the Petronas Tower in Malaysia, when we erected Taipei 101 in Taiwan," Manager Jang said in a trembling voice. "I was always proud of this company. A small trading company that Chairman Lee Byung-chul started in Daegu in 1938 became the world's greatest construction firm."

The merger was an unavoidable choice. Global competition was intensifying, and the group's governance structure needed reorganization. The merger with Cheil Industries was expected to create a massive conglomerate encompassing construction, trading, fashion, and resorts.

"This is not the end but a new beginning," Kim said.

Manager Jang took out a worn photograph from his wallet. Petronas Tower site in Malaysia in 1995. It was a photo taken with his father. His father had also been a Sejin Commerce engineer and the project director for that undertaking.

"Before my father passed away, he said something to me," Manager Jang said, gently stroking the photo. "What we build is not buildings but Korea's pride. We show our technology to the world under the name Sejin Commerce."

That night, Manager Jang stayed at the office and pulled out project materials spanning 27 years. Saudi high-speed rail, Australian LNG plant, Singapore Marina Bay Sands. Each project bore the sweat and tears of colleagues. All-night meetings, hundreds of design reviews, dangers and hardships on site.

At 4 a.m., he signed the agreement. When he put down his pen, tears fell. It was not sadness. They were tears of gratitude. For everything this company had given him, for everything he had given.

A month later, the merger was completed. A new Sejin Commerce was born. Manager Jang still led ultra-high-rise projects from the same office. Now he understood. The company name was not what mattered; the passion of the people gathered under it was the true legacy.

In 2023, as retirement drew near, he completed his final project. A 555-meter mixed-use complex in Seoul's Gangnam. A small plaque hung in the lobby.

"Sejin Commerce Construction Division 1938-2023: Dreams Connecting the World"

Before the plaque, he murmured. "Father, we did it. The name has changed, but the dream continues."

Kim, now a manager, approached. "Senior, could you help with the Saudi project bid next week?"

Manager Jang laughed. "Of course. Our story isn't over yet."
