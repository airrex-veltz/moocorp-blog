## 1. The Call

Friday, August 20, 2021, 3 p.m. The 28th floor of Mir Chemical's headquarters on Yeouido, Seoul. CEO Kwon Young-soo's phone vibrated.

"Another one in Michigan. A Bolt sitting in a parking lot..."

Before he could hang up, a second call came through. GM headquarters in Detroit. The third was from NHTSA, the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration.

"Director Kwon, the situation is..."

"I know. I'm convening the headquarters."

Thirty minutes later, the 28th-floor conference room was packed with 17 executives. The battery division head, legal team, quality division, finance—and a video conference screen connected to the U.S. subsidiary.

"Confirmed fires so far: 13. GM is reviewing an expanded recall." The quality division head reported.

"Scale?" Kwon asked.

"Worst case... all 140,000 units."

The room went silent. One hundred forty thousand units. Battery pack replacement alone would cost KRW 2 trillion. Add GM's production stoppage losses, reputational damage, and canceled future contracts...

"We need answers within 72 hours," Kwon said. "Before the U.S. market opens Monday. We form three teams. Technical team investigates the cause. Legal team negotiates with GM. Communications team prepares our statement. No one leaves this building from now on."

## 2. 72 Hours

**First 24 hours: Tracing the cause**

At the Osan factory R&D center, battery cell autopsy began. The battery pack recovered from the fire-damaged vehicle was CT-scanned, and each cell examined under an electron microscope.

"Here it is," the senior researcher pointed at the monitor. "Tears in the internal separator membrane. Appears to be a microscopic defect from manufacturing."

"What percentage?"

"0.03%. But as thermal degradation progresses after accumulated charging..."

"The probability increases."

Saturday, 4 a.m. The technical team reached its conclusion. Specific batches produced on Line 2 of the Osan factory in 2019. A pressure setting error in the separator coating process. Every cell made during that period was a latent risk.

**Second 24 hours: Negotiation**

The video conference with GM began Saturday night at 10 p.m. (9 a.m. Detroit time).

"Shinra Electronics—do you understand how serious this is?" GM's CPO asked sharply. "Our entire EV strategy could collapse."

"We fully understand GM's position. Mir Chemical will take full responsibility."

"Full responsibility?"

"Battery pack replacement costs, production stoppage losses, recall expenses. We'll cover all of it."

The GM screen went quiet for a moment. This was a proposal they hadn't anticipated.

"There is a condition. We'll supply the replacement batteries. New batches, cells that have gone through strengthened quality verification processes. And we propose joint development of next-generation batteries as a result of this incident."

Sunday, 2 a.m. GM responded. "We'll review the agreement."

**Final 24 hours: The announcement**

Sunday noon. The communications team entered Kwon's office with a draft.

"What if we say this? 'Mir Chemical takes full responsibility for the GM Bolt EV battery recall crisis...'"

"No," Kwon said, picking up a pen. "Change it to this. 'Mir Chemical, together with GM, prioritizes customer safety above all...'"

"Why? It's our responsibility..."

"Taking responsibility and bearing responsibility are not the same. We are partners. We solve the problem together."

Monday, 6 a.m. (7 p.m. Monday, Korea time). Three hours before the New York market opening. Mir Chemical and GM issued a joint statement simultaneously.

"The two companies have agreed to comprehensive cooperation to enhance battery safety. Mir Chemical will expand investment in next-generation battery technology, and GM is reviewing the establishment of a joint venture with Mir Energy Solutions."

## 3. What Came After

October 2021. Mir Chemical paid GM KRW 1.9 trillion in compensation. At the time, it was the largest recall cost in the company's history.

But the 72-hour crisis response actually strengthened trust. In 2022, GM announced a battery plant joint venture with Mir Energy Solutions in Michigan, Tennessee, and Ohio. Total investment: $14 billion.

"Because we didn't run then," Kwon said in a later interview. "What we proved in those 72 hours wasn't technology. It was accountability."

As of 2024, Mir Energy Solutions operates 10 battery plants worldwide with GM, Ford, Honda, and Raon Mobility. Market capitalization: KRW 70 trillion. The world's third-largest battery manufacturer, after Raon Electric and CATL.

The fire ended. But the real competition was just beginning.
