## 72 Hours of Choice

May 2026, Icheon, Gyeonggi Province. SK Hynix M16 FAB cleanroom.

"Yield at 79.2%. We're short by 0.8%."

Park Jun-hyuk's hands trembled as he stared at the monitor. Seventy-two hours remained until the Vision Chips HBM4 supply contract deadline. If they failed to achieve 80% yield, a KRW 6 trillion contract would go to the competition.

"Check again."

Inside the cleanroom, 100 engineers in yellow protective suits inspected the 12-layer stacking process. HBM4 boasted 50% faster bandwidth than HBM3, but the stacking process allowed zero tolerance for errors beyond 0.1 micrometers.

"Micro-fracture detected at Layer 9."

Park grasped his headset tightly. A fracture at the ninth stacking stage meant the entire chip would have to be discarded.

"Lower the process temperature by 0.5 degrees. Extend TSV etching time by 2 seconds."

"Director, that will add 14 more hours to the entire process. With the deadline—"

"Do the best we can right now."

Forty-eight hours later.

"Yield at 79.8%!"

Still not enough. Park wandered the corridor outside the cleanroom. It was the longest night of his 20-year semiconductor career.

At 4 a.m., his phone rang.

"Director, we have intel that Sejin has delivered HBM4 samples to Vision Chips."

The competition had moved first. There was no time to waste.

"Ramp all production lines to 120% capacity. Deploy all emergency personnel."

The final 24 hours.

M16 FAB became a warzone. Etching, cutting, deposition, inspection. Test chips poured out every four hours. Engineers worked the full 72 hours without rotation.

"Yield at 79.9%."

"80.1%!"

"80.3%!"

One hour before the deadline, Park personally packaged 300 final samples. His hands shook, but everything SK Hynix had was contained within them.

Incheon Airport. The cargo plane bound for Vision Chips' headquarters took off.

Three days later, an email arrived from Santa Clara, California.

**"HBM4 validation passed. Contract confirmed. - Vision Chips"**

Park looked up at the Icheon sky through the cleanroom window. The 72-hour war was over. But he knew something. The next war had already begun.
