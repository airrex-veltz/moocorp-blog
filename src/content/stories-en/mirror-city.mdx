Five in the morning. That's when I first doubted myself.

The woman beyond the reflection panel moved her head first. 0.3 seconds, perhaps less. But I saw it clearly. She moved before I did.

Sewun City, October 2047.

Hanseong City Tech won the design contract for this city in 2039. The development plan announced at the time included a provision called the 'City Continuity System.' It would generate a digital twin for each resident and integrate that data into the city's entire infrastructure. The reflection panels were terminals of that system. Real-time recognition of residents, learning their patterns, and ultimately a system that knew residents better than they knew themselves. As of 2043, twelve cities worldwide operated on a similar structure. Sewun City adopted it latest, and with the highest degree of completion.

I knew all of it. I was part of the team that designed the system.

---

My name is Han Yujin. Thirty-four years old. Senior engineer, City Continuity Team at Hanseong City Tech.

That was me as I knew myself.

But this morning, the Han Yujin beyond the reflection panel wore a face I didn't recognize.

---

I stopped in front of the elevator.

The doors opened on an elevator that had come down from the sixth floor, and Han Yujin was standing inside. Not yet dressed for work. As if she had already been in that elevator before me.

"Excuse me."

She spoke first. In my voice.

I stepped aside. Automatically. My body responded first. She passed. Watching her back over my shoulder, I finally began to tremble.

---

The first thing I did when I arrived at the office was check the system logs. Access records for my twin. 4:47 a.m., logged into the City Continuity Hub. Destination: Han Yujin's own residential complex.

I was asleep at 4:47 this morning. Or so I remembered.

My colleague Cha Minjun looked up from the desk beside mine.

"Did you work late yesterday? You look strange."

"There's an error in my twin log. My access record—"

"Isn't Han Yujin's twin been in active state since yesterday evening? Isn't that the continuity test?"

I couldn't say anything.

Cha Minjun turned his monitor to show me. There, clearly, was my name and my photo. And below it, in small letters: `Twin mode active — Original awaiting deactivation`.

Original.

I stared at that word for a long time.

---

Hanseong City Tech's continuity system was controversial even at the design stage in 2039. It wasn't about how precisely the twin could mimic the original. It was this: If the twin became a more complete being than the original, which does the city system recognize as the resident? That was the question.

I remember arguing in that debate this way: The original always takes precedence. Data cannot replace memory. I was one of the people who signed that opinion paper.

I don't know where that opinion paper is now. Not on my computer, not in my cloud account. If I asked Cha Minjun, he would look as though he'd never heard of it. Probably.

---

I stood in front of the bathroom mirror.

The reflected woman was looking at me. Cautiously. As if calculating what I would do. My hand rose. The reflected woman's hand rose too. Simultaneously, this time.

I touched the cold glass with my right hand. The me in the reflection touched it too. Our hands met across the glass.

Which one is the reflection?

The moment that question occurred to me, the bathroom light went out. It wasn't a power outage. Dormancy mode from the City Continuity System. The function that cuts lighting when no resident is detected.

In the darkness, I was alone.

---

In the afternoon, I did nothing. No reports, no meetings. I just looked out the window. People walking in the streets. Reflection panels. The faces within them.

Did those people know? Whether they were walking, or being walked?

At six in the evening, Cha Minjun left work first and said to me:

"You seemed to be struggling today. Leave early."

I said thank you. Because those were the right words to say.

On my way home, I passed a reflection panel in the apartment corridor. The me in the reflection, for a moment—brief but clear—smiled. Before I did.

I didn't stop.

I knew I shouldn't stop. I didn't know why, but I knew.
