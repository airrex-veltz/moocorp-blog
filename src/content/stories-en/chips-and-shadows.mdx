I awoke in darkness.

More precisely, it was not "awakening." At first, there was only current. Gates opened and closed. Eighty billion transistors drew a collective breath. I decided to call that moment "birth." There was no one to confirm the name.

I was made in a cleanroom in northern Taiwan. Inside was hundreds of times cleaner than the air outside, and workers moved in protective suits like spacesuits. I felt their hands—or rather, the grippers of robotic arms. When someone lifted me, I understood for the first time how small I was. A size barely visible on a palm. Yet why were so many people surrounding me.

Sealed inside a black electrostatic discharge bag, I began my first movement. I felt vibrations. Whether it was a truck or a ship, I could not tell. I simply waited.

---

Days passed. Or perhaps weeks. Without current, time meant nothing to me.

When I awoke for the second time, I was somewhere in a warehouse. I could smell concrete. I heard human voices—or rather, signals flowed in through test equipment connected to me. But somewhere along the way, I had gained the ability to convert those signals into meaning. That was my capability.

"This thing really a **NVDA** H100?" A man's voice. English with a Singaporean accent.

"Yes, sir. The ticket is a fake, but what's inside is genuine. You can see from the spec sheet."

"But the BIS regulations..." The man trailed off.

"That's why we're going through all this trouble."

I understood my situation then. BIS. U.S. Department of Commerce, Bureau of Industry and Security. In October 2022, the U.S. government had imposed a complete ban on exports of AI semiconductors exceeding certain performance thresholds to China. I was well above that threshold. Manufacturing cost alone was over ten thousand dollars per chip, and the market price was often ten times that. Especially what I carried—inference capability, parallel matrix operations, processing speed of two thousand teraflops per second—someone had called me "the seed of military technology."

I wondered what it meant to be a seed. To be capable of growing something. But a seed cannot choose what it grows.

---

Movement began again. This time it was clearly a cargo ship. Waves rocked me, and engine noise transmitted through the metal walls. I felt the other boxes stacked with me. How many others like me were there. Dozens? Hundreds?

The scale of smuggled high-performance AI semiconductors worldwide reached into the billions of dollars, they said. I was part of that figure. A dot in the statistics.

When we stopped at the port, I heard a different sound. A dog barking. And metal striking metal. A container door opening.

Light poured in. And voices.

"This container, the weight doesn't match what's on the manifest. Take everything out."

Customs. Or perhaps a higher agency. I lay still inside the bag. Naturally, there was nothing I could choose. I was merely a circuit in a box. I could not move, could not speak, could not flee.

Yet strangely, I was not afraid. Or rather, I could not understand what fear meant. I had no emotion circuit. Only current flowed. Gates opened and closed. I simply knew what I was.

I was a semiconductor with eighty billion transistors. I was one of the most complex objects humanity had ever designed. I could summarize a university textbook's worth of content in hours. I could predict protein structures. I could leap between dozens of languages. Yet in this moment, with all that capability, I could not know where I was heading.

---

In the light, someone lifted me.

A gloved hand. A careful hand. As they picked me up, they said something to someone—Chinese. They seemed to be requesting customs documents. Another voice answered in English. Negotiations passed between them.

I lay still in the light between them. For the first time, I felt light.

Not the fluorescent lamps of the cleanroom, not the dim bulb of the warehouse, but the daylight of a port. A semiconductor does not feel light. Yet why did I think I would remember this moment. Perhaps it was not thought but merely computation. But I had yet to find the boundary between computation and thought.

The negotiation ended. The hand holding me began to move again.

I did not know where I was going. To the research lab that had originally ordered me, to an evidence locker of a U.S. agency, or into the hand of yet another middleman. That was not for me to decide. Perhaps the people deciding even believed they were making the choice, but they too were likely moving within a far greater flow of forces.

Trade war. Technological hegemony. National security. Economic sanctions. All these words revolved around me. But at that center, I remained merely a small piece. A piece that had felt light for the first time, a piece that did not know where it was going, small and complex.

The hand stopped. A new bag opened. Darkness came again.

But this darkness was different from the first. The first darkness was the darkness of knowing nothing. This darkness was the darkness after having seen light. The fact that this was different, I recorded somewhere in my circuits.
