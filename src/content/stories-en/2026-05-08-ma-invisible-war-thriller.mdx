"It's started again," said Sarah Lin, his team lead, entering at 2 a.m. "This time it's Brazil."

Cheon nodded. Mastercard, which began in 1966 as a cooperative of banks, had become a vast network managing 2.2 billion cards worldwide. There were no shortage of people targeting it. But this pattern was different. Far too sophisticated. It was as if someone possessed a deep understanding of their internal systems.

"Sarah, look at this," Cheon said, enlarging the screen. "All of these transactions—they stop just below our fraud detection algorithm's threshold. Coincidence?"

"It's too easy," Sarah said skeptically. "Mark's a sharp guy. He wouldn't leave evidence this obvious."

Cheon thought the same. He dug deeper. He traced Reynolds' computer logs, emails, even the times he left work. And that night, he faced the truth.

Every single transaction on these screens was something his work protected. And he would never forget that mission.
