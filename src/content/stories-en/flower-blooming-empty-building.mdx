Dust sat on every window.

The moment architect Lee Seol stepped into the lobby of the eleven-story building, she knew it. The space soared through two floors, with a rusty lighting rail hanging overhead. Behind the counter, a faded rectangular outline marked where an old company logo once hung—the wall still bore a different shade where someone had removed the sign.

"It's been empty since 2020."

A voice came from behind. She turned to see a man with a stack of papers tucked under his arm. Mid-thirties, tie loosened, dark circles deep beneath his eyes.

"You must be Kim Joon-seo?"

"Yes. Asset Management at Saebbit Development." He handed her a business card. "To be honest, we're skeptical about this direction. Demolition and new construction would yield far higher returns than residential conversion."

Lee Seol took the card while glancing up at the ceiling.

"When was this building completed?"

"1991. Thirty-three years ago."

"So this building survived the Asian financial crisis and the global financial crisis."

Joon-seo said nothing. He opened his files to check something.

---

On the fourth day, Lee Seol was alone on the fourth floor.

She'd gone up without permission, but she could feel Joon-seo turning a blind eye from somewhere below—he made no sound of disapproval.

An old whiteboard remained on one wall of the fourth floor. Someone's handwriting read, `2019. 12. 31. Goodbye!` Below it, meeting notes no one had bothered to erase were crowded together. "Q2 targets." "Marketing budget." "New hiring plans." Three months after that date, the world stopped.

Lee Seol stood before it, doing nothing for a long time.

"There you are."

Joon-seo opened the stairwell door. Two cans of coffee in his hands.

"You said I shouldn't come up here."

"I'm staff, so I can." He stood beside her, looking at the whiteboard together. Silence stretched. "I saw this when I first took over the space. I wanted to erase it, but I couldn't. I don't know why."

Lee Seol glanced at him sideways. He held out one of the coffee cans.

"You said residential conversion has lower returns," Lee Seol said. "You're right in the short term. But office vacancies in this area have been going on for over four years since the pandemic. Demolition permits will take another two years anyway. Meanwhile, the building gets older, and demolition costs rise."

"I know."

"You do?"

Joon-seo opened his can while looking down.

"Knowing something and having the power to change what the company wants are different things. I'm just someone who was assigned a task."

Lee Seol thought about that. Someone assigned a task. That was her too. The project came in, and she came with it. If her client decided on demolition, there was nothing she could do. Yet here she was on the fourth day, wandering this building.

"The column spacing on the first floor is unusually wide," Lee Seol said. "For early nineties design, it fits contemporary trends perfectly. A cafe or gallery would be ideal. From the second to fifth floors, you could convert to duplex residences—the ceiling height would work. The rooftop faces south. You could even create a garden."

Joon-seo listened quietly.

"You've really scoped all this out."

"In three days, I did everything I could."

He looked out the window for a moment. Beyond the fourth-floor glass, the roofs of nearby alleyways were visible. Old buildings with tile roofs, someone hanging laundry between them.

"There are some long-time residents around this building," Joon-seo said. "I've seen neighbors from below putting their hands against the windows trying to see inside. At first I wondered why."

"Aren't you curious?"

"I don't know." He finished his coffee and stood there for a moment. "Have you ever restored a building like this?"

"Once. I renovated a warehouse in Incheon. It's an independent bookstore now."

"Did that take this long?"

"Much longer." Lee Seol said with a slight smile. "Three years."

Joon-seo looked at her. A real look, for the first time.

---

A week later, Lee Seol gave a presentation at Saebbit Development's office.

Fifteen slides. First-floor community hall, twelve residential units on floors two through five, shared office space on floors six through eight, rooftop garden. The payback period relative to initial investment: seven years. It was true that the profits from demolition and reconstruction would be higher, but the timeline was nearly double.

The executives said nothing. One adjusted his glasses and asked to see the slides again.

Joon-seo leaned against a wall of the conference room, pretending to take notes while watching her.

When they ran into each other in the hallway after the presentation, he said briefly,

"You did well."

"What's the decision?"

"I don't know yet."

He said that and walked ahead. Lee Seol watched his retreating figure for a moment. Loosely tied tie, documents tucked under one arm. He didn't look like someone merely assigned a task.

---

Ten days passed with no word.

Lee Seol sat with architectural drawings spread before her, then pulled photographs of the building from her desk drawer. The fourth-floor whiteboard. The rusted lighting rail in the lobby. The window view of tile-roofed buildings. She laid them out one by one on the table.

Her phone rang.

"Lee Seol, it's Joon-seo."

"Hi."

"The company made a decision."

She held her breath. Rain was beginning to fall outside her window.

"I still don't know everything. But could you develop the first-floor community hall design in more detail? One of the executives has taken an interest in that section."

Silence stretched out.

"Is that possible?"

"Yes," Lee Seol said. "It is."

After hanging up, she looked down at the photographs on the table again. The handwriting on the fourth-floor whiteboard. The person who wrote "goodbye" was already gone, but the words remained.

Rain tapped against the glass. The building was still empty.
