I am the 123,478th car to leave Seo Gwang Motors' assembly line this year.

On the day I rolled off, people applauded. Camera flashes burst in sequence. The CEO took the podium and announced, "We've hit our target of 200,000 vehicles this year," and the stock jumped 7 percent that afternoon. Hearing the cheers beyond the windshield, I felt oddly proud.

The problem started three days later.

My first owner was a business traveler heading somewhere in Chungcheong Province. Battery at 88 percent before departure. The navigation said the destination was 220 kilometers away. By the math, it was enough. Just one charge along the way should do it.

The charging station the navigation led us to was at a rest stop in South Chungcheong Province. There were two fast chargers there. One had a fault light blinking. In front of the other, three cars were already queued.

While I waited, I looked around. The other cars in line either had Bun-jjeok Chargers stickers like mine, or sat with a competitor's charging card gripped tight, windows cracked on the passenger side with sighs. One car complained about waiting over an hour. Another said it had changed its destination altogether.

In truth, this scene was something you could see anywhere in the country.

As of 2024, over 500,000 electric vehicles had been registered in the country. But the number of public fast chargers was only about 20,000. Simple math: that meant 25 electric vehicles per fast charger. Seo Gwang Motors alone announced a target of 200,000 vehicles this year, while Bun-jjeok Chargers, which operates the charging network, announced it would install 1,500 new chargers this year. 1,500 versus 200,000. That gap is what put me in that line.

An hour and a half later, finally, it was my turn.

After 30 minutes of fast charging, the battery rose to 78 percent. The owner let out a sigh of relief. Hearing that sigh, I felt a little sorry. The sound of applause on the day I rolled off the line, the barrage of flashes, the stock price jumping 7 percent—it all flashed through my mind.

The 200,000th car will emerge into the world soon enough. That car, too, will feel proud on its first day. And it will likely find itself, before long, standing in a long line at some rest stop charger, taking a queue number.

The line does not grow shorter today.
