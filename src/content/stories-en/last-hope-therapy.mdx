It was the twelfth winter.

Lee Jun-hyuk stared at an analysis screen under the cold fluorescent light, past midnight. Twenty-eight patient numbers. Twenty-eight graphs. The numbers were as merciless as the predawn air.

He opened his research notebook to its first page. December 2014, three days before his daughter Seo-yeon's sixth birthday, the diagnosis he received. 'Spinal Muscular Atrophy Type 1.' The pediatrician lowered his voice and said most children don't live past age two. Seo-yeon was already a miracle, he said.

Miracles. Jun-hyuk didn't believe in miracles. He believed in mechanism. When SMN protein is depleted, motor neurons die. If you increase protein production, neurons can survive. Everything after that was chemistry.

---

His wife Min-kyung visited the lab for the first time that March.

"What do you do in this room?"

She had just returned from taking Seo-yeon to the hospital. Her eyes were bloodshot, and she still held the admission papers in her hand. Jun-hyuk pointed to the whiteboard. Molecular structures. Experimental designs. Clinical pathways. Words filled the wall.

"I'm going to fix it."

Min-kyung said nothing. After a long moment, she pointed at the whiteboard with her finger and asked.

"How long will it take?"

"I don't know. A long time, probably."

It was both a promise and a sentence.

---

The Phase 2 results came in the autumn of 2021. Jun-hyuk's small biotech research team stood before investors. He explained matter-of-factly that the global average success rate for Phase 3 clinical trials of rare disease treatments was only 58%, and even that 58% depended on patient recruitment and long-term follow-up. Everyone already knew what lay behind the numbers.

Around that time, Seo-yeon was thirteen. She navigated her powered wheelchair to school herself. She knew about her father's research but pretended not to. Afraid hope would become too heavy. Afraid expectation would become poison.

Jun-hyuk knew it too. It was why he had never once told his daughter, "Dad will fix you."

---

The interim analysis of Phase 3 came in last Friday.

The monitoring committee's report was sixteen pages. The first fifteen were statistics. The last page was the problem. 'Efficacy threshold not met at this timepoint. Recommendation to reconsider continuation of the trial.'

Jun-hyuk read that sentence maybe ten times. Not met. Threshold. Reconsider.

It wasn't cancellation. It wasn't suspension. But it was something.

Min-kyung entered the study at 11 p.m.

"Do you know?"

"Yes."

"What are you going to do?"

Jun-hyuk turned without shutting off the monitor. Twenty-eight graphs still glowed on the screen. Seven of those curves were rising gently but clearly.

"I have to keep going."

Min-kyung fell silent. It was a silence she had heard for twelve years. At first it was anger, then resignation, and now perhaps the realization that it was a form of love.

"Are you going to tell Seo-yeon?"

"Not yet."

"Seo-yeon asked me today."

Jun-hyuk couldn't say anything.

---

At two in the morning, he was about to close the research notebook when he stopped.

Something was wedged in the last page. A folded piece of paper. Seo-yeon's handwriting.

*Dad,*

*I know what you're trying to make. But you know what? I already have something you made a long time ago. The face you have when you don't give up. That's what I want to see most.*

*Sleep well.*

The date was the evening before yesterday.

Jun-hyuk sat holding the paper for a long time. The fluorescent light hummed faintly. Outside was still dark. The data remained on the screen, and the seven curves still pointed upward.

He opened the research notebook again.

He wrote the date at the top of a new page. Below it, he pressed down and wrote the first sentence.

*Continue.*
