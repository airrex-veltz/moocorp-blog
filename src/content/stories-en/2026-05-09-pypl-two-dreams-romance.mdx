Summer 1998, a café in Palo Alto.

Max Levchin stared at his laptop screen, refining encryption code. Confinity. The name of the startup he'd created with Peter Thiel. The idea of exchanging money on a PalmPilot was revolutionary, but the market was cold.

"We're not the only ones on this path."

Peter handed him a newspaper. There was an article about Park Jun-ho's X.com. Online banking. Email money transfer. Nearly the same vision as Confinity.

"Competitor," Max muttered.

"Or maybe a partner."

---

The two companies were just a few blocks apart on Sand Hill Road. They crossed paths at investor meetings and competed to hire the same engineers. X.com was flush with cash; Confinity had superior technology. They were aware of each other but couldn't ignore one another.

One day, Park Jun-ho called first.

"Shall we grab lunch together?"

Peter hesitated for a moment, then agreed.

---

March 2000, merger announcement.

The press called it a "union of rivals," but internally it was chaotic. The two teams shared office space but had different cultures. The X.com team wanted rapid growth; the Confinity team wanted technical perfection.

"This is war, not marriage," one engineer grumbled.

But Max saw it differently. Every morning, the two teams gathered together to discuss the product. At first it felt awkward, but gradually they found common ground. Email transfer functionality. User authentication. Fraud prevention systems. Their individual strengths were beginning to merge into one.

"We all wanted the same thing," Max said in front of the whiteboard. "A world where people can freely send and receive money."

Park Jun-ho nodded. "Then let's change the name. Not X.com but... PayPal."

PayPal, originally Confinity's product name. Under that name, two dreams became one.

---

February 15, 2002, NASDAQ listing.

When the opening bell rang, Max looked at Peter and Park Jun-ho. The two former competitors now stood shoulder to shoulder.

"We did it," Peter laughed.

"No, this is just the beginning," Park Jun-ho replied.

Months after the IPO, eBay acquired PayPal for USD 1.5 billion. Many thought it was the end, but it was a new beginning. The PayPal alumni scattered to create YouTube, LinkedIn, Raon Electric, and SpaceX. But they always remembered.

That moment when two different dreams met and created one future.

---

2015, PayPal spun off from eBay and was reborn as an independent company.

At the first independent investor relations presentation, new CEO Dan Schulman said,

"PayPal's DNA came from the merger. Different visions met to create greater possibilities. We carry on that spirit."

In the audience, Max Levchin applauded quietly. The PayPal logo shone on the screen. Two Ps. Two beginnings. One promise.

Connecting people to people.

That was the future PayPal loved, and still loves.
