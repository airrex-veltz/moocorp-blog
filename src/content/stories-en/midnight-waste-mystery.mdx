Past two in the morning. The conveyor belt never stopped.

Park Tae-soo had held this post for ten years. Night manager at Cheonghwan Purification Facility No. 3. The title sounded impressive, but in reality, it meant watching three monitors alone, permitted to sleep as long as the line didn't stop. Preprocessing section, crushing section, sorting section. Green screens meant no problems.

At 2:17 AM, the sorting line's second camera turned yellow.

Foreign object detected. Automatic shutdown.

Park rose and headed downstairs to the site. There were two people in the night crew—one was inspecting the crushing section, the other was snoring on the office sofa. It was faster to handle this himself.

On the belt sat a single chunk of metal.

The size of a fist. Its surface was corroded but strangely smooth. The color was a bluish gray. Not the ordinary rust of common iron. Park pulled on cotton gloves and picked it up.

It was heavy. Double what he expected.

And it vibrated in his palm.

Subtly, but distinctly. Park worked at a facility that processed thousands of tons of waste annually. He'd handled refrigerators, battery packs, and once an old medical device had arrived on the belt. Nothing had ever felt like this.

He carried it upstairs and set it on the desk. The vibration didn't stop.

He pulled out his phone and opened an EMF meter app. The screen spiked immediately. Well over 150 microtesla. Hundreds of times the average reading from household electronics. It easily exceeded the caution threshold listed in the manual.

Park placed the chunk in a plastic bag. He put the bag in a metal storage container. He locked the door. But his unease lingered.

That year, global electronic waste generation exceeded 62 million tons, according to reports. Only 22 percent of it was officially processed, he'd read. Where was the rest? Some of it, unnamed and unclassified, rode in someone's collection truck before it landed on this belt.

Park opened the collection route logs. The district was an industrial area in Incheon's western ward. A region dense with electronics manufacturers. It was difficult to narrow down which building it came from, but the truck number was recorded.

Three in the morning. He called the Ministry of Environment's night hotline.

"We've found a metal object with a strong electromagnetic response in our sorting line."

The officer's voice showed no surprise.

"Can you trace the collection route?"

Park hesitated for a moment. That was the question used when identifying the source. The civil servant had asked first.

"We have records."

"I'll contact you after confirming."

The line went dead.

Outside the window, two headlights turned into the parking lot. 3:20 AM. External vehicles arrived on days like this maybe once a month.

Park turned his head toward the storage container. He couldn't tell if it was still vibrating. He heard car doors closing.

He placed his phone on the table. And for the first time, he wrote down the truck number on paper.
