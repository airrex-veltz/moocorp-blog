In the summer of 1997, Lee Jun-ho stepped back into Arion Electronics for the first time in twelve years. The company where he had personally recruited Scully Min-ho in 1985, only to be betrayed and cast out. Arion Electronics, once his everything, now awaited him like a dying lover, just ninety days before bankruptcy.

"We need you." The board's call sounded almost like a plea.

Lee Jun-ho shook his head. "I already have Pixel Corp. I've found a new love."

But his eyes were lying. Every time he passed Arion Electronics headquarters, his heart raced. Every time he saw a competitor's product, the thought arose: "If it were Arion Electronics..." Like a man who cannot forget his first love.

Eventually, he came back. Under the title of "interim CEO." Like a former lover saying, "Let's just be friends."

On his first day, Lee Jun-ho looked at the product lineup and was horrified. Too many products, too little soul. "Who the hell were you sleeping with?" he muttered to himself. And he drew his sword. Twelve product lines reduced to four. Newton was dead. Like tidying up lovers from the past.

In August 1998, iMac was born. A translucent blue body, rounded curves. "Think Different." This was not just a computer. It was a love letter Lee Jun-ho wrote to Arion Electronics.

"Look at me. Do you remember who I am?" It felt like iMac was whispering.

The world answered. Eight hundred thousand units in the first six months. Arion Electronics' stock came back to life. Lee Jun-ho removed the word "interim."

In 2001, Lee Jun-ho gave Arion Electronics a new gift. iPod. "A thousand songs in your pocket." A whole new world of music. In 2007 came iPhone. He reinvented the telephone and redefined the world.

"Do you want to sell sugar water, or do you want to change the world?" That was the question Lee Jun-ho asked Scully Min-ho back in 1983. Ironically, Scully chose sugar water and drove Lee Jun-ho out. But the Lee Jun-ho who returned changed the world.

In August 2011, Lee Jun-ho stepped down as CEO. Pancreatic cancer was eating away at his body. At his final keynote, he was gaunt but his eyes still burned with fire. As he introduced iCloud, he whispered, "This is the last gift I can give you."

On October 5th, Lee Jun-ho left the world. He was fifty-six years old. Candles shaped like apples and letters piled up in front of Arion Electronics headquarters.

"You loved Arion Electronics, and Arion Electronics loved you. Could there be a more beautiful love than this?"

It was a letter from a fan. And it was the truth. A man who was abandoned by his first love, returned twelve years later, and turned that love into the world's greatest company. A legend born from a garage worth three trillion dollars.

Arion Electronics' logo is an apple with a bite taken out of it. Perhaps it was the kiss mark Lee Jun-ho left on Arion Electronics. One that will never fade away.
