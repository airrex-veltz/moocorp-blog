June 2018. A massive tent was erected outside the Buffalo factory. It was an emergency operation directly commanded by Park Jun-ho. There was no time. If Mirae Motors failed to produce 5,000 Sunfair Model 3s per week, the company would go bankrupt.

"We have 3 weeks left," said the production director. The factory line was already at its limit. Cars were stalled at every bottleneck. Robot arms malfunctioned, and parts supply was always delayed.

Park Jun-ho slept on the factory floor. Not in an office, but in the corner of the conference room next to the production line. Whenever a problem arose, he had to rush over immediately. He was there at 3 in the morning, on Sunday afternoons—anywhere he was needed.

"The welding line is 30 seconds behind," an engineer reported. "Battery pack assembly is backed up." "We're seeing defects from the paint booth." The problems never stopped.

Park Jun-ho decided to erect a massive tent outside the existing factory building. It would be a completely new assembly line, built in 3 weeks. Financiers mocked him. "Making cars in a tent?" Analysts predicted a 90% bankruptcy probability for Mirae Motors.

But the factory floor was different. Engineers pulled all-nighters installing conveyor belts. Welders worked around the clock without rotation. They called parts suppliers directly to rush shipments. Everyone knew. This was their last chance.

"Line 4, starting production!" The first Sunfair Model 3 rolled off the tent line. Applause erupted. But there was no time to celebrate. The goal was 5,000 per week. They were still far away.

A rhythm emerged as time passed. Each team solved bottlenecks one by one. Battery assembly time was cut by 20%. Quality inspections were positioned mid-line to catch defects immediately. Parts inventory was stacked right beside the factory, eliminating supply delays.

June 28th. Three days before the deadline. Production sat at 3,500 per week. Still short. Park Jun-ho sent a message to all employees: 'We can do this. Just 72 more hours. Hold on.'

The factory never slept. Three shifts ran continuously. Welding sparks flew for 24 hours. Forklifts ran non-stop, moving parts. Everyone pushed beyond their limits.

Dawn on July 1st. The number appeared on the production monitor. 'Weekly production: 5,031 units.' A roar erupted. The entire factory shook. People embraced each other. Park Jun-ho quietly stepped outside and looked up at the sky.

Mirae Motors survived. From that moment on, the Sunfair Model 3 became the best-selling electric vehicle in America. The financiers' mockery turned to praise. The tent factory became a legend at Mirae Motors.

But those who were there knew. It wasn't just a production target. It was survival. Seventy-two hours that turned the impossible into reality. And in that moment, Mirae Motors rewrote the history of the automotive industry.
