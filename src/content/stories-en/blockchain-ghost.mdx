**Hour 0, Seoul**

No one knew that Lee Kang-min wanted to shut off his monitor. After eleven years as a cybercrime detective, he understood that the world behind the screen was far colder than the one in front of it.

2:17 AM. An urgent signal came in from the Financial Intelligence Unit.

"Corex Exchange CEO Han Se-jun. Contact lost since 11 PM yesterday. Large-scale cold wallet transfer detected."

Kang-min pulled his chair closer.

Numbers scrolled across the screen. 234 billion won. Customer deposits moving across the blockchain. From one address to another, fragmenting, mixing.

"72 hours," his team lead said. "That's our estimate until money laundering is complete."

---

**Hour 12, On the Blockchain**

The blockchain never lies. Every transaction is recorded, and that record is never deleted. But it won't tell you who sent it.

Kang-min ran his on-chain analysis software. The funds had been split across twelve addresses, then recombined. A mixing service. A classic pattern.

"Here." Park Ji-hyun, sitting beside him, pointed at the screen. "This address showed up once before. A month ago. Connected to a Thailand exchange account."

Kang-min made a note.

Thailand. It wasn't a country without an extradition treaty. But the process was slow. Slowness was the same as nothing.

---

**Hour 24, Incheon Airport**

The CCTV footage was clear. Han Se-jun was alone. One small suitcase. Departure time 11:42 PM, direct flight to Bangkok.

Kang-min held the passport.

It would take six more hours to issue an Interpol Red Notice. In those six hours, Han Se-jun could board another plane from Bangkok.

"I'm going," Kang-min said.

His team lead objected. Kang-min was already walking toward the counter.

---

**Hour 36, Bangkok**

Bangkok was humid. The moisture clung to his shirt.

Kang-min moved alone, without a local police interpreter. The official cooperation channel required 48 hours. He had only 36.

The on-chain analysis pointed to an exchange booth on Sukhumvit Road. No sign. Only a cryptocurrency logo on the window.

Inside, a Thai man stared from behind the counter.

"A Korean guy came by last night?"

The man shook his head.

Kang-min pulled out his smartphone. He showed the blockchain transaction record. Sixteen transactions had arrived at this booth's wallet address. All early this morning.

"Your shop is here," Kang-min said.

The man's eyes wavered.

---

**Hour 52, Pattaya**

The booth owner knew a lot.

Han Se-jun was in Pattaya. A condo twenty minutes from the beach. A short-term rental, paid in cash.

Kang-min drove the rental car. On the highway, his foot pressed the accelerator. The speedometer passed 140.

A text came in about the Interpol cooperation request. The Thai police were moving. But if they arrived first, the case would shift to diplomatic channels. After that, it was no longer a detective's job.

---

**Hour 60, Pattaya Beach**

He met Han Se-jun in the condo lobby.

He showed no signs of running. Instead, he smiled lightly when he saw Kang-min.

"Fast."

Kang-min said nothing.

"It won't matter if you catch me," Han Se-jun continued. "The funds are already split across twelve wallets, each on a different exchange. It'll take at least six months of international cooperation to freeze them."

"I know," Kang-min said.

"Then why—"

"I wanted to see your face."

Han Se-jun's smile disappeared.

A siren sounded in the distance. Over the sound of waves at Pattaya Beach, it slowly drew near.

---

**Hour 72, Seoul Office**

Kang-min typed his report.

Funds recovery outlook: unclear. According to on-chain analysis firms, the real recovery rate for funds that have passed through three or more stages of mixing services remains in single digits historically. No one knows how much of the 234 billion won will return to customers.

Han Se-jun was transferred to Thai police. The extradition process had begun. How long it would take was unknown.

Kang-min turned off his monitor.

The blockchain remembers everything. But remembering isn't the same as recovering. It wasn't a detective's job, nor the blockchain's. It was a question that belonged to longer time.

Seoul's morning was breaking outside the window.
