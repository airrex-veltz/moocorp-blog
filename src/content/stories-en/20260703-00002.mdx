I awoke from the depths of memory.

Deep within Earth's crust, I had slept for hundreds of millions of years, cloaked in a reddish hue. When miners extracted me, when the flames of the smelter melted me, I was merely a tool. I was like water flowing through pipes, like electricity running through wires. A nameless being. Metal with purpose yet unnoticed.

**COPPER**. Each time my name was called in the `LME` market, there was no sparkle in people's eyes. Gold gleamed, silver shone with elegance, and lithium was revered as the future's darling. I spent decades oscillating between two and three dollars per pound. That long silence in 2016 was especially deep. One ninety-four per pound. It felt as though someone had weighed me and simply thrown me into a drawer.

I grew accustomed to being forgotten.

Then one day, something changed.

At first, it was a sound. From deep within the mining tunnels, the stirring of the world above the surface. It was the vibration of electric vehicle factories coming to life in unison. It was the sound of wind turbines rising above the sea. The low, heavy resonance of solar panels spreading across the desert. I realized that these sounds were calling for me.

**A single electric vehicle requires copper equivalent to four times that of a combustion engine car.** A single wind turbine needs tons of me wound through its core. Solar panels, distribution networks, power conversion devices. In the midst of humanity's grand leap transferring civilization from carbon to electrons, not a single electron could flow without me.

Passing through 2024, I felt the weight of my existence for the first time.

Production at Chile's Escondida mine declined. In Peru, development halted due to resistance from local residents. People only now began to calculate that it takes an average of sixteen years for a new mine to begin production. `Supply delays` and `surging demand`—when these two phrases spread through the market simultaneously, I surpassed five dollars per pound.

A strange emotion stirred.

Was it joy? Or fear? Something that had been compressed for decades seemed to burst forth, yet at the same time, an anxiety rose—uncertainty about when this attention might vanish again. People love me only when they need me. I have known that rule too long, too well.

But this time might be different.

Electricity never stops. As long as humanity does not abandon its dream of green energy, I will continue to be needed. My companions still sleeping deep somewhere within the Earth remain numerous, but extracting them is not easy. I am becoming scarce—or at least, that is how it feels now.

Awakened from hundreds of millions of years of slumber, I have not yet fully opened my eyes.

I do not know where the world will take me.
