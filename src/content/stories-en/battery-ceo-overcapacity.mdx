At the moment the opening ceremony ribbon was cut, Kang Min-jun's left pocket began to vibrate.

He didn't lower his hand. Camera flashes poured down, and the executives standing on either side of him applauded. The factory standing in the middle of the Northern Industrial Complex—officially named Cell-2 Line—was the biggest gamble of his three-year tenure. Total floor area: 180,000 square meters. Total investment from groundbreaking to today: KRW 2.9 trillion. Annual production target: 35 gigawatt-hours.

The vibration didn't stop.

"Thank you for your hard work, CEO."

The head of public relations whispered the next schedule in his ear: VIP luncheon, factory tour, evening roundtable discussion. Kang Min-jun nodded and stepped down from the platform. While shaking hands with the crowd, he took his phone out of his pocket.

The sender of the message was Oh Hyung-jun, head of strategy and planning.

> "CEO, you need to check this immediately. GreenPower made an announcement."

Kang Min-jun stopped. The aide following him bumped into his back. Without even a gesture of apology, he slipped around the corner into the hallway. There was a link attached. It was breaking news from an economics newspaper on the Terra Continent.

**GreenPower Announces Achieving $52 Per Kilowatt-Hour for Next-Generation Battery Cells. Industry Estimated Cost Level: 61%.**

He reread the numbers.

$52 per kilowatt-hour.

His company's current cost was $85. This year's target was $78. The 2027 target was $68. GreenPower—the largest battery company on the Nova Continent, publicly traded under ticker **GPA**—was already past the point he was trying to reach. Three years ahead of schedule.

Executives were waiting for him at the entrance to the luncheon hall. Local provincial council members, an industrial ministry bureau director, and a bank branch manager already had their seats at the table. These seats had been reserved two months ahead for today's opening ceremony.

Kang Min-jun put his phone in his pocket. And summoned a smile.

"Sorry to keep you waiting."

---

The luncheon lasted an hour and a half. In that time, three more messages came from Oh Hyung-jun. He didn't read any of them. He placed his phone face-down under the table.

The bureau director spoke. "A factory like this hasn't come to this region in twenty years. Just the employment effect alone—"

"2,400 people. On a direct employment basis."

Kang Min-jun answered automatically. It was a figure in the press kit. The director looked satisfied.

His mind was doing different calculations.

Cost difference: $33 per kilowatt-hour. Annual production: 35 gigawatt-hours. One gigawatt-hour equals one million kilowatt-hours. So 35 gigawatt-hours equals 3.5 billion kilowatt-hours. Multiply $33 by 3.5 billion. To compete with GreenPower at the same cost, his company would need to either absorb approximately 115.5 billion dollars in annual cost difference or achieve that much in cost reduction.

Converting to won.

He stopped the calculation.

Converting to won.

He stopped the calculation.

---

As the factory tour was ending, Oh Hyung-jun appeared in person. He was waiting for Kang Min-jun at the end of the line, still wearing a visitor's hard hat.

"Could we step away for a moment?"

The two of them went out through an emergency stairwell on the outer wall. The wind blew. In the distance, three smokestacks pierced the sky.

"The GreenPower announcement—our analysis team has been working on it all morning." Oh Hyung-jun's voice was low. "The numbers check out. Some of their raw material procurement contracts from last quarter leaked out as public regulatory filings on the Nova Continent. Working backward, $52 per kilowatt-hour is definitely achievable."

"Cathode materials?"

"They raised the lithium iron phosphate conversion ratio to over 80 percent. The energy density is lower than ours, but they're competing on price. We've already heard that Alpha Continent EV makers are inquiring."

Kang Min-jun rested his hand on the railing. In the parking lot below, buses that had been brought in for today's event were lined up.

Alpha Continent makers. That was the problem. Three of the companies listed in Cell-2 Line's business plan to consume volume by 2028 were from that continent. It wasn't a long-term contract. A memorandum of understanding. Letters of intent. Papers with no legal binding force.

"What's the possibility of contract conversion?"

"Legal's opinion is 30 percent. Sales' opinion is 50 percent."

"Everyone seems to be telling me what I want to hear."

Oh Hyung-jun didn't answer.

Kang Min-jun looked at the sky. Clouds hung low. When he first conceived this factory, he walked into the board meeting with a consulting report predicting that the global battery market would grow at over 20 percent annually through 2030. The board members asked twice and approved. Back then, GPA's stock price was 40 percent lower than now, and no one had predicted their cost competitiveness would be this strong.

"What about media response?"

"Today only the opening ceremony will be reported. The GreenPower story will be the market's first reaction tomorrow."

Kang Min-jun nodded. When the market opened tomorrow, his company's stock price would probably face adjustment. How much? That was impossible to know. All he knew for certain was that the ribbon cut and photos taken at this place today would run side by side in tomorrow's news. The opening of a KRW 2.9 trillion factory. And a competitor's cost innovation announcement.

"Do you think they noticed I slipped out from the luncheon?"

"The director didn't seem to notice."

"That's fortunate."

He summoned a smile again. The evening roundtable discussion still lay ahead.

---

After midnight, Kang Min-jun sat alone in his office. Documents accumulated throughout the day lay on his desk. He didn't touch them. Through the window, the outline of Cell-2 Line lay submerged in darkness. Exterior wall lighting was on, so the shape was visible. It was massive and new.

He picked up a single sheet of paper resting on the approval stamp. It was today's event pamphlet. A bird's-eye view of the factory was printed on the cover, and a slogan was written at the bottom.

*Whoever builds the future first has the future.*

He set the pamphlet down again.

It wasn't wrong. The problem was how much earlier one could build it. And someone had already built it even earlier.

The factory outside the window was quiet. Operations would begin on the first of next month.
