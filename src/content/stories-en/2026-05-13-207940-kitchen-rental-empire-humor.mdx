When Taeyang Electronics' biotech division briefing began, Vice-Chairman Lee Jae-young crossed his legs and settled in. Beside him sat Director Kim, a seasoned pharmaceutical industry expert.

"So what exactly is this biotech business?"

Director Kim unfolded his prepared materials. "Simply put, other pharmaceutical companies develop drugs, and we manufacture them instead."

"So..." VP Lee paused to think. "We don't make our own drugs?"

"We do make drugs, just not our own."

"So you manufacture someone else's drugs?"

"That's correct."

VP Lee shook his head. "Is that really a business? Our semiconductors are our products, our smartphones are our products."

Director Kim had anticipated this. "VP, these days it costs over 1 trillion won just to develop a new drug. Then another 500 billion just to build a factory. Pharmaceutical companies want to focus only on research. They want to leave the manufacturing to us."

"Ah, so we're renting out our kitchen?"

"Exactly!" Director Kim clapped his hands. "We're renting out an enormous kitchen. In Incheon."

In 2011, Sejin Biologics officially launched. They secured land in Songdo, Incheon. The problem was the scale.

"Why does a bioreactor have to be so large?" asked the construction team lead, looking at the blueprints.

"This one holds 15,000 liters," explained the engineer.

"How big is that?"

"About the size of one or two swimming pools."

The team lead dropped his pen. "You manufacture drugs in a swimming pool?"

"More precisely, we culture cells in stainless steel tanks the size of swimming pools to produce antibodies."

"Why do you need to make so much at once?"

"To drive down unit costs through mass production. Pharmaceutical companies around the world are lining up."

As soon as the first factory was completed, Sejin immediately broke ground on Factory 2. And before Factory 2 was even finished, they started Factory 3.

In 2016, the company was preparing for its IPO. The marketing team was struggling.

"How do we explain this to the general public?" asked the junior associate.

"We call it a CMO."

"What's that?"

"Contract Manufacturing Organization."

"Isn't that just a drug factory?"

The team lead slammed his desk. "Never say it that way! We are an advanced biopharmaceutical production facility. This isn't simple manufacturing—it's precision cell culture technology."

"But in the end, we are making drugs for others, right?"

"...True, but if you say it like that, the stock price won't go up."

On IPO day, the offering price was 95,000 won. Stock prices soared the moment trading opened. Investors were simple: if the company had 'Taeyang Electronics' attached to its name, it was automatically good.

In the trading room, Director Kim stared at the screen. "Do you think those people have any idea what we actually do?"

The employee next to him replied, "Probably not. They just think 'biotech' means the future."

"But we just make other people's drugs."

"And we make them very well!"

In 2018, Factory 3 was completed. Production capacity reached 360,000 liters. Now they could handle volumes from any pharmaceutical company in the world.

One day, an American pharmaceutical executive visited the Songdo plant. As he toured the facility with his guide, he was left speechless.

"Is all of this... for us?"

"No, we have multiple clients."

"Then whose tank is that next to ours?"

The guide smiled. "That's a secret. I can't tell you who owns that tank either."

"Do you keep security that tight?"

"Of course. This is where each pharmaceutical company manufactures its core medicines. The recipes are top secret."

The American executive nodded. "So in the end, Sejin can't cook, but you're the world's best kitchen staff."

"That's an apt analogy."

That evening, there was a team dinner at the Seoul headquarters. The junior associate asked, "Team lead, how do you answer when someone asks what our company does?"

The team lead emptied his soju glass and answered, "Just say you work at Taeyang Electronics. If you start explaining biotech, your drink will get cold."

"But shouldn't we explain it accurately?"

"Okay." The team lead refilled his glass. "We're the world's largest contract drug manufacturing company. If there were an antibody cultivation Olympics, we'd have the gold medal. We're a major corporation with over 1 trillion won in revenue. Got it?"

"So a large corporation's part-time job?"

The team lead set down the soju bottle. "...You're going to the Incheon factory tomorrow for a tour."

In fact, as of 2025, Sejin Biologics surpassed 3 trillion won in revenue and rose to first place in global CMO market share. It had become the world's leader in the business of manufacturing drugs for others.

At one conference, an analyst asked, "How do you view the future of the biotech CMO business?"

The presenter answered confidently. "Pharmaceutical companies will keep developing new drugs. But they won't want to build factories. We fill that gap. While others cook, we rent them our kitchen. And we do it at world-class standards."

As he stepped down from the stage, a junior colleague approached. "That was an excellent explanation."

"Thanks. It took ten years to refine that pitch."

"But what do you tell your family?"

The presenter smiled wryly. "Just that I work at Taeyang Electronics. That's the fastest way."
