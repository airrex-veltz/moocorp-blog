## Hangar, Midnight

In Hangar 3, where fluorescent lights flickered, Han Do-young pushed his flashlight under the left wing. For Skyline Airlines Flight 07—the late-night inbound from Incheon to Bangkok—47 minutes remained until the maintenance deadline.

The crack looked like a shadow at first. At the junction where the wing spar met the fuselage, a line barely thicker than a hair ran across a row of rivets beneath the paint. Do-young ran his fingertip across the surface. It was smooth. A flaw visible only to the eye.

He pulled out his crack gauge and positioned it. 0.4 millimeters. The manual limit was 0.3 millimeters.

A difference of one digit. 0.1 millimeters. About half the thickness of a sheet of paper.

Do-young remained on his knees for a long time. Across the hangar, two night-shift workers were replacing tires on another aircraft, talking in low voices. Radio chatter from the late-night news drifted over—a report about an accident from across the Pacific last month, still circulating as news clips, showing passengers screaming in oxygen masks after part of the fuselage had separated shortly after takeoff. On the bulletin board of the hangar, a printed summary of the accident investigation was posted. Someone's red pen marks reading "It could happen to us too" were visible beneath where they had been erased.

Do-young picked up his radio, then put it back down.

## The Call

"What is it." Maintenance Foreman O Hyung-seok's voice was slurred with sleep. It was past midnight when the call came through.

"There's a crack in the left wing spar connection. The gauge reads 0.4 millimeters."

Silence flowed through the phone. Three seconds. Four.

"You sure? You didn't measure it wrong?"

"I measured it three times."

"...Damn." A low curse escaped. "Flight 07, 214 passengers on board. You know if we cancel now, it takes a minimum of six hours to assign a replacement aircraft. All the connecting passengers in Bangkok miss their flights."

"I understand."

"The manual limit is 0.3, so 0.4 is out of limits, right? But do you know how much safety margin is built into the actual structural strength tests? That number itself is set conservatively. A 0.1 overage doesn't mean the wing breaks."

Do-young said nothing. O Hyung-seok wasn't wrong. Maintenance manual limits are typically set far below actual failure strength. That's how safety margins work. But how much margin there actually was, how far material fatigue had progressed in this specific joint—Do-young had no way of knowing. Nobody did for certain. Margin was probability, not guarantee.

"Just take a photo and send it to me. I'll report it directly to the maintenance director tomorrow morning. For tonight, we'll clear it on the books."

"Foreman, that violates regulations. Any items exceeding the limit must be tagged as unserviceable for maintenance immediately and reported to the aviation safety authority."

"Regulations, regulations, regulations. Listen, Do-young." O Hyung-seok's voice lowered. "You've been a mechanic for three years. I've been doing this for eighteen. I've seen hundreds of borderline cases like this. They all went like this. Nothing ever happened."

"There's no guarantee nothing will happen this time."

"You're asking where the guarantee is, while suggesting we delay a plane with 214 people on it six hours based on some conviction with no guarantee?"

The call ended.

## Behind the Maintenance Shed

Do-young didn't smoke, but in that moment he wanted to burn something. He sat on the emergency stairs behind the maintenance shed, staring at the photo of the gauge again.

Three years ago, when he first joined the company, the training officer's words came back to him. "A mechanic's signature is the heaviest document in this company. The moment your three-character name goes under that signature, the lives of everyone on that plane hang on it." Back then, those words had seemed theatrical somehow. Now, the weight of them felt like it was actually pressing down on his palm.

At the same time, another memory surfaced. His first year on the job, when he'd discovered a hairline leak in the landing gear hydraulic line and reported it, only to be labeled as having an "overreaction." The item was ultimately deferred to the next maintenance cycle, and nothing happened. After that, his performance review bore the notation "excessive caution, needs to develop better field judgment."

Where was the line between an oversensitive mechanic and a careless one? Do-young was most frightened by the fact that this boundary only becomes clear after an accident.

He pulled out his phone and searched for the aviation safety authority's anonymous reporting channel. The access page appeared on the screen. His finger hovered over the input field.

He knew what would happen if he reported. Flight 07 would be immediately cancelled, the aircraft would undergo detailed inspection, and the company would suffer a loss of tens of millions of won. And this incident would remain in internal records, and O Hyung-seok's conversation with him might be revealed during the investigation. O Hyung-seok could end an 18-year career this way. Do-young knew he had two daughters in college.

On the other hand, if he didn't report. The crack remains. 0.4 millimeters. It might not progress until the next maintenance cycle, or it might progress during this very flight. Nobody knew. That was the problem. Nobody knows.

## The Bulletin Board

Do-young walked back into the hangar. Passing in front of the bulletin board, he read the accident investigation summary again. According to the findings, signs of trouble with the part in question had been reported several weeks before the accident, but detailed inspection had been delayed due to "operational schedule pressure." The responsible mechanic had communicated his concerns to his supervisor but received the response that it was "within safety margins" and moved on. The mechanic's name appeared nowhere in the report. What happened to him afterward wasn't mentioned either. Only that the airline's entire fleet underwent a month of special inspection following the incident.

Do-young gripped the gauge again. And stood in front of Flight 07.

The aircraft was quiet. Under the maintenance light, the aluminum fuselage gleamed palely. He placed his hand on the connection where the crack was. It was cold.

In the distance, runway lights flickered. At this moment, the ground control tower was preparing the gate assignment for Flight 07. In 90 minutes, 214 passengers would begin boarding. Among them would be a couple on their honeymoon, an elderly person visiting grandchildren for the first time in ages, a businessman who would fall asleep with a report open on his laptop. None of them would think about the 0.1-millimeter difference in the spar connection. There was no reason they should. They believed it was being taken care of. And it was the job of people like Do-young to protect that belief.

The radio crackled again. It was O Hyung-seok.

"Have you decided."

Do-young didn't answer and stared at the radio. The call timer ticked on the screen. One second. Two. Three.

He put the gauge in his pocket. Then he pulled out the maintenance tag paper. The hand holding the pen paused on the paper for a moment.

The radio was moving on from the accident news to other reports. The anchor's voice read the next headline with practiced indifference.

The hangar clock showed 1:13 a.m. Two hours 47 minutes remained until Flight 07's departure.
