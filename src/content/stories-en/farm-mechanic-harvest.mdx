Kim Giseul sighed as he opened the repair shop door early that spring morning. The parking lot was bustling like an emergency room.

The combine hobbled at the front door on a wobbly front wheel, two tractors lay side by side, and the cultivator trembled in the corner. It was like a hospital waiting room.

"Brother, look at me. I've never been this busy except during harvest," the combine said in a squeaky voice. "A rock got into the field last week."

"My engine won't even start," the red tractor sighed with a sad expression.

"Teacher, please look at me too," the yellow cultivator clanged. "I need to plow the field this weekend..."

Kim Giseul sat in a shop chair and held his head. In fifteen years of work, spring was always like this. When machines woke up from their winter rest, they all started hurting at once. And since farmers' schedules couldn't change, everything was urgent and everything was priority number one.

"All right, let's calm down," Kim Giseul said. "Who couldn't move first?"

"Me! Me!" the blue tractor shouted loudly. "I've been completely stuck since yesterday morning."

"No way, I've been like this for four days," the combine protested.

"But I..." the cultivator said quietly. "I'm really needed this afternoon. Grandfather's leg hurts and he can't plow the field."

Kim Giseul looked up. The cultivator's owner, Grandfather Park Ki-young. He was the one who had become the main character last year.

His decision was made.

"Let's fix the cultivator first."

"What? You're not going to look at me?" the blue tractor said sadly.

"You can wait a few more days. Your father only has one job. But grandfather..." Kim Giseul said as he stood up. "If grandfather's leg doesn't get better, he can't work. That's something we need to help with."

He opened the cultivator's engine cover. The spark plug was charred black. Thirty minutes would be enough.

"You watch the combine's belt, tractors," Kim Giseul said to the other machines. "Don't sit alone and shake the dust off each other. Until I come back."

He replaced the spark plug with a new one, and the cultivator started up on the first try. A small engine sound echoed through the repair shop.

"Oh, oh! This feels great!" the cultivator cried happily.

Kim Giseul laughed. That sound was like listening to grandfather laugh.

In the afternoon, Grandfather Park Ki-young drove the cultivator away, and when he came back in the evening, he was covered in dirt but his face was bright. "Thank you, Kim Giseul."

As soon as those words fell, the blue tractor cried from outside the window. "Uh... please fix me quickly too..."

"All right, all right," Kim Giseul said, raising his hand with a laugh.

As night fell, the repair shop got busy again. He checked the blue tractor's battery, cleaned the red tractor's fuel mixture, and finally replaced the combine's belt. Midnight passed and it became dawn.

Kim Giseul sipped his coffee and looked around the repair shop. Now all the machines were resting quietly. Tomorrow they would all go out to the fields.

"Thank you, all of you," he murmured. "Spring doesn't truly begin in our village until you all get to work."

In the early morning air, the repair shop was quiet, and the machines waited for tomorrow. Kim Giseul gathered a blanket and lay in the chair. Three more days remained, and the farmers would bring another machine. But that was okay too.

That was spring, and that was his job.
