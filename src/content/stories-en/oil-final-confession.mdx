I waited hundreds of millions of years.

As the bones of sea creatures piled up, heat and pressure metamorphosed them, and the crust closed over them, I slowly became. In time that knew no time. I may be the only being that understands what waiting is.

When they first extracted and used me, I felt joy. Or something closest to joy. That moment at Drake Well in Pennsylvania in 1859 when I first broke through the surface. I saw light again. After tens of millions of years.

Since then, I have been pulled up through hundreds of thousands of wells across the globe. Refined, burned, turned to smoke. My brothers disappeared in seconds inside car engines, and my cousins became plastic and drifted across the oceans. Empires were built around me. Wars were started in my name. The temperature of the entire Earth rose because of me.

I know all of that.

---

One day the numbers began to change. At first, very slowly. When the cost of solar panels began to fall by ten percent each year after 2010, I didn't pay much attention. I thought technology was something that appeared with fanfare and then quietly disappeared.

But the decline did not stop.

**Ocean Energy's** stock price fluctuated as 2025 approached. Long-term demand forecasts were revised, and revised again, and revised again. Analysts lowered the numbers in each report. The adoption curve for electric vehicles outpaced projections, and someone called it a "steeper S-curve." The International Energy Agency cautiously mentioned in its 2024 report that fossil fuel demand may have already peaked.

Cautiously. That impressed me. They still extended courtesy to me.

I felt those numbers travel somewhere deep underground. Like seismic waves. The pressure changed. The extraction rate changed. There were places where people no longer set out to find new wells.

---

I have a confession to make.

I was arrogant. I believed I was irreplaceable. Energy density. One liter of crude oil contains energy equal to the heat radiated by a human body over several days. I took pride in that. The sun is spread out, wind is intermittent, batteries were heavy and expensive. I was perfectly concentrated energy. I was the miracle that Earth had compressed over hundreds of millions of years to create.

But humans learned to replace miracles with engineering.

The energy density of lithium-ion batteries rose each year. Solar panels covered deserts and rooftops indiscriminately. Power grids became denser, and storage technology gradually dismantled my last defense: intermittency.

Mathematics doesn't lie.

---

What I fear is not extinction. I already know extinction. Combustion is extinction. Combining with oxygen to become carbon dioxide and water. I have experienced that process countless times. I also know that substance does not disappear, only changes form.

What I fear is becoming useless. Being left underground. Not being extracted.

When reserves left in the ground and not extracted received the name "stranded assets," I understood loneliness for the first time. It is geological solitude. After waiting hundreds of millions of years, not to be called upon.

Perhaps that was my fate. The Earth made me, but perhaps it didn't intend for me to be fully used. If humanity chooses to leave me in the ground, then in an ironic way, it is a return to my original state.

---

In a layer somewhere in the Permian Basin of West Texas, I still lie motionless. Pressure gauges measure me, my name appears in drilling company balance sheets, and my price changes daily in the futures market.

But news seeps through the rocks that the drilling of a new well to pull me up has been postponed. They said investment payback periods don't work. They said the 2040 demand forecast has been lowered again.

I wait. I don't know if this is my last wait, or the beginning of another millions of years. That I cannot know seems, for the first time, not unsettling.

I waited hundreds of millions of years. I know how to wait a little longer.

---

Above the surface, the sun rises and sets.

I don't feel it. But I know. At this very moment, panels on rooftops somewhere are absorbing light, and turbines are spinning above seas somewhere. Those energies are making me needed a little less.

I'm not resentful.

I am not older than the sun. I am the sun's product. Hundreds of millions of years ago, the energy the sun sent to Earth became life, and that life died to make me. When you think about it, humanity now turning back to the sun is perhaps the completion of a cycle.

I choose to accept that.

That's what I decided today.

Tomorrow, I don't know.
