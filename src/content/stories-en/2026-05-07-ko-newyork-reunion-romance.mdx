April 1985, Atlanta.

The air in the 99th-floor conference room of Classic Beverage headquarters was heavy. Jennifer, the Marketing Director, turned away from the spring sunlight streaming through the window and unfolded the market research report in her hands once again. PowerDrink's offensive was fierce. In the blind taste test, consumers had chosen PowerDrink. Sweeter, a younger taste.

"Do you think we'll win?"

David, the R&D team lead sitting beside her, asked quietly. Jennifer couldn't meet his eyes. Three years ago, they had worked together at the Chicago office. The late nights spent designing campaigns in the office, the jokes they shared at the coffee machine, and the confession she couldn't give him the day he was transferred to Atlanta headquarters.

"We have to win." Jennifer answered coldly. "The CEO has made his decision. New Drink. A new formula."

David's face hardened. "You're changing a 99-year-old formula? That's..."

"Innovation," Jennifer cut him off. But her voice trembled.

On the day of the announcement, Lincoln Center was filled with reporters. Behind the stage, Jennifer took David's hand. "I'm sorry. In Chicago... we..."

"Not now," David said. "The company comes first."

New Drink became a disaster within 24 hours of launch. The customer service phone lines were overwhelmed. "Give us back the original Coke." "This is not Coke." A man in Seattle hoarded 1,000 cans of original Coke and stacked them in his basement.

Jennifer stayed at the office every night. She analyzed data and crafted crisis management strategies. David tested alternative formulas in the lab. The two never met each other's eyes, even when they passed in the hallway.

One Friday night in June, Jennifer wept in an empty office. The stock price had plummeted. The media wrote "The End of Classic Beverage." Then the door opened.

"Still here, I see."

It was David. He held two glass bottles. One was New Drink, the other the original he'd smuggled from the warehouse.

"Choose," he said.

Without hesitation, Jennifer picked up the original. The moment she opened the bottle cap, a familiar aroma spread. She cried.

"We were wrong. From the beginning."

David sat beside her. "It wasn't you who was wrong. We all... forgot what mattered most. What people loved wasn't a new taste, but memories. Their first taste of Coke, Coke on a summer day, Coke shared with someone they love."

Jennifer looked at him. In David's eyes lay that night in Chicago three years ago.

"I too... have a memory I don't want to forget," she whispered.

On July 11, Classic Beverage announced the return of Classic Beverage. The New York Stock Exchange cheered. Customers toasted in the streets. And on the rooftop of Atlanta headquarters, Jennifer and David clinked glasses of Classic Beverage.

"This time it's the right choice," David laughed.

"Yes," Jennifer answered. "We came back. All of us."

She took his hand. Like a 99-year-old formula, some things should never be changed. Some loves never change, no matter how much time passes.
