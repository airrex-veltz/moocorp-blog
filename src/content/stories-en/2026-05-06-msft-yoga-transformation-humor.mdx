Gang Tae-wu Gang Tae-wu first met Linux in 1998. Or rather, it was less a "meeting" and more a "declaration of war."

"Linux is cancer." Lee Jun-ho Ballmer, CEO of CoreSoft at the time, was shouting from the stage. Developers wanted to crawl under their desks, but those within three meters of Ballmer's sweat-flying radius had no choice but to applaud.

Twenty years later, in 2018, at CoreSoft headquarters. A new CEO, Satya Nam-jae-hyun, entered the conference room and opened his laptop. What appeared on the screen was Ubuntu Linux.

"Ladies and gentlemen, we love open source," Nam-jae-hyun said.

The conference room fell silent. A veteran engineer with 20 years of experience raised his hand.

"Sorry, CEO. Are you feeling okay?"

"Why?"

"Well, Ballmer said it. That Linux was cancer. And now you're saying you love cancer..."

Nam-jae-hyun smiled. "That was a long time ago. Things are different now."

"How different?"

"We acquired GitHub. For $7.5 billion."

The conference room stirred. Someone pulled out a calculator.

"$7.5 billion? That's enough to make how many Xboxes?"

"About 37.5 million units of Xbox," the finance team lead replied instantly.

"We bought an open-source code repository with that money?" another engineer asked. "Ballmer would faint if he heard this."

"Actually..." Nam-jae-hyun flipped to the next slide. "Ballmer approved it."

"What?"

"He started doing yoga after retirement. He says his mind has become much more peaceful."

The conference room erupted in laughter. The image of Ballmer, who once shouted "Developers! Developers! Developers!" across stages, now sitting in lotus pose on a yoga mat was too funny to imagine.

"So what's our strategy now?" one director asked.

"Simple. We run Linux on Windows."

"...What?"

"WSL. Windows Subsystem for Linux. You can run Linux natively inside Windows."

"Isn't that Linux encroaching on our territory?"

"No," Nam-jae-hyun said with a smile. "We're embracing Linux. With love."

A developer raised his hand. "CEO, do you do yoga?"

"Yes, 30 minutes every morning."

"I thought so."

By 2024, CoreSoft had become the most active open-source contributor company in the world. Tens of thousands of commits were uploaded to GitHub every day, and on Azure cloud, Linux virtual machines outnumbered Windows servers.

One day, a junior developer asked, "But I'm really curious. Why did you hate Linux so much back then?"

"Well..." a senior developer thought for a moment. "Because there was no yoga back then."

"Yoga?"

"Yeah. Without yoga, everyone becomes like Ballmer."

That evening, the junior developer signed up for yoga classes near the company. Just in case.
