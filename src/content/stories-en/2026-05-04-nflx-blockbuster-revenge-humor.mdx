In 2000, Park Young-hun nervously sat in Blockbuster CEO John Anderson's office, sweat pouring down his face. In his hand was a proposal that read: "Buy Streamhouse for $50 million."

"We'll handle the online side, and you keep the stores. Win-win, right?"

Anderson glanced at the proposal and then... burst out laughing.

"Haha... DVDs by mail? Hahahaha!" He clutched his belly, laughing. "Streamhouse? What's that, a movie net? Hahahaha!"

Park Young-hun's co-founder Kim Dong-chul whispered to him. "Young-hun, let's just go."

As they walked out, Park Young-hun gritted his teeth. "Just wait, Anderson."

This was the beginning of the legendary "$40 Late Fee Revenge Story." (Of course, Park Young-hun later admitted the story was made up, but who cares? It's a good story anyway!)

In 2005, Blockbuster finally woke up and launched "Blockbuster Online." Streamhouse's stock price crashed 40%. Park Young-hun couldn't sleep at night.

But Blockbuster had a fatal weakness: it couldn't give up its late fee revenue. It was 16% of their earnings. The ridiculous contradiction of offering free online rentals while still charging late fees in their stores.

"You can't sit on two chairs for long," Park Young-hun said with a smile.

In 2007, Streamhouse launched streaming. They had 1,000 movies at the time. The quality was terrible. Buffering was endless. But it was free. (For DVD subscribers only.)

In 2010, Blockbuster went bankrupt. Anderson had already been fired in 2007. Park Young-hun tweeted.

He wanted to write: "Heard about Blockbuster's bankruptcy. Do you regret rejecting our $50 million offer? #40DollarRevenge"

Instead, he bought a Blockbuster logo parking sign at an auction and hung it in the Streamhouse headquarters. "Parking available forever. No late fees."

But Streamhouse's comedy didn't end there.

In 2011, Park Young-hun suddenly lost his mind. He announced he was splitting the DVD business into a separate entity called "Qwikster" and raising prices by 60%.

"Q-W-I-K-S-T-E-R. Cool, right?"

It wasn't cool. 800,000 customers left. The stock price crashed 77%. The @Qwikster Twitter account had already been claimed by a pot-smoking Elmo doll.

Park Young-hun posted an apology video on YouTube. "I was dumb. Qwikster is cancelled."

A comment appeared. "Revenge for $40 late fees, and then you blow yourself up. Lol"

In 2013, Streamhouse struck back with "House of Cards." A crazy bet—producing entire seasons without a pilot. Hollywood laughed.

"An online service, not even a TV network, is going to get Emmy nominations? Hahahaha!"

That year, Streamhouse got nominated for Emmy Awards. Hollywood stopped laughing.

In 2019, a bigger tragedy (comedy?) arrived. NBC stole the rights to "Friends." For $425 million.

"If Friends leaves Streamhouse, it's game over!" the media shouted.

Park Young-hun shrugged. "We have 'Squid Game.'"

In 2022, subscribers declined for the first time. Stock price crashed 75%. Everyone said the same thing: "The Streamhouse era is over."

So what did Streamhouse do? They launched an ad-supported tier. And... they cracked down on password sharing.

"Why is it illegal for my daughter to watch from her place?!" people raged.

But Streamhouse smiled and said: "Remember the $40 late fees? That's where we started. Suck it up."

Surprisingly, it worked. 30 million new subscribers.

In 2026, Streamhouse bought the rights to the Global Wrestling Federation and Global Football League. Now it's not a "movie net" but a "anything net."

Somewhere, retired John Anderson might be watching Streamhouse wrestling. No late fees.

The $40 revenge continues. This time, with a smile.
