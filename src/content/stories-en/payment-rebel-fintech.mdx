At three in the morning, lights never dimmed in Hangyeol Pay's server room.

Myungjun sat before five monitors, staring at the numbers. It was the night the third acquisition proposal from Geoyoung Payment arrived. The first had been courteous, the second impatient, the third different—not a condition but a warning.

Hangyeol Pay processed 23 million monthly transactions. By scale alone, it was the level of a peripheral division of Geoyoung Payment. But the data was different. Hangyeol Pay knew the purchase patterns of 140,000 small and medium merchants, hourly sales flows, repurchase cycles—who bought what, where, and when. What Geoyoung Payment wanted wasn't the platform. It was that knowledge.

"If you delete the data portability clause, we'll walk away from the table first."

Sujin, the legal director, said this while closing the proposal PDF. Myungjun didn't nod. The market already knew—the window for independent payment platforms to survive was narrowing rapidly. By the mid-2020s, 68 percent of the global payment market was in the hands of the top five players. Hundreds of startups split the remaining 32 percent. And every quarter, that number shrank.

Geoyoung Payment's strategy was simple. Independent platforms that refused acquisition faced fee dumping. When merchants wavered, the platform wavered. PayJoy, ClearTab, and MoaPayment had already disappeared that way.

Myungjun pushed his chair back. Hangyeol Pay had one thing. The principle of returning data to merchants. While other platforms locked data and sold convenience, Hangyeol Pay let merchants see their own customers directly. That's why 140,000 stayed. Regulators had also begun the data portability discussion in this direction—provisions requiring users to own their transaction data were simultaneously under legislative debate on three continents.

"If you keep the data portability clause, Geoyoung Payment will withdraw the offer. Then the dumping begins."

Sujin's voice dropped. Myungjun knew it too. Keeping the principle meant war. Selling it meant losing the reason for being. And merchants didn't take long to notice a platform that had lost its reason for being. Merchants whose data had been stripped away eventually realized the same thing would happen on the next platform.

He looked out the window. Seoul's dawn lights lay low across the skyline. Somewhere, transactions were happening now through Hangyeol Pay's API. Someone was buying a late-night snack at a convenience store, someone was receiving a dawn delivery, someone's business was running on those numbers.

The deadline to respond was 72 hours away.

Myungjun pulled the screen back to him. The numbers flowed. The decision hadn't been made yet.
