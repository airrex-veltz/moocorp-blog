## Perfect Things

The grandmother held a tomato. The red was exactly the same. The knife in the grandson's hand stopped.

"The taste used to be different every year."

"Now it's always the same," the grandmother said. "That's what they promised."

Vegetables from the clone farm flooded the market. No size variation. Sugar content deviation minimal. Vitamin content, and so forth. The chefs applauded. They could follow recipes precisely.

But the grandmother's refrigerator always held unpredictable things.

"This isn't food—it's a promise," the grandmother told her grandson. A promise from the old farmers. That in some years the sunlight is strong and in others the rain is sparse, and things grown that way differ each year.

The grandson chewed the perfect tomato and wondered. What did we gain while losing the unpredictable? Stability? Reliability?

The grandmother's finger tapped the grandson's forehead lightly.

"Does perfection frighten you?"
