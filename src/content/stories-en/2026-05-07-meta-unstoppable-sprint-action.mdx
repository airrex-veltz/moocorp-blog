February 2004, Room H33, Kirkland Dormitory, Harvard University. The CEO's fingers raced across the keyboard. Four in the morning. His roommates were asleep, but his monitor glowed with blue light. A simple logo flickered on the screen. "The Social Media."

"Let's launch."

The moment he hit Enter, the world began to move.

In the first 24 hours, 1,200 people signed up. In a week, half of Harvard's students had created a profile. The CEO didn't sleep. He survived on coffee and pizza, writing code. Yale, Columbia, Stanford... as each school was added, the server room temperature soared. By the end of 2004, one million users. 2005, five million. The graph shot straight up.

Silicon Valley took notice. Peter Thiel invested $500,000. The CEO left school and headed to Palo Alto. A cramped office, an old desk, but the future was there.

"Move fast. And break things."

This was Social Net's philosophy. Competitors stood still and thought. The CEO ran. In 2006, Yahoo offered to acquire them for $1 billion. At 22, the CEO rejected it outright. The board was shocked.

"We haven't even started yet. We're connecting the world."

He was right. In 2007, they opened the platform. Developers poured in. Games, apps, advertising... the ecosystem exploded. In 2012, the IPO was the largest in history. A market cap of $104 billion. That same year, they acquired Photo Social for $1 billion. At the time, people called it insane. But it was a stroke of genius. In 2014, they swallowed a messaging app for $19 billion. Social Net had become a massive empire.

But empires always have enemies.

On March 17, 2018, "NewsTime" and "Press Corp" detonated bombs simultaneously. A data scandal. The data of 87 million users had been illegally collected and weaponized in political campaigns. The media erupted. The "#DeleteFacebook" hashtag dominated the trends.

On April 10, 2018, in a hearing room in Washington, D.C. The CEO stood before 44 senators. Hundreds of cameras pointed at him. Five hours of criticism rained down.

"CEO, can you take responsibility?"

The stock crashed. Advertisers fled. Social Net faced its worst crisis in history. But the CEO didn't buckle.

"We will take responsibility. And we will do better."

They built an AI-powered content filtering system. They published transparency reports every quarter. They hired thousands of reviewers. Step by step, they regained trust. In 2019, users grew again. Advertising revenue hit an all-time high.

And on October 28, 2021, the CEO surprised the world once again.

"We are now Eeum Social."

They abandoned the Social Net name. They bet everything on Eeum Social Bus. They poured over $10 billion into VR research. Critics mocked them. "A failed gamble," they said. "The CEO's downfall."

But the CEO kept running. VR headsets sold 15 million units. The metaverse drew millions into virtual worlds. In 2023, Headset 3 launched. A new era in mixed reality had opened.

In 2024, Eeum Social stood at the forefront of the AI revolution. They open-sourced Language Model 3, directly challenging the search company and AI companies. AI glasses developed in collaboration with a glasses brand brought the future to reality. The stock price hit an all-time high.

The sprint that began in Kirkland H33 never stopped. The CEO still races across the keyboard. Moving fast, breaking things, rebuilding. Falling and rising again. Criticized but moving forward.

Eeum Social's war continues. And as always, he never looks back.
