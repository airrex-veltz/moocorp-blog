December 2020, Manhattan, New York.

22nd floor of the pharmaceutical company headquarters research building, 3 a.m. Outside the window, only the city lights flickered. Phase 3 clinical trial final data. 95% efficacy.

"It's done." Her voice trembled. "We... made it."

Young researcher Mark at the next desk looked up. His eyes were bloodshot. He hadn't taken a single day off in the past 11 months. "Really? Really... it's done?"

Catherine nodded. But these were not tears of joy. She opened her desk drawer and pulled out an old photograph. A photo with her young daughter. Those moments they couldn't spend together on her last birthday. Every night, her daughter's voice asking "When will Mom come home?" echoed in her ears.

"Mark, did you... call your grandmother?" Catherine asked quietly.

Mark shook his head. "I couldn't. She passed away last week. At the nursing home." His voice cracked. "From COVID. She never even got to try the vaccine I made."

The lab fell silent. Only the hum of the fluorescent lights filled the space.

The pharmaceutical company was a small firm founded in 1849 by two German immigrants. It started with making dewormers. But what they dreamed of was not simple medicine. It was easing people's pain—that was their promise.

170 years have passed. That promise has been passed down through generations. Giving hope through medicine, protecting hearts with medicine, and now creating a vaccine to fight the virus threatening the entire world.

But behind that were countless sacrifices.

Catherine recalled the early days of collaboration with a biotech company. March 2020, when the world stopped. The CEO called an emergency meeting. "If we don't do this, who will? We have a responsibility to make this happen."

A marathon that began that day. Compressing what normally takes ten years into eleven months. No weekends, no holidays. Researchers lived away from their families. Some sacrificed their own health. One of Mark's colleagues collapsed from overwork and was hospitalized.

"Why go to such lengths?" someone asked. Catherine answered. "If we're late by even one day, someone's mother, grandmother, or child... will die."

December 11, emergency use authorization from the regulatory agency. December 14, the first vaccination. A nurse in New York became the first in the world to receive the pharmaceutical company's vaccine. As the world watched, she smiled and said. "This is the beginning of hope."

At that moment, Catherine cried alone in the lab. It was tears mixed with joy and sadness. She called her daughter.

"Mom, I saw it on the news. You made it, didn't you?"

"...Yes. Your mom and many people made it together."

"Mom, I'm so proud of you. But... will you come home now?"

Catherine looked out the window. Dawn was breaking. "Soon. Wait just a little longer."

But she knew. Vaccine production, distribution, handling variants... The fight had only just begun.

In 2021, the pharmaceutical company supplied 3 billion doses of vaccine worldwide. In 2022, 4 billion doses. Countless lives were saved. But behind those numbers lay unnamed scientists' sleepless nights, separations from family, sacrificed health and time.
