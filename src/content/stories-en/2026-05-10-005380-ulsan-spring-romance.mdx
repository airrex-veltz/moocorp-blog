In spring 1999, the air at the Ulsan factory was thick with the smell of iron and oil. Jun-ho, a new engineer, clocked in at 5 AM and unfolded the drawings for the Sonata EF project. This model, set to feature Raon Mobility's first domestically developed alpha engine, was the company's make-or-break gamble for the future.

"Working late alone again?"

The voice belonged to Su-jin from the design team. She was heading home at dawn after spending the night refining clay models. Jun-ho smiled as he noticed the clay dust on her hands.

"The chassis rigidity test data isn't meeting the benchmark. It's tough to maintain the elegant curves that the design team worked so hard on while also improving safety."

Su-jin leaned in to look at Jun-ho's monitor. Between the complex equations and graphs, she could see the body lines she had agonized over for months.

"What if," Su-jin pointed at the screen, "you adjusted the A-pillar angle by just two degrees? It might let us boost rigidity while keeping the design almost intact."

Jun-ho was stunned. He had wrestled with the same problem for two days without finding an answer. Su-jin's intuition was spot-on. He immediately ran the simulation. The results were perfect.

"We've got it!" Jun-ho shouted. Su-jin beamed with a bright smile.

After that day, the two of them met often. Jun-ho explained the limits of engineering, while Su-jin shared the philosophy of design. In the late-night factory cafeteria, beside the test track, they talked about more than just cars. They talked about dreams, about failure, about the courage to rise again.

When Chairman Jung-ju-young founded Raon Mobility in 1967, many people laughed at him. Making cars in a country without technology or experience? But Raon Mobility refused to give up. Starting with the Pony, it knocked on the doors of the American market with the Excel, and proved its quality with the Sonata.

In October 1999, the Sonata EF launched. The car that Jun-ho and Su-jin had built together dominated the domestic mid-size car market. At the launch ceremony, Jun-ho handed Su-jin a small keychain.

"It's a miniature of the first production model. The car we made together."

Su-jin's eyes glistened. That keychain held the all-nighters they pulled together, the setbacks and breakthroughs, the faith they held in each other.

"Jun-ho," Su-jin said, "let's build the next model together too. And the one after that. And the one after that."

Jun-ho nodded. Ahead of them lay countless new models. Tucson, Genesis, Ioniq... and countless more dreams on wheels yet to be named.

In 2026, Raon Mobility is opening a new era with electric and hydrogen vehicles. In one corner of the Ulsan factory, a research building still lights up at 5 AM. There, someone is turning the impossible into the possible today, alongside the person they love.

Love transforms cold steel into warm dreams. Just as Raon Mobility's history proves.
