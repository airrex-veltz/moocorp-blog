Park Su-jin set down her coffee cup as she stared at three monitors simultaneously. 2:37 AM. She was alone in the fraud investigation team office on the 17th floor of Majoo Pay's headquarters.

"There it is again."

A red dot blinked on the transaction log screen. A ledger entry lasting 0.003 seconds. The amount: $0.01. Yet over the past 72 hours, the same pattern had repeated 1.47 million times. Total: $14,700. On its own, it was nothing. But to Park Su-jin's eyes, something was different.

*This thing is alive.*

She had joined Majoo Pay's fraud detection team six years ago. Master's degree in financial engineering, former member of the prosecution's digital forensics unit, and an instinct no one would acknowledge. An eye for patterns. The ability to read how numbers lie.

That instinct was screaming now.

---

The account existed. Yet it didn't exist.

In the intermediate processing layer of the Majoo Pay payment network, in the section known as the 'clearing buffer,' millions of temporary accounts were created and destroyed every second. Normal. Except these accounts weren't being destroyed. Precisely, they were being reborn in different forms while appearing to be destroyed.

Park Su-jin flipped the code. She analyzed the account ID generation algorithm. Three hours later, she slowly straightened in her chair.

"A ghost network."

Someone knew Majoo Pay's internal API keys. They understood the clearing buffer's lifecycle. They created accounts, used them, and deleted them. Without a trace. Except for the rounding error in the third decimal place.

This error had given her the tail.

---

At 9 AM, Park Su-jin walked into Team Lead Lee Seung-ho's office with her report.

"How much total exposure?" Lee Seung-ho asked, skimming the documents.

"Currently confirmed, $3.4 million. But if this structure has been operating for the past six months—"

"How much?"

Park Su-jin took a breath. "At least $200 million."

Lee Seung-ho's face hardened. Outside the window, downtown Seoul glittered in the morning sunlight.

"Is it an inside job?" he asked quietly.

"The API keys were issued internally. At least three teams have access at that level."

Lee Seung-ho said nothing for a while. Then slowly he closed the documents.

"Su-jin, you can't take this one alone."

"I know. That's why I'm reporting it."

"No." He met her eyes. "I mean you can't take this to the Financial Services Commission or the National Investigation Service right now. We need to sort it out first."

*Sort it out.* That word struck Park Su-jin's ear cold.

---

That evening, Park Su-jin sat on a bench along the Han River and took a call. The caller was a number she didn't recognize.

"You're Investigator Park Su-jin, aren't you?"

The voice was low and clear.

"Delete the clearing buffer analysis report directly. You have family too."

The call ended.

She stared at the river for a long time. Her hands trembled. But somewhere deep in her heart, another tremor rose. Anger.

*If I'm getting a call like this, it means I'm on the right path.*

---

Park Su-jin spent the night digging into the endpoint of the account chain. There was a point where the ghost accounts ultimately converged. The cross-border settlement channel between International Payment and Majoo Pay. A regulatory blind spot.

The money had 'evaporated' there. Or more precisely—it had been distributed to accounts somewhere. To trace it, she needed cooperation from International Payment. And the person who could authorize that cooperation request was Lee Seung-ho.

The next morning, Park Su-jin knocked on Lee Seung-ho's office door.

"I'm filing a coordination request with International Payment."

Lee Seung-ho looked at her for a moment. "Su-jin, you know Majoo Pay is preparing for an IPO right now?"

"I know."

"If this breaks, the listing goes away. Stock options for thousands of employees go away. Yours too."

Park Su-jin didn't answer.

Lee Seung-ho sighed. "There's a way to handle it internally first. Quietly. Compensate the victims. Plug the hole. No one knows."

"The victims need to know."

"The victims already agreed to the terms of service when they used Majoo Pay. Legally—"

"I'm not asking if it's legal. I'm asking if it's right, sir."

---

Silence filled the office. Lee Seung-ho walked to the window.

"I know," he said quietly. "I know, Su-jin."

That caught Park Su-jin off guard.

"So?"

"So I'm scared. I have 15 years of my life tied up in this company. A family too." He turned back. His eyes were red. "But you're different. You still have reasons not to lose."

Park Su-jin slowly accepted the weight of those words.

---

Four days later, an anonymous tip was filed with the Financial Services Commission's Cyber Financial Investigation Division. The informant didn't reveal their identity. But the attached log file was 147 pages, and the account tracking diagram was as precise as if it had been drawn by hand.

Park Su-jin drafted her resignation letter. She didn't submit it. Not yet.

That night, she sat in front of her three monitors again. The clearing buffer was quiet. Whether someone had begun plugging the hole or they had simply started being more careful, she couldn't yet tell.

She drank her coffee.

*Numbers can never hide the truth. It just takes time.*

A new red dot blinked in the corner of one monitor.

Park Su-jin opened the log.
