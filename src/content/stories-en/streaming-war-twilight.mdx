That night, Hanbit Stream turned off her dashboard.

The numbers still lingered in her eyes. They weren't decreasing, nor increasing—just frozen. Like an old clock whose battery had run out, unable to lift its second hand any further. She looked out the window. The city's lights flickered here and there, and somewhere, someone was surely watching her. Through a screen, beneath the glow of a small remote.

A golden age is invisible when you're inside it.

Hanbit Stream remembered the 2010s. Back then, she truly shined all through the night. People lay on sofas, pulled blankets over themselves, and simply let "Next episode plays in 5 seconds" play at two in the morning. Subscriber numbers climbed like stairs every quarter. The world seemed to revolve around her. Everyone talked about her, everyone wanted her.

But in the spring of 2022, that staircase descended for the first time.

In the first quarter, 200,000 subscribers left. The number itself was small. Out of 220 million total subscribers, 200,000 wasn't even one percent on a calculator—mere noise. But Hanbit Stream knew it wasn't about the number. It was the first time. After more than ten years of only going up, this was the first time going down. When the stock price fell more than 35 percent in a single day, Hanbit Stream realized the blow struck not at her body but at her pride.

She thought: should I raise the price?

That thought lodged like a thorn. If she raised it, more would leave. If she lowered it, revenue would shrink. If she stopped password sharing, she'd face backlash. If she added advertisements, the image she had built would crumble. No matter which direction she chose, it felt like losing something irreversible. It wasn't a matter of choice—it was accepting a world that had already changed.

Competitors flooded in. One day Byeolmuri Pictures appeared, another day a major device manufacturer, and another day a massive telecom company each emerged with their own streaming kingdoms. Hanbit Stream recalled when she was alone. Back then, this market was hers. Vast like a wilderness, boundless, full of possibilities. Now that wilderness had become a crowded city. Someone's sign hung on every corner.

The word saturation circled in her mind. It meant there was no more fertile ground to seep into.

About a month ago, she sat across from numbers in a conference room. Or rather, she faced the many voices surrounding her. Raise prices, create more content, attract advertisers, grab live sports. Every voice was persuasive, each pointing in a different direction. In the midst of them, Hanbit Stream recalled a certain early morning long ago. The dawn when she first decided to make original dramas. She was afraid, but something was clear. She knew what had to be done.

This was different. Nothing was clear.

Sometimes she thought she had grown too large. When she was small, she could run lightly. She could try anything. Now one move brought reactions from millions. That weight sometimes suffocated her.

Tonight she decided to make no decision at all.

She just looked at the city lights, thinking that somewhere, someone was watching something through her screen. Someone crying, someone laughing, someone lonely. The fact that she existed for those people hadn't changed. Even if her golden age had ended, that much remained the same.

Whether that was enough, she still didn't know.

The lights outside the window went out one by one. Hanbit Stream watched them, knowing she would open her dashboard again tomorrow. She would sit face to face with the numbers again. But tonight, just tonight, she sat without numbers. In a saturated world, slowly looking at who she had become.

The sound of seasons changing is always this quiet.
