Ha-joon's fingers had endured nine seasons.

I'm not sure if that's impressive. But over nine years in front of the disassembly workstation, the tip of his right index finger had gradually flattened. Holding a thin spatula tool for twelve hours a day does that. The doctor called it occupational compression. Ha-joon just called it the mark of a worker.

The recycled terminal processing facility on the outskirts of Incheon. Small for a warehouse, with sixteen tables and twenty-five workers. The fluorescent lights turn on at 6:30 AM and off at 8 PM. Lunch break at 12:30 for forty minutes. Otherwise, no movement. In winter, fingers get stiff first at the windowless seats. Ha-joon always takes that seat. If someone who arrives earlier sits there, it's actually inconvenient.

---

After 2 PM, Miran at the next station said quietly:

"Look at this."

A seventh-generation model. Something was stuck inside the back protective film.

A photograph.

A family photo. Three kids and parents, in holiday clothes. It looked like something from a department store photo studio. Either it was placed there before the film was attached, or it had slid in over a long time as the film loosened.

"What are you gonna do about it?" Ha-joon said. More out of habit than anything.

"It's weird to just leave it."

"Put it up there. We'll handle it before we leave."

Miran set the photo in the corner of the workstation. Ha-joon moved on to the next device. Boxes of devices with failed screen recovery assessments were stacked in a line. Camera modules, batteries, main board fragments. Each takes a different path for sale or disposal. Batteries go to a separate collection point. If thermal processing quality fails, the workshop smoke appears in the worker's direction first.

---

About 4,000 terminals come into this warehouse per month on average. Half are collected from three domestic secondhand trading platforms; the other half are corporate returns. Globally, the secondhand smartphone market keeps growing. One research firm reported that the worldwide secondhand terminal market exceeded 65 billion dollars in 2023. Sustainable consumption. Eco-friendly. Reduced carbon footprint. These words were always attached around those numbers.

Ha-joon had never thought about those words at the workstation. Never had the time to think about them. Breaking down a device completely takes an average of ten minutes, and to hit the daily quota, you can't stop.

"How are your hands?" Miran asked.

"Fine."

"You said yesterday you had no feeling."

"I'm fine now."

It wasn't a lie. They were actually fine this morning. After 2 PM, the right index finger felt heavy again. Making a fist felt like the force arrived a beat late.

---

When he started this job, Ha-joon was 23. It was when the secondhand terminal business was just beginning to grow. Whenever someone talked about environment, they'd also mention used phones. Ha-joon didn't think it was a bad thing. He still doesn't.

But he learned one thing.

How far sustainability applies.

It's possible to extend device lifespan. No one counts how long the worker's fingers last. The carbon reduction from returned devices is recorded somewhere. No one records how flat Ha-joon's right index finger has become. Nowhere.

---

At 8 PM, the fluorescent lights turned off. Ha-joon took off his gloves and made and unmade a fist. Three times, four times. It took a moment for the stiff sensation to return. Today it seemed to take a little longer than yesterday.

Before leaving, Miran put the photo from the corner of the workstation into the disposal bin. The family in holiday clothes disappeared among small piles of paper scraps.

Ha-joon said nothing.

Tomorrow morning, the fluorescent lights will come on again.
