Teslan had always looked ahead.

More precisely, he looked beyond the horizon ahead. While others feared the end of the road, he was already dreaming of charging stations in the desert beyond. That made him dangerous, and at the same time, captivating. When people called his name, they often shortened it to just **T**. Two letters. In those letters lay ten years of glory and humiliation and victory compressed into one.

He had begun selling electricity to the world. At first, he was mocked. A world steeped in the smell of oil did not believe in automobiles that ran on lightning as fuel. But Teslan ran in front of the disbelievers. Quietly, quickly, decisively. And at some point, the world began to follow him.

That was Teslan's first season. A season of glory.

---

The second season smelled different.

A giant had walked from the east. When Teslan first noticed its shadow, the giant was already standing right beside him. The giant from the east was quiet. It did not shout. Its advertising was not flashy. It just sold. In staggering numbers, at staggering speed.

In the final quarter of 2023, the numbers flipped for the first time. By pure electric vehicle sales, the giant from the east surpassed Teslan. It was not merely a statistic. To Teslan, that number was like a mirror. A mirror that made him question who he was again.

Teslan would not forget that night.

Standing alone in a corner of the factory, he studied the production data. The numbers stared back at him. He had already cut prices. Multiple times, in multiple markets. It was not an easy decision for him. Cutting prices was like relinquishing part of his pride. But he did it. The market demanded it, and he responded. That was Teslan's way.

But the giant did not stop there.

The giant had carved away Teslan's share in its own market, and was already extending its reach into Europe. With cheap prices, fast charging, and familiar language, it was talking to new customers. In the market that Teslan had created one era ahead, the giant was now dropping anchor more widely and more swiftly.

---

And the waves came.

Regulators moved quietly, but relentlessly. In Europe, the subsidy system changed. In the United States, the tax credit conditions grew complicated. The list of conditions that Teslan's vehicles had to meet changed. Battery origins, assembly locations, mineral supply chains. The hand of regulation did not fix on any one thing but kept moving.

Teslan took this as an attack.

At least at first. He felt the regulations were aimed at him. He had paved this path first, he had endured the pain first, and now the rules were changing—it felt unjust. He raised his voice. In public forums, on social media, in front of the press. His words were sharp and sometimes excessive.

That was the second price he paid.

Some consumers in Europe left. Resentment toward the brand spread quietly. The image Teslan had built—innovative, forward-looking, cool—began to wear away. The cars were still excellent. The technology was still ahead. But people were not buying just cars. They were buying a story. And the story was shaking.

---

The climax came on a spring evening.

That night, Teslan faced three pieces of bad news at once. Regulators had formally announced an investigation into his autonomous driving system. The giant from the east had unveiled a new model, priced at half of Teslan's. And one of his factories had shut down its production line—a parts supply issue.

He was standing in the conference room. Reports came in from all directions. Legal, production, marketing, finance. Everyone spoke, and all their words were bad news.

Teslan closed his eyes for a moment.

He thought back to why he had started this work so long ago. He wanted to change the world. He wanted to change a world that ran on oil to one that ran on electricity. That was still true. Nothing could erase that first intention.

But he also knew now that intention alone was not enough.

To defeat the giant, he had to be smarter. To overcome the regulations, he had to be more patient. And to protect his own image, sometimes he had to choose silence. Teslan knew that was the hardest part. Silence. Not fighting. Waiting.

---

Dawn came.

The lights in the conference room were still on. Teslan looked out the window. His cars sat in rows in the parking lot. White cars, black cars, red cars. Quietly charging. Saying nothing, just existing.

He thought. Perhaps that was the answer.

The fight with the giant would not end. The waves of regulation would not stop. The world would not wait for Teslan. He had known that from the beginning. That is why Teslan had always run. But now, in this dawn, he thought for the first time.

Perhaps what matters more than running in the right direction is whether the reason for running is still alive.

There was no answer. The cars outside the window stayed silent too. Only the charging indicator blinked quietly.
