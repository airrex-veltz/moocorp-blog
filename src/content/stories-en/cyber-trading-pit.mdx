At two in the morning, Han Jae-won was alone in the office space next to the server room.

The office was filled with dark monitors and cold coffee cups. A single fluorescent light flickered at irregular intervals. He had noticed its uneven rhythm three months ago, but no one had submitted a replacement request. In this company, the list of things no one cared about was always long.

He worked at GuardianNet. A cybersecurity specialization firm. The company was known to the world as one that protected the networks of financial institutions, large retailers, and government agencies. At least that's what the official introduction said.

Han Jae-won was a senior analyst at the Security Analysis Division 2. His job was to detect external penetration attempts and classify patterns. He had a reputation as a good worker. Quiet, thorough, and willing to work late. His superiors liked him. They still didn't know he was a person who asked uncomfortable questions.

He first noticed the strange traffic pattern three weeks ago.

While organizing log files, a very subtle anomaly caught his eye. In the late night hours, a small stream of data flowing from a specific internal server to an external IP. The volume was so small that the automated detection system treated it as noise. But he began to collect it by date. Four days, five days, ten days. A small trickle was becoming a river.

He traced the destination IP in reverse. The Netherlands. Romania. Hong Kong. The server changed each time. But the transmission method, the structure of the encryption layer, the way packets were divided—all were identical. Someone, or something, had been extracting data in the same way for a long time.

He began to track the content of the transmitted data. Gradually elevating his access privileges, careful not to leave records. It took six days.

The files were encrypted, but the metadata survived. The rules in the filename, the creation time, the pattern of sizes. That alone was enough to see the outline. What was flowing out were employee rosters from client companies, internal contact lists, account access histories, and some financial data. All collected through the agent software that GuardianNet had installed for its contract clients. The original purpose of that software was to detect threats. But in reality, it was doing something else too.

Han Jae-won pushed his chair back and stared at the ceiling. Above, the sound of the air conditioning fan spinning reached him. His heartbeat was faster than that.

He already knew. In 2023 and 2024, security researchers around the world had repeatedly warned about this. Corporate security solution vendors were actually selling internal data from their clients to third parties, and in some cases, millions of corporate employee information and transaction histories were being distributed through dark web brokers. It had felt like a distant story. Now it was right next to him.

He began to organize the evidence.

The USB drive was brought from home. He accessed it using tethering from his personal phone rather than the company network. All files were organized by date, and hash values were recorded. He was a security expert. He knew how to handle evidence.

---

Two days later, team lead Park Seong-min called him in.

"I saw access logs to the internal system after you left yesterday. What work were you doing?"

Han Jae-won met his eyes. Park Seong-min was his boss, someone he had worked with for five years. He was competent and loyal to the company.

"I found an unusual pattern while organizing client-side logs, so I took a closer look."

"What kind of pattern?"

"I'm not certain yet. I'll look into it more and report back."

Park Seong-min looked at him for a moment. There was something in that gaze. Han Jae-won couldn't quite read whether it was caution or warning.

"Don't overexert yourself. Do it during work hours."

As he left the room, Han Jae-won felt his hands trembling slightly.

That afternoon, he detected that his access history had been viewed by someone else. It was a record from the internal security system's administrator log. He himself was being monitored.

---

He discovered the name of a headquarters executive the following week.

While tracing the originator of the transmitted data packets, he found an internal account that had no general employee access. Periodic access logs to server areas that required higher authorization levels. The name was Choi Woo-jin. Director of Technical Strategy.

He pretended not to notice Choi Woo-jin's name. He closed the screen and went to the restroom. He stood in front of the mirror for a long time. His face was pale.

It was now clear that he could not submit evidence within the company. He didn't know who to trust. Whether Park Seong-min was involved, or whether he had simply been trying to protect him from suspicion. Whether there was someone above Choi Woo-jin. He didn't know.

That night at home, he stared at the whistleblower channels of the Financial Supervisory Service and the Personal Information Protection Commission for a long time. He read how whistleblower protection regulations applied. He roughly calculated how much a lawyer would cost. He checked his deposit balance.

The USB drive was in a drawer. He couldn't sleep.

---

Four days later in the morning, he ran into Choi Woo-jin in the building lobby as he arrived at work.

Choi Woo-jin nodded naturally when he saw him. The way he did every day. An ordinary greeting on an ordinary morning. Han Jae-won nodded too.

The elevator doors closed.

In that moment, Han Jae-won made a decision. If you could call it that. More precisely, he had decided not to postpone making a decision any longer.

During lunch, he opened his personal laptop at a cafe outside the company. He attached the file and entered the recipient addresses. The Financial Supervisory Service's whistleblower reporting email, the Personal Information Protection Commission's reporting channel, and one cybersecurity journalist. He wrote the subject line. He wrote the body. His finger hovered over the send button.

Outside the cafe, people passed by to grab lunch. Someone was laughing while taking a phone call. A pigeon that had wandered toward the road stopped.

He pressed the button.

A notification appeared that the email had been sent. He closed the laptop and took a sip of coffee. He couldn't taste it well.

---

He didn't know what would happen.

Whether the report would be accepted. Whether an investigation would begin. How and when his name would be revealed. How GuardianNet would respond. What expression Park Seong-min would have on his face when he found out.

He returned to the office. He sat at his desk and turned on the monitor. Afternoon work was waiting. Log files, pattern analysis, reports. Everything was the same as yesterday, and nothing was the same.

The afternoon of Seoul was progressing outside the window. Han Jae-won placed his hands on the keyboard.

The flickering fluorescent light continued to shine, still unreplaced.
