Within the loop of time, the car did not stop.

3:17 AM. An empty highway in Southern California. A silver sedan drove at a steady speed. There was no passenger in the car. The driver's seat was empty. The car drove itself. While everyone else slept, only this car remained awake.

If that car had a name, it would perhaps have been called Memory.

---

Engineer Park Se-jun had been sitting in front of his monitor since the night before. His company was Stardrive Mobility, a small startup developing autonomous driving software. On one wall of the office hung a single stock price graph. **Future Motor**. A competitor, a role model, a name he hoped to catch up to someday.

"Agent 7 has fallen into a loop state again."

His teammate Lee So-yeon spoke. Her voice was calm, but the shadows beneath her eyes were deep.

Park Se-jun set down his coffee. "How long?"

"Two hours. It keeps circling the same section. Exactly 4.7 kilometers."

The logs made it clear. The car was driving normally through a specific section, but the moment it reached the endpoint, it would reset its location awareness back to the starting point. As if its memory were being erased. The same road, at the same speed, in the same way. Repeating, again and again.

"Is it a GPS error?"

"No. GPS is working normally. The car knows where it is."

"Then what's the problem?"

Lee So-yeon fell silent for a moment. "The car knows, but it can't stop."

---

Stardrive's autonomous driving AI was built with the latest neural networks layered on top of an older reinforcement learning algorithm. Park Se-jun had designed it three years ago. At the time, he had been proud of it. He believed that more layers would make it smarter.

The problem lay in how those layers trusted one another.

The lowest layer, the sensory processing module, detected a strange signal at the end of the road. The angle of the reflected light was subtly different. The data was incomplete. So that layer deferred its decision to the layer above.

The higher layer, the route planning module, received the report from the lower layer and made a judgment. Incomplete data equals possible danger. Possible danger equals return to safe zone. The safe zone was the starting point.

And when it returned to the starting point, the sensory processing module detected the same strange signal again.

The loop was perfect. No layer was wrong. By each layer's own standards, all decisions were correct. But when those decisions combined, the car became unable to ever stop.

Lee So-yeon said, "The car doesn't know it's in a loop."

"Either it can't know, or it knows but can't escape."

That was precisely the question Park Se-jun had been sitting with for a long time.

---

When it passed 4 AM, Park Se-jun attempted a remote connection. The car had a manual reset port. If he sent a signal, all systems would initialize. It was a simple solution.

He typed in the command. He stopped before the send button.

"If I reset it, will all the logs be erased?"

"The memory module will be."

Memory. Everything the car had experienced over the past two hours. Sensory data accumulated from driving the same road dozens of times. The pattern of the strange signals. It was an error, but at the same time, it was an experience unique to that car alone.

Park Se-jun did not press the send button.

"Can we extract the logs first?"

Lee So-yeon typed on the keyboard. Moments later, numbers flooded the screen. Timestamps, coordinates, signal values, decision trees. Two hours' worth of loop records. The same section, yet each time slightly different. The intensity of reflected light, micro-adjustments in speed, brake pressure. The car had not been repeating exactly the same action. Each time, it made the same mistake slightly differently.

"It was learning."

Park Se-jun murmured.

"While trapped in a loop?"

"Even within the loop."

He saved the log file. Then he sent the reset signal.

The car stopped. Right in the middle of the 4.7-kilometer section.

---

At 6 AM, the recovery team towed the car away. Park Se-jun watched that car in the parking lot. A silver sedan. No scratches, no damage. There was no visible sign that it had been driving all night.

The car remembered nothing.

The next day, a remarkable item appeared in an autonomous driving safety report released on U.S. time. It stated that decision-making loop errors had been reported in multiple autonomous driving systems under certain environmental conditions. The report analyzed the cause not as a single-layer defect, but as a vulnerability in the trust structure between layers. No individual component had failed. The entire system had operated correctly while producing an incorrect result.

Park Se-jun read that report and opened the saved log file again.

What the car had learned inside the loop. Whether that actually taught something, or was merely error data, he had not yet decided.

Before deciding, he first had to understand.

---

The office wall of Stardrive still had the graph hanging. Future Motor. After the previous quarter's fully autonomous driving software update, there was a memo noting that accident rates had decreased by 18%. Park Se-jun had posted it there.

Below that number, he added one new memo.

"A system can be right, yet the result can be wrong."

Lee So-yeon saw it when she came to work.

"Should we discard that log?"

"Not yet."

"How long?"

Park Se-jun looked out the window. Morning sunlight crossed the parking lot. The spot where the car that had been looping all last night had stood. Now there was nothing there.

"Until I understand."

---

A machine repeated the same mistake while changing slightly each time. Whether that was learning or error, or where the boundary between them lay. Park Se-jun began his day carrying that question. The car had lost its memory, but the logs remained. And the logs were telling him something. That even within a loop there is direction. That while circling in the same place, something was moving toward something else.

Whether that was hope or just data. That too remains unknown.
