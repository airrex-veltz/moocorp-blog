## 1. The Age of Glory

Late 1990s, Santa Clara, California. In his seventh-floor research laboratory at Nanochip's headquarters, David Chun gazed out the window. The Silicon Valley sunset reflected in the glass, bathing his face in red light.

"Team lead, the Pentium III yield has exceeded our target!"

A young engineer reported excitedly. David smiled. He had come to America from Korea two decades ago to start as an engineer, and now he was a senior researcher. Nanochip controlled eighty percent of the global semiconductor market, and Moore's Law was treated as if it were a law of nature itself.

"We're changing the world."

That night, raising a glass with his colleagues, David was certain. Nanochip would last forever. He hoped to work there until retirement, and for his son to become an engineer there as well.

## 2. The First Crack

In 2007, Jun-ho Shin announced NewTech's new phone.

"Why aren't they using our chips?"

Someone asked in the meeting room. David remained silent. He already knew the answer. Nanochip's mobile processors consumed too much power. Chips based on advanced technology were beginning to dominate the smartphone market, and Nanochip could only watch.

"The PC market is still strong, so we're fine."

The executives said so. But David was uneasy. Young engineers began leaving one by one for NewTech and ChipTech. Fewer people stayed late in the laboratory.

## 3. The Beginning of the Fall

By 2015, David was in his mid-fifties. That year, Nanochip failed to develop a 10-nanometer process. Competitor EastSemicon had already succeeded in mass-producing 7-nanometer chips.

"We're falling behind."

David said carefully in the technology meeting. But the executives' response was cold.

"We are Nanochip. We set the standard."

That night, David updated his resume for the first time. But he didn't send it. He couldn't leave the company he had dedicated twenty-five years to, not yet.

## 4. A Mirage of Hope

In 2018, a new CEO took office. He came from the data center business and promised change.

"We will rise again. You are at the center of it."

The CEO declared at the company-wide meeting. Applause erupted, but David's hands clapped slowly. He had seen too many CEOs. All of them had promised change, and all of them had failed.

Later that year, Nanochip laid off ten thousand employees. Three people left from David's team. A colleague he had worked with for twenty years packed his things and said:

"David, you should leave too. This ship is sinking."

David shook his head. He couldn't leave. This place was everything to him.

## 5. The End of Dominance

In 2020, NewTech announced its own self-designed S1 chip. It was faster than Nanochip's processors and vastly superior in power efficiency. EastSemicon handled the manufacturing.

David shed tears watching the announcement video. It felt like watching thirty years of accumulated technological advantage collapse in an instant.

"Dad, are you okay?"

His son, now in college, asked with concern. David nodded, but his voice trembled.

"I'm fine. Dad is fine."

But he wasn't fine. Stock prices plummeted, and the company atmosphere grew gloomy. The colleagues he passed in the hallways wore expressions of defeat.

## 6. The Last Choice

In 2023, David turned fifty-eight. CoreChips announced a massive restructuring. This time, veteran engineers were the primary target.

"David, I'm sorry."

The HR representative said. He was offered an early retirement package. He could have refused, but he read the room. Even if he refused, he would be forced out next year anyway.

"How much time do I have?"

"Three months."

That evening, David left the company building early for the first time. He sat in his car in the parking lot for a long time, staring at nothing. Thirty years were coming to an end.

## 7. The Fading Light

On his last day of work, David arrived at the office early. He slowly walked through the still-empty laboratory. Photos of the Pentium series chips he had helped design hung on the wall.

"This was my life."

He spoke to himself and gently touched the photos. He remembered his younger self, the young engineer who dreamed of changing the world.

When lunchtime came, his colleagues held a small farewell party for him. Everyone laughed as they cut the cake, but their eyes were red. It wasn't just David who was leaving. An era of CoreChips was ending.

"Stay healthy, team lead."

A young engineer offered his hand for a handshake. His hands trembled. David gripped them firmly.

"You will rebuild this company. All of you."

But there was no certainty in those words.

## 8. What Remains

Spring 2026, David opened an old photo album in his home study. Photos from his early days at Nanochip in the 1990s, a party celebrating the launch of the Pentium processor, him smiling with colleagues.

"Dad, who is this?"

His grandson pointed to a photo. A young engineer full of hope.

"It's your dad. A very long time ago."

Outside the window, the sun was setting. Nanochip's stock price still hadn't recovered, and the news reported on the advances of PhillipsSemicon and Techforce Semicon. The giant that once held the throne was fading into a page of history.

David closed the photo album. His heart ached, but he had no regrets. In those days, they were truly changing the world. Though it didn't last forever, that light was definitely real.

On the study shelf, a commemorative plaque engraved with the Nanochip logo still rested. Dust had accumulated on it, but David didn't wipe it away. Even faded glory was his life.
