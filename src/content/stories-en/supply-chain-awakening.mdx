The Container's Route

---

At 2 a.m., the fluorescent lights in the port logistics center never went dark, not even at this hour. Ji-jun-ho sat in front of the monitor, staring at the screen with his coffee cup forgotten. His eyes stopped at a single number. Container number **GlobelciU4473891**.

On the vessel tracking system of the Israeli shipping company **Globelci**, that single box was blinking. It had been tied up at a port somewhere in the Middle East before starting to move four days ago. Ji-jun-ho had first received this container three months earlier. Back then, it was just a job. Twelve thousand pairs of sneakers completed at a Vietnamese factory. It was an order from a European sports brand. The goods had to leave Ho Chi Minh Harbor, pass through Singapore, and then be loaded onto a container ship operated by Globelci.

But the world did not move according to that plan.

---

The Red Sea route began to destabilize in late 2023. Attacks by Houthi rebels from Yemen had effectively crippled shipping lanes through the Suez Canal, and major shipping companies including Globelci announced they would go around the Cape of Good Hope. The voyage distance increased by approximately 6,000 nautical miles. In terms of time, that meant ten days; in terms of cost, it meant thousands of dollars added per container. The freight charges for the cargo that Ji-jun-ho managed nearly doubled around that time.

He drafted an email to communicate this fact to his client. The sentences were professional. Cold and concise. But even after sending the email, he stared at the screen for a long time. His hand on the keyboard did not move.

Rising freight charges also meant that somewhere, someone was working longer hours.

---

GlobelciU4473891 had just rounded Cape Town. It was now heading north after turning the southern tip of the African continent. On a map, it appeared to be an elegant curve, but on that line were 5-meter swells, sailors working in shifts, and people who had not seen land for weeks.

Ji-jun-ho looked up from the vessel tracking screen and gazed out the window. The cranes at Incheon Harbor stood under the night lights. There were days when they appeared beautiful to him. But tonight was different.

He opened a file. It was a report he had received from a trade magazine a few weeks earlier. The document quoted excerpts from Globelci's 2024 annual report and contained details about significant performance improvements due to the Red Sea detour. Rising freight charges had translated directly into profits. The numbers were clean. Investors were pleased.

He closed the report.

---

The global supply chain is a complex machine. When one gear turns, the other follows, and they are so entangled that it is impossible to tell which came first. When conflict blocks a shipping route, freight charges rise; when freight charges rise, somewhere a consumer buys more expensive sneakers, and somewhere a factory worker works overtime to meet the deadline.

Ji-jun-ho did not know how many hands had made the sneakers inside GlobelciU4473891. He did not know how much those hands had earned. The logistics system tracks only the box, not the weight of the labor contained within it.

He looked at the container number once more. The blinking dot was slowly moving northward. There were still twelve days until it reached Rotterdam Harbor.

---

It became 3 a.m. Ji-jun-ho stood up from his seat without logging out. On the monitor, GlobelciU4473891 continued moving. In the middle of the sea, even in the hours no one watched.

The container knew its destination. Of that much, he was certain. As for everything beyond that, Ji-jun-ho had yet to find any words.
