"Why can't I see this?"

Summer 2003, Pyeongtaek factory in Gyeonggi Province. Kim Cheol-su, a deputy manager in the quality inspection team at Hanmi Semiconductor, had been staring at the same chip through a microscope for three hours. His eyes were bloodshot, and his back felt like it might break.

Just then, Manager Park approached with a smile.

"Cheol-su, why are you looking with just your eyes? What does our company do?"

Kim suddenly realized. Hanmi Semiconductor was a company that manufactured semiconductor 'inspection equipment.' While he was performing visual inspections, the vision inspection system built by the company on the next line was sorting out hundreds of defects per second.

"...Could I borrow some equipment?"

"Ha, that's for delivery to customers! We have to trust our own eyes."

It was ironic. The company that manufactured the world's best inspection equipment was inspecting its own products by eye. It was like an employee at a water purifier company drinking tap water.

***

By the 2010s, things had changed. As circuit line widths narrowed to nanometer units, defects that weren't visible to the human eye had to be detected. The research institute developed an AI-based next-generation inspection system.

On the day of the prototype test.

"Defect detection rate: 99.97%!"

Cheers erupted. But Deputy Manager Lee of the quality team tilted his head.

"What about the 0.03%?"

Upon investigation, it was an ultra-fine crack that the inspection system had missed. It was so small that the algorithm had classified it as 'noise.'

"So human eyes are the best after all?"

"No, this wasn't visible to human eyes either. It was just... pure luck."

The conference room fell silent. Neither machine nor human was perfect.

***

The solution was simple.

"Why not use both machine and human together?"

Everyone nodded at the suggestion of the new researcher. A hybrid system where AI performed the first inspection and humans re-examined suspicious areas. The detection rate rose to 99.99%.

In 2020, Hanmi Semiconductor received the Sejin Electronics Partner Quality Award. At the awards ceremony, Executive Kim Cheol-su spoke.

"Seventeen years ago, I injured my back in front of a microscope. That's when I realized. It wasn't about machines replacing humans, but about working together."

Executive Park applauded and laughed from the audience.

"Maybe we should have let you borrow our equipment back then?"

"No, if we had, we wouldn't have learned this lesson!"

The masters of semiconductor inspection continue their 'doubt'—the thing only humans can do—beside the machine even today. Because they know that the best inspection comes not from the machine or the human alone, but from the collaboration of both.

Hanmi Semiconductor asks itself every day.

"Can you see this?"

Whether they can see it or not, they will find it.
