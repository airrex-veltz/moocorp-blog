At 2 a.m., Jae-hyuk's phone rang.

The bedroom ceiling turned blue. Before checking the screen, he knew the caller. Only one person called at this hour.

"Director." Sales Manager Min-su's voice trembled. "An order came through. Huge."

Jae-hyuk sat up. His wife stirred but didn't wake.

"How much?"

"Six months of inventory. All at once."

When he heard the number, sleep evaporated entirely. Gwang-jin Materials would need half a year to earn what this single buyer offered upfront. The condition was one: raise the unit price three times.

---

Gwang-jin Materials was a specialty semiconductor materials firm based in Anseong, Gyeonggi. 240 employees. Founded twelve years ago. Core products: etching process fluorine compounds and ultra-high-purity cleaning solutions. An unfamiliar name. By the time purified chips entered smartphones and automotive modules, only supply-chain managers knew Gwang-jin existed.

That invisibility had been Jae-hyuk's pride for twelve years. Essential but unnoticed. A cog meshing perfectly in place.

That place suddenly felt too small.

---

Signals emerged two years prior.

When war erupted in Eastern Europe, the semiconductor specialty gas market flipped. That region was a primary neon gas supplier for lithography. Global supplies suddenly uncertain overnight. Spot neon prices quintupled—some reports said sixfold. Jae-hyuk saw those numbers in news but didn't feel them. Gwang-jin wasn't a gas supplier.

But the cascade never stopped.

When gas suppliers raised prices, materials suppliers followed. When materials suppliers raised, Gwang-jin's raw costs climbed. Meanwhile, auto and consumer makers stockpiled. Foundries begged for order visibility. Orders piled up, canceled, then bigger orders arrived. Two years of chaos continued.

Jae-hyuk never raised prices.

The reason was simple: old customers.

---

**Dae-un Semiconductor** was Gwang-jin's first buyer.

In startup days, when Jae-hyuk couldn't meet a delivery deadline, he didn't negotiate down. He stayed up nights with Dae-un's process engineers, hunting defect rates. Their procurement head once covered Gwang-jin's research budget from his own budget. That was back when Jae-hyuk couldn't sleep for worry.

Now Dae-un represented 40 percent of Gwang-jin's revenue.

And tonight's new buyer—**Ace Fab**—was a US-based fabless design house, expanding rapidly in Asia. The ask was clear: triple unit price, exclusive supply. Once signed, no volume to allocate elsewhere.

Min-su kept talking: "Director, we can't justify costs without this. Raw materials jumped 30 percent in two years. Our margins are halved. No capex budget without this deal. Next year we can't guarantee quality."

Jae-hyuk said nothing.

Min-su paused, then continued. "I understand Dae-un is tough. But they were planning supply diversification anyway. We're not the only ones waiting. This is our moment—or our moment dies."

---

Next morning, alone in the conference room.

Two papers on the table. Ace Fab's contract draft. Dae-un's existing annual supply agreement. The agreement had a price adjustment clause. Legally non-binding. Just trust.

He looked out at the factory yard. Two cargo trucks rolling in for morning shipment. He knew roughly where their materials went. Hospital CT machines. Home refrigerators. Somewhere data centers. Invisible things.

If he tripled the price, Dae-un's line wouldn't survive.

If he rejected Ace Fab, Gwang-jin's investment disappeared. His 240 employees depended on this. The logistics partner whose trucks just rolled in depended on this.

---

3 p.m. Min-su knocked.

"Ace Fab called. Response by tomorrow morning."

Jae-hyuk nodded. Min-su hesitated at the door.

"Director... can I speak frankly?"

"Go ahead."

"I don't know either. The numbers say yes. But I keep thinking... I was subcontractor before joining Gwang-jin. I worked small parts supply. I watched suppliers cut unit prices when buyers pushed. I survived. And now..." He trailed off. "I don't know what's different."

Jae-hyuk had no answer.

Min-su left. The room filled with silence again.

---

6 p.m. A call came.

"Want to have lunch tomorrow?" It was Park Tae-jun from Dae-un.

"Sure."

Tomorrow would bring an answer. The board backed Ace Fab—necessary, he'd said. Quality needs investment now. Dae-un needed stronger supply anyway. But Jae-hyuk's face when remembering that purchasing chief who'd covered research costs... that face, the board never erased.

If I sign Ace Fab, Dae-un dies.

But if I reject Ace Fab, Gwang-jin dies. 240 employees. The trucks rolling tomorrow. That logistics company. All depend on this signature.

---

Late evening, he looked at his phone.

The address book opened. His thumb froze. He didn't know where it would land.

Deadline: dawn.
