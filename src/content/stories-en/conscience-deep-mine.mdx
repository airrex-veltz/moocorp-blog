Kang Min-jun paused before opening the file.

Seoul, Mapo-gu office, 2 a.m. The other employees had left long ago. One fluorescent light flickered and went out. Only the laptop screen lit his face. The email attachment's name was simple.

`supply-chain-audit_draft_mark-for-deletion.pdf`

---

Kang Min-jun had been at Daejisomae for seven years. He was a senior engineer dedicated entirely to battery raw materials, particularly cobalt procurement. He had chosen this work because he wanted to be at the center of the electric vehicle revolution. Clean energy. A better world. Those words had not felt light at first.

He opened the file.

As he scrolled down, his fingers grew slower. The document covered three subcontracted mines in the Democratic Republic of Congo, one of Daejisomae's cobalt suppliers. Charts, satellite photos, excerpts from interviews recorded by local NGOs. One paragraph had a line drawn under it—by the audit team leader's hand, it seemed.

*Children between twelve and fifteen years old have been found inside the tunnels. Without equipment. With their bare hands. In shafts with no ventilation.*

Kang Min-jun pulled his eyes from the screen. The Han River outside the window lay like a black stripe.

More than 70 percent of the world's cobalt production came from Congo. That was common knowledge in the industry. And the fact that documents like this existed behind that knowledge—he had, in truth, known it half-consciously. But knowing was different from confronting it in a file. When you knew, there was still a choice. Once you opened the file, that choice disappeared.

The email sender was Lee Jae-won, the audit team leader. The recipient was Kang Min-jun alone. The message was brief.

*Min-jun, I'm sending this only to you. I thought you should know before the executive briefing next week. I've already decided. What about you?*

Lee Jae-won did not come to work the next day. HR said he was taking a long leave "for health reasons."

---

Kang Min-jun moved through the week normally. He attended meetings, wrote reports, answered his junior colleagues' questions. On the surface, nothing had changed.

Inside, everything was different.

He reread that file every night. The more he read, the more concrete the details became. The height of the children. The width of the tunnels. The hours of a daily shift. And the unit price of the contract that Daejisomae had made with those mines. The unit price was low. Impossibly low. That lowness was the structure that made all of this possible.

The night before the executive briefing, he sent a message to the executive.

*I have something to tell you regarding the supply chain audit. Could you spare a moment before tomorrow's briefing?*

The reply came in five minutes.

*Min-jun, I already know about it. We're coordinating with the legal team. You don't need to worry about it.*

Kang Min-jun put his phone down. *I already know.* The weight of those words sank slowly. Knowing it but not writing it in the report. Knowing it and maintaining the contract.

On the last page of the file Lee Jae-won had sent, there was a single footnote. *The original of this document is kept in three places.* Below it were three paths. One was the internal server at Daejisomae. One was the audit team leader's personal drive. One was a third path that Kang Min-jun stared at for a long time before he understood—it had been left blank.

Lee Jae-won had left that space empty and departed.

---

The executive briefing started at 10 a.m.

Kang Min-jun stood before the conference room door, his hand on the handle. Voices came from inside. The sound of PowerPoint slides advancing. Someone laughed. The quarter's results were good.

The handle was cold.

He had two things. Time to pass the file outside before the executive briefing ended. And the choice not to use that time.

Seven years of career were on one side. Next month's tuition for his child's school admission was there. The faces of four team members were there.

On the other side were the width of the tunnels and the height of the children.

The door did not open. Kang Min-jun stood there. At the end of the hallway, he heard the elevator doors close. Someone was coming up. The time given to him was now just minutes.

He took out his phone.

The screen lit up. The saved contacts list appeared. Where the scrolling stopped, there was one name. Whether to press that name or not, Kang Min-jun had not yet decided. The sound of the elevator stopping came. The doors opened.
