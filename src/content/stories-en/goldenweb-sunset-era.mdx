Choi Young-soo still remembered the day in spring 1999 when he founded Goldenweb. A semi-basement office in Guro-dong, Seoul. Empty instant noodle cups scattered across the floor. And in that moment when the portal's homepage first appeared on his monitor, he had silently cried out, "This is it."

Twenty-five years had passed. Now Choi Young-soo stood before the glass windows of the company's 32nd-floor office. The Han River flowed below. Below that, evening commuters moved like ants on the roads.

A tablet on his desk displayed the quarterly report.

Monthly unique visitors. 9.2% compared to the 2002 peak.

2002. The era when Korea boasted the fastest broadband internet penetration rate in the world. Back then, tens of millions of people visited Goldenweb's homepage each day. They checked the weather, read the news, and sent emails. Choi Young-soo's company had been the gateway to the Korean internet of that age.

That gateway was now slowly closing, without a sound.

As AI assistant transitions accelerated earlier this year, portal search queries had dropped 23 percent compared to the previous quarter. People no longer typed keywords into search boxes. They asked by voice and received answers in chat windows. The index database, advertising platform, and content ecosystem that Goldenweb had built over 25 years—all of it was slowly losing weight in a world where AI answered with a single sentence.

Two months ago, the board had used the word "service consolidation" for the first time. Choi Young-soo had said nothing at that meeting. He looked at each director's face in turn, then at the window outside. That conference room window also looked out onto the Han River.

---

This month, an internal notice went up. The schedules for ending Mail and Cafe services. Search to be shut down in the first half of next year.

Choi Young-soo had written the draft of that notice himself. The legal team revised it and the PR team polished it, but the first sentence remained exactly as he had written it.

"Goldenweb has been with you for 25 years."

Only later did he realize that his hand had not shaken when writing that sentence. He was surprised. Twenty-five years compressed into one sentence, and his hand had not shaken.

It was not resignation. Choi Young-soo knew what resignation felt like. This was different. It was something quieter.

---

After work, he remained alone in the office. Without turning off the screen, he opened the internal archive containing the evolution of Goldenweb's homepage from 1999 to now.

The first screen. White background. Category text links packed densely. The font looked crude by today's standards. Yet that crude screen had been someone's first encounter with the internet.

As he flipped through the screens one page at a time, he stopped. A World Cup special page from 2002. Red banner. The title "South Korea Advances to the Semifinals." That day, Goldenweb's server had crashed twice from traffic overload. Choi Young-soo and his team had worked through the night at the office. Someone had ordered chicken, and at three in the morning when the server came back online, they had all clapped like fools.

The way AI was changing the world had nothing to do with such technical failures. No one worked through the night. The servers did not crash. People simply walked away to other places. Without a sound.

Choi Young-soo stared at that red screen for a long time.

Outside the window, the Han River still flowed.

---

He left his seat without closing the screen. The hallway lights were off, and the office was lit only by the glow of a single monitor. The 2002 World Cup special page of Goldenweb shone alone in the empty office.

Tomorrow morning, someone would come in and find that screen. And without saying a word, they would close it.

Choi Young-soo did not yet know what kind of ending this was.
