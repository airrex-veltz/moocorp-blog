The sky over Silicon Valley was ash-gray. In the fall of 2013, Lee Jun-seok's steps felt heavy as he walked through the hallways of TechNewWorld headquarters. The rain visible through the window seemed to symbolize the company's future.

"We're already finished." The voices of engineers drifted down the hallway. TechPhone had failed, the cloud market had been ceded to SkyStore, and this company, once an icon of innovation, was now called a "dinosaur of the old era."

Lee Jun-seok stopped before an old photograph taken by Lee Gun-ho and Kim Seok-ho together. A small garage office in 1975. The eyes of the two young men burned with passion to change the world. "A computer on every desk, in every home." Their dream had become reality. But when did it happen? When did that passion transform into inertia?

In the CEO candidate interview, the board asked, "How will you revive TechNewWorld?" Lee Jun-seok answered quietly, "We need to love again what we loved at first. Developers. Open source. And solving people's problems."

In February 2014, as the third CEO, he wrote in his first company-wide email: "Our mission is to help every person and organization on the planet achieve more." It was a simple-seeming sentence, but it contained fundamental change.

First, he embraced Linux, the company's 20-year adversary. "TechNewWorld loves Linux." When these words appeared on the screen at the developer conference, the audience was shocked. But Lee Jun-seok smiled. Future collaboration mattered more than past pride.

CloudServices grew by actively adopting open-source technology. When CodeHub was acquired for $7.5 billion, the developer community worried. But TechNewWorld guaranteed CodeHub's independence and even offered free private repositories. Trust became confidence.

In the fall of 2019, Lee Jun-seok walked that hallway again. This time, his steps were light. Silicon Valley still saw rain outside the windows, but now the sound of that rain seemed like the rhythm of growth. CloudServices had risen to the world's second-largest cloud provider, and the market capitalization exceeded $1 trillion.

In early 2023, at an event announcing a partnership with FutureAI, Lee Jun-seok said, "AI is the most important technological revolution of our generation. And we are at the center of this journey." Amid the audience's cheers, he recalled again the eyes of those young men from 1975.

On the way back to Silicon Valley, Lee Jun-seok reflected. Love does not change. Only its expression does. TechNewWorld still dreamed of changing the world. And this time, not alone, but with everyone.

A newly hung phrase caught his eye in the lobby: "Empower every person and organization on the planet to achieve more." He smiled. True love is not about changing the other person, but about growing together.

Stars appeared in the night sky. The lights of Silicon Valley flickered on one by one, as if announcing a new beginning. TechNewWorld had fallen in love again. With the world, with technology, and with the future.
