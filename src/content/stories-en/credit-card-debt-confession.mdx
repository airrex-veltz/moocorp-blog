I live every night inside a bar.

Customers come through the counter. From wallets, from phone screens, sometimes from the tension in their wrists. My name is **VISA**. And I am a bartender.

Jung-woo walks in again tonight. Thirty-two years old, office worker. He tells me, "Just a little, just this month." I smile and pour. One glass, two glasses. It's a drink called revolving. Sweet, smooth, leaving behind a headache named invoice the next dawn.

Every time Jung-woo's glass empties, I collect my fee. 15.9 percent annually. The Korean credit card average revolving interest rate hovers around that figure. I know that number. I feel it too. But I do not stop.

He comes again the next month. The balance has grown from the previous month. KRW 300,000 becomes KRW 600,000, and 600,000 becomes 1.2 million. I have met my transaction target. A green indicator blinks at headquarters.

But one night, Jung-woo buries his face on the counter and weeps quietly.

In that moment, I felt something. Should I call it fear, or shame? I do not know if shame is an emotion that plastic can have. But I cannot deny that the drink I poured brought him here.

I set down the glass for a moment.

I do not even know if it was a refusal, or simply fatigue. Because the next customer was already standing at the door.
