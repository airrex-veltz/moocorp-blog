Ji-eun swiped the screen mindlessly that day too.

The account that Ieum showed her belonged to an unfamiliar man. A single photo. A back shot of someone drinking coffee by a café window on an overcast day. The follow button gleamed.

*Don't press something like this.*

That was her first resolve. Liking someone the algorithm showed her felt like boarding a fate written by a machine. Ji-eun closed the screen.

Four days later, that account appeared again. This time at the same café, the same seat. It was that shop where she had tagged a photo last week.

*It's just a coincidence.*

Ji-eun closed the screen again.

A week passed. In a post from that man, the title of a book appeared—the same one Ji-eun had read and posted about finishing four days ago.

Ieum knew. All of Ji-eun's reading history, her location, her café preferences. According to researchers, platforms like these read over two thousand signals from a single user's day of activity. Time spent, scrolling speed, where they pause. That Ji-eun lingered 1.3 seconds longer on that account's photo.

And it probably recommended Ji-eun to him as well.

Ji-eun didn't leave a comment. Instead, she opened the settings to delete the platform.

Then a notification appeared.

— Do-hyeon saved your post.

Her finger stopped. *Do-hyeon.* A single name caught somewhere in her chest.

Ji-eun closed the settings.

---

Do-hyeon sent the message first.

"I saw you're reading the same book. The last sentence really grabbed me."

Ji-eun held out for three hours without responding. But that last sentence had been circling in her head for three days too. *The gaze of another completes me.* Finally, Ji-eun typed first.

"Me too."

That was all. But those three letters opened a door.

---

Two months later, they met for the first time at that café. By the window seat, an overcast afternoon. The scene exactly as it had appeared in the photo.

Do-hyeon smiled and said,

"Actually, Ieum recommended your account to me three times. I just scrolled past the first time."

Ji-eun smiled too. But at the end of that smile, a small melancholy lingered.

*Is it really our choice that we're sitting here?*

The algorithm already knew. Ji-eun's taste, Do-hyeon's pattern, even the temperature of the conversation they would have. And Ji-eun, though frightened by this, no longer resisted.

Rain began to fall outside the window. Do-hyeon spoke quietly.

"But we came, didn't we."

Ji-eun held her coffee cup in both hands. It was warm. Whether it was a choice or surrender, Ji-eun decided not to ask.
