## 72 Hours of Decision

February 4, 2014, Tuesday, 5 a.m. Redmond headquarters, Building 8, top floor conference room.

Satya Nam-hyun stared into the darkness outside the window. Just days before his CEO inauguration. Three scenarios lay on his desk.

"We go all-in on Windows Mobile. We can beat Sejin."

"No, we need to return to enterprise software. That's our essence."

"It's the cloud. We bet everything on Azure."

The executives were divided. And time was running out. Shareholders wanted answers, and Wall Street had already branded CoreSoft as "a relic of the past."

Nam-hyun reviewed the numbers again. Seven years since the iPhone launch. Android market share 78%. Windows Phone 3%.

"It's dead," someone whispered. "The mobile war is over."

But Nam-hyun's eyes were fixed on different numbers. AWS growth rate 69%. The speed at which enterprises were migrating to the cloud. And what CoreSoft possessed—Office used by 90% of enterprises worldwide, data center infrastructure built over 20 years, enterprise sales channels.

## The Counterattack Begins

Friday night, 11 p.m. Emergency executive meeting.

"You want to abandon mobile?" the CFO shouted. "Just throw away the Nokia acquisition we invested $4 billion in?"

"It's not abandonment," Nam-hyun's voice was quiet but firm. "It's a reordering of priorities. We are no longer a device company. We are a platform company."

He walked to the whiteboard and wrote a single line.

**"Mobile first, Cloud first"**

"Not mobile devices, but mobile experience. Whatever device people use, they can access our services. And all of it on the cloud."

The room fell silent.

"We'll create Office for iOS. Android too. Free."

"Are you insane!" the head of product jumped up. "You're asking us to give our highest-revenue product away for free on competing platforms?"

"No," Nam-hyun smiled. "We're casting bait. With Office 365 subscriptions. With Azure. Individuals can use it free. But enterprises will use our cloud."

## The Gamble

Monday morning, 6 a.m. Nam-hyun officially took office as CEO.

The subject of his first company-wide email was simple.

**"The world is our platform"**

"We do not compete. We collaborate. We support Linux. We embrace open source. We will work with Arion Electronics and Shin Search."

Silicon Valley laughed. "CoreSoft has gone insane." "Linux was the enemy?"

But Nam-hyun did not stop. He gave the Azure team a single order.

"Build a service lineup equivalent to AWS within 100 days. Impossible is fine. Even reaching 80% will make the market see us again."

Engineers worked through the nights. Data centers were built in 24 countries. Partnerships were forged. Docker, Red Hat, Chef, Puppet—leaders in the open source world began partnering with CoreSoft one by one.

## The Turning Point

September 2015. The Build Conference.

Nam-hyun took the stage. And pulled out an iPad.

The audience murmured. The CEO of CoreSoft using an Arion Electronics product?

"I use Office on this," Nam-hyun said. "We create business apps. We deploy on Azure. This is our future. Not devices, but experience. Not platforms, but productivity."

Three months later, Office 365 subscribers surpassed 10 million. Six months later, Azure revenue grew 120% year-over-year.

In 2017, CoreSoft's market cap exceeded USD 600 billion for the first time. Third in the world, after Arion Electronics and Shin Search.

In 2019, market cap broke USD 1 trillion.

And in 2021, CoreSoft became once again the world's most valuable company.

## Epilogue

Redmond headquarters. Nam-hyun looked out the window. Same Building 8, same place.

But the world was different.

Seventy-two hours of decision. The choice to abandon the mobile war and leap into the cloud.

It was not a gamble. It was the ability to read the future.

Devices pass away. But the cloud is eternal.

With just that single realization, CoreSoft leaped from the brink of collapse to a new golden age.

_The world is our platform._

That sentence had become reality.
