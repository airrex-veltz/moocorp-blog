Christmas Eve 2008. Park Jun-ho's hands trembled as he stared at his bank account. Future Motors faced bankruptcy within days. The Desert Runner's transmission defects had angered customers, and the financial crisis had sent investors fleeing. All he had left was forty million dollars from selling PaymentSystem.

"Should I pour it into Spaceship? Or Future Motors?"

Both companies were dying. If he could save just one? His friends said: "Give up on Future Motors. Electric cars are impossible. Even the big automakers failed."

But Park Jun-ho chose Future Motors. Why? Love. A love for the impossible dream of electric cars.

"Everyone says you can't. But I believe in you." He whispered this while falling asleep on the factory floor.

In 2012, Premium S debuted. Auto critics were shocked. "This is... an electric car?" Car of the Year. Zero-to-100 in 4.2 seconds. Range of 426 kilometers. They had done what was called "impossible."

But love was not easy. In 2013, three Premium S vehicles caught fire in succession. Stock price plummeted 15%. The media descended. "Future Motors' end."

Park Jun-ho spoke at a press conference: "Has anyone never been hurt by love? We will become stronger."

He upgraded the battery protection shield. For free. On every vehicle.

In 2017, "production hell" began. Half a million Premium 3 reservations. But weekly production was only in the hundreds. Park Jun-ho lived at the factory. He slept on couches, skipped showers, and talked to customers all night on SocialMedia.

"Only weeks until bankruptcy," he confessed later. "Single digits."

People around him asked: "Why go this far? You already have enough money."

He replied: "Love is not money. It is leaving behind a breathable planet for the next generation."

In 2018, Park Jun-ho collapsed. Public admission of "imminent bankruptcy," fraud charges from the Financial Services Commission, the SocialMedia incident over "funding secured"—he lost his chairmanship. On a radio talk show, he laughed. But his eyes were weeping.

"Love drives you mad," he muttered.

In 2020, the world stopped. A pandemic. But Future Motors survived. The Shanghai factory came online, the stock entered global indices, and the share price surged five-fold. Market cap: one trillion dollars. Park Jun-ho became the world's richest person.

But in 2022, he bought SocialMedia for forty-four billion dollars. By selling Future Motors stock. The share price plummeted 65%. Fans wailed in betrayal.

"You said you loved Future Motors! Why are you abandoning it?"

Park Jun-ho did not answer. Perhaps he already knew. That love sometimes destroys you.

May 2026: Future Motors stock at one hundred eighty dollars. Half its peak. Autonomous vehicles had disappointed, the product line had aged, and East Motors surpassed it in sales.

But Park Jun-ho still visits the factory. Still texts engineers at three in the morning. Still dreams of the impossible.

"Love is not rational," he said in an interview. "But without love, nothing can become great."

Future Motors proved electric cars possible. That which was called "impossible." As if proving that love is possible too.
