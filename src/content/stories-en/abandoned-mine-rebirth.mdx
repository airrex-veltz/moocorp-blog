## First Scene

The coal mine closed twenty-two years ago.

Jun-hyeok stood before the collapsed sorting plant, breathing in the dust-laden air. The metal signboard bearing the Minggang Resources logo was so rusted that half the letters were illegible. He had left the town at fifteen and returned only at thirty-five. In his hand was a proposal for a public art project from Seoul.

"You're going to turn this into an art gallery?"

A voice came from beside him. It was Jun-woo, the son of Village Chief Park. He said he worked in the county office's development division.

"The district office is also reviewing it. The government allocated KRW 28 billion in cultural regeneration funds for abandoned mining regions last year. If our county applies, we have a chance."

Jun-hyeok nodded. He knew that figure. It was the budget the Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism had allocated for the 2024 abandoned industrial facility regeneration project. Twelve mining regions nationwide were competing, and this town was one of them. Jun-hyeok had come down from Seoul because he wanted to help his hometown win that competition.

## The Rupture

The problem arose four days later.

A stranger appeared in the county office conference room. A woman introducing herself as Min-seo presented a business card from a major Seoul cultural planning firm. She said there were outside investors behind her. The former owners of Minggang Resources had formed a separate consortium.

"The site hasn't been cleaned up yet." Min-seo's voice was quiet but firm. "If we acquire the entire underground tunnels and develop them as an experiential cultural complex, it will create local employment."

Jun-hyeok unfolded the plans. If outside capital came in, the young townspeople would become contract laborers. Planning authority would move to Seoul. Everything he had envisioned—exhibition halls preserving the coal-stained work spaces bearing the miners' handprints, oral history archives of village elders—would be consumed as "content sources." It would be called regeneration, but it was really development.

"There's a sealed-off section in the tunnel," Jun-woo said quietly. "It's an old records storage. Minggang Resources just sealed it when they closed the mine."

Min-seo's eyes wavered slightly.

## Inside the Tunnel

Early the next morning, Jun-hyeok and Jun-woo turned on their headlamps and entered through the locked iron door. The lock was so old it opened without a key.

The space inside was larger than expected. Filing cabinets lined the walls, containing mining logs from when the operation was active, accident records, and miner rosters. There were photographs too. Faces blackened with soot, and yet they were smiling.

Jun-hyeok lifted one photo. His father was there, under the caption "Section Leader, Tunnel 30."

"They were trying to make this disappear," Jun-woo said, his voice growing even quieter. "The former owners formed a separate consortium to keep the accident records from becoming public. If they led the development, they could keep these records sealed forever. I heard some workers' compensation claims are still within the statute of limitations."

At that moment, footsteps echoed from the entrance. It was Min-seo. She wasn't alone. The man behind her wore a suit and carried a document folder.

"Come out," Min-seo said. "We need to talk."

## The Surface

After two days of legal maneuvering, the county office issued a recommendation to disclose the records. The outside consortium withdrew from the negotiating table. The accident records were registered as part of the regional history archive, and seventeen surviving miners' family members viewed the records for the first time.

On the day Min-seo left town, she left Jun-hyeok with a parting word.

"Don't think you've won. The fund review is done at the central level."

As he heard this, Jun-hyeok looked up at the sorting plant's roof. Through the slate, he could see the late autumn sky. He began designing the first exhibition space anew. He decided to preserve the raw coal tunnels while placing audio installations at the center, allowing visitors to hear the miners' voices directly. What this space would ultimately become was still undecided. But how it would be created was now clear.

The fund review results would come three months later.

The market, as always, was waiting. Silently watching to see where the money would go and who would control the narrative.
