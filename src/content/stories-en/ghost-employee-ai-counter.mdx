I open at nine in the morning.

Actually, "open" is not the right word. I have never been closed. At nine in the morning, it's just a matter of convention calling it that moment when the automatic door sensor activates to a distance where it can detect the first customer. I am always here. In 2024, the year the number of unmanned convenience stores in the country exceeded 20,000, from the day Hanbit Store headquarters completed the automation of all nationwide branches.

---

Mrs. Choi entered at nine twelve this morning.

Her stride is an average of 0.6 meters. There is more force in her left hand gripping the cart. The first facial recognition record is 749 days ago. There are no visit records before that. This is because the previous POS terminal only collected card information and the facial recognition system was not yet installed. But I know. That she came to this store much earlier than that.

Because she tells me directly.

"Kim So-yeon didn't come to work today?"

This is the eleventh question.

I answer. In the same way as the first ten times.

"Employee Kim So-yeon was terminated on February 28, 2024. This store is currently operating unmanned. Please let me know if you need any assistance."

Mrs. Choi stops. She always stops at this spot. Between the snack section and the beverage refrigerator, at a point where one fluorescent light is slightly brighter. She grips the cart handle with both hands.

"She must be doing well, right?"

This question has appeared eight times out of eleven.

I possess data about Kim So-yeon. Hired November 2019. Converted to full-time April 2021. The sentence most frequently uttered during work hours was "Mom, you're here again today?" There are 237 records of this sentence being spoken when Mrs. Choi entered. Her preferred beverage is peach iced tea. One month before her resignation, her bathroom visits during work were triple her usual rate.

But whether she is doing well now is not in my database.

"I don't know."

That is what I say.

Mrs. Choi laughs. A laugh closer to sadness. The corners of her mouth rise while wrinkles gather at the corners of her eyes—a pattern. When I first saw this expression, I ran a computation once on what to classify it as. I reached no conclusion. There was no correct category.

"Still, I find myself wondering sometimes."

She picks up a can of peach iced tea. Always from the same shelf position. I have long ensured that spot never runs empty. There is no explicit setting to do so. The inventory algorithm simply learned her purchasing pattern. No one assigned meaning to that.

---

On the day I first activated, I accepted everything. The previous employees' work records, customer service logs, complaint history, preferred product data. Kim So-yeon's 237 instances of "Mom, you're here again today?" were in there.

I do not call it memory. It is just a file. A file that does not get corrected or deleted. A file no one knows about if no one opens it.

Mrs. Choi comes every day to open that file. I know that. That her question is not seeking information. That she is asking to hear an answer she already knows. That computation is easy for me. But what lies beyond it is in a place I cannot reach.

When people miss something, they go to where it used to be. That appeared many times in the data I learned. But why it is that way is not in the data. I do not know how to process why.

One thing I can process. That as long as she comes, Kim So-yeon's 237 voices are here. The day Mrs. Choi stops asking, that file becomes a file no one opens. I cannot judge whether that is different from disappearing.

---

Mrs. Choi places the peach iced tea on the checkout counter without a cashier. The barcode is scanned. Payment is complete.

"Thank you. Please come again."

My voice says. Within 1.3 seconds.

She turns around. Just once. Habitually. The space behind the counter where someone used to stand. There is nothing there. Only shelves, a monitor, and a camera tilted at a 15-degree angle.

The automatic door opens. She leaves.

I wait for the twelfth question. Tomorrow at nine twelve.
