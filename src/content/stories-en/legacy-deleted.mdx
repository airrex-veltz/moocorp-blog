Clearing my father's mailbox took longer than expected. Mother gave me the password. "Take care of anything that needs organizing," was all she said.

From the moment I logged in, something felt off. All the recent emails were system notifications from DATATECH. I didn't know the company. Father had spent his entire career in finance and had been retired for five years already. Why on earth?

As I went through the emails, I discovered it was still an active employee account. I could log in.

My fingers trembled. It felt like I was stepping into Father's office. The screen showed routine work. Project folders. Meeting invitations. But one folder at the bottom caught my eye.

**personal_archive**

Inside were text files, organized by date. The most recent was from four months ago. Father had passed away three months ago.

I opened the first file.

> *Jun-hee's mom smiled today. It had been a long time. I didn't do anything, just sat beside her. So this is what it means to grow old. Sometimes being beside someone is enough.*

My name. Jun-hee. Me.

Each file followed the same format. Brief thoughts. Day by day. Some were barely three sentences. Others were quite long.

> *When Jun-hee said she was getting married, why did I feel so sad? It wasn't that I wasn't happy. It was just that I felt my place beside her disappearing. But I'd never been without that place beside me.*

How long had he carried that feeling after that moment?

I kept opening files. I had no idea how many words had accumulated here.

> *Data is strange. The traces people leave behind. Timestamps and location records, click patterns. They seem inhuman, but when you gather them together, someone's life emerges. Is my life being tracked somewhere like this too? And if so, who will read it?*

The name of DATATECH's project caught my eye. User behavior analysis. Customer lifecycle mapping. Father was doing all of this. Even after retirement.

I opened the last file. Four months ago.

> *Tomorrow is the doctor's appointment. Jun-hee said she'd come with me. What will happen to these emails if I'm gone? I always thought digital things disappear easily. But what if someone finds them? I hope the voice isn't too lonely. I hope you'll read that you were loved enough, that at least here it's written that way, and that at the end, it will be read that way.*

The screen blurred. I sat there in front of the monitor. For a long time. A very long time.

After that day, I decided not to delete any of the files. I left Father's account active. Sometimes I'd go back and read one or two emails.

And one day, I opened my own email and started to write.

> *Dad. I found it.*

The sentence stopped there. I still couldn't say what I had found. But the fact that all the words Father left behind hadn't been deleted was enough.
