November 1998, Kia Motor headquarters. Executive Director Park Min-su gazed out at the ash-gray Han River below.

"We need to file for court-supervised receivership." The finance manager's voice sounded distant.

Kia Motor, which had begun as Gyeongseong Precision in 1944 and anchored Korea's automotive industry, was collapsing. The bright smiles in the photo on his desk from 1987, when Pride was launched, felt distant.

"Chairman Jung of Raon Mobility has requested a meeting."

A competitor. Yet now, their last hope.

---

Their first meeting took place in a quiet Korean restaurant. Chairman Jung was unhurried.

"Kia is a good company. A company I respect."

Min-su was surprised. These were not words he'd expected to hear from a competitor.

"Times are difficult now. But Kia's technology and spirit live on." Chairman Jung spoke slowly. "I want to preserve that together."

"Are you talking about an acquisition?"

"I'm talking about a partnership."

That night, Min-su couldn't sleep. His pride was wounded, but his heart felt warm. Someone still called Kia a "good company."

---

December 31, 1998. Signatures were exchanged on the contract. The Raon Mobility consortium's acquisition of Kia was complete.

Alone in the empty conference room, Min-su was swept up in conflicting emotions. Defeat or salvation? Snow began to fall outside the window.

---

Five years later, spring 2004.

"Executive Director! Sportage hit number one in monthly sales!" A young employee smiled brightly.

"The Raon-Kia Motor Group has risen to fifth place in the world."

The once-unfamiliar name "Raon-Kia" now felt natural.

That evening, a regular meeting with Chairman Jung. "Kia is growing well."

"Thanks to Raon Mobility."

"No. Kia was strong from the start. We simply walked the path together."

The Han River sparkled beneath the spring sun. Not the ash-gray of 1998, but a living river.

---

2023, new employee orientation.

Vice Chairman Min-su spoke. "Kia began in 1944, but the real Kia was reborn in 1998."

"Are you talking about the merger with Raon Mobility?"

"It wasn't a merger. It was a meeting." He displayed the photograph of the 1998 December handshake. "We met in crisis, endured together, and grew strong slowly."

"Business is cold, they say. But sometimes sincerity connects. Kia and Raon Mobility proved that."

That evening, gazing at Seoul's night skyline, Min-su smiled. Countless cars on the roads. Half of them were Kia and Raon.

Some loves don't begin brilliantly. They meet in crisis, endure together, and deepen. That's what Kia and Raon Mobility's 25 years have been.

That love continues even now.
