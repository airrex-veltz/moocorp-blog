The old mailbox had been there for a long time.

Min-ju discovered it one afternoon while preparing to move. A wooden box buried in a corner of the house. The lid had rusty hinges and dust from the years had settled evenly across its surface. There was a keyhole but no key. As soon as she lifted the lid, a creaking sound emerged and with it came the smell of old paper from inside.

They were letters. Multiple of them. Each envelope had the recipient's name written on it, but there was not a single stamp.

She took out the first letter.

*If you are reading this, I have surely lost the courage to voice those words.*

Min-ju's hand stopped. The date was spring of 1987.

The second letter.

*I could not send it today either. I saw that person pass by through the window, but I could only watch them disappear. The words come out on paper, but why do they freeze outside my lips?*

Third. Fourth. The letters were all in the same person's handwriting and all written to the same person. Yet not a single one had been sent.

Min-ju read the letters for over an hour. At some point, she forgot that the person who wrote them had no name. It was just one voice. A voice of unspoken things that had crossed the years.

The final letter had only a date written on it. Summer of 1994.

The content was a single line.

*I will stop sending them now. The letters.*

Min-ju gazed at that sentence for a long time. She could not tell whether 'sending' referred to the letters or something else.

She closed the box. But she did not discard it.

The sun descended. The mailbox was carried along to the new house, and the letters inside it remained quietly, still having reached no one.
