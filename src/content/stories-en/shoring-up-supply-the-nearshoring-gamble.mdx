Distance had long felt wronged by its name. People always measured it in kilometers, and those kilometers had always been the share of its friend, Transportation Cost.

It began in Hangyeol Electronics' procurement conference room. Hangyeol Electronics, which had been importing parts from distant factories across the sea, had experienced delayed shipments and port blockages for years. Each time, Transportation Cost would fold its arms and say, "Those people are trying to raise me again."

"Let's move closer," the procurement manager said. "With the town just below the border, it's a four-day truck drive. Shorter distance means lower costs."

Distance felt grateful and straightened its shoulders. For the first time, someone had said they would reduce it. Transportation Cost also stepped back and conceded. "That's true. I can definitely see savings."

But in the corner of the conference room, someone quietly raised their hand. It was Trust. This friend went unnamed in the ledgers, so no one usually called on it.

"I need to move too. But I have a lot of baggage."

The new town's factory was close but unfamiliar. Communication worked halfway—no better, no worse. It took three months to explain quality standards, and the fact that "delivery date" was being interpreted as something like "hoped-for schedule" only became clear after the first defective products arrived. Labor costs were higher than expected. Skilled workers were still unavailable in town, and the technicians Hangyeol Electronics brought in flew in weekly. Distance had shortened, but a new guest called Business Travel Expenses had quietly taken a seat in the ledgers.

Transportation Cost whispered to Distance, "You shrank, so why won't the invoice shrink?"

"I don't know either," Distance answered sheepishly. "Looks like I only shrank on the map."

That winter, for the first time, Hangyeol Electronics' procurement manager visited the new factory and had lunch. The factory manager stumbled through stories of family, and the manager stumbled through stories of children. The translation app got it wrong twice, and the two of them laughed twice. That afternoon, the quality standards discussion that had dragged on was finished in an hour.

Watching this, Distance understood something. It could be measured in kilometers or dollars, but there was another, more accurate measure. It was a measure like how many times you'd shared a meal together, or on which ring of the phone you'd answer their call.

As spring came, business travel expenses gradually decreased in the ledgers. Transportation Cost still grumbled, but now it sat beside Trust. And Distance, for the first time, was pleased with its name.

In small letters at the corner of the ledger, it wrote: closeness is not determined on a map, but created through time.
