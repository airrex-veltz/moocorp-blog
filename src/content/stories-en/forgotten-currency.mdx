Forgotten Currency

Somewhere in Yeouido, Seoul, an office at two in the morning reeked of empty coffee cups and the hum of fluorescent lights. K scanned back and forth between two monitors, his eyes strained. The left screen displayed an internal database snapshot from the **CYON** exchange. The right held a reconciliation spreadsheet he had painstakingly compiled over four sleepless nights.

The numbers didn't match.

More precisely, they matched too perfectly. Suspiciously flawless, and in a completely wrong direction.

K was a senior analyst in the bank's compliance division. Forty-three years old. Divorced two years ago. His third-grader lived with her mother in Bundang. Every weekend he called his daughter, and their conversations were mostly silence. He hated that silence but never hung up. He sometimes thought that habit resembled his work.

The CYON exchange was the third-largest player in the domestic cryptocurrency market. Six months ago, it had approached the bank for a partnership, submitting due diligence materials. K's team had handled the review. Most figures were straightforward. Except for one trading volume dataset. It caught him. The trading volume of a particular coin pair increased precisely 1.73 times during peak hours. Every day. Without variance.

Nature didn't move that way.

K followed that thread, conducting his comparison work entirely within the bank's internal network. Public reports from chain analysis firms. On-chain data aggregation services. Appendices from CYON's submitted audit reports. When he cross-referenced the three sources, the outline emerged.

CYON was inflating trading volume using clusters of bot accounts. For at least two and a half years. The scale was up to 300 percent above actual trading. The most inflated asset was the exchange's own issued coin. The same coin that had attracted retail investors on launch by touting "the nation's highest liquidity."

K shut down the screen without saving the spreadsheet.

The next morning he sent a request email to his team lead, J. The subject: "Additional Review Needed for CYON Due Diligence." J replied in the afternoon. "Let's talk briefly tomorrow."

J spoke first, closing the conference room door.

"How much have you uncovered?"

K explained without opening the file. It took less than three minutes. J closed the blinds and asked:

"Did you document it?"

"Not yet."

"That's fortunate."

That was odd. K asked slowly:

"What do you mean, fortunate?"

J clasped his hands, looking out the window.

"The partnership is still active. Upstairs sees this as key to new business. CYON itself is in the middle of restructuring, and if this gets out now, the whole industry shakes."

"Isn't that what should happen?"

"That's how you see it." J turned. His expression was exhausted. "But do you know how many people are holding that coin? Eight hundred thousand accounts. Mostly salaried workers. If you blow this open, who's responsible for their losses?"

K didn't answer.

That evening he opened the regulatory complaint portal of Seoul's financial authorities in his browser. He sat for two hours without closing it or submitting anything.

Eight hundred thousand accounts. J wasn't wrong. K knew the numbers too. According to public disclosures, the average balance in retail accounts was in the KRW 2.7 million range. Not a large sum, but not a small one either. Someone's emergency fund, or someone's first investment. And now that money sat atop fictionally inflated figures.

At the same time, he was calculating something else. What would happen if this continued? A bubble pops no matter what. Even if no one popped it. By then it wouldn't be eight hundred thousand but one and a half million, two million who could be caught. Time was never on the bubble's side, always on gravity's.

He raised his hands to the keyboard, then lowered them.

He called his daughter. S picked up, her voice drowsy.

"Dad, it's late."

"Yeah, sorry. I just wanted to hear your voice."

"I did all my homework."

"Good job."

Silence fell. K didn't hang up. Neither did S. Outside, the Yeouido buildings glowed dimly. In some building, some server room, bots were churning out orders tonight. Numbers kept accumulating. As if no one was watching, yet unmistakably.

After hanging up, K reopened the complaint portal tab. His cursor paused over the attachment button.

He hadn't clicked yet.
