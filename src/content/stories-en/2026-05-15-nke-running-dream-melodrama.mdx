"Is this really the right path?"

The rain grew heavier. Standing alone in the parking lot of a track and field stadium, his figure looked forlorn. By day he worked at an accounting firm; by night he traveled to stadiums like this to sell shoes. His colleagues laughed. "What's an accountant doing selling shoes?" His family worried. "How long will you keep this up?" His fiancée Penny held his hand without a word, but anxiety was evident in her eyes.

Blue Ribbon Sports. It was the name of the company he had founded together with Kang Tae-woo Bowerman, a university track coach. It was a humble start, with each investing $500. But to Knight, this was more than just a business. As a track athlete at the University of Oregon, he had achieved only ordinary records. He had no exceptional talent, but he genuinely loved running. He wanted to share that love with more people. If everyone could experience that freedom of circling the track in sweat.

"If there were better shoes, more people could run." That was what Coach Bowerman had said. Those words kindled a fire deep in Knight's chest. He carefully removed and displayed the shoes one pair by one. Even as they grew wet in the rain.

By 1971, the company was on the brink of bankruptcy. Bank loans had reached their limit, and relations with the Japanese supplier Onitsuka were strained. Knight ultimately borrowed money by mortgaging his house. Penny was now the mother of two children. Every night he sat at the kitchen table poring over ledgers, heaving deep sighs. The numbers grew increasingly red, and debt snowballed.

"Honey, you can stop now. You've tried hard enough." Penny said, placing her hand on his shoulder.

Knight shook his head. "Not yet... not yet. Just a little longer." His voice trembled.
