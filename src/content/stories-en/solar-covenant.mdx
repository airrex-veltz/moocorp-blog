Park Min-jun had last looked up at the sun four days before the bid deadline.

Solar panels stood densely on the rooftop, glinting in the afternoon light. His gaze at the completed site held no satisfaction. Holding the completion report, his mind was already running the next calculation.

Haneulbit Energy Tech. Founded 11 years ago. 83 employees. Last year's revenue: KRW 42 billion. And now—a bank balance of KRW 1.7 billion.

The government's announcement to end renewable energy equipment subsidies by year-end had come two months earlier. The industry was in shock. Without subsidies, solar installations were not a simple revenue business from the client's perspective. The payback period would double. Inquiries had noticeably dwindled. Two competitors had already shut down.

But that afternoon, a text arrived on Park Min-jun's phone.

Seohae General Development was opening a public bid for solar installation contractors on a logistics center rooftop. System capacity: 4.8 megawatts. Estimated contract value: KRW 9 billion. Bid deadline: 72 hours.

---

The first night, the Haneulbit Energy Tech office lights never went out.

Park Min-jun stood before a whiteboard, erasing and rewriting numbers. Raw material unit cost, labor, installation period, whether maintenance was included. The figure KRW 9 billion no longer felt as large as it had the first time he saw it. Calculating actual margins came to between KRW 800 million and 1 billion. Even that could be cut in half if material costs fluctuated.

"Gangnam Solar is bidding."

Lee Su-bin, the newest sales team member, held up her laptop screen. Gangnam Solar. 230 employees. Revenue three times Haneulbit's size. A company that had swept up five public bids last year alone.

Park Min-jun turned away from the whiteboard and sat down.

"How much do you think they'll come in at?"

"Below KRW 8.5 billion. They can do it with second-grade materials instead of first-grade."

He said nothing for a moment.

"Prepare the documents. We're going at KRW 8.8 billion."

"At KRW 8.8 billion, the margin will be…"

"I know."

Lee Su-bin fell silent. The printer began running. It was one in the morning.

---

The second morning at ten, Park Min-jun met Choi Jae-won, manager at Seohae General Development.

This was not a scheduled appointment. The bid notice had the manager's name, and Park Min-jun waited in the lobby building entrance with two coffees. When Choi Jae-won stepped out of the elevator, his expression mixed bewilderment and exhaustion.

"You shouldn't be doing this."

"I know. Give me five minutes."

They sat on a sofa in a corner of the lobby.

Park Min-jun pulled out an envelope of documents. Three construction sites where Gangnam Solar had worked over the past three years, along with their maintenance records. Panel replacement rate, monitoring error rate, number of complaints. The numbers spoke without words.

"I know we're more expensive. But if a KRW 9 billion installation loses 30 percent efficiency within five years, the additional cost comes back on you."

Choi Jae-won looked through the documents.

"Where did this data come from?"

"Public warranty claim records and site management reports. Obtained legally."

He took a sip of coffee.

"If I ask to see you again before the final report next week, will you come?"

Park Min-jun stood up.

"Anytime."

---

That afternoon, when he returned to the office, CEO Jung Su-hyeon was waiting.

"Gangnam Solar dropped to KRW 8.2 billion."

Park Min-jun stood without removing his coat.

"Where did you hear this?"

"Their team lead tipped off someone on our side. It's a number we can't reach. If we match them, we'll have to cut something from the project."

"Cut what?"

Jung fell silent for a moment.

"Swapping the advanced monitoring system for the basic one saves KRW 300 million."

Park Min-jun looked toward the whiteboard. The number 8.8 still remained there.

"Then the logic we presented to Seohae this morning falls apart. All the data we showed this morning gets turned upside down."

"I know. But if we bid higher than KRW 8.2 billion, we lose the contract entirely."

The room grew quiet. From outside came the sound of Lee Su-bin taking a call. The printer rang again.

"CEO, if you give me decision-making authority, I'm staying at KRW 8.8 billion."

Jung Su-hyeon studied him for a long time.

"You have to think about the 83 employees too."

"Yes. That's why I'm going with KRW 8.8 billion."

The CEO turned toward the window. For a long time, no one spoke. Outside, buildings lined the skyline, and solar panels sat on some rooftops. Some were Haneulbit's work. Some were Gangnam Solar's. From the outside, they were indistinguishable.

"Do it."

Jung Su-hyeon said. That was all.

---

The final morning at ten. Haneulbit Energy Tech's bid proposal was received by Seohae General Development.

Top-tier equipment specifications. 25-year long-term monitoring contract included. Guarantee of response within 48 hours in case of defects. And on the final page of the proposal, one line was added.

*For solar to be profitable in an age without subsidies, equipment lifespan is everything.*

Park Min-jun received the submission confirmation text and climbed the office stairs to the rooftop.

The building rooftop held nothing. The sun shone dimly from behind gray clouds. He reached for a cigarette, then put it back in his pocket. His fingers tensed, then slowly relaxed.

Results would come Thursday afternoon. Until then, there was nothing to lean on.

Below in the office, Lee Su-bin was organizing next quarter's material supply schedule. CEO Jung Su-hyeon was on a call with his bank contact. Someone brewed coffee, and its aroma rose through the ventilation shaft.

Park Min-jun leaned against the railing.

The first year without subsidies. Panel costs now had to be recovered from the market without support. That weight was heavier than any number. But the sun was still there. At the same angle as yesterday. With the same brightness.

That much had not changed.
