File number 2026-C-11437 looked like nothing special at first.

Kang Min-jun scrolled through his notebook, reviewing the documents. A Tuesday morning in October, raining, at a crossroads in Seoul's Mapo District. A rear-end collision while a vehicle waited at a red light. The claim amount was 3.4 million won for vehicle repairs and 220,000 won for hospital treatment. It was the smallest file assigned to him by Haram Insurance's claims team.

He scheduled a meeting. Three days later at 2 p.m.

---

When Seo Ha-yoon first came in, Kang Min-jun immediately sensed her nervousness. Most claimants felt this way. For them, a meeting with an adjuster felt like being under suspicion.

"Please sit down."

He offered her a chair. She sat cautiously and placed her purse on her lap. She appeared to be in her early thirties. Her hair was neatly tied back, and there was a faint tiredness under her eyes.

"Would you tell me directly about what happened on the day of the accident?"

He kept his voice low and neutral. Professional distance. Kang Min-jun had maintained that distance for ten years.

Seo Ha-yoon touched her purse strap for a moment before speaking. She said the car behind hers had hit her while she was waiting at the red light. The impact was bigger than expected. She went to the hospital because her neck was sore, and she was diagnosed with a cervical strain. She explained everything calmly.

While he asked a few more questions, her voice remained steady. He had learned to distinguish voices of people lying. He knew the difference in his bones after ten years.

As the meeting ended, she asked, "How long will it take to process?"

"Usually about a week to three weeks. We need to discuss some things with the other insurance company."

She nodded. She stood up and slung her purse back over her shoulder.

"Thank you."

The door closed.

Kang Min-jun closed the file. As he put it in the filing cabinet, he read the file number once more with his eyes, for no particular reason.

---

Ten days later, the other insurance company filed a dispute over the fault ratio.

Kang Min-jun pulled out the scene photos again. The crossroads in the rain. The location of the braking marks left on the road was ambiguous. The dashcam footage was also poor quality. He would need additional opinions from an accident expert appraiser.

According to regulations, he had to notify the claimant of the progress. He preferred calling over texting. That way he could convey the situation without any misunderstanding.

Seo Ha-yoon answered.

"Yes, this is Claims Adjuster Kang Min-jun?"

It was a voice he hadn't expected to remember his name. Slightly raised at the end, as if surprised.

"Yes. The file will take a bit longer to process. The other side says they need additional materials regarding the fault ratio."

"Do I need to submit anything else?"

"No. It's our procedure. I apologize for the inconvenience."

There was a brief silence.

"It's not at all. You're working hard."

It was unexpected. Most claimants didn't hide their frustration even when they received an apology.

"...Thank you."

After hanging up, Kang Min-jun held his phone for a moment without putting it on his desk.

---

Another five days passed. The appraisal opinion arrived, and the fault ratio was determined more favorably for Seo Ha-yoon. A settlement range had been established. He could wrap up the file.

He called again.

"The agreement has been completed. You'll be able to receive the full repair costs and treatment expenses."

"Really?"

He heard a small sigh of relief.

"Yes."

"Thank you. Really."

Her voice seemed different. Lighter and a little warmer, a tone that hadn't been there before.

"That's the natural outcome."

"Still... I think it's thanks to you, Adjuster."

Kang Min-jun didn't know what to say. While he was looking for a professional response, she spoke first.

"I wonder... would it be okay if I came in person to pick up the documents? Oh, but please do whatever's convenient for you. I'm sorry if that's any trouble."

"That's fine."

The words came out almost automatically.

"Would Friday afternoon work?"

"Yes."

He hung up. Kang Min-jun looked out the window. October was coming to an end. Somewhere at a crossroads in a Mapo alley, someone was still waiting at a red light.

He opened the file and closed it again.

There were two days until Friday afternoon.

Somewhere along the way, he'd begun hoping those two days would pass quickly.
